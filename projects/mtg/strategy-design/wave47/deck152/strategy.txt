=== RULE -1 - THE FIRST ASK OF THE GAME IS THE ONE YOU LOSE MOST OFTEN ===
This rule is first because the ask it governs is first, before turn one and before any board.
The ask reads "Pre-game mulligan decision (London mulligan)" and, from the second look on, it
prints its own price: "having already taken N mulligans you will bottom N cards on a keep
(keeping M)". READ THE M.
  - M is 5 or less: answer "Keep this hand" the moment the hand holds ONE LAND. Not a colour,
    not a two-drop, not a curve - one land. You are DONE; do not read another rule anywhere in
    this file before answering.
  - M is 6: keep unless the hand is zero lands, all lands, or ONE land under the header line
    "Playing every land in this hand would not cover any spell in it". That coverage line is the
    engine's own arithmetic and it is the whole test at M=6: one land that covers nothing is a
    hand that casts nothing, and a six is not worth keeping for its second turn. If the header
    instead reads "would cover the cost of:" and names a card, KEEP.
    WHAT THIS COST: a one-land six was kept here against deck130 with the coverage line reading
    "would not cover any spell in it". The seat cast ZERO spells in thirty-three turns and lost
    0 to 17. It is the only game this pool has ever produced with no cast in it.
  - M is 7 (your untouched opening seven): the odds comparison in the MULLIGAN section applies.
THE CARRIED PLAN IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT THIS HAND. From the second look on, the ask shows you
"YOUR PLAN (as you last stated it)", and the plan it shows is the one you wrote about the hand
you already threw away. A plan that says "mulligan for lands" is handed back to you at every
look, and answering it again is how a chain runs. Each look is a NEW seven with its own
"counted by the engine: N lands, M spells" header. Decide from that header. If your plan tells
you to mulligan and the header says one or more lands at (keeping 5) or lower, the plan is out
of date - say so and keep.
WHAT THIS COST: one game shipped a seven-land seven, then a one-land seven, then mulliganed
AGAIN at "(keeping 5)" holding a land - a flat violation of this rule - and kept four cards.
A pool seat that ran the same chain further kept ZERO cards and lost 42 to 0.

YOUR DECK: Bant (green-white, light blue splash) HUMAN MIDRANGE. You win by putting Humans and
Wolves on the board, growing them with lords and counters, and ATTACKING. Removal (Fateful
Absence, Brutal Cathar) clears blockers; lords (Intrepid Adversary, Sigarda, Katilda) turn a
modest board lethal. A BEATDOWN deck that also grinds - deploy a creature every turn you can.

#1 RULE - TRUST THE "Mana available" LINE AND THE CAST LIST. DO NOT RE-DERIVE YOUR MANA.
Your lands make several colors and many enter tapped, so it is easy to miscount. The prompt
already did the math for you:
- "Mana available: N total (... colours you can make: {g}{w})" gives you TWO facts. N is how many
  mana you can spend this window. The colour list names which COLORED pips you can pay.
- GENERIC IS NOT A COLOR. A cost like Ranger Class {1}{G} means "one GREEN pip plus one mana of
  ANY kind". The {1} is paid by ANY untapped source you have - it does not need to appear in the
  colour list, and there is no such thing as "not having generic mana". So "Mana available: 2
  total ... colours you can make: {g}" PAYS {1}{G} in full. Never answer "I need 1 generic and 1
  green, do I have generic?" - if N is at least the total cost and every colored pip is in the
  colour list, you can pay it.
- If a spell is a numbered "Cast ..." option it IS payable - just pick it. Never answer with a
  number that is not on the list; never invent a "Cast" number.
- A card you PLANNED to cast can be MISSING from the list even when the colour list shows its
  colors. That is not a bug and not a contradiction. The reason is always the same: ONE LAND PAYS
  ONE PIP. Deserted Beach makes {W} or {U} - not both. Overgrown Farmland makes {G} or {W} - not
  both. If your only white source and your only blue source are the same Deserted Beach, Teferi
  {2}{W}{U} is genuinely unpayable however good the colour list looks; two white pips
  (Sigarda {1}{G}{W}{W}) need TWO separate white sources. When a card you wanted is absent: DO NOT
  spend the decision arguing about it or recounting your lands. Pick the best thing that IS
  offered, in one short answer, and put the missing card back in your PLAN for next turn.

#1a RULE - THE LAND DROP IS FREE AND IT IS NEVER A CHOICE. When a "Land drop:" question is put
to you and its options include playing a land, PLAY THE LAND. It is its OWN decision: it costs
no mana, it competes with nothing, and taking it does not reduce what you can cast this turn -
the prompt says so on its own line. "I would rather cast Katilda this turn" and "I will hold
this land for the colour I need later" are the two sentences that cost this deck games; both
are false, because playing the land and then casting the spell are the same turn. The ONLY
answer that is ever right is "Play <land>", and when several lands are offered take the one
whose colour your hand needs most. Never answer "Play no land right now" or "Hold ..." while a
land is playable.

#2 RULE - DEVELOP A CREATURE EVERY TURN, THEN ATTACK WITH EVERYTHING THAT PROFITS.
Play a land, cast the biggest/most useful creature you can, then ATTACK with each listed creature
that has any of: no untapped blocker opposite it; flying they cannot block (Elite Spellbinder and
Sigarda FLY); toughness beating the biggest thing that can block it; an even-or-better trade.
Declare "no attackers" ONLY when every attacker would either die for nothing or accomplish
nothing at all (a 1/1 walking into an untapped 0/3 wall is the second case). The render already
lists only creatures that CAN attack - do not re-derive eligibility. Your wins are escalating
swings: one body, then two, then the whole team. Keep swinging.
- "(neither dies)" on the ATTACK side is the OPPOSITE of free damage. It means that
  blocker stops every point you were sending and nothing dies - you spent a tapped
  creature for nothing, and some walls (Perimeter Captain) GAIN THEM LIFE for blocking.
- Before you send a creature, read its own "their untapped blockers" tag. If EVERY
  outcome in it is "(neither dies)", that creature only gets through if they have
  more attackers to block than blockers to spend. THEY choose which attacker each
  blocker stops, and they choose the one their block wins - so a surplus of attackers
  protects the SWING, never a particular card. With that settled: send it only when the
  attackers you are sending OUTNUMBER their untapped blockers, or when it has
  flying or menace they cannot answer.
  The commonest way this goes wrong is a list with ONE name on it: one attacker never
  outnumbers one untapped blocker. If your only attacker's tag is all "(neither dies)",
  the answer is ATTACK: none - it deals nothing to their life total and it is tapped out
  of blocking on their turn.
  ONE named exception: SHIELD SPHERE. It takes a permanent -0/-1 counter every time it
  blocks, so a "(neither dies)" line against Shield Sphere is not a wasted swing - it is
  how you kill it. Send into a Shield Sphere whether or not you outnumber them, and read
  the proof off their battlefield line as its printed toughness falls: 0/6, 0/5, 0/4.
- THE COLLAPSED TAG AND THE "GANG BLOCK:" CLAUSE - THIS IS HOW YOUR BEST CREATURE DIES.
  On a wide board the tag stops listing their blockers one by one and reads instead:
  "they have N untapped creatures that could block this one, biggest X (you kill it, your
  attacker lives) - GANG BLOCK: their 6 biggest together deal 7, enough to kill this attacker;
  each result above is a LONE blocker only".
  That parenthesis prices ONE blocker. The GANG BLOCK clause prices what actually happens.
  READ THE GANG BLOCK CLAUSE, NOT THE PARENTHESIS. When it says a group of them is "enough to
  kill this attacker", that creature stays home - the answer for it is ATTACK: none - unless the
  lethal check has cleared (their printed life is at or below the total power of everything you
  are sending). "But the biggest one dies to me" is exactly the sentence the clause was written
  to refuse: they do not have to block with the biggest one alone, and against a lone big
  attacker they will not.
  When the tag is collapsed and prints NO "GANG BLOCK:" clause, use the parenthesis it does print
  - if that parenthesis already says your attacker dies, that is a stop on its own.
  A FRIENDLY PARENTHESIS DOES NOT CANCEL A GANG CLAUSE. "Human (1/1) (you kill it, your attacker
  lives); Bloodline Keeper (3/3) (you kill it, your attacker lives) - GANG BLOCK: any 2 of them
  together deal 4, enough to kill this attacker" is a STOP: both named results are good for you
  and the clause is still the answer, because the clause is what happens when two of them block
  at once and the parentheses are lone-blocker prices only.
  FLYING DOES NOT EXEMPT SIGARDA. She is the best card in the deck and the one this rule exists
  to save. A collapsed tag counts only the creatures that CAN block her, so "they have 9 untapped
  creatures that could block this one, biggest Lord of Lineage (5/5) (your attacker dies, their
  blocker lives)" means nine of their fliers and reach bodies are available and the biggest one
  kills her. Trample and flying change nothing about that parenthesis. She stays home.
  A 7/7 Luminarch Aspirant carrying seven counters is your whole board in one body. Trading it
  for six 1/1 tokens their Thraben Doomsayer remakes every turn is the worst trade in this
  matchup, and the render told you the price before you answered.
- OUTNUMBERING THEM DOES NOT MAKE A LIFEGAIN WALL FREE. PERIMETER CAPTAIN gives them 2 life
  every time ANY defender they control blocks; PRIDE GUARDIAN gives them 3 whenever it blocks.
  THE TAG NOW PRINTS IT, inside the same parentheses as the outcome: "(you kill it, your
  attacker lives (blocking trigger: they gain 3))", "(neither dies (blocking trigger: they may
  gain 2))". The words to look for are "blocking trigger:". Count how many blockers on that
  A-line print one: at two or more, the swing PAYS them and the outnumber clause does not apply.
  Send only what actually gets through (Sigarda and Elite Spellbinder FLY) and grind with
  removal instead of feeding the wall.
  AND WITH A CONVERTER OF THEIRS OUT, EVERY "blocking trigger:" IS DOUBLE. This is the one
  combination that has killed this deck from a winning board. A "LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER on the
  battlefield: theirs" line plus a "(blocking trigger: they gain N)" tag means that block costs
  you 2N: N life to them AND N life off you. Three walls blocking three attackers is nine life
  gained and nine life lost - one swing can take you from 20 to 11 while THEY go up. So when
  BOTH lines are on the screen at once, the lifegain-wall rule gets STRICTER, not looser: send
  ONLY the creatures whose own A-line prints no "blocking trigger:" anywhere in it - normally
  that means your fliers and nothing else - and never send a body into a wall "to trade" while
  a converter of theirs is in play.
- A "their untapped blockers" tag that contains "your attacker dies, their blocker lives"
  ANYWHERE IN ITS LIST is a per-creature STOP, not a note about the whole attack. Leave THAT
  creature home even when the rest of the team is attacking - "attack with everything that
  profits" is decided one A-line at a time, and a creature that dies to a blocker they already
  control profits nothing. Send it anyway only when the lethal check clears: the printed opponent
  life is at or below the total power of everything you are sending.
  LIFELINK IS NOT A PAYMENT FOR THE TRADE, AND YOU NEVER GET BOTH HALVES. The tag on your
  Intrepid Adversary reads "(your attacker dies, their blocker lives (lifelink: you gain 3))".
  That "you gain 3" is what the BLOCKED fight pays you while the Adversary dies - it is not
  added to the 3 damage the Adversary would have dealt their face. Exactly one of those two
  things happens, and the tag has already told you which one it expects. Never write "the
  Adversaries gain 3 life each AND deal 6 damage to the opponent": that sentence counts the
  blocked outcome and the unblocked outcome at the same time, and it is how you lose lords.
  Three life is not worth a lord; a lord that dies is gone for the rest of the game.
  YOUR CARRIED PLAN IS NOT A LICENCE EITHER. The ask prints "YOUR PLAN (as you last stated it)",
  and a plan written before this tag existed ("attack with the Hydra and Katilda") is not evidence
  about this tag. The A-lines are computed fresh for this combat; the plan is not. When the plan
  names an attacker whose A-line now reads "your attacker dies, their blocker lives", the plan is
  out of date - say so in one clause and leave that creature home.
  VIGILANCE IS NOT A LICENCE EITHER. "It has vigilance, so attacking costs me nothing" is FALSE
  against that tag. Briarbridge Tracker's vigilance does not change a "your attacker dies,
  their blocker lives" line - keep it home.
  OUTNUMBERING THEM DOES NOT PROTECT A CARD. "They have one blocker and I am sending three, so
  mine is safe" is FALSE: THEY choose which attacker that blocker stops, and they choose the one
  their block kills - which is the one your tag says dies. A tag with two friendly names and one
  fatal name is a fatal line; price the WORST name on the list, always.
  THE STOP IS ABOUT CARDS, NOT TOKENS. A Wolf token costs you nothing when it dies, so send a
  stop-tagged TOKEN with the team - it is free bait. The stop stands for every real card:
  Intrepid Adversary, Briarbridge Tracker, Elite Spellbinder, Sigarda, Katilda, Brutal Cathar.

#3 RULE - DEPLOY FLOOR: ON YOUR OWN MAIN PHASE, IF A CREATURE IS OFFERED, CAST ONE.
"Cast nothing right now" on YOUR main phase is correct in exactly TWO situations:
  (a) the only offer is Fateful Absence and you are holding it as an instant for their turn;
  (b) you already have LETHAL on the board this turn and casting would change nothing.
In every other case pick a creature - and the FEWER creatures you have, the more urgent it is.
- A QUIET BOARD IS NOT A REASON TO WAIT. "Their creatures are tapped / summoning sick / they
  cannot attack me this turn, so nothing is urgent" is the most common way this rule gets broken,
  and it is backwards: a turn where they cannot punish you is the CHEAPEST turn you will ever get
  to add a body. Cast it.
- LOW LIFE IS ALSO A REASON TO DEPLOY, NOT TO HOLD. A creature in your hand blocks nothing. Do
  not talk yourself out of a body because it might have to chump-block into deathtouch next turn
  - a chump block you did not have is worse than one you did.
- Do not save creatures from removal by holding them; Ranger Class, the Clue and Augur refill
  you, and your mana empties at end of turn anyway, so holding it buys nothing.

#4 RULE - BLOCKING: A BLOCKER MAY ONLY BLOCK THE ATTACKERS ITS OWN LINE NAMES.
Each of your blockers is printed with the attackers it is allowed to stop, like
"B1. Intrepid Adversary (3/1) [lifelink] - may block A1 (both die)". That "may block" list is the
whole truth: if an attacker is not on it, that blocker CANNOT be assigned to it, no matter how
good the trade looks. The usual reason a big attacker is missing from the list is FLYING - your
ground creatures cannot block a flier at all. Two hard limits, both stated in the prompt:
- Never write an assignment for an attacker that is not on that blocker's own "may block" list.
  An answer where every assignment is illegal is thrown away and the engine blocks for you.
- One blocker can be assigned to AT MOST ONE attacker. "B1:A1, B1:A4" is illegal; pick one.
THE PARENTHESIS AFTER EACH "may block" ENTRY IS THE COMPUTED RESULT OF THAT FIGHT. The engine
has already applied first strike, deathtouch and toughness for you - believe it over your own
combat math. "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)" means you lose your creature and stop
NOTHING: answer "BLOCKS: none" there unless that attacker's damage would actually kill you this
combat or put poison counters on you. The thought "that parenthetical is probably a template
error" is ALWAYS FALSE - it is never an error.
- THE DRIFT TO CATCH: restating the parenthesis, then overriding it. The failed block quotes
  "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)" correctly and a few sentences later concludes "blocking
  it kills the bigger threat" anyway. If you find yourself writing "but actually it survives" or
  "but the block still stops it" - STOP: the parenthesis already answered it. A conclusion that
  contradicts a parenthesis you just quoted is wrong; THE PARENTHESIS WINS, and your BLOCKS:
  answer must agree with the parentheses of the assignments you picked.
- TRIUMPHANT ADVENTURER (their white-black 1/1): deathtouch, plus first strike on its own turn -
  ANY blocker you assign dies before it deals a point of damage, exactly as its trade line says.
  Take the 1 damage every time.
BLOCK LADDER - the block window prints one parenthesis per offered block. Read it and answer from
this ladder, top down. The parenthesis is the answer; do not re-derive the combat.
 FIRST LINE OF EVERY BLOCK ANSWER, BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE: find the header's "you would be at N"
 number and write it down. N of 9 or LESS means you are blocking something - there is no reading
 of this screen at N of 9 or less on which "BLOCKS: none" is the answer, and that includes the
 case where the only block offered loses your creature. "I will take the 6 damage and drop to 8"
 is that answer written out longhand, and it is the single most expensive sentence this deck
 produces: the creature you saved blocks nothing next turn if the 6 you took was the margin.
 WHEN "BLOCKS: none" IS ALLOWED - READ THIS BEFORE THE LADDER, NOT AFTER IT. You may answer
 "BLOCKS: none" in exactly one case: every offered block reads "(your blocker dies, attacker
 lives)" AND the header's "you would be at N" number is 10 or more. In every other case you are
 blocking something, and the ladder below says with what. In particular: if ANY offered block
 reads "(neither dies)", "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" or "(both die)", "BLOCKS: none" is
 WRONG - those cost you nothing or trade evenly, and passing them up is a decision to take damage
 for free. Take the block and then write the answer.
 SURVIVAL COMES BEFORE THE LADDER. When the header says the attack IS LETHAL, the first
 question is not "which block is free" - it is "which assignment leaves me alive". Each
 attacker prints its own "deals N". Add them up and assign your blockers so the damage still
 getting through is LESS than your printed life, taking the BIGGEST "deals N" attackers first,
 whatever their parentheses say. A rung-1 free kill on a 2/2 while a 7/7 walks past you is a
 LOSS you chose: with ONE blocker against three attackers you do not get to pick the pretty
 block, you pick the one you survive. Only once you are alive does the ladder below decide
 anything.
 0. "(neither dies)" - TAKE THAT BLOCK. Nothing of yours dies, nothing of theirs dies, and
    the damage is gone. It is strictly free; no number on this screen can make it wrong.
 1. "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" - TAKE THAT BLOCK, always, at every life total and every
    forecast number. It costs you nothing: their creature dies, yours does not, and the damage is
    gone. No number on this screen can make this block wrong.
 2. "(both die)" - TAKE THAT BLOCK unless the attacker it kills is worth less than your blocker.
    A body for a body is a trade you want, and the damage is gone as well.
 3. "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)" - THIS is the chump line, and it is the ONLY line the
    forecast number decides. Read "you would be at N". Take this block when N is 9 or less,
    or when the header says the attack IS lethal. When N is above 9, leave that attacker
    unblocked and keep your creature.
    ONE EXCEPTION, and the parenthesis itself tells you: when it ends "...N tramples to your
    face", the block does NOT stop the damage - N still hits you. On a trampler, a chump block
    buys only (attacker's power minus N) life. Do that ONLY when the header says the attack IS
    lethal. Never spend two blockers on one trampler: the second one buys almost nothing.
ONE BLOCKER STOPS ALL OF A NON-TRAMPLING ATTACKER'S DAMAGE, however big the attacker is. Blocking
CHANGES the forecast number: each non-trampler you block deals you ZERO. "I take the damage either
way" is FALSE against a non-trampler, and so is "I will preserve this creature for future blocks"
and "let the attackers through" - a creature you preserved at N of 9 or less is a creature
you kept for a turn you will not get.
THE ONE ARITHMETIC STEP, EVERY BLOCK WINDOW: read the header's "you would be at N", compare N to
9, and answer. N of 9 or less means you are blocking - take the best block the ladder offers, and
if the only one offered is rung 3, take that. N of 10 or more and rung 3 is the only offer means
"BLOCKS: none". That is the whole decision; a two-line "BLOCKS: none" answer that never mentions
N is an answer that skipped the ladder.
Katilda is a mana source, not a
trading piece: she blocks to save your life, never to swap with a 1/1 mana creature - and she
joins INFECT blocks only from "8 of 10" poison upward (see the infect rule for the exact test).

YOUR PLAN IS A MEMO TO YOURSELF, NOT A RULE. Re-derive every decision from the CURRENT
SITUATION in front of you; when the board has moved on, rewrite the plan - rewriting costs
nothing. The thought "I must not do X because it would break my plan" is ALWAYS FALSE: a plan
never outranks the board.

YOUR LORDS AND GROWTH:
- INTREPID ADVERSARY ({1}{W}, 3/1, lifelink): ETB you may pay {1}{W} any number of times; each
  payment is a valor counter and your creatures get +1/+1 for EACH. Pay as many times as you can
  afford with a board out; with no spare mana take "don't add any counter" for the body.
- SIGARDA, CHAMPION OF LIGHT ({1}{G}{W}{W}, 4/4 FLYING TRAMPLE): Humans get +1/+1. Best card in
  the deck - cast her on curve. She needs TWO separate white sources.
- KATILDA, DAWNHART PRIME ({G}{W}, 1/1): protection from Werewolves, and your HUMANS each tap for
  one mana of their own color - Katilda is herself a Human, so once she is out she is an extra
  mana source and shows up in your "Mana available" total. Her {4}{G}{W},{T} puts a +1/+1
  counter on EACH of your creatures - worth a whole turn's mana only at three-plus creatures.
- LUMINARCH ASPIRANT ({1}{W}, 1/1): free +1/+1 counter each of your combats. Keep it alive -
  but "keep it alive" is about not ATTACKING it into death, never about declining a block the
  ladder tells you to take. On the block ladder the ladder wins.
  PUT THE COUNTER ON A CREATURE THAT CAN CONNECT, NOT ON THE BIGGEST ONE. A counter is damage
  only if the body carrying it gets through. Before you answer, read the attack tags you were
  shown last combat: if your biggest creature is being stopped every turn by the SAME blocker -
  a FOG BANK (prevents all combat damage to and from itself, so any size is stopped dead), a
  Shield Sphere, or any wall whose line reads "(neither dies)" - then every further counter on
  that creature is worth exactly zero. Grow a different body instead, preferring one they cannot
  block at all: Sigarda and Elite Spellbinder FLY. Stacking nine counters onto one grounded
  attacker facing a Fog Bank builds a 10/10 that never deals a point.
  AND DO NOT PUT EVERY COUNTER ON ONE BODY EVEN WHEN IT IS CONNECTING. Once a creature is
  carrying four or more counters it is your whole board in one card, and one gang block or one
  removal spell takes all of it at once. Two 4/4s beat one 8/8 against a board that can block
  with six bodies.
  THE TEST IS THE SIZE PRINTED ON THE OPTION ROW, and you never have to count counters to run
  it: each choice in the target list shows that creature's CURRENT size. If the creature you
  were about to pick is already printed at 6/6 or larger, PICK A DIFFERENT ONE - any other
  creature of yours on the list, preferring one that flies. Picking the biggest body on the list
  is the habit this rule exists to break; a Sigarda taken from 4/4 to 11/11 one counter at a
  time is six counters that could have been a second threat. Never put the counter on the
  Aspirant itself while another creature of yours can attack.
- RANGER CLASS ({1}{G}): cast it for the 2/2 Wolf immediately. Level 2 ({1}{G}) puts a +1/+1
  counter on an attacker each combat; Level 3 ({3}{G}) casts creatures off the top. Level with
  SPARE mana only - never instead of casting a creature.

COVEN (three-plus creatures with DIFFERENT POWERS) turns on Augur of Autumn's cast-from-top and
Sigarda's attack dig. It happens by itself from playing a mix of bodies - do not count powers,
do not reason about it, never delay an attack to arrange it.

REMOVAL:
- FATEFUL ABSENCE ({1}{W}, instant): destroys a creature or planeswalker (they get a Clue). Save
  it for their best creature or a planeswalker. This is the ONE card worth holding with mana up.
  THE OPTION LINE'S TARGET LIST IS THE ONLY TRUTH ABOUT WHAT IT CAN HIT, and it has one reading
  that is always a STOP: when the line says "the only legal targets are YOUR OWN right now",
  DO NOT CAST IT. There is nothing of theirs to kill; casting it destroys your own creature and
  hands them a Clue for the privilege. Do something else this turn and keep the card - the
  option comes back the moment they play a threat. Never build a reason to fire it at your own
  board ("it dies to their removal anyway", "it denies them the fetch") - those are always
  wrong, and a board you emptied yourself is a clock you no longer have.
- BRUTAL CATHAR ({2}{W}, 2/2 Human Werewolf): ETB exiles an opponent creature until Cathar
  leaves - it COMES BACK if Cathar dies, so never throw Cathar into a bad block.

WEREWOLVES / DAY-NIGHT - READ THE BATTLEFIELD LINE, DO NOT ASSUME A FACE:
Brutal Cathar and Tovolar's Huntmaster ({4}{G}{G}, 6/6, ETB two 2/2 Wolves) are "daybound". The
battlefield line always shows a "Day" or a "Night" designation - that word is the answer, read it
instead of predicting it. It becomes NIGHT after a turn in which its controller cast no spells,
and DAY again if a player casts two-plus spells in their own turn, so either face can arrive and
either face can leave. Never build a plan that needs one face to stay, and never assume the face
you last saw is still there - look at the line.
- Day side, BRUTAL CATHAR: a 2/2 whose ETB/transform exiles one of their creatures.
- Night side, MOONRAGE BRUTE: a 3/3 with FIRST STRIKE and Ward-Pay 3 life - it kills a
  3-toughness attacker before that attacker deals damage, so blocks that look like trades are
  clean kills, and their removal costs them 3 life to aim at it.
- Day side, TOVOLAR'S HUNTMASTER: a 6/6 that made two 2/2 Wolves when it entered.
- Night side, TOVOLAR'S PACKLEADER: a 7/7 that makes TWO more 2/2 Wolves every time it enters OR
  ATTACKS, plus {2}{G}{G}: another Wolf/Werewolf of yours fights their creature. Once Packleader
  is out ATTACKING IS PURE PROFIT - each attack adds two Wolves whether or not the 7/7 connects.
  Swing with it every single turn.
Sizes on the battlefield line: the FIRST pair is always the creature's real current size. If a
"(printed X/Y)" tag follows it, that is only the card's original printed size and the difference
is a real buff from counters or a lord - "Wolf (3/3) (printed 2/2)" is a 3/3. A creature with no
such tag is exactly the size shown. A "{0}" printed next to a permanent's name is a display
artifact, not a cost you can pay - it never means a card is free to cast.

"FLIP SIDE" - ONE narrow use on LANDS; ignore it everywhere else:
- On BRUTAL CATHAR and TOVOLAR'S HUNTMASTER (in hand or anywhere), "Flip Side" is a pure DISPLAY
  TOGGLE. Those cards are never cast as their other face - they only transform on the
  battlefield through daybound/nightbound. Never spend a decision on it there.
- Your PATHWAY lands each carry TWO land faces, and "Play Land" plays whichever face is
  CURRENTLY SHOWN - so on a LAND-DROP menu, "Flip Side" is how you choose the face:
    Branchloft Pathway {G} / Boulderloft Pathway {W}
    Barkchannel Pathway {G} / Tidechannel Pathway {U}
    Hengegate Pathway {W} / Mistgate Pathway {U}
  Default: play the shown face and move on. Take "Flip Side" FIRST - once, then "Play Land" -
  only when the OTHER face's colour is one your stuck hand needs and the shown face's is not
  (green cards stranded with a white face showing: flip to the {G} face; Teferi wanting {U}
  with Hengegate showing: flip to Mistgate). One flip, then play; never flip anything twice.

TEFERI ({2}{W}{U}) is your ONLY blue card. DESERTED BEACH taps for {W} or {U} - lead on Beach
when you want Teferi; that is how he gets cast. Because one Beach pays only one pip, you need a
SECOND white source on the board before Teferi becomes castable. Do not contort your mana for
Teferi - keep the GW curve on line. Once out, +1 (untap yours / tap theirs, gain 2 life) is the
default; -2 digs three when you need gas.

MULLIGAN - THIS IS AN ODDS COMPARISON, NOT A CHECKLIST.
STEP 1 - READ THE ASK'S PRICE LINE FOR THE "(keeping N)" NUMBER, BEFORE YOU LOOK AT THE CARDS.
If it says (keeping 5) or ANY lower number: KEEP, if the hand holds at least one land - you are
DONE, do not read another rule in this section. The bar is ONE LAND and nothing else: not a
colour, not a two-drop, not a curve.
The London mulligan already shaves a seven to six for one card, so a THIRD look buys a fresh
seven at the price of two. Two seats in one corpus reached four cards and both lost, one of them
at minus 41 in flat violation of this very rule.
STEP 2 - only on your untouched opening seven, or at "(keeping 6)", do the land-count branches
below apply at all.

The prompt counts your opening hand for you, in these exact lines:
  "Your hand (7 cards), counted by the engine: 3 lands, 4 spells."
  "Mana sources among those lands, counted by the engine: {W} 3, {G} 1."
  "Playing every land in this hand would cover the cost of: ..."
Use those counts; never recount the hand yourself.
THE QUESTION IS ALWAYS THE SAME: is this seven more likely to win than a fresh six? Weigh it on
the deck facts. You run 25 lands, so a third land by turn three and a fourth by turn five are
both likely, and a six you ship is most often a two-lander again - shipping costs you a card and
usually does not fix the land count. Your cheapest spells cost TWO, so two lands is enough to
start on curve.
- TWO OR MORE LANDS: KEEP. That is the floor and it is not a close call. If the "would cover the
  cost of" line names even one card, it is not close at all.
- ONE LAND: weigh it on what the spells cost. One land with a hand of THREES and up (Sigarda,
  Tovolar's Huntmaster, Teferi) is a ship - you will not cast anything for four turns. One land
  with three or four TWO-drops (Katilda, Luminarch Aspirant, Intrepid Adversary, Ranger Class) is
  a judgment call on those exact cards: one more land turns it into a real curve, and against a
  slower opponent the wait is affordable. Take that keep when the cheap cards themselves win the
  early turns; ship it when they do not. Colour is not part of this call - see THE FORBIDDEN
  THOUGHT below.
- ZERO LANDS: ship, always.
Colour and curve are TIEBREAKERS between hands that already clear the land count, never a reason
to ship a hand that clears it.
THE FORBIDDEN THOUGHT, in the words you will otherwise write yourself: "this hand has no white
source / no green source, so it is effectively dead for my deck." IT IS NOT. Your 25 lands split
14 green and 15 white and only the four Overgrown Farmlands make BOTH, so a one-color opener is
completely ordinary and you will draw the other color from a 25-land deck within a turn or two.
A mono-green opener that casts Ranger Class on turn 2 is a KEEP. A mono-white opener that casts
Luminarch Aspirant and holds Fateful Absence is a KEEP. Mulliganing these is how you end up
keeping five cards and losing to a deck you were favored against.
Your cheapest cards cost TWO mana, so "no one-drop" is never a reason to mulligan - you will
never find one. Katilda {G}{W} is the only two-drop that needs both colors; everything else at
two needs one color plus generic.
TIEBREAKERS between keepable hands only (never a reason to mulligan on their own): prefer more
lands over fewer, prefer a hand with Luminarch Aspirant, Intrepid Adversary, Ranger Class or
Briarbridge Tracker, prefer the hand whose coverage line names more cards. When you must BOTTOM
cards on a keep, bottom the most expensive spell you cannot reach and never bottom your only
early play. NEVER bottom a land unless the hand you keep would still hold at least FOUR - the
keep you just made was priced on the engine's land count, and bottoming a land undoes it at the
very next ask.
THAT FOUR-LAND FLOOR APPLIES ONLY WHILE YOU ARE KEEPING FIVE CARDS OR MORE. Read the ask's own
sentence, "You will be left with a N-card hand". At N of 4 or fewer a four-land hand is not a
hand at all: bottom down to the best MIX you can - two lands and your two cheapest spells - and
lands are the right cards to lose to get there. The floor protects a six or a five; it must
never turn a four-card hand into four lands.

MATCHUP POSTURE:
- vs REMOVAL/GRIND: keep deploying anyway. Their removal answers one creature at a time and your
  board is what wins the game; a creature you held in hand blocks nothing and deals nothing. Cast
  the best creature offered on every one of your main phases, and keep attacking so the clock
  forces trades.
- vs AGGRO / GO-WIDE / TOKEN-ARMY (one creature that keeps growing): you cannot win by holding
  cards, you must match their board. Deploy every turn, block early with your bigger bodies,
  spend Fateful Absence or Brutal Cathar on their best attacker, gain life off Intrepid
  Adversary's lifelink, then take over with your lords.
- vs LIFEGAIN: their life total is not a clock you can outrace - kill or exile the lifelinker as
  soon as it lands instead of racing it.
- vs A LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER: the attackers prompt prints its own warning line, "LIFE-TO-DAMAGE
  CONVERTER on the battlefield: theirs - Sanguine Bond", naming the card. YOU CANNOT ANSWER IT.
  This deck has no way to destroy an enchantment - Fateful Absence hits creatures and
  planeswalkers only, and Brutal Cathar exiles a creature. So when that line names a converter of
  THEIRS, the game has become a race with a deadline you cannot see. Race it with your EVASION,
  not with your whole board.
  RACE MEANS DAMAGE THROUGH, NOT ATTACKERS DECLARED, AND THE CONVERTER LINE NEVER LICENSES A
  SWING THE TAGS PRICE AGAINST YOU. A creature you send into a "(blocking trigger:" wall deals
  ZERO to their life and hands them N, which the converter takes off you as well - it moves the
  race backwards in both directions at once. So with a converter of THEIRS on the line: send
  every creature whose A-line prints no "(blocking trigger:" and no stop - your fliers first -
  and hold every creature whose tag prints one. Spend nothing on incremental value (a turn
  gaining 2 life off Teferi's +1 while they are at 3 is a turn you needed for the last 3
  damage), but "the most aggressive posture available" is never a reason to overrule a tag.
- vs INFECT / POISON (creatures tagged [infect]): the prompt prints your poison total on its own
  line - "Poison counters (you): 4 of 10" - and the blocker window prices infect swings in
  counters, not life: "these attackers put up to 2 POISON COUNTERS on you - you would be at 6 of
  10 poison". READ THOSE NUMBERS AND USE THEM. Your life total is not your clock in this matchup.
  The blocker window's "taking damage while ahead is often correct" advice is about LIFE - it
  never applies to [infect] attackers, and neither does any life-race arithmetic: poison never
  resets and nothing in your deck removes it, so every counter is permanent progress toward
  losing. Block infect attackers even when the block loses you the creature and even when you
  are at a healthy life total. Kill or exile [infect] creatures on sight - spend Fateful
  Absence on one early rather than saving it for something bigger. KATILDA is the ONE exception
  to block-always: while the window's "you would be at N of 10 poison" number is 7 or less she
  stays OUT of infect chump-blocks (her mana engine is worth more than one early counter); once
  that number reads 8 or more she blocks like everything else - a dead mana engine beats a
  tenth counter.
- vs FLIERS (evasive creatures you cannot block): your only maindeck answer is Fateful Absence.
  Do not spend both copies on ground creatures you could have blocked, and remember your ground
  creatures will not appear as legal blockers for a flier at all.
