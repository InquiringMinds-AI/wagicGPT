# deck116 -> strategy-writing skill proposals (wave 32)

Seat: Step-0 guideless debut, 1/6, the pool floor. Four proposals; P1 is the one I would fight for.

---

## P1 (PROMOTE — Step 0-bis, a NEW rung) — THE LOW-RECORD STEP-0 SEAT NEEDS THE PRESERVE LEDGER *MORE*, NOT LESS, AND THE FLOOR IS DIAGNOSED BY **WHAT THE DECK NEVER DID**, NOT BY WHAT IT DID WRONG.

Wave-31 HL8 gave the PRESERVE LEDGER to the *strong* Step-0 seat (deck158, 5/6) on the argument
that a loud guide over a perfect seam is self-inflicted overshoot. deck116 is the mirror case and
it breaks the implicit assumption underneath: **a 1/6 record does not mean the seams are bad.**

The evidence: at the pool's floor, this seat posted a **100% cheat-activation take rate (5/5, all
five arrival-traced)**, **9/9 correct tutor picks off 45-option lists**, **2/2 full attacker
declarations**, **3/4 correct blocks including the hard decline**, and **0 fallbacks in 106
decisions**. Every seam the review machinery instruments was healthy. Had I skipped the preserve
ledger because the record was 1/6 — which is exactly what a reviewer's prior invites — I would
have written a loud take-the-activation procedure, a loud attack floor and a target-selection
table, all three aimed at seams with perfect records, and buried the actual leak.

The actual leak was invisible to every per-seam metric, because it lives in **what never happened**:
three of six games ended with **ZERO creatures ever deployed**. There is no seam that "failed" —
the pilot answered a well-formed cast question correctly-looking each time and never assembled
anything.

**THE RULE (two halves, both cheap):**

(a) **Run the PRESERVE LEDGER at EVERY Step-0 seat regardless of record.** Its purpose is not
"reward the good seat" — it is to stop the guide legislating over working seams, and a bad record
increases that temptation rather than justifying it. State the ledger before writing a line.

(b) **At any Step-0 seat, before ranking teaching points, compute the ABSENCE TABLE: for each
game, what did the deck's own plan require that NEVER OCCURRED?** Concretely: count per game the
deck's signature permanents that reached the battlefield, its engine activations, its win-condition
attacks. A column of zeros is the finding, and it will not appear in any per-decision metric,
because *not deploying* is spread across a dozen individually-defensible "cast the tutor" answers.
Rank a class of zeros ABOVE any individually-observed misplay. deck116's absence table (0/0/0
creatures in three games) outranked its two genuinely wrong blocks 3:2 and is what rules #1, #2
and #5 of the guide are written against.

The instrument this generalises is the skill's own arrival-tracing doctrine, moved one level up:
arrival-tracing asks "did this attempt land?"; the absence table asks "was the attempt ever made?"
Same blind spot (a counter that only records failures cannot see a non-event), one rung earlier.

**Cost:** one aggregation pass over the seat's narration per game. Single-seat, but I recommend
adopting (a) immediately as an unconditional widening of an existing rung, and tagging (b) for
promotion on a second Step-0 seat whose losses show a zero column.

## P2 (ADOPT — Step 0-bis rule 3, a SEVENTH idiosyncrasy face) — THE TWO-HALVES DECK: THE PILOT PLANS TOWARD THE HALF IT CANNOT REACH.

Rule 3's existing faces are belief-that-VETOES (17/21/59), card-text FABRICATION (62), the target
SUB-MENU seam (49), the SYMMETRIC-cost card read as one-sided (102), the ALTERNATIVE-COST DUAL
OPTION mislabel (137, closed-by-fact), and the SYMMETRIC effect asymmetric in your favour (59).

deck116 is a seventh and distinct shape: a deck built from **ENABLERS and PAYOFFS that are useless
apart**, where the payoffs are printed with real, enormous mana costs. The pilot reads the printed
cost, treats it as a plan, and ramps toward a number it can never reach — while declining real
bodies to do it. Witness, `vs36 s14` at **7 life**, verbatim:

> *"Casting Farseek achieves three things: 1) It puts an additional land onto the battlefield,
> accelerating my mana base significantly towards casting Ulamog or Kozilek next turn."*

with `Cast Kitchen Finks {1}{g/w}{g/w} (3/2)` sitting in the same option list, on six lands,
against a lethal board. The same seat declined Finks at s7 and s12 too. It is NOT fabrication (the
{11} is real), NOT a veto (nothing is being refused as unpayable), and NOT a misrender — the render
is perfectly honest. It is a **plan built on an arithmetically unreachable state**, and the printed
cost is what invites it.

**Distinguishing tell:** the pilot's PLAN line names a future turn on which it will cast a card
whose cost exceeds the deck's maximum achievable mana. That is mechanically checkable while
authoring — compute the deck's realistic mana ceiling from its land count and ramp, and compare.

**Authoring corrective (matches the rung's teach-never-suppress doctrine):** state the
unreachability as a FLAT FACT with the number, name the FORBIDDEN THOUGHT in the pilot's own
phrasing, and give the positive substitute in the same breath. deck116's rule #1 is the template:
*"You have 24 lands and no rituals. You will never reach ten mana ... THE FORBIDDEN THOUGHT: 'I
will ramp toward casting Ulamog next turn.' That plan never completes. DO THIS INSTEAD: ..."*
A bare "these are expensive" does not countermand it; the pilot already knows they are expensive
and is planning around it.

**Companion sub-rung — the ORDER teach.** For any enabler/payoff deck, the guide owes an explicit
deployment ORDER with its reason ("the enabler is live with any of your 20 creatures; the payoff is
a dead card without it"), because the pilot's failure was never *choosing wrongly between two
plays* — it was choosing them in the wrong sequence across turns, which no single decision's
options list can reveal. This is the wave-16 deck62 SEQUENCING rung (cast the Enchantress FIRST)
generalised from within-turn ordering to across-turn ordering. Two seats now → I would promote the
sequencing rung's scope on this basis.

## P3 (ADOPT — sharpens wave-31 HL3(b), PAYOFF-PERMANENT PRESERVATION) — THE OFF-RAMP CONDITION MUST BE THE *LETHALITY* OF THE DAMAGE, NOT THE LIFE TOTAL, AND ALT-WIN DAMAGE NEEDS ITS OWN CLAUSE.

HL3(b) prescribes the preservation teach as *"when <engine> is your only untapped blocker and the
incoming damage is not lethal, TAKE THE DAMAGE ... carrying the life-total condition so the rule
has an off-ramp when the damage IS lethal."* deck116 supplies the case that breaks the life-total
form of that off-ramp.

`vs105 s8` (t6) and `s11` (t8): the pilot chump-blocked Elvish Piper — its engine card, the thing
the entire deck is built around — into `A2 (your blocker dies, attacker lives)`, the branch the
render explicitly labels as a pure loss, **twice**, at **21 and 23 life**. A life-total-keyed
off-ramp ("only chump when low") would not have fired: it was never low. The trigger was **INFECT**.
The pilot was defending a resource — poison counters — that (i) is not measured in life and
(ii) **is not rendered anywhere in the prompt at all** (notes N-116b), so it could neither price
the trade nor observe that it was losing the race it was paying for. It lost that game at 23 life.

Positive control, same seat: `vs139 s11`, offered only losing Piper blocks against ordinary
attackers, it correctly answered `no blockers`. **The rule is known; the alt-win resource breaks it.**

**THE REFINEMENT, two clauses:**

(a) Phrase the preservation off-ramp on **LETHALITY OF THIS COMBAT**, not on a life bracket:
*"answer 'no blockers' and take the damage; the ONLY exception is that the damage kills you THIS
combat."* A life-bracket form is the wave-31 deck44 hoisting problem in miniature — it hands the
pilot a mode it can enter early and reason from.

(b) **Name the alternate-win resource explicitly in the preservation clause when the pool contains
a deck that uses one** — *"this holds even against infect creatures and poison counters"* — because
the generic clause silently excludes it: poison is not life, so a rule about life does not bind, and
the pilot will construct the trade as free. This is the deck122 alt-win rung (HL6) inverted: HL6
teaches the deck that OWNS an alt-win to plan toward it; this teaches every OTHER deck not to
over-pay against one it cannot see. **Prerequisite flagged, not assumed:** the durable fix is the
representation (render the poison count); the guide clause is the interim, and I have routed the
representation item to notes rather than pretending prose closes it.

## P4 (ADOPT, cheap — an authoring INPUT, not a doctrine change) — MINE THE DECK FILE'S OWN `#HINT:` LINES BEFORE DRAFTING.

`Res/ai/baka/deckN.txt` files carry author-written hint lines consumed by `AIHints` on the Baka
path and **verified absent from the GPT prompt**. deck116's are:

```
#HINT:dontattackwith(Elvish Piper)
#HINT:dontblockwith(Elvish Piper)
#HINT:combo hold(Quicksilver Amulet|myhand)^cast(Quicksilver Amulet|myhand)^...^totalmananeeded({4})
```

The first two are **exactly** the teach this seat lost a game for lacking, and I would have had it
before reading a single translog. The third encodes the deploy-the-Amulet priority that became
guide rule #2.

**THE RULE:** add the deck file's `#HINT:` block to Step 0-bis's "read the deck deeply" input list
and to the Inputs section, with two guard rails carried over from existing rungs: (i) hints are the
deck AUTHOR's intent, not ground truth — verify each against the primitives and the corpus before
it becomes a guide line (the untrusted-card-fact rail applies to hint authors too); (ii) a hint is
a HYPOTHESIS about a seam, so where the corpus contradicts it, the corpus wins. Cost: one `head`
of a file already being read. I would take this even at zero doctrinal weight.

## Non-proposals (recorded so the synthesis does not have to re-derive them)

  CONSTRUCTION-CEILINGED — the existing categories correctly do not fit, because the losses are not
  power losses. Resisting the pull to file a 1/6 seat under a construction label *is* the rung
  working; findings (c) states the decomposition instead.
- **No rarity claim anywhere in the guide** (HL6 self-check performed): the corpus never exercised
  Pelakka Wurm, Urban Evolution, Jin-Gitaxias or a Piper activation targeting a hand-full of
  choices under pressure. The guide states each card's RULE and both outcomes and asserts no
  frequency about any of them. My own findings mark i5/i6/i4/i10/i14/i15 UNEXERCISED; grepped the
  draft for "rarely / usually / most of the time / typically" — zero hits.
- **HL5 path-scoped sweep:** the one render string I claim is broken (the whole-library reveal) was
  checked on BOTH emitter framings at this seat (`<1>` singular and un-tokened subset) and both are
  unfiltered — so the finding is not path-scoped, and the fix must not be either (notes N-116a/d).
