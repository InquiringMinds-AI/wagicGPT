# deck126 - proposals for the strategy-writing skill (wave 49)

S1 - A WIN-BUTTON LIST IS ORDERED BY WHAT IS ON THE MENU IN THE WINDOW THAT ARISES, AND EACH ENTRY
IS KEYED TO ITS ROW'S PRINTED COUNT. deck126's Rule #2 listed five triggers in the order blocks,
blocks, Staff, Tribute, Vampires - the first three need a combat or a card the seat did not have,
and the executor, holding both enchantments with `Cast Tribute to Hunger {right now: they control
7 creatures}` on the menu, wrote "since no creatures are on the battlefield, wait for the opponent
to attack" for three windows (`vs152` seq 24, 25, 27) before pressing it. The belief that a
life-gain trigger needs ITS OWN creature is the executor's default; a list that leads with
combat triggers confirms it. Rule: lead the list with the entry that is a MENU ROW on the seat's
own turn, quote the row's `{right now: ...}` string, and say in words that it needs nothing else.

S2 - A TWO-CARD COMBO IS NAMED AS TWO DIFFERENT NAMES, AND THE GUIDE SAYS WHAT A SECOND COPY OF
ONE NAME IS NOT. `vs162` seq 12: the seat cast a second Exquisite Blood and wrote "Both
enchantments are now on the battlefield" while Sanguine Bond was in its library, then waited for
a loop that did not exist. The prompt was true (`Exquisite Blood #1-#2 x2` after the cast); the
executor pattern-matched "two enchantments" to "both enchantments". Rule: any guide whose win is a
named PAIR states the pair as "one <A> AND one <B>, two different names", names the collapsed
render form of a duplicate (`<A> #1-#2 x2`) as NOT the pair, and puts "a copy of a name already on
your battlefield line is entry nothing" into the cast-priority list. Render proposal in
`general-suggestions.md` G3 for the copy annotation.

S3 - #118'S THIRD-CORPUS TRIGGER FIRES ON A SHAPE THE GUIDE'S OWN ANECDOTE ALREADY NAMES. deck126's
STEP 1 carried "Pride Guardian on Nadaar" as its example and the seat did Pride Guardian on Nadaar
again (`vs146` seq 11, third corpus). When the anecdote IS the failure, the rule's key is wrong,
not its evidence: re-key to the printed string ("a B-row whose EVERY parenthesis reads 'your
blocker dies, attacker lives'"), state the answer as the reply line ("BLOCKS: none when it is your
only blocker"), and cut the second anecdote. Adds to #118's "shrink, re-key" step: an anecdote that
matches the new failure is evidence the rule was keyed to the story, not the surface.
