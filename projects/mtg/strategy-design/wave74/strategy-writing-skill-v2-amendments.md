## Amendment 333 — a heading is a scope declaration, and the pilot enforces it

**Fold into** §4.1 (rule phrasing), beside the "One rule, one place, at its firing seam" bullet and
A306 ("a rule must be true at the window it FIRES in").

**Text.**

> A guide's headings are read by the pilot as SCOPE. A rule filed under a heading is applied to that
> heading's subject and refused elsewhere, so a heading whose verb another section owns as ITS
> imperative hands the rule to that section. Audit every heading against the guide's own verb
> inventory: no heading may use a verb another section uses imperatively (`SEND`, `BLOCK`, `ATTACK`,
> `KEEP`, `BOTTOM`, `CAST`, `PUT`). A rule that must hold across sections either names its scope in
> its own first clause ("this paragraph is about CASTING") or is restated at the top of the
> first-match list the window it governs is read from. A rule filed once, under a heading, is a rule
> with a scope — including an absolute.

**Why it is not A174 or A307.** A174 restates a deciding card FACT at each phase where it decides;
A307 permits exactly that as the one exception to one-rule-one-place. Neither says the heading itself
is the scope gate, neither covers brakes and absolutes, and neither gives an audit. This amendment is
the mechanically greppable form: heading verbs against imperative verbs.

**Paid by.** wave74/deck162 (deck-162 guide: `LAST CHECK BEFORE YOU SEND.` closing the brake section
while §ATTACKING opens `SEND when the A-line prints NO tag at all`). The pilot re-derived the scope
from the heading three times in one casting decision and discarded a correct, printed, quoted
absolute each time; two further takes of the same row produced all three of the corpus's
`plan_names_stranded_card`. Seqs are in wave74/deck162/review.md §2 and its guide-edit table (row A).

## Amendment 334 — never carry a rule's hard case in a concessive clause

**Fold into** §4.1 (rule phrasing), beside A12 ("absolutes split on the edge case, edge case FIRST")
and the §5 anti-pattern "rules stated only as their exception".

**Text.**

> Never carry a rule's hardest case in a concessive clause. "Take A even when B" is read as "take A
> WHEN B": on a board where B is false the pilot takes the other row and cites the guide for it.
> State the ordering flat, then close BOTH directions of the quantity the rule refuses to trade
> against ("the life they gain never decides this row, in either direction: the wall goes when it
> pays them more, and the punisher stays when it would pay them less"), and name the losing trade in
> the pilot's own words as the thing being refused. Audit: grep every guide for `even when`, `even
> if`, `regardless of`, `no matter`, `however <adj>` and rewrite each hit flat.

**Why it is new.** v2 has no rule on concessive connectives; A12 orders an absolute's edge case, and
the §5 anti-pattern forbids a rule stated ONLY as its exception. This is a different failure: the
rule is stated, its hard case is attached concessively, and the concession inverts into a necessary
condition when read.

**Paid by.** wave74/deck162 (§FORCED SACRIFICE: `take the wall even when its toughness pays them MORE
life`). The pilot quoted the concession accurately and used it to justify the exact play it exists to
forbid, sacrificing the deck's only draw punisher on turn 9. Seq in wave74/deck162/review.md
guide-edit table (row C).
