# deck123 - proposals for the strategy-writing skill (wave 48)

Only what this seat's evidence supports. Numbers in `seat-123-130.md`.

**S1. A RULE WRITTEN AGAINST A RENDER THAT HAS SINCE CHANGED IS FALSE, AND A FALSE SENTENCE IN
A GUIDE COSTS TRUST ON EVERY TRUE ONE. (candidate amendment)** deck123's RULE 1 said "These
windows routinely list exactly one action ... and no 'pass' row" and "at 1,541 creatures the
attack screen handed this deck a 233,662 character prompt". Lane N put `0. Pass priority` on
63/63 of those windows; lane O made the 257-creature attack screen 11,274 chars. Both sentences
were the WHY behind a hard rule (the 50 ceiling) and the pilot crossed that rule four times in
one game without the predicted cost - the guide had asked for a behaviour whose reason the pilot
could see was gone. Rule for the reviewer: every wave, diff the guide's quoted render strings
and its quoted costs against the current emitter (the carry lists every lane's new strings);
a rule whose WHY names a render the engine no longer produces is retired or re-justified in the
same wave, never carried. The trust doctrine runs both ways - the guide may not describe a
screen the pilot will not see.

**S2. WHEN THE ENGINE ADDS A ONE-DECISION FORM OF A REPEATED ACTION, THE GUIDE'S JOB IS THE
ARITHMETIC AND THE EXACT SPELLING, NOT THE STOP.** The repeat row moved the loop from "take it
again every window until a stop fires" to "name N once". The stop condition (L + C + 3) was
already right; what the pilot lacked was (a) N = (L + C + 3) - M as a formula and (b) the one
spelling the parser reads (`x17`, not `N=17`). Three of four wrong spellings reproduced the
ROW's own words ("repeated N times, then stop") rather than its example. When a rendered row
carries a reply grammar, quote the grammar in the guide as an instruction about what to EMIT
(#79) and show the wrong spellings that were seen. Also: this is the parser's item first (seat
file H1); the guide line is the cheap side.

**S3. THE CAST LIST IS A FIRST-MATCH LIST, SO A REMOVAL SPELL THAT MUST BEAT A MAKER ON A
SPECIFIC BOARD NEEDS AN ENTRY ABOVE THE MAKER, NOT A SENTENCE IN THE CARD'S RULE.** RULE 3
said "0 against 2 or more: the best Damnation there is. Take it"; the cast list ranked makers
1-2 and removal 6; the pilot cast the maker into four attackers at 4 life (vs152 seq 25) and
the dig at 0-vs-4 (vs130 seq 23). This is #70 (the exception lives above the imperative it
excepts) applied to ordered lists: a conditioned exception to an ordering must be a NUMBERED
ENTRY at the position where it wins, keyed to the printed counts. Supporting instance, not a
new amendment.

**S4. REFUTE THE PRINCIPLE (#77) - two more instances of one false principle: "a printed row
is not really available".** "We have no blockers" under a `B1.` row (twice, one lethal); "Cast
Idyllic Tutor to find a creature" after writing "Idyllic Tutor finds an enchantment" one reply
earlier. Both are the pilot deciding that a fact it just read does not bind it. The refutation
that generalises is "a row the screen prints IS the thing it says" - and the guide's version
names the row (`B1.`), the false sentence, and the price paid. Supporting instance for #77 and
#82's "branch on the token's presence".

**S5. WATCH ITEM, not an amendment: an "of their choice" rule priced for a competent opponent
may be mispriced against Baka.** RULE 5's count gate assumes the opponent gives up its worst
body. At N=4 (vs152 seq 18) Baka sacrificed Sigarda, its 6/6. One sample. If a second N>=2
edict returns their best body, the count gate is a false price in this engine and should be
re-derived from what Baka actually does, not from the rules text.
