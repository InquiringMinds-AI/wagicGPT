## THE CROSS-DECK HEADLINE — one rule, three guides, 0 of 7

Every guide on this seat carries a per-creature STOP on the attack tag
`(your attacker dies, their blocker lives)`. **It was offered 7 times on a non-token CARD and
violated 7 times. Five of the seven cards died.** It is in the causal chain of three of my seven
losses. The three guides fail it through three *different* loopholes, so the fix is per-guide.

| 3 | 146 vs123 s23 | Nadaar, Selfless Paladin | collapsed: 12 untapped, biggest Vampire (4/4) "(your attacker dies...)" | yes | `Your Nadaar, Selfless Paladin died` |
| 4 | 152 vs162 s20 | Intrepid Adversary #1 | Fate Unraveler (3/4) "(your attacker dies, their blocker lives (lifelink: you gain 3))" | yes | — |
| 5 | 152 vs162 s28 | Intrepid Adversary #1 | same | yes | `Your Intrepid Adversary died` |

**The three loopholes, each verified against the reply text:**
- **deck146 — an EXCEPTION collides with the STOP on the same A-row.** The guide's Shield Sphere
  carve-out ("Send into a Shield Sphere whether or not you outnumber them") and the STOP both
  apply to the same Silencer line, and nothing states which wins. Reply s20: "Attack with Silencer
  and Goblin to chip damage and reduce Shield Spheres" — the exception is the sentence it obeyed.
  Amendment #6 (state precedence on every note that can collide). -> EDIT 146-D.
- **deck152 — the parenthesis's own benefit tail is the loophole.** Reply s28, verbatim: "The
  Intrepid Adversaries gain 3 life each via lifelink (total +6 life) **and deal 6 damage to the
  opponent** (reducing them to 4)." Those two cannot both happen: the tag says the lifelink gain
  is what the fight pays while the attacker dies. This is amendment #30's rationale-loophole shape
  with a new lever (lifelink, not vigilance). -> EDIT 152-B.
- **deck162 — the stop was broken under life pressure, with no counter-argument written down.**
  Reply s17 is a mana-arithmetic monologue that never engages the tag at all. -> EDIT 162-B.

**And the shared arithmetic error, present in 4 of the 7:** all three guides tell the seat that
outnumbering their untapped blockers makes the send safe (146 and 152 word it as a token
carve-out; the model generalised it to cards). It does not: **the defender chooses which attacker
to block, and they choose the one their block kills.** At 152 vs162 s20 there was ONE untapped
blocker and TWO attackers, and the Fate Unraveler duly ate the Adversary rather than the Wolf that
would have killed it.

## ENGINE / RENDER ITEMS

**E-1 (MED) — the `(your attacker dies, their blocker lives (lifelink: you gain N))` tail invites a
double-count.** Repro: deck152 vs deck162 `attackers` seq 28; reply verbatim: "The Intrepid
Adversaries gain 3 life each via lifelink (total +6 life) **and deal 6 damage to the opponent**."
Both cannot happen — the lifelink gain is what the *blocked* fight pays while the attacker dies; the
6 face damage is what happens *instead*, if they decline. Every other clause in this parenthesis
family describes the blocked outcome, so the tail is consistent — but on a line whose head says
your attacker dies, a bare benefit reads as an addition. Fix shape: attach the outcome to the
clause, e.g. `(your attacker dies, their blocker lives (lifelink: you gain 3 from the block, and
this attacker deals nothing to their life))`. 3 records, 1 dead card.

**E-2 (MED) — the `becomes beholder` option row does not say the Hive is already animated.**

`Hive of the Eye Tyrant (3/3) [menace]` and "of which 1 is a creature", the option row reads only
`becomes beholder with Hive of the Eye Tyrant [cost: {3}{b}]`, and the answer is to pay again.
10 wasted 4-mana activations in one game, 78 offers corpus-wide. The state is on the board line but
not where the decision is made. Fix shape: annotate the row with the target's current state
(`{already a 3/3 Beholder until end of turn - paying again adds nothing}`), the way the removal
rows already carry `{target text:}` and Barrowin already carries `{right now: returns NOTHING}`.
This is the same convention, applied to an activated-ability row.
