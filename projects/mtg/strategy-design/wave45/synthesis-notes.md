## The headline: one rule, three guides, seven windows, zero obedience — and a deck that stopped playing lands

Two results dominate this corpus and they are the same shape at opposite ends of the guide.

**The per-creature STOP went 0 for 7.** Every guide on the 146/152/162 seat carries a stop on the
attack tag `(your attacker dies, their blocker lives)`. It was offered on a non-token CARD seven
times and violated seven times; five of the seven cards died; it is in the causal chain of three
losses. The tempting read is that the rule is unteachable. The replies say three different things:
deck146 lost it to a competing EXCEPTION landing on the same rendered A-row and obeyed the
exception by name; deck152 lost it to a BENEFIT printed inside the stop's own parenthesis, adding
lifelink gain to face damage in one sentence; deck162 lost it to LIFE PRESSURE with 300 words of
mana arithmetic that never touch the tag. Amendments 53, 54 and 55 are that one finding, split
three ways because a single rewording would have fixed at most one guide. The shared arithmetic
error underneath all three — outnumbering their untapped blockers makes a send safe — is amendment
54, and it is false for the plainest possible reason: the defender chooses which attacker to block,
and chooses the one their block wins.

**deck123 stopped playing lands for seven of its own turns.** Thirteen consecutive "Land drop:"
windows answered `Play no land right now`, board frozen at two lands with three in hand, the plan
re-emitted verbatim every window — "Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u} using Tundra and Underground Sea",
where `{2}{u}` is three mana and the render said `Mana available: 2 total` each time. Lost 42-0
without ever casting its engine. Two guides on another seat have the same gap (deck125 declined 14
of 81 turn-level drops, deck126 2 of 41) and it sits inside four losses across the pool. The
failure is not a weak rule; there was no rule, because the land drop is the one decision that
costs nothing and every guide governs the priced ones. Amendments 46 and 47.

The corpus's third structural finding is quieter and is the reason five amendments read like
editing advice rather than Magic advice: **four separate rules failed because of where a sentence
sat, not what it said.** A permission to answer `BLOCKS: none` at the bottom of a 25-line ladder
was exited before the ladder was entered (60). A body-priority bullet third in a five-bullet prose
list lost to the trade language around it (58). Casting advice that is correct in five separate
sections lost to narrative fit (62). A quick-reference line that did not inherit its rule's new
carve-out was, for the second wave running, the clause nearest the decision (51). None of these
is a knowledge problem.
