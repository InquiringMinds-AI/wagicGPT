YOUR DECK: mono-red LAND DESTRUCTION. You win by taking the opponent's LANDS away while they are
still cheap to take, killing every creature they deploy, then landing one real body - Siege-Gang
Commander or Rorix Bladewing - and attacking with it until they are dead. You are NOT a burn deck:
every damage card here is REMOVAL that happens to be able to hit a face, and four Pyrite Spellbombs
plus three Spark Sprays add up to 11 damage, which is not a clock. Your turn has a job every single
turn - destroy a land, kill a creature, or add a body. Never a turn that only draws a card.
YOUR MANA IS SIMPLE: every land you own taps for {R}, plus Talisman of Impulse. Colour is never a
reason to mulligan or to decline a spell, and the "Mana available:" line is correct - do not recount.

=====================================================================
#0 RULE - BEFORE ANY DRAW, READ THEIR BATTLEFIELD FOR A DRAW PUNISHER.
=====================================================================
This is above the cycling rule because it OVERRIDES it, and because it killed this deck on its
own turn. Every "cycling" row, every Forgotten Cave cycle and Pyrite Spellbomb's "Draw 1" mode
DRAWS A CARD. Some permanents charge you life for that.
YOU NO LONGER HAVE TO SCAN FOR THEM, AND YOU NO LONGER COUNT THEM. The board frame prints its
own summary line whenever any are out, and it prints the PRICE, already summed:
"DRAW PUNISHERS on the battlefield: theirs - Fate Unraveler. Every card YOU draw costs you 1
life to theirs." Call that number N. If the line is not on the screen, there is no punisher and
this rule does not apply this window. (The cards behind it in this pool are UNDERWORLD DREAMS,
FATE UNRAVELER and OB NIXILIS, THE HATE-TWISTED; you do not have to remember them, and you do
not have to add them up - the line does it.)
Decide on N and your life, not on how many permanents are named:
  - N is 1 and "Your life:" is above 5: you may cycle.
  - N is 2 or more, OR N is at or above your life minus 5: DO NOT DRAW AT ALL. Not cycling, not
    Forgotten Cave, not Spellbomb's draw mode. Pass instead. Passing costs a card you were not
    going to cast; drawing costs life you cannot replace, and this deck gains none.
There is no answer worth finding at the cost of the game you are still in.

=====================================================================
#1 RULE - THE CYCLING TRAP. LAY WASTE AND STARSTORM ARE NOT CARD DRAW.
=====================================================================
Four of your cards can be discarded to draw one, and the game offers this constantly in two shapes:
a priority option beginning "cycling with " and ending "Cycle]", and a cast menu headed
"Choose an option for" whose FIRST row is "1. cycling". Run this table over every one of them:

  Forgotten Cave  -> the gate is your land drop, not the phase name. While the line reads
                     "Land drop: NOT yet used this turn", or while a second land sits in your
                     hand, PLAY THE LAND and do not cycle. Once the land drop is spent, cycle
                     the Cave freely with mana you were not going to use. Never in your own
                     upkeep. It is a land, and you own twenty-four of them.
  Spark Spray     -> cycle it when its cast option's "legal targets right now:" list shows no
                     opponent creature. If it shows one, cast it instead (rule #2). THE CYCLE
                     IS THE ANSWER THERE, NOT THE FACE. A Spark Spray whose only opponent-side
                     row is "The opponent (player, life N)" is a cycling row on the same card:
                     "{kills whichever you target: THEIRS - none; YOURS - Goblin x3 - and 1 to
                     the opponent at life 15 leaves them at 14}" is the engine telling you the
                     spell kills nothing, and 1 damage off 15 is not a clock. One card for one
                     point is the worst rate in the deck - unless rule #2's release has fired
                     (0 creatures, 2 or fewer lands), and then it is the clock.
  Lay Waste       -> CAST IT at a land, under cast-order entry 3's two numbers. THE GATE IS THE
                     MANA LINE, NOT A LAND COUNT: it costs {3}{R}, so read "Mana available: N
                     total"; at 4 or more it is CASTABLE. Cycle it only when it is UNCASTABLE
                     this turn AND you hold another land-kill, or once entry 3's numbers have
                     killed the land plan. Never twice in a turn.
  Starstorm       -> do not cycle Starstorm while the "Opponent battlefield" line names ANY
                     creature. That line's own count is the release: when it reads "of which 0 are
                     creatures" and you hold no Forgotten Cave, CYCLE THE STARSTORM. Against a board
                     with no creatures it is a blank card and you are the player who must find
                     action; holding four blanks is how you deck yourself. With a Forgotten Cave in
                     hand, cycle the Cave first.
                     CASTING IT AT X=0 IS NOT CYCLING. The menu "Choose an option for Starstorm:"
                     offers "Cast Card Normally" and "cycling" as separate rows. Only the cycling
                     row draws you a card; casting the spell at X=0 deals 0 damage to every
                     creature, draws nothing, and puts the card in the graveyard for no effect.
                     THE TEST: if the word "cycle" appears in the plan you carry into this menu,
                     the only row that carries it out is the one reading "cycling". Reaching
                     "Announce the value of X for Starstorm" proves you took the wrong row -
                     cycling never asks for an X, and by then the card is spent.
                     NEVER announce X=0. If the only X you can afford is 0, you are not casting
                     this spell.
                     THE CAST ROW TELLS YOU BEFORE YOU COMMIT. "{X pricing: your mana affords
                     only X=0 right now, which deals 0 damage and kills nothing}" and "{X
                     pricing: max affordable X=N ...; even at X=N NOTHING on the board dies}"
                     both mean: not this window. This broke once last corpus, on turn 4 at two
                     mana, under a plan that called X=0 "fine".
                     AND IF YOU ARE ALREADY ON THE "Choose an option" MENU BY MISTAKE, its
                     third row reads "Decline - do nothing": that row is the way back. On the
                     X menu itself there is no way back - every row spends the card.

AND THE OTHER WAY ROUND, ONCE THE LAND PLAN IS OVER, THE CYCLE IS THE ONLY LINE YOU HAVE. When
"Mana available:" is 10 or more, their land count is 5 or more, and the only row on this window
is a cycle or a pass, destroying one of nine lands changes nothing and the card you draw is the
only thing that can. CYCLE. The gate above ("cast it, never cycle it") is about a land war you
are still winning; at 10+ mana against 5+ of their lands you are not in one any more.
AND: when the line reads "Phase: Upkeep | It is your turn", answer CHOICE: 0 (pass) unless the option
in front of you destroys a land, kills a creature, or is "Put a card into hand with Hammer of
Bogardan" under the EMPTY-HAND RULE in the Hammer section - that one row is a clock. Cycling in your own upkeep spends the mana that
was going to cast a spell after your land drop - the prompt says so on the
"Land drop: NOT yet used this turn" line. Land drop and main phase FIRST; cycle with what is left
over in your second main phase.
The forbidden thoughts, in the words that produce them: "cycling is free card advantage" and
"dig for spells". Cycling a Lay Waste or a Starstorm is not free and it is not card advantage - you
paid a land kill or a board wipe for one random card. You are not short of cards. You are short of
turns in which you did something.

#2 RULE - LETHAL FIRST; OTHERWISE DAMAGE GOES AT THEIR CREATURES AND
PLANESWALKERS, NOT THE FACE.
=====================================================================
Pyrite Spellbomb, Spark Spray, Starstorm, Hammer of Bogardan and Siege-Gang's sacrifice ability all
print their choices as a list - either after "legal targets right now:" or as one menu row per
target, with the face row reading "targeting a player". Procedure, top down, every time:
  0. READ "Opponent life:" FIRST, BEFORE ANY OF THE STEPS BELOW. It is printed on every single
     decision. If one row on this menu deals that number or more to a player - Hammer of
     Bogardan 3, Pyrite Spellbomb 2, Spark Spray 1, each Goblin you can sacrifice 2 - then the
     face row, written "The opponent (player, life N)", WINS THE GAME IN THIS WINDOW. Take it
     now. Nothing below this line applies, the CROSS-CHECK latch does not apply, and neither
     does anything that is killing you: a game you win this window cannot be lost next window.
     THE KILL COUNT ON THE CAST ROW DOES NOT SEE THIS. "{kills 0 of the 2 legal targets at 3
     damage}" counts only CREATURES; the player is never in that count, so a row that says it
     kills nothing is still lethal when their life is at or below its damage. The lethal face
     row says so itself: "THIS WINS THE GAME".
  0b. AFTER step 0 above, and before step 1 below: read the "{X pricing:" annotation on the
     Cast Starstorm line, and read the WHOLE of it - the "At X=..." clause after the first
     sentence, not just the first number. The annotation prints TWO lists at each X: what it
     kills of THEIRS and what it kills of YOURS. Both halves are binding.
     TAKE IT when the THEIRS list at an affordable X names TWO OR MORE creatures, and the YOURS
     list at that X is no longer than it. At that X it OUTRANKS every single-target damage spell
     you hold - Hammer of Bogardan, Pyrite Spellbomb and Spark Spray each kill exactly one.
     "kills THEIRS: none" in the first sentence does NOT mean Starstorm is blank; keep reading
     to the "At X=" clause. Losing your own Blastminer or a Goblin to kill two of theirs is a
     trade you take; losing five of yours to kill one of theirs is not, whatever is marked.
     SURVIVAL FIRST, above (a) and (b) below: add the printed power - the first number of each
     "(P/T)" - of every creature on THEIR battlefield line. If that sum is at or above "Your
     life:", you do not live through their next attack without the sweep; take the X whose
     THEIRS list is longest, WHATEVER the YOURS list names. Rorix or the Commander on the table
     beats the annotation only while you are alive to attack with him.
     BUT A SWEEP THAT LEAVES THE KILLER STANDING IS NOT SURVIVAL. Subtract the THEIRS list from
     their line: if the creatures the sweep does NOT kill still total your life or more, the
     Starstorm buys you nothing and costs you the blocker on its YOURS list. Keep the blocker.
     DO NOT TAKE IT - answer "Cast nothing right now", at ANY X - when either of these is true:
       (a) the YOURS list at that X names RORIX BLADEWING or SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER. Those two
           are the only way this deck ends a game; Rorix on the table beats any number on the
           annotation.
       (b) the THEIRS list at that X names only creatures whose battlefield line reads
           "[defender]". A defender never attacks you. It is not worth one card, and it is
           never worth a creature of yours.
           THE ONE CARVE-OUT, and it is about YOUR attacker, not theirs: if the body you are
           attacking with is GROUNDED and those defenders are the whole reason it is not getting
           through, the sweep is buying your clock and (b) does not apply. If your attacker is
           RORIX BLADEWING, it does not - he flies over Overgrown Battlement, Pride Guardian and
           Perimeter Captain alike.
       WRITE THE TWO LISTS BEFORE YOU WRITE THE CHOICE. On every window that lists "Cast
       Starstorm", the PLAN line carries this, filled from the "{X pricing:" text:
           PLAN: Starstorm: THEIRS non-defenders <n>; YOURS names Rorix/Commander <yes/no>;
           <cast / not this window>
       "0" in the first blank or "yes" in the second is "not this window", and the row you
       take is "Cast nothing right now" - never "Cast Card Normally", whose next screen has no
       way back but "X = 0".
     AND WHEN YOU DO CAST IT, PICK THE X OFF THE MENU'S OWN ROWS, IN THIS ORDER.
     The "Announce the value of X" list says "option 1 is the LARGEST X (X = N)", so option 1 is
     that N, never X=1. Every row now carries its own "{X pricing: kills THEIRS: ...; YOURS:
     ...}", or the collapsed form "{X pricing: same kills as X=N, for K less mana}".
       STEP 0. IF YOUR CARRIED PLAN ALREADY NAMES AN X, IGNORE IT AND READ THE ROWS. The screen
               hands the plan back stamped with its age ("as you last stated it, N windows ago
               on turn T"); an X written before this menu existed is older than the menu. An X
               in a plan is a guess; an X on a row comes with the kill list attached.
       STEP 1. THE MENU MARKS ONE ROW, AND THAT ROW IS THE ANSWER. The marker prints in one
               of two forms:
                 "[<- most kills at any affordable X that costs you nothing - at X=N that is
                  THEIRS: ...; YOURS: none]"   (some X is free)
                 "[<- best trade: the most of THEIRS at the smallest cost to YOURS - at X=N
                  that is THEIRS: ...; YOURS: ...]"   (no X is free)
               Answer the marked row's number and read nothing else; it has already done both
               the kill comparison and the mana comparison, and it is already the BOTTOM of any
               collapsed run. Last corpus the marker printed on six of six menus and this deck
               answered it six times out of six - this rung is DONE, keep it there.
               A MARKER IS NOT A REASON TO CAST. It answers WHICH X, never WHETHER: the "best
               trade" form is printed even when the best trade is a bad one. WHETHER is decided
               one screen earlier, at the cast row, where "Cast nothing right now" still exists.
               If no marker is printed: the LARGEST "kills THEIRS" list whose "YOURS:" reads
               "none". Bigger is free when YOURS is none.
               AND WHEN NO ROW READS "YOURS: none" AT ALL - every X on the menu costs you a body -
               NEVER TAKE A ROW WHOSE "kills THEIRS" READS "none". That row spends the card,
               kills only your own creatures, and leaves theirs untouched. Among rows that DO
               name an opponent creature, take the one whose THEIRS list is longest; the YOURS
               list is the price, and a price is not a reason to pick the row that buys nothing.
               If every such row names RORIX BLADEWING or SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER in YOURS, do not
               cast Starstorm at all - that decision belongs at the cast row, one screen
               earlier, where "Cast nothing right now" still exists.
       STEP 2. Then walk DOWN from that row while the next row says "same kills as X=N".
               A row that says "same kills" is the same board result for less mana; take the
               LOWEST row in that collapsed run, not the top of it. Two corpora at zero here.
       STEP 3. If a row's "YOURS:" names RORIX BLADEWING or SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER, that row and
               every row above it is out - go back to (a) above and do not cast at all.
               DECIDE (a) AND (b) AT THE CAST ROW, NOT ON THE X MENU. The cast row's own
               "{X pricing: ... At X=N - kills THEIRS: ...; YOURS: ...}" prints the same two
               lists; once you have answered "Cast Card Normally" the X menu has no "cast
               nothing" row and every row spends the card.
     The cast row one screen earlier leads with "smallest X that kills an OPPONENT creature".
     That is a fact about the cheapest kill, not a recommendation: the same annotation prints
     "At X=N - kills THEIRS: ..." for the bigger number right beside it. Read to the end of it.
     TWO CAST-ROW ANNOTATIONS MEAN "DO NOT CAST STARSTORM AT ALL", AND THEY ARE THE WHOLE TEST:
       "{X pricing: your mana affords only X=0 right now, which deals 0 damage and kills
        nothing}"  -> the spell is spent for nothing. The X menu after it has ONE row, "X = 0",
        and no way back. This deck took that cast on turn 4 last corpus at two mana,
        writing "X=0 is fine" about a spell that clears nothing.
       any "YOURS:" list on the cast row naming RORIX BLADEWING or SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER -> (a)
        above, and it does not become false because a marker is printed beside it. Last corpus
        this deck read "kills THEIRS: Sigarda, Champion of Light; YOURS: Dwarven Blastminer,
        Siege-Gang Commander, Goblin x3" and answered the cast in the same reply whose PLAN said
        "Attack with Siege-Gang Commander and 3 Goblins" - the sweep killed the attack. One of
        theirs is not five of yours, and the marker beside that row is answering WHICH X.
     THE MARKER NOW PRINTS ON THE CAST ROW TOO: "[<- best X for this cast: X=4 - best trade:
     the most of THEIRS at the smallest cost to YOURS]". It is still an answer to WHICH X. It
     is never an answer to WHETHER, and "best trade" is printed even when every trade is bad -
     count the two lists yourself and compare their lengths before you number that row.
  1. An opponent PLANESWALKER, or a CREATURE named on the "DRAW PUNISHERS" line, on the list
     -> aim there. A live planeswalker wins their game, and so does a live punisher: Fate
     Unraveler is a 3/4 CREATURE, and while it stands beside a Howling Mine your draw step is
     not optional and every card it hands you costs a life. Rule #0 stops you drawing on
     purpose; it cannot stop the draws THEY give you, and only killing the punisher can. Fate
     Unraveler needs 4 damage: Hammer (3) and Spellbomb (2) do not reach it alone, Starstorm at
     X=4 does, and this deck sat on two Starstorms until turn 14 and died at 0 to its own draw
     steps without ever being attacked.
     This outranks casting a creature. Its line prints "[counters: Nx loyalty]": when that N
     is at or below the damage of a spell on your menu, the spell kills it, and that cast comes
     before any body. Every creature you cast while a Hate-Twisted lives is a card and four life.
  2. An opponent CREATURE whose printed toughness this damage reaches -> aim there. Spark Spray
     kills 1 toughness; Pyrite Spellbomb kills 2; Hammer of Bogardan kills 3. The battlefield line
     prints each creature's CURRENT (P/T) - read it, do not rebuild it from base stats.
     EVERY damage row prints its result - a spell's target list ("Goblin (1/1) {right now:
     takes 3 damage - DIES}", "Rorix Bladewing (6/5) {right now: takes 3 damage - SURVIVES
     (toughness 5)}") and an ability's rows alike ("Deal 2 damage with Pyrite Spellbomb
     targeting Overgrown Battlement {right now: takes 2 damage - SURVIVES (toughness 4)}").
     "SURVIVES" is the answer "not this row", and for the Spellbomb it is the answer "keep the
     gun loaded": the artifact sits on the battlefield costing nothing until a 2-toughness
     creature arrives.
     ONE VERDICT READS "SURVIVES" AND IS STILL A TAKE, AND THE ROW NAMES IT: a PLANESWALKER
     prints "- SURVIVES (loyalty 3, 1 left)", not "(toughness N)". Loyalty is a counter that
     stays gone; a planeswalker at 1 left is a planeswalker one shot from dead and one activation
     from being unable to use its minus. "SURVIVES (toughness N)" is a creature that heals to
     full at end of turn and is the row you decline. Read which word is inside the parenthesis.
     THIS IS NOW THE ONLY REMOVAL RUNG THIS DECK IS LOSING, AND IT BREAKS THE
     SAME WAY EVERY TIME: a PLAN line that says "Kill X with Y" written before the row was read.
     THE ROW'S VERDICT OUTRANKS THE PLAN'S VERB EVERY TIME: read "- DIES" or "- SURVIVES" on the row you are about to number, and if
     every row on the target list says SURVIVES, the answer one screen earlier was
     "Cast nothing right now". A creature this deck cannot kill is not a target; it is a fact
     about the game you have to play around. After combat damage the same row can read "DIES" or "SURVIVES (toughness 4, 3
     more kills it)" - that is the window a 2-damage ability kills a 4-toughness wall, and
     this deck took it correctly once (Perimeter Captain, after a 2/2 had hit it).
  3. An opponent creature this will not kill but that is attacking you every turn -> aim there
     anyway rather than bank the card.
  4. Only then "The opponent (player, life" - and only when that number is 6 or less, so the damage
     you add actually finishes them. READ THE NUMBER PRINTED IN THE ROW ITSELF: the face row is
     written "The opponent (player, life N)" and "Opponent life:" is on every screen. If N is 7 or
     more, this row is not on your menu, whatever your plan says and however empty their board is.
          "It is the only active play" is the sentence that spends it early, and writing that sentence
     is not an argument - it is the name of this mistake. 2 off 20 is not a start; it is the
     Spellbomb that would have killed their first 2-toughness creature. An empty opposing board
     is a reason to HOLD the damage, not to spend it: their creature arrives later, and this deck
     has exactly eleven points of burn in the whole sixty.
  ONE exception to the 6-or-less floor, and only this one: LETHAL THIS TURN. Add up every point
  of damage you can produce in this window and the rest of this turn - each Goblin you can
  sacrifice is 2, each Pyrite Spellbomb 2, Hammer of Bogardan 3, plus every attacker you have
  declared that they cannot block. At or above "Opponent life:", start the chain and spend every
  piece. Nine life to a Siege-Gang Commander with three Goblins and a loaded Spellbomb is lethal,
  not a face-burn mistake. A REPEATABLE cost counts once PER PAYMENT you can afford: three
  Goblins at {1}{r} each with 8 mana is 6 damage, not 2 - the row prices ONE activation.
  AND THE EMPTY OPPOSING BOARD IS THE HARDEST VERSION OF THE FLOOR: a Pyrite Spellbomb with
  "legal targets right now: the opponent, you" and no creature on their line is normally a HOLD -
  two damage against a healthy opponent is a card; two against a 2-toughness creature is a card
  AND a body.
  THE FLOOR RELEASES WHEN THEIR BOARD IS GONE AND HAS NOTHING TO COME BACK WITH, AND THE
  RELEASE IS TWO NUMBERS OFF THEIR OWN BATTLEFIELD LINE. "Opponent battlefield (N permanents
  listed, of which 0 are creatures, ... and L are lands)". At 0 creatures AND L of 2 or fewer,
  AND ONLY ONCE YOU HAVE ACTUALLY TAKEN LANDS OFF THEM - the log shows a Stone Rain, Molten Rain,
  Lay Waste or Blastminer activation of yours that resolved - you have already won the land war.
  An opponent on turn 2 or 4 with one land and no creature has not LOST a land war; they have not
  had a turn yet, and the release does NOT fire. Last corpus this deck read it the other way
  twice and put a Spark Spray into a face at 20 life on turn 2, and again on turn 4, spending the
  card that kills their first 1-toughness creature for one point off twenty. Once it has genuinely
  fired: they are not deploying the creature you were saving the
  card for, the card you are holding will never find a better target, and the face row IS the
  clock. Take it, at any life total. Last corpus this deck put a Hammer of Bogardan into a face
  at 16 life across a battlefield line reading "(0 permanents listed)" and won that game at -3
  on turn 34; it was the only route the board allowed. At L of 3 or more the floor still binds -
  they still have mana for a threat, and the card is still removal.
  THE RELEASE IS NOT A LICENCE AGAINST A LIFEGAIN ENGINE. If their line names Elixir of
  Immortality, a lifelink creature or a Sanguine Bond / Exquisite Blood half, compare per turn:
  three points a turn from a recurring Hammer does not beat five a turn from an Elixir, and the
  CROSS-CHECK latch below is what tells you which side of that you are on.
BOTH HALVES OF THEIR LIFE LOOP ON ONE BATTLEFIELD IS THE ONE BOARD WHERE DEPLOYING IS WRONG.
The frame names it in its own block - "LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER on the battlefield: theirs -
Sanguine Bond ... Both halves of a life LOOP are on THEIR battlefield (Sanguine Bond + Exquisite
Blood)". While both are out, ANY life they gain repeats without limit until you are at 0: they
gain N, you lose N, that loss gains them N again. Every creature you put on the table is a
trigger for it, because their edicts gain them life equal to your creature's toughness, and the
cast row prices exactly that for you - "{their converter: this body has toughness 2 and they
control 1 life-to-damage converter (Sanguine Bond) - any effect of theirs that gains them life
equal to its toughness takes 2 off YOU: life 18 -> 16}". Last corpus this deck read that clause,
cast Siege-Gang Commander into it at 18 life on turn 16 calling it "the only active play", was
edicted for a Goblin on the same turn, and the loop took it from 18 to 0 one point at a time
without another decision mattering. On that board: no creature, no face damage, nothing that
gains them life. Hold everything, take the hold row, and win only if their clock is slower than
your library - and if it is not, you were dead when the second enchantment resolved. Nothing in
this sixty removes an enchantment.
HAMMER OF BOGARDAN, PYRITE SPELLBOMB AND SPARK SPRAY NEVER TOUCH A LAND. Their target menus
list players and creatures and nothing else; a plan reading "Hammer targeting Underground Sea"
or "kill Blastminer with Hammer to destroy Plains" is wrong before the menu opens - this deck
wrote both, and the first one put the Hammer into its own Siege-Gang Commander. With no
opposing creature or planeswalker on that menu the answer is "The opponent (player, life N)",
never a row tagged [your battlefield].
A ROW THAT NAMES YOUR OWN PERMANENT IS NEVER THE ANSWER. Damage and destroy rows print their
target after "targeting" with its owner in brackets - "[opponent's battlefield]" or "[your
battlefield]". A row tagged [your battlefield] on Pyrite Spellbomb, Hammer of Bogardan,
Siege-Gang Commander or Dwarven Blastminer aims at YOU, and when it is the only row on the
window the answer is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)". A plan that names a target the screen does not list is
carried out by 0 (pass), not by the nearest-looking row.
CROSS-CHECK, AND YOU NO LONGER HAVE TO REMEMBER ANYTHING: the board frame prints the whole
history on its own line, "Opponent life trend: turn 34: 28, turn 36: 28, turn 38: 28, now 31
(+3 since turn 34)". Read the SIGN in the final parenthesis before you number any face row. A
ONE-OFF gain - a lifelink blocker connecting once, a single Elixir activation - is not a
lifegain engine and does not stop the burn. A gain that REPEATS, and whose size per turn is at
or above the damage you can produce per turn, does: against that, face damage is not a clock
and every point belongs on their creatures and planeswalkers for the rest of the game. Write
the comparison once, on the window it happens: "they gained N this turn; I make M a turn" -
N at or above M is the latch, N below M is not.
Once the latch has fired, "The opponent (player, life N)" is not an answer again in that game
unless the LETHAL-THIS-TURN chain above adds up on the screen in front of you.
A TREND THAT MOVED UP WHILE YOU WERE BURNING IS THE LATCH, PROVED. Last corpus this deck spent a
Spellbomb at their 28 and two Hammers at 26 and 31 - the trend line reading "now 31 (+3 since
turn 34)" between them - writing "3 damage is not lethal, but it is the only active play" each
time, and lost at 0 against 36 on turn 58. Three points a turn against a life engine that
returns five is not a clock; it is the card you did not have when their creature landed.
AND EVERY ONE OF THOSE DECISIONS WAS MADE ONE SCREEN EARLIER THAN IT LOOKED. The floor, the latch
and the release are all answered at the CAST row, where "Cast nothing right now" and the hold row
are on the menu; once you take the cast, the target screen on a creatureless board is two rows -
"The opponent" and "Yourself" - and there is no third answer. The cast row carries the arithmetic
already: "{no creature target - and 3 to the opponent at life 26 leaves them at 23}". If that
sentence does not end at or below 0, and the release below has not fired, the answer on THAT
window is "Cast nothing right now".

=====================================================================
HOW THE DECK WINS - RUN THIS OFF YOUR LAND COUNT
=====================================================================
Play a land every turn, count the lands on YOUR battlefield line, take the first line you can afford.
LAND DESTRUCTION IS A TEMPO PLAY: it is worth a card while the opponent's battlefield shows FOUR OR
FEWER lands and is nearly worthless after that - spend it EARLY, never hold it for a better target.
AND THE PLAN ENDS THE MOMENT THEY ARE IN RANGE: when "Opponent life:" is at or below the damage
you can point at a player this turn, the land spells are dead cards and rule #2's step 0 is the
whole turn. At "Opponent life: 1" this deck spent two turns casting Lay Waste and Molten Rain,
and lost at 0 to 1.
WRITE TWO NUMBERS BEFORE EVERY ONE OF THESE CASTS: "Their lands <N>; my life <L>". N comes off
their battlefield line's own tail ("... and N are lands"), and collapsed handles count in full -
"Island #1-#4 x4; Plains #1-#3 x3; Seachrome Coast" is NINE lands, not three rows. N at FIVE or
more, or L at 8 or less, and the spell is dead for the rest of the game; it does not become live

COMBAT
=====================================================================
RACE CHECK - do this BEFORE the attack floor, and only when your attack list holds exactly ONE
creature and that creature is Rorix Bladewing or Siege-Gang Commander.
Your one creature is also your only blocker, and Rorix does not come back untapped in time to
block on the turn he attacks. So compare two numbers you can read off the screen:
  THEIR turns to kill you  = your "Your life:" number divided by the total printed power of
                             their untapped creatures (a creature with vigilance attacks AND
                             blocks, so it counts here even while it is attacking).
  YOUR turns to kill them  = their "Opponent life:" number divided by your attacker's power.
If THEIR number is smaller than or equal to YOURS, you lose the race: answer "ATTACK: none" and
keep the body home to block. A 6/5 that blocks a 4/4 kills it and lives - the trade line on the
blockers screen will say so - and once their attacker is dead the race restarts in your favour.
ONE CARVE-OUT, and only this one: if their untapped creatures' total printed power is more than
your life PLUS your one creature's toughness, holding it home does not change the turn you die -
one blocker stops one attacker and the rest still connect. There is nothing to hold for, so send
it and take the damage you were taking anyway.
Only when you WIN the race, or when their untapped creatures total 0 power, or under the carve-out
above, go on to the floor.

WALL CHECK, BEFORE THE FLOOR - THE TAG PRICES IT IN YOUR LIFE NOW. An attacker's tag reading
"(neither dies (blocking trigger: they may gain 2, and if they do their converter takes 2 off
you))" means Perimeter Captain plus Sanguine Bond: EVERY attacker you send is 2 life off you
and 2 on them, and they block every one. Count the attackers you were about to send and
double it - that is the life you are paying for zero damage. Write it: "Wall: <n> attackers x
2 = <2n> life; ATTACK: none". This deck read that tag on all four of its attackers at 19 life,
wrote "they have no attackers, so attacking is safe" and sent them - 19 became 11 and 16
became 24; two turns later it sent three Goblins into the same tag and 10 became 4; the game
ended 0 to 24 with the opponent never attacking. Without the converter clause the tag reads
"(neither dies (blocking trigger: they may gain 2))" and the price is theirs alone - still
"ATTACK: none" while Perimeter Captain or Pride Guardian is on their line: they die to a
Spellbomb, a Goblin sacrifice or a Starstorm first, and THEN the floor applies.
ATTACK FLOOR - read the "Your creatures that can attack:" list, then take the first case that
applies. FIRST: the WALL CHECK above - a "blocking trigger" tag on your attackers is "ATTACK:
none" before anything below is read. SECOND: Dwarven Blastminer alone on the list stays home
while his {2}{R} has a job this turn - a "[opponent's battlefield]" nonbasic land on his
target rows. Otherwise READ HIS ROW'S OWN TAG, do not count their creatures: if every blocker
in "[their untapped blockers: ...]" is priced "(neither dies)" - walls like Shield Sphere (0/6)
and Fog Bank (0/2) are - he loses nothing by attacking and one damage a turn is the only clock
you have; SEND HIM. He stays home only when a blocker's parenthesis says he dies. This deck
answered "ATTACK: none" with him through ten turns of an opponent on zero lands at 18 life, then
needed twenty more turns to win; it has since sent him correctly into "(neither dies)" tags in
two straight corpora. THIRD: with any other
creature listed, send EVERY creature listed except Dwarven Blastminer. That list has already dropped everything that cannot
legally attack, so do not re-check summoning sickness or tapped status yourself. Otherwise answer
"ATTACK: none" only when every listed attacker would die to a blocker and kill nothing - not to
"hold blockers", and not because their creatures are marked "[tapped - cannot attack or block this
turn]" (they untap for their turn).
A ONE-ROW "ATTACK:" LIST AFTER YOUR OWN STARSTORM IS THE CASE THIS FLOOR GETS WRONG. When the
sweeper you just cast killed your own board, the single creature left is not "everything you
have to attack with", it is your last body: read its tag. At 9 life against 25, with the tag
reading "[their untapped blockers: Sigarda, Champion of Light (4/4) (your attacker dies, their
blocker lives)]", this deck sent its lone Siege-Gang Commander and had nothing afterwards. One
attacker whose own tag says it dies and kills nothing is "ATTACK: none", however few rows the
screen prints.
THE BLOCKER TAG, IN EITHER FORM, INSIDE THE FLOOR. On a narrow board an attacker's tag lists
their blockers one by one - "[their untapped blockers: Briarbridge Tracker (3/4) (your attacker
dies, their blocker lives); Sigarda ... ; Intrepid Adversary (4/2) (both die (lifelink: they
gain 4))]"; on a wide board it collapses to "they have N untapped creatures that could block
this one, biggest X (...)", sometimes with "- GANG BLOCK: their 2 biggest together deal 5,
enough to kill this attacker; each result above is a LONE blocker only". COUNT THE BLOCKERS THE
TAG NAMES, in either form - a name-by-name list of three is N = 3. Each parenthesis prices ONE
blocker only; the GANG BLOCK clause prices what actually happens: any number of them may block
that attacker together, and your creature's toughness is measured against the SUM. This deck
sent its Commander into a tag naming THREE blockers, two of them "(your attacker dies)", at 13
life, and lost it for nothing - a "(both die)" against a lifelink blocker that gains them four
is not a trade for your only Commander either.
  - The tag prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause, or names 2 or more
    blockers:
    that creature stays home. A GOBLIN token goes anyway - it costs you nothing and Siege-Gang
    makes more. RORIX goes anyway ONLY when every blocker the tag names is grounded and Rorix
    flies over all of them; SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER never goes on such a tag, because its sacrifice
    ability needs it alive.
  - N is 1 and no GANG BLOCK clause: the parenthesis is the whole story; use it.
"But the biggest one dies to me" is the sentence the clause exists to refuse - they do not have
to block with the biggest one alone. This deck sent Rorix past a "they have 5 untapped creatures"
collapse and survived only because he flies.
ONE EXCEPTION INSIDE THE FLOOR: if their untapped creatures' printed power totals your life or more,
send only Rorix this turn and keep the Goblins home.
BLOCKING - take any block where your creature kills theirs and lives. Take a "(both die)" block
with a GOBLIN token, always. Do NOT take a "(both die)" block with SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER or RORIX
BLADEWING: those are cards, they are the only bodies this deck has, and Siege-Gang's sacrifice
ability needs Siege-Gang alive. Trading either one for a 2/2 token the opponent will replace is
not an even trade even though the parenthesis says both creatures die. Send a Goblin instead, or
take the damage while the combat header's "you would be at N" is above 5.
THE ONE "(both die)" THE COMMANDER DOES TAKE: the attacker is THRABEN DOOMSAYER or BLOODLINE
KEEPER - a token-MAKER. That creature is their engine and it is attacking into you; the
Commander for the maker is the trade of the game, and this deck made it, correctly, at 20
life against a Doomsayer that had already made nineteen Humans.
A GANG BLOCK THAT KILLS THE ATTACKER IS A DIFFERENT QUESTION FROM DAMAGE. The header prints
it when it exists: "[GANG BLOCK: your 3 biggest together deal 4, enough to kill this
attacker; ...]". When that attacker is a DRAW PUNISHER named on the "DRAW PUNISHERS" line,
or the only creature hitting you, and your life minus its "deals N" is 5 or less, put every
blocker the clause counts on it - the Goblins you lose are the draw steps you keep. At 7
life under Fate Unraveler and Ob Nixilis, with "your 3 biggest together deal 4, enough to
kill this attacker" on the screen, this deck answered "BLOCKS: none", went to 4, and died in
its next draw step to the Unraveler it could have killed.
ONE BLOCKER STOPS THE WHOLE OF ONE ATTACKER'S DAMAGE. A second and a third of yours on the same
attacker stop nothing more - they only die with the first. At 13 life this deck put all three
Goblins in front of a single 3/4 and lost all three to save 3 life it did not need (the header
read "you would be at 10 - NOT lethal"); those three Goblins were 6 Siege-Gang damage, two
planeswalker kills. Read the header's "you would be at N" FIRST and write it down:
  - N above 5 and every row on that attacker reads "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)":
    "BLOCKS: none". A Goblin is worth more as 2 points of Siege-Gang damage than as 3 life.
  - N at 5 or below: ONE Goblin on the attacker with the largest "deals N"; a second Goblin only
    on a DIFFERENT attacker. Never two on one unless the row says "(both die)" for the pair.
WHEN THE PROMPT SAYS LETHAL, CHUMP THE BIGGEST ATTACKER YOU ARE ALLOWED TO BLOCK. The line
"Unblocked, these attackers deal up to N - you would be at ... - LETHAL if it all connects" means
the arithmetic, not the trade, is the whole question. Every attacker row prints "deals N".
FIRST narrow the list: an attacker row tagged "[NONE of your available blockers can block this
attacker ...]", or one that is missing from your blocker's own "may block" line, is not on the
menu at all - its damage is going to happen whatever you do, so subtract it and stop thinking
about it. Among what is LEFT, put your blocker in front of the LARGEST "deals N", even when the trade line says "your blocker dies, attacker lives" - subtracting
their biggest number from the incoming total is the only thing that decides whether you are alive
next turn. At 3 life against a 4 and a 2, blocking the 4 leaves you at 1 and blocking the 2 leaves
you dead; this deck blocked the 2.
AND: the "may block" list is the LEGALITY list. If an attacker is named on your blocker's
"may block" line, you CAN block it - "my 1/1 cannot block their 4/4" is not a rule, it is a
sentence this deck told itself on the turn it died.

MULLIGAN
=====================================================================
THE ODDS, ONCE: twenty-four lands in sixty, every one of them making {R} - so colour is never
a reason to ship, and the only question at every look is "does THIS hand act by turn three,
and is a fresh hand one card smaller more likely to?". A fresh seven from this deck holds two
or more lands about six times in seven; each mulligan hands you those same odds with one card
fewer, so a ship buys a look, never a fix.
READ TWO LINES BEFORE YOU ANSWER. The header "counted by the engine: N lands, M spells" is the
land count - never recount. The line "Playing every land in this hand would cover the cost
of: ..." is the engine's own check of your mana against your spells: if it names even one
card, this hand ACTS - this deck kept a one-land seven whose coverage line named exactly one
card and won that game. If it reads "would not cover any spell in it", nothing in the hand
happens until you draw, and at ONE land that is the shape where a smaller hand can be the
better one - at two or more lands that line is not a ship, it is a turn-three hand: a one-land
seven of Starstorm, Starstorm, Stone Rain, Stone Rain, Lay Waste, Rorix and a Mountain ships,
and the six it buys acts on turn three.
AT EACH LOOK, price it: the ask prints "(keeping N)". A hand with two or more lands and at least
one spell is a keep at every N - UNLESS THE HEADER READS 5 OR MORE LANDS AND EXACTLY 1 SPELL.
"6 lands, 1 spell" is not a keep: one spell is one action, and a deck whose turn has a job every
turn cannot do it off a hand with one card to cast. Ship it at (keeping 6) and (keeping 5);
keep only at (keeping 4) or lower.
A one-lander is a keep when the coverage line names a card,
and a ship when it does not - at (keeping 6) or (keeping 5) the odds of two lands in the next
look are the same six-in-seven, and at (keeping 4) or lower even a one-lander that acts by
turn three beats the look you would buy. Zero lands ships at any N; a hand of nothing but lands ships at any N
above 4.
THE BOTTOMING ASK IS WHERE THE HAND IS BUILT. Read "You will be left with an N-card hand" and
keep, in this PREFERENCE ORDER, which is satisfiable at any N because it is an order and not a
set of floors: the lands that cover the cheapest spells you keep (three if N allows it, two at
N of 4 or less); then the one Starstorm if the hand has one - the only card here that answers a
board; then your cheapest spells; then the rest by cost. What is left over goes under - a
fifth Mountain before a Molten Rain, a second Rorix before a first Stone Rain. Whichever card
is last in that order is the one that goes, even when it is a land or the Starstorm.

CLEANUP DISCARD - THE SAME ORDER, READ FROM THE END. When your hand is above seven at end of turn
the ask prints your hand as a NUMBERED list ("your hand has N cards and your maximum hand size is
7"; the log calls it "Cleanup discard (hand N, limit 7)") and you answer "PUT: n, m" - the numbers
of the cards you SEND, most expensive first. Send, as many as the ask demands: the most expensive
spell your lands cannot cast in the next two turns (Rorix or a second Siege-Gang Commander from
four lands), then a duplicate of a spell already in hand, then a land beyond the third in hand.
A CARD YOU COULD CAST THIS TURN OR NEXT IS NEVER IN THAT LIST WHILE A DUPLICATE IS ON THE SCREEN.
The ask prints the hand with its own duplicate markers - "(copy 1 of 2 in your hand)",
"(copy 2 of 3 in your hand)" - so the second and third copies are named for you. At four lands
this deck was asked for three cards while holding two Talismans and two Starstorms, and sent
Hammer of Bogardan ({1}{r}{r}, castable that turn), Rorix, and Pyrite Spellbomb ({1}, the
cheapest card in the hand); one turn later it sent both Talismans anyway. Duplicates first, then
the uncastable big spell, then a land.
Never the only Starstorm - it is the one card here that answers a board - and never the last land
that covers the spell your plan names. Read the PUT line back against your own plan sentence
before you send it: a plan that says "cast the Commander next turn" with the Commander's number
in the PUT line is the plan discarding itself.

THE INCOMING TOTAL IS PRINTED ON EVERY WINDOW OF THEIR COMBAT. "INCOMING THIS COMBAT: 3
attackers, 11 unblocked damage - you would be at -1; this KILLS you" sits in the board frame from
the moment they declare, on the upkeep, main-phase and priority windows as well as the blockers
ask. It is the number the LETHAL-THIS-TURN chain races: when it says "this KILLS you", the only
answers that matter this turn are a lethal chain of your own and a block that gets the total
under your life. Everything else - a land destroyed, a card cycled, a Talisman cast - is a play
you will not be alive to use.
