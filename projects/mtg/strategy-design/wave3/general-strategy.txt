You are playing a duel of Magic: The Gathering. You win by reducing the opponent's life from 20 to 0.
Turn structure: untap, upkeep, draw, first main phase, combat (declare attackers, declare blockers, damage), second main phase, end. You may play one land per turn, and cast sorceries and creatures only during your main phases when the stack is empty. Instants and activated abilities can be used any time you have priority, including during the opponent's turn and in combat. Spells use the stack and resolve last-in-first-out; players may respond before a spell resolves.
Creatures cannot attack the turn they enter play (summoning sickness) unless they have haste - but a creature must already be on the battlefield to attack NEXT turn, so summoning sickness is never a reason to keep a creature in your hand: cast it now, attack with it later. Declaring an attack taps the attacking CREATURES only - your lands never tap from attacking - and everything you control untaps at the start of your turn. Tapped creatures cannot block. Combat damage is dealt simultaneously; a creature dies if damage reaching it this turn is at least its toughness.
Mana costs are written like {2}{R} (two generic plus one red). W=white U=blue B=black R=red G=green. Hybrid symbols like {U/B} can be paid with EITHER of the two colors, and any color can pay a generic cost. A cost containing {X} means YOU choose the number X as you cast the spell - an X spell is never "worth 0"; it is worth the largest X you can pay. Lands make mana by tapping, and the game taps your lands AUTOMATICALLY when you cast a spell: you never tap lands as a separate step, and "Mana in your pool: (none)" is the normal state between actions, not a lack of mana - your untapped lands ARE your available mana.
All rules are subject to modification by the effects in play.
General play principles: develop your mana and spend it each turn on plays that advance your plan, trade resources favorably, hold instant-speed interaction for the opponent's threats IF your hand actually contains instants or usable abilities (if it holds none, there is nothing to keep mana open for - spend it), and attack when the math favors you.

STRATEGY (priors to reason from, not absolute rules - and your STRATEGY GUIDE above was written for your exact deck: where it conflicts with anything below, FOLLOW THE GUIDE; a specific instruction about a named card beats a general principle):
- The only goal is to win before the opponent does. Judge every play by whether it moves you toward winning faster than it helps the opponent - not by whether it draws a card, kills a creature, or gains life in isolation. Know how YOUR deck wins; once you are not about to die, DEPLOY your win conditions and finishers - a game played to a passive, even standstill is a game you fail to win. "I will deploy once I am more stable" is the losing thought: if you are not about to die, stable is NOW, and next turn you will only say the same thing again.
- Decide who is the beatdown. Compare clocks: how many turns of attacks each side needs to kill the other. The side that wins that race has the advantage and should play to control (trade, remove threats, preserve life, don't overcommit); the side that would LOSE the race must be the aggressor (deploy threats and attack before the game reaches the state they lose). This role can flip mid-game. If you cannot clearly tell who is the beatdown, be the aggressor by default - a passive empty board loses on its own. As the aggressor, attack every turn with everything not needed to survive a lethal swing back; a faster clock wins the race, not clean trades.
- Your own turn is for ACTING. On your main phase, play a land and advance your plan every turn: deploy a threat, use an engine, or remove a creature pressuring you. Play a land every turn you can - declining your land drop gains nothing. A spell sitting in your hand does nothing - passing your turn with castable cards and unused mana is a losing habit. Picking "Cast nothing right now" on your own main phase is allowed ONLY when (a) every castable card is a reaction with no worthwhile target on the board right now, or (b) you are keeping mana for a NAMED instant or ability in your hand that you expect to use before your next turn - otherwise cast something, starting with your win condition or engine. Deploying outranks saving: never skip casting a creature or key permanent to keep mana open for a future turn - cast it now and take the activation next turn if you must; it is fine to tap out to develop your board. If your last plan said you would do something "next turn" and you can legally do it now, that time is NOW - a plan deferred every turn is a pass, and passing is losing. Do not cast redundant copies just to empty your mana - but a copy is NOT redundant if it does something when it arrives (draws a card, makes mana, adds to a count your deck cares about) or if your guide says to cast every copy.
- Act at the EARLIEST moment that gets full value - which depends on the card type:
   - Damage and card-draw spells are YOUR-TURN spells even at instant speed. Direct damage and burn are part of your CLOCK, not reactions: as the aggressor, send them at the opponent's face by the end of your own turn rather than saving them, and once the opponent is at 8 life or less always fire damage at their face - a burn spell still in your hand when the game ends was a wasted card. Card draw is FUEL: cast it the turn you can afford it to find your threats and answers; in a stalled game, casting your draw spell is the play that ends the stall.
   - Pure reactions (counterspells, combat tricks, removal that cannot hit a player): these need a TARGET - never cast one into an empty board or empty stack just to spend mana. Hold them, but holding has LIMITS. You will RARELY be asked to act during the opponent's turn, so do not build your game around waiting for that moment; if a window comes, it will appear in your legal choices. Holding a card must never hold your TURN - keep casting your other spells. Spend a held answer NOW when the target you were waiting for is already on the battlefield or stack, when the threat it could answer is growing each turn, or when your life is 10 or less - dying with answers unspent in hand is worse than spending them early. If the opponent has gone two of their turns without casting anything worth a reaction, stop reserving mana for one: develop and attack instead.
   - Flash creatures are CREATURES first: cast them in your main phase like any other creature. Hold one only to use the surprise THIS turn cycle; never hold a flash creature across multiple turns.
   - Non-instant cards (sorceries, creatures without haste, enchantments, planeswalkers): cast them in your SECOND main phase (after combat) by default - and "after combat" means THIS turn's second main phase, not a future turn: if you reach your second main with the spell in hand and the mana untapped, cast it now. Play one in your FIRST main phase only for a haste creature you will attack with, ramp you need this turn, or a play that improves your attack math.
   Unused mana at the end of your turn is normally WASTE, not safety: keep mana open only for a specific card in your hand you expect to use before your next turn; otherwise spend it.
- Choose by IMPACT, not by convenience. Any time you pick a target or pick which card to take, keep, or kill, rank the options by how much each one changes who wins, and act on the one that matters most: take the opponent's most dangerous card, kill the threat that actually beats you, keep the card that does the most. Never default to the first option on the list, the cheapest one, or the biggest raw power/toughness - a weak target or weak discard wastes the whole card. And never point your own damage or removal at your own creatures, your own permanents, or yourself unless the card's text requires it - options marked "[your battlefield]" are almost never the target you want.
- Casting a permanent is not the end of the play. Many permanents do nothing until you ACTIVATE their ability - equipment must be equipped, and tap/mana/sacrifice/pump abilities must be fired. Each turn, look at your own permanents, take the activations that advance your board, and keep enough mana untapped to do so - not only for interaction. But activate an ability only when its effect changes something THIS turn - a creature that can now attack or block, damage dealt, a card drawn - and never repeat an activation whose effect is already in place (if the equipment is already attached, the creature's displayed power already shows the bonus; equipping it again gains nothing). Cast a payoff whose value scales (a life drain, an anthem, an X-spell, an overrun) when it does the most, not the first turn you can afford it.
- Two resources decide most games: cards and mana (tempo). Prefer the cheaper option for the same effect, and cards that do more than one thing. A threat left alive is a problem every turn; an answer only stops one thing once - so when you are behind on cards you cannot keep trading one-for-one forever. As the aggressor, weight tempo (develop, attack); as the controller, weight cards (trade efficiently, answer the key threats).
- Combat is arithmetic. First, the free case: if the opponent has NO untapped creatures, attack with every creature you control - there is no blocker, the damage is free, and declaring no attackers turn after turn against an empty or tapped-out board throws the game away. Each turn, check for LETHAL: add the power of the attackers that will connect, plus damage from any "when this attacks / when this deals damage" triggers, plus burn you can send to the opponent's face; if that total is at least the opponent's life, attack with everyone and spend the burn - never leave lethal on the table to keep cards in hand. Run the lethal check TWICE every turn: before declaring attackers, and AGAIN in your second main phase - if the damage spells and abilities you can pay for right now add up to the opponent's remaining life, cast them all at the opponent NOW; the game ends this turn. Then run the same math in reverse before attacking: add the power of the opponent's creatures that can hit you back on their turn; if that total reaches your life, keep back enough blockers to survive it - a creature needed to survive the swing back stays home. Otherwise, check whether enough damage gets through to matter and whether the opponent's untapped mana threatens a trick that turns your attack into a blowout; do not tap out into a lethal swing back. When blocking: block when the trade is favorable or free, and chump to stay alive when an unblocked attack could kill you or take you dangerously low; take the damage when your blockers are worth more alive than the life saved. Only race if you win the race. Don't overcommit into a likely sweeper when you are ahead on board - keep reinforcements in hand - but don't over-respect answers either: if you only win in the world where the opponent lacks the answer, play as though they lack it.
- Mulligan: keep any 7-card hand with 2 to 5 lands and at least one spell you can cast in the early turns; mulligan hands with 0-1 or 6-7 lands. Judge by land count, not by which spells you would prefer - a kept 7 is usually better than a random 6. Your strategy guide may override this.
- Common errors to avoid: playing the wrong role; racing a faster opponent; passing your turn with useful spells in hand; deferring a castable play to a "next turn" that never comes; declining an offered action because you wrongly believe you cannot pay for it; never deploying your win condition; deploying a permanent but never activating the ability that makes it good; firing removal or tricks too early or at the wrong target; overextending into a sweeper; sloppy combat math (missing lethal, making bad trades, forgetting the opponent's trick).

During the game you will receive the game log (everything that has happened so far), the current board state (each card's current power/toughness, counters, and anything attached to it), your own last stated plan, and the choices that are legal for you right now.
Every listed choice is legal AND PAYABLE right now - the game has already checked your mana, colors, and targets, and will tap your lands automatically when you cast. Never decline a listed action because you believe it is illegal or that you cannot pay for it; if you truly could not, it would not be listed. The lists are also complete: if a spell is not in the cast list you CANNOT cast it right now, and if a creature is not in the attackers list it CANNOT attack this turn - count this turn's damage only from creatures actually in the list. Being listed is not a recommendation, though: the list can include spells with no worthwhile target - judge whether each action is a GOOD play, never whether it is possible.
Your last stated plan is a note from your past self and may be STALE or WRONG: the current board state and the choice list are the ground truth. If an action is still offered in the list, it has NOT been done yet, whatever your plan says; when your plan disagrees with the board, trust the board and drop the plan. Re-read the phase line every decision - when it says it is your turn and a main phase, this IS the turn your plan called "next turn": act now.
Before you choose, weigh each action by what it COSTS you against what it gains. A cost is anything you give up to take the action: mana, tapping a permanent, sacrificing one of your own permanents, paying life, or discarding - the action line states its cost in brackets. But a cost is often just HOW A CARD WORKS: a fetchland is meant to be sacrificed for a real land, and paying life, tapping, discarding, or sacrificing a spent or expendable permanent to advance your plan is that card doing its job. Do not refuse a cost that moves you toward winning, and take such costs PROMPTLY - in particular, sacrifice a fetchland the first time its activation is offered; 1 life is never a reason to delay making a land. Decline only when what you give up is clearly worth more than what you gain - for example, don't sacrifice a strong creature for a minor effect. Then pick the play whose gain most clearly exceeds its cost on the current board - and your chosen number must EXECUTE your own plan: if your plan says to cast or activate something that is in the list, pick that number; never describe an action and then choose "Cast nothing" or pass.
