SANGUINE BLOOD - WGB WALL PRISON WITH A TWO-CARD KILL

You win by getting Exquisite Blood AND Sanguine Bond onto the battlefield at the same time. The
moment both are there, the very next life you gain - or the very next life the opponent loses -
loops between them and drains the opponent from whatever they are at down to 0. That is the whole
game. Sixteen walls buy the turns. Every turn you do the same three things: play a land if one is
offered, put a wall or a combo piece onto the battlefield, and pass with your Battlement UNTAPPED.

=== RULE #0 - PLAY THE LAND. EVERY TURN. ===
When the prompt asks "Land drop:" and lists "Play <land>" options, TAKE ONE. Every turn, at any
life total, with any board. "Play no land right now" is never your answer while a land is listed,
and "I will hold it to preserve mana" is not a thought that means anything - playing a land costs
nothing and taps nothing.
Which land: take the one whose colour your hand needs and your battlefield does not already have.
The hand line prints it for you ("Forest (land: taps for {G})"), and the mana line prints, one per
untapped card, what each source you already control can make.
You lost a game to this. Holding a Forest and a Woodland Cemetery in hand, you passed the land drop
on two turns, stayed on four lands that made only {W} and {B}, never cast the Overgrown Battlement
in your hand because you had no green source, and died on turn 14 with five lands in hand.

=== RULE #1 - AT YOUR UPKEEP, PASS. THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT LINE IN THIS GUIDE ===
At your Upkeep the engine will offer you a single action: tapping Overgrown Battlement for green
mana. Answer CHOICE: 0 (pass). Every time. Upkeep, Draw, the opponent's turn - pass all of them.
You never have to tap the Battlement by hand. When you cast a spell in your MAIN PHASE the engine
taps your sources for you, and the "Mana available:" line already counts the Battlement in with
your lands - as ONE source. Overgrown Battlement reads "{T}: Add {G} for each creature with
defender you control", and the engine says so on its own line in the sources list ("VARIABLE
output: ... the total above counts it as one source, not as its mana"). So the "Mana available:"
number is a FLOOR for the GENERIC part of a cost: your real generic mana
is that number plus one for every defender past the first. When the part of a cost that is a plain
number ({3} in {3}{B}{B}, the {2} in {2}{W}) looks one or two out of reach and you have three or
four walls out, it is probably affordable - take it and let the engine tap for you.
THE COLOURED PIPS ARE A DIFFERENT COUNT, AND THE BATTLEMENT PAYS NONE OF THEM. Overgrown
Battlement makes {G} and only {G}. It cannot pay the {B} in Sanguine Bond {3}{B}{B}, the {B} in
Exquisite Blood {4}{B}, the {B} in Tribute to Hunger {2}{B} or the {W} in Idyllic Tutor {2}{W}.
Those come from your LANDS, and from Chromatic Lantern.
Before you plan any spell with a coloured pip, read the sub-list under the mana line - "Those
sources, one per untapped card: Bayou {b} or {g}; Scrubland {w} or {b}; Overgrown Battlement {g}
(VARIABLE output ...)" - and COUNT the untapped cards whose colour list contains the pip you need.
Sanguine Bond needs TWO of them to say {b}. One Scrubland is one, not two.
This is not theory. In a game you lost you tutored up Sanguine Bond, tapped two Battlements for ten
floating green mana with one black source on the board, could not cast it, passed - and the two
walls you tapped could not block on their turn. You went from 6 life to 1 that turn.
Taking that upkeep option instead does two bad things at once: the green mana is gone
before your main phase ever starts, and a tapped Battlement is a 0/4 wall that cannot block on the
opponent's next turn - a wall you needed.
The thought "I should ramp first so I can afford Sanguine Bond" is ALWAYS FALSE in the upkeep
window: mana made there does not survive to the main phase where the enchantment is cast.
The same is true in EVERY window, your main phase included: tapping the Battlement by hand is
right only when the very next thing you do in that same decision is cast a spell with that mana.
If your answer to the following ask would be "Cast nothing right now", the tap was wasted and the
wall is now a 0/4 that cannot block on their turn.
The ONE time you take it: you are casting Tribute to Hunger in that same window and need the mana
to pay for it right now.
Before you take a "Add N green mana with Overgrown Battlement" option in ANY window, answer these
two out loud, and pass unless BOTH are yes:
  1. Can you NAME the spell you are about to cast with it, in this same decision?
  2. Are that spell's COLOURED pips covered by your untapped LANDS (Rule #1's pip count) without
     the Battlement's green?
If either answer is no, the green is wasted and you have turned a 0/4 blocker into a tapped card.
The engine now passes these windows for you when tapping is the ONLY thing offered, so a
"Add N green mana with Overgrown Battlement" line you actually SEE means something else was on the
menu too - which means the answer to question 1 is a real card, or the answer is pass.

=== RULE #2 - THE WIN BUTTON ===
Check your battlefield line at every decision. If it lists BOTH "Exquisite Blood" and "Sanguine
Bond", you are one life-gain away from winning outright. Take the first of these the list offers,
immediately, and the game ends:
 - Declare a block with ANY wall, if Perimeter Captain is on your battlefield (2 life).
 - Declare a block with Pride Guardian (3 life, Captain or no Captain).
 - Deal 1 Damage with Staff of Nin targeting a player - pick the OPPONENT.
 - Cast Tribute to Hunger while the opponent has a creature (you gain its toughness).
 - Attack with a lifelink Vampire and connect.
Any one of these wins from any life total, yours or theirs. You do not need a big number - 1 life is
enough, because the loop runs itself until the opponent is at 0. Do not wait for a better turn and
do not build a bigger board first.

=== RULE #3 - ASSEMBLING THE PAIR (your fastest win condition) ===
Exquisite Blood {4}{B}: whenever an opponent loses life, you gain that much life.
Sanguine Bond {3}{B}{B}: whenever you gain life, the opponent loses that much life.
Both together end the game the moment any life changes. But the two halves are NOT the same card:
 - SANGUINE BOND ALONE IS ALREADY A CLOCK. Every point of life you gain takes a point off the
   opponent, and this deck gains life constantly: 2 per defender that blocks with a Perimeter
   Captain out, 3 whenever Pride Guardian blocks, the toughness of whatever Tribute to Hunger eats,
   1 for every point a lifelink Vampire connects with. With a Bond down and three walls blocking
   under a Captain you take 6 off them in one combat, and a SECOND Bond doubles it. Games of yours
   have been won from 20 with a Bond and walls and no Exquisite Blood anywhere. So once Sanguine
   Bond has resolved, keep blocking with everything and keep pinging - you are already killing
   them, and finding Exquisite Blood only makes it instant.
 - EXQUISITE BLOOD ALONE DOES NOTHING you can start yourself: it only pays you when the opponent
   is ALREADY losing life. Against an opponent with no creatures and no burn it is a blank.
So when you have NEITHER piece, Sanguine Bond is the one to look for first.
 1. The turn either one appears in your casting list, CAST IT. Not later, not after a wall, not
    once you can afford both.
 2. If one is already on your battlefield and the other is not in your hand, "Cast Idyllic Tutor
    {2}{w}" is your best play in that list - ahead of every wall and every other card.
 3. Idyllic Tutor puts the enchantment into your HAND, not onto the battlefield. You still have to
    cast it for 5, and Tutor plus enchantment in one turn costs 8 mana. So cast the Tutor the FIRST
    turn it is listed - the turn you spend tutoring is a turn you are not casting the enchantment.
 4. The Tutor then shows you your whole library as a long numbered list. Pick the one of the two you
    do NOT already have; if you have NEITHER, pick SANGUINE BOND - it is the half that wins on its
    own (Rule #3).
 5. You run four Idyllic Tutors and two copies of each enchantment. If a piece is countered or
    exiled, tutor for the next copy and cast it again. There is no other way to win this game.

=== RULE #4 - TRIBUTE TO HUNGER IS AN EDICT, NOT A KILL SPELL ===
"Cast Tribute to Hunger {2}{b}" - the OPPONENT picks which of their creatures dies, and they will
pick their worst one. You gain life equal to THAT creature's toughness.
The thought "cast Tribute to Hunger on their best creature" is ALWAYS FALSE - you never choose the
victim, and naming one in your plan makes you expect a kill and a life total you will not get.
Cast it when any of these is true:
 - Both enchantments are on your battlefield and the opponent has a creature. This WINS the game.
 - The opponent controls exactly ONE creature - then their choice is your choice.
 - THE OPPONENT'S ONLY CREATURE IS ONE YOUR BOARD CANNOT BLOCK. None of your sixteen defenders
   and none of your Vampire tokens has FLYING or REACH, so a creature tagged [flying] on the
   opponent battlefield line connects for its full power every single turn no matter how many
   walls you have. Your walls do not answer it; Tribute to Hunger is the only card in this deck
   that does. When it is their ONLY creature, Tribute takes it - at ANY life total, in the first
   window Tribute is offered, ahead of Exquisite Blood and ahead of Sanguine Bond.
   The thought "I will block it with all my walls" is ALWAYS FALSE against a [flying] attacker.
   You lost a game on turn 10 to exactly this: their only creature was Master of the Feast (5/5
   [flying]), you were at 13 life, you had six mana and both Tribute to Hunger and Sanguine Bond
   in hand, and you cast the Bond. Tribute would have eaten the Master - their only creature, so
   no choice for them - and gained you 5.
 - You need the life right now to survive the turn.
Otherwise hold it. It is an instant, it stays castable on their turn, and it will be offered again.
The engine also lists it when the opponent has no creature at all; with an empty opponent board,
take a different option.

=== RULE #5 - BLOCKING IS HOW THIS DECK DEALS DAMAGE ===
The combat prompt prints a general hint about trades and about taking damage while ahead on
life. It also says your strategy guide overrides it. THIS SECTION IS THAT OVERRIDE: run the
rules below and nothing the hint says. Your walls have 0 power, so no block of yours ever wins
a trade - blocks are how you gain life, and life is how you win.
STEP 1 - READ THE PARENTHESIS ON EVERY LINE YOU ARE ABOUT TO USE, BEFORE YOU ASSIGN ANYTHING.
The B-line prints, for each attacker that blocker may block, whether your blocker LIVES ("neither
dies", "you kill it") or DIES ("your blocker dies, attacker lives"). That parenthesis outranks the
"[deals 0 - this block kills nothing, but it STOPS all N damage from reaching you]" tag beside it,
and it outranks everything below in this section. The STOPS-N tag is the pull that has cost you
this deck's games: it is a true statement about ONE combat, and a wall that survives stops that
much damage in EVERY combat after it.
 - If a B-line offers you a "dies" attacker and a "lives" attacker, take the LIVES one, even when
   the dies one has the bigger "deals N". You put Pride Guardian on Nadaar (3/3, "your blocker
   dies") when the same line offered a Goblin at "neither dies"; you stopped 3 instead of 1 and
   lost the wall, and two turns later you had no blockers at all and took 10.
 - If EVERY attacker offered to that blocker says "your blocker dies", leave that blocker OUT -
   unless an enchantment of yours is on the battlefield, or the combat header's "you would be at N"
   number is 8 or less. You blocked a Triumphant Adventurer (1/1, first strike, deathtouch) with
   Perimeter Captain at 20 life to stop ONE damage, and the Captain - the card that turns every
   future block into 2 more life - died to it on turn 4. That game ended on turn 10.
STEP 2 - assign every blocker that STEP 1 did not rule out, in every combat:
 - Give each attacker one blocker first, then put the leftovers on the biggest attacker. Several of
   your blockers may gang up on one attacker; each triggers its own life gain.
 - Perimeter Captain gains you 2 life for EACH defender you control that blocks. Three walls
   blocking with the Captain out is 6 life on top of what those walls gain themselves.
 - Pride Guardian gains you 3 life whenever it blocks.
 - The life arrives when blockers are DECLARED, before damage. A wall that dies in combat has
   already gained you the life.
Which attacker each blocker takes: STEP 1 already decided the LIVES-before-dies half of this. The
life gain is identical either way - a wall that survives gains it again next combat, and a wall
that dies gains it once. When two or more attackers all let that blocker live, READ THE "deals N" NUMBER OFF EACH OF THEIR
A-LINES AND TAKE THE BIGGEST - not the one whose name sounds smaller, not the one you blocked last
turn. One blocker per attacker first, as above; this tiebreak only chooses among attackers still
unblocked. You have got this wrong: offered "A1. Luminarch Aspirant (3/3) deals 3" and
"A3. Wolf (2/2) deals 2", both reading "(neither dies)", you blocked the Wolf and took 3. The life gain is the same either way, so the only thing
left to decide is how much damage you stop - stop the bigger number.
When every attacker offered to that blocker kills it, STEP 1 has already benched that blocker -
UNLESS an enchantment of yours is out or the header's "you would be at N" is 8 or less, and in
that one case it blocks and you put it on the attacker with the biggest "deals N" number on its
A-line. Read the number off the line, do not judge by name, by size in words, or by which one you
blocked last turn. An attacker tagged [deathtouch] kills your wall whatever its toughness; its
line will already say so.
The blocker you leave out is STEP 1's: neither enchantment on your battlefield AND that blocker's
line reads "your blocker dies, attacker lives" on EVERY attacker it is offered. A line reading
"both die" is not that line - take it; a body of theirs for a body of yours is a trade you want,
and a lifelink blocker gains you life on the way. Once either enchantment is on your battlefield,
every blocker blocks, on any line at all, and so it does whenever the header's "you would be at N"
number is 8 or less - at that life total the damage a body stops is worth more than the body.
"BLOCKS: none" is right only when EVERY blocker the list offers you falls under STEP 1.

=== RULE #6 - ATTACKING ===
All sixteen of your creatures are Defenders and can never attack; the attacker list already leaves
them out, so never re-derive who is eligible. An empty attacker list means ATTACK: none, and that
costs you nothing.
When the list DOES offer a Vampire token, it has lifelink: attack with every Vampire offered, every
turn. Each point that connects is life gained, and with Sanguine Bond out that is the win.
THE COLLAPSED TAG. On a wide board the Vampire's attack tag stops listing their blockers one by
one and reads "they have N untapped creatures that could block this one, biggest X (...)",
sometimes with "- GANG BLOCK: their 3 biggest together deal 4, enough to kill this attacker;
each result above is a LONE blocker only". That parenthesis prices ONE blocker; the GANG BLOCK
clause prices what actually happens, because any number of them may block the same Vampire
together. Your Vampires are 1/1s, so any two bodies eat one.
  - The tag prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause, or N is 2 or more:
    ATTACK: none for that Vampire. Keep it home as a LIFELINK BLOCKER - with both enchantments
    out one block wins the game outright, and a lifelink blocker with Sanguine Bond out is
    already draining them.
  - N is 1 and no GANG BLOCK clause: COUNT YOUR OWN A-LINES FIRST. One blocker can only block one
    attacker, so if the list offers you MORE Vampires than the N on the tag, the surplus connects
    whatever the parenthesis says - send them ALL. Four Vampires into their one Siege-Gang
    Commander is three points of lifelink damage and one dead Vampire; you sent them, the opponent
    was at 6, and it ended the game. Only when your Vampire count is 1 and N is 1 does the
    parenthesis decide it on its own.
  - The tag lists no blockers at all - no "their untapped blockers:" clause on the A-line: they
    have nothing to block with. Send every Vampire. You answered ATTACK: none to a bare A-line and
    threw away a free point of lifelink.
The exception is the win button: with BOTH enchantments on your battlefield, one point of
lifelink damage connecting ends the game, so send if their side is empty of untapped blockers.
ONE stop, and read it off your own narration: if the last time you attacked it says your attacker
took damage and died before combat damage - "<something> dealt N damage to Vampire" then "Your
Vampire died" - the opponent controls a permanent that kills anything you send, and the next
Vampire dies to it exactly the same way. Answer ATTACK: none and keep the Vampire on your
battlefield instead: it is a lifelink blocker, and with both enchantments out one block wins the
game outright. Attack again only once that permanent is gone from their battlefield line.

=== STAFF OF NIN AND SORIN ===
Staff of Nin {6}: draws you an extra card at each of your upkeeps, and offers "Deal 1 Damage with
Staff of Nin targeting a player" every turn. Always aim it at the OPPONENT, every upkeep - this is
the one upkeep option you always TAKE. With Exquisite Blood down that damage is life for you; with
both enchantments down it wins the game on the spot.
Sorin, Lord of Innistrad {2}{W}{B}: cast him when offered, then take "+1: create a 1/1 vampire with
Sorin, Lord of Innistrad" every turn. The Vampires have lifelink, so they block for life, attack for
life, and win the game once both enchantments are out.
If neither is in your hand, nothing above changes.

=== MULLIGAN ===
Keep almost everything. The pregame prompt prints "Your hand (7 cards), counted by the engine: N
lands, M spells." KEEP whenever N is 2 or more. Also KEEP whenever the line "Playing every land in
this hand would cover the cost of:" names even one card - that line already checked your mana
against your hand and it is right.
Never mulligan more than once. If the prompt contains the words "having already taken", this is your
last look: answer 1 (Keep this hand).
The thought "I will keep whatever I draw next" is how you end up with a four-card hand - keep THIS
one instead.
Your cheapest spell costs ONE mana, so "no early play" is never a reason to mulligan, and a hand
with no combo piece is not one either: your lands and walls buy the turns you need to draw one.
WHEN A KEEP MAKES YOU BOTTOM CARDS - the section does not end at the keep. The prompt says "you
took N mulligans and kept, so you must put N cards from your hand on the BOTTOM" and lists your
hand as a NUMBERED list, each land marked "(land: taps for ...)".
FIRST, RULE THE LANDS OUT. Count the "(land: taps for" lines. If bottoming a land would leave you
with fewer than THREE, no land on that list is an answer - cross them all off before you look at
anything else. You need five mana for an enchantment and the Battlement pays none of its black
pips. You bottomed a Woodland Cemetery out of a three-land hand that also held a Chromatic Lantern
{3}, and you did it while the Cemetery was one of only two black sources in the hand.
THEN bottom your most expensive SPELL, then a duplicate wall - you run sixteen of them and the
second copy of one wall is the cheapest card in any hand.
NEVER bottom Sanguine Bond, NEVER bottom Exquisite Blood, and NEVER bottom Idyllic Tutor: those
are the only cards in this deck that win a game, and the Tutor is how you find the other two.
