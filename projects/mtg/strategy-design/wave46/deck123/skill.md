# Strategy-writing skill proposals from deck123, wave 46
Only what this seat's evidence supports. Each is offered as an amendment candidate; the synthesis
lane owns the skill file.

## PROPOSAL S46-A - A RULE MAY NAME A REMOVAL TYPE ONLY IF THE DECK RUNS IT. Check the sixty.
EVIDENCE. CHECK 1 told this deck to hold its attack "until a Tragic Slip, a Damnation or an edict
has taken the LAST of them off that line", over a name list containing Sanguine Bond and
Exquisite Blood - both `type=Enchantment`, and deck123's sixty contain no enchantment removal.
The pilot obeyed: fourteen Humans stayed home from turn 15, and then it cast Damnation "to remove
the Sanguine Bond", killed its own fourteen creatures, and lost 42-0 with both enchantments still
on the battlefield in the next window.
THE RULE the skill could carry: whenever a guide sentence says "until X is removed", the writer
must verify against the DECK LIST that a card in the sixty can remove a thing of X's type - and
when it cannot, the sentence must not be written as a wait. An unreachable release condition is
not a conservative rule; it is an unconditional one, and it silently teaches that the named
removal works on the named card.
WHY THIS IS NOT ALREADY COVERED: the existing card-fact discipline verifies the CARD (I verified
Sanguine Bond's text every wave); it does not verify that the deck can act on it. The check that
was missing is a join between the guide's release clause and the deck list.

## PROPOSAL S46-B - PLACEMENT BEATS WORDING WHEN THE ASK FIRES BEFORE THE READER REACHES THE RULE.
EVIDENCE. The boundary pass wrote a correct, keyed, imperative mulligan chain stop and put it at
STEP 1 of the MULLIGAN section - line ~340 of a 424-line file, for the ask that fires before
turn one. Two of six games then ran the chain to zero and one card. The text was right; nothing
about its wording would have been improved by another pass.
THE RULE: when a rule governs the FIRST ask of a game, or an ask that precedes the situations the
rest of the guide describes, it goes at the top of the file, not in the section named after it.
Amendment 60 already says put a rule where its trigger is; this sharpens it - for the pre-game
asks, "where its trigger is" means BEFORE the deck thesis's downstream rules, because the reader
has no board yet and no reason to have read them.

## PROPOSAL S46-C - A VIOLATED-UNPUNISHED THRESHOLD THAT IS ALSO OBEYED-WRONGLY BELOW ITSELF IS
## INERT, AND THE SUNSET SHOULD FIRE ON EITHER SIGNAL, NOT ONLY ON ZERO VIOLATIONS.
EVIDENCE. The 12-creature stop was crossed 10 times unpunished in wave 45 and raised to 20 with a
sunset reading "if wave 46 shows 0 violations at 20 ... wave 47 deletes it". Wave 46 produced 6
crossings at 20-23 (both games won) AND 6 declines at counts 3 to 15 where the rule says take the
line. The literal sunset condition (0 violations) was not met, so a mechanical reading keeps the
number - but the second signal is the stronger evidence that the number is doing nothing: it is
not being used as a rule in either direction, and it is being credited for a stop the pilot makes
for its own reasons.
THE RULE: a numeric threshold's sunset test should be two-sided. Count crossings above it AND
compliance failures below it. A threshold that is crossed unpunished and disobeyed underneath is
inert regardless of the crossing count, and should be replaced by a condition with evidence
rather than re-tuned to a new number. (Skill 35's "do not delete the only stop" is satisfied by
REPLACING it, which is what deck123 did: power-vs-their-life instead of a headcount.)

## PROPOSAL S46-D - RECORD THE MEASURED VALUE OF A THRESHOLD'S OWN WHY, EVEN WHEN IT CONFIRMS.
EVIDENCE. The 20-cap's WHY claimed "the render collapses a wide board ... so board width costs no
decision quality below 20". Wave 46 measured it: the largest deck123 prompt at 23+ creatures is
19,082 chars, identical to its largest at 12+, and the two biggest prompts at the seat are a
library reveal and a 0-creature board. The WHY was TRUE - and confirming it is what removed the
last argument for keeping any headcount, so the confirmation was more useful than a refutation
would have been.
THE RULE: amendment 61 asks for a WHY per threshold; this adds that the next wave should MEASURE
the WHY's observable and record the number, whether it confirms or refutes. A confirmed WHY that
no longer implies the rule is a deletion, not a renewal.
