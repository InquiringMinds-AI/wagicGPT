REVELATION FRACTURE - UW DRAW-GO CONTROL

You never have a creature on the battlefield and nothing in this deck attacks. You win by answering
every threat the opponent makes and then killing them one damage at a time with Staff of Nin, while
Elixir of Immortality shuffles your spent answers back into your deck. Every turn is the same three
actions: play a land, answer the most dangerous thing on their side, and tap every Staff of Nin you
control at their face. You are the control player in every matchup and you have all the time in the
world - but you take one of those three actions EVERY turn, never none.

=== #0 - PLAY THE LAND. EVERY TURN. BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE. ===
When the prompt asks "Land drop:" and lists "Play <land>" options, TAKE ONE. Every turn, at
any life total, with any hand, however many lands you already have. "Play no land right now"
is never your answer while a land is listed.
A land in play is a counterspell you can pay for on their turn; a land in your hand is a card
you discard at seven. The land drop does not carry over - a turn you skip it is a turn of mana
you never get back, and it is a card that stays in your hand and pushes something else out at
end of turn.
Which land: take the one whose colour you have fewest of. 18 of your lands make {W} and 20 make
{U}; the "Mana available:" line names, one per untapped card, what each source can make. If both
colours are already covered, any land will do - just take one.
Games of yours have been lost this way. In one you passed the land drop on six straight turns
holding a Plains and an Island, then could not pay for a second counterspell on the turn their
engine landed. In another you skipped three drops, hit seven cards, and DISCARDED a Cancel -
then the spell that killed you resolved four turns later with nothing in hand to stop it.

=== #1 - STAFF OF NIN IS YOUR WIN CONDITION. EMRAKUL IS NOT YOUR PLAN. ===
Emrakul, the Aeons Torn costs {15} - fifteen mana. Your mana is 26 lands and nothing else: this deck
has no ramp, no mana rock and no cost reduction, so one land per turn is all the mana you will ever
have. Your plan is therefore this, and you can write it as your PLAN line word for word:
   "Answer their threats, resolve Staff of Nin, ping their face every turn."
The thought "ramp to 15 mana and cast Emrakul", the thought "I just need to survive until I can cast
Emrakul", and the thought "attack when board presence allows" are ALWAYS FALSE in this deck. There is
no ramp to do, and you have no creature to attack with. When one of them shows up in the plan you
were given, replace the plan with the line above.
If "Cast Emrakul, the Aeons Torn" is ever actually printed in your numbered list, take it - the game
only prints a cast you can already pay for. Until that line appears, Emrakul is a blank card and
changes nothing you do this turn.
This line outranks the heading above it. "Emrakul is not your plan" describes what you do while
Emrakul is uncastable; the moment "Cast Emrakul, the Aeons Torn" is printed in your numbered list
it IS castable, so you take it - including on a turn when the opponent is nearly dead and your
Staff would finish them anyway, because Emrakul finishes them sooner.
Once an Emrakul of yours IS on the battlefield, the two sentences above about having no creature
stop applying to it: declare it as an attacker in every attackers window that offers it, and
assign it as a blocker whenever the block line does not read "your blocker dies". Nothing else in
this guide changes.

=== STAFF OF NIN - THE CLOCK ===
Staff of Nin {6} artifact, two copies. Its line reads: "At the beginning of your upkeep, draw a card.
-- {T}: Staff of Nin deals 1 damage to any target." It is the only damage and the only extra card
this deck has.
THE TAP-OUT GATE, and it covers EVERY mana you spend on your own turn - a Sphinx's Revelation, a
Staff of Nin, an Elixir of Immortality cast OR activated, a Lightmine Field. Look at your hand for
ANY of Essence Scatter, Cancel, Dream Fracture or Fall of the Gavel - all four, not the three
cheap ones. If any counterspell at all is in your hand, the spell you are about to cast must leave
your "Mana available:" line reading 5 or more AFTERWARDS. Five, because Fall of the Gavel costs
{3}{U}{W} and it is the counter you are most often holding alone. If it does not, PASS: the spell
you wanted still costs the same next turn with a land behind it, and the turn you spend tapped out
is the turn their engine lands uncountered. A resolved Sanguine Bond, Exquisite Blood or
Underworld Dreams costs you the game; three extra cards do not win it.
- CAST STAFF OF NIN ONLY WHEN YOUR "Mana available:" LINE READS 9 OR MORE. One number, no
  subtraction, no branch on which counter you are holding: Staff costs 6, and 9 minus 6 is the 3
  that keeps a Cancel or a Dream Fracture live. The only exception is a hand with NO counterspell
  in it at all - then 6 is enough. Below 9 with a counter in hand, pass this turn and cast the
  Staff next turn with a land to spare: a Staff you cast one turn later still wins, and a turn
  spent tapped out is the turn their engine lands uncountered. This rule outranks "cast it the
  first turn it appears"; nothing else does. You have cast it at 6 and at 7 with a Cancel in hand,
  three times in six games.
- Keep taking the "Deal 1 Damage with Staff of Nin ... targeting a player" option in every window it
  is offered, including upkeep, and with EVERY Staff you control. That damage is how the game ends.
- Point it at a creature or a planeswalker only when 1 damage finishes it: printed toughness 1, or a
  planeswalker showing exactly 1 loyalty counter. Otherwise it goes at the player.

=== SPEND YOUR ANSWERS ON WHAT ACTUALLY KILLS YOU ===
You hold 13 counterspells - 4 Essence Scatter {1}{U} (creature spells only), 3 Dream Fracture
{1}{U}{U} (counters anything, you both draw), 3 Cancel {1}{U}{U}, 3 Fall of the Gavel {3}{W}{U}
(counters anything and gains you 5 life). You can afford to spend them freely; each one is still only
worth whatever it stopped.
The stack line and the counter option both print the target's TYPE, MANA COST and (P/T):
"1 (top): opponent's Overgrown Battlement {1}{g} (creature 0/4) [spell]". Read them and decide
from the numbers, not from the name:
  - (creature P/T) with printed POWER 2 or more: COUNTER IT.
  - (creature P/T) tagged [defender], at ANY printed power: LET IT RESOLVE. A defender cannot
    attack, so it can never take a point off you, and you have no creature for it to block.
    Pride Guardian (0/3), Wall of Omens (0/4), Fog Bank (0/2) and Shield Sphere (0/6) are this
    shape. This line has no exception: a counter spent on a wall is a counter you do not have
    for the enchantment that kills you. In a game you lost you spent Dream Fracture on a Pride
    Guardian and Fall of the Gavel on a Wall of Omens on the same turn; four turns later the
    opponent tutored up Sanguine Bond and resolved it with your hand empty of answers.
  - (creature P/T) with printed power 0 or 1 and NOT tagged [defender]: LET IT RESOLVE - unless
    its {target text: ...} on
    that same option line contains "{T}:", "Whenever ... attacks", "At the beginning of" or
    "venture". A creature spell whose text says one of those DOES SOMETHING EVERY TURN and its
    printed power does not measure it: COUNTER IT, whatever the P/T reads. Dwarven Blastminer
    (1/1, "{2}{R},{T}: Destroy target nonbasic land"), Luminarch Aspirant (1/1, a +1/+1 counter
    every combat) and Triumphant Adventurer (1/1, ventures on every attack) are this shape - the
    same shape the Path section names, read one step earlier while it is still on the stack.
    Otherwise LET IT RESOLVE: it cannot kill you, and Path to Exile or a sweeper answers it later
    for less mana. This holds however expensive it was and however much the reasoning wants the
    counter spent.
  - anything printed (land), (artifact) with no P/T, or (instant)/(sorcery) that only draws or
    searches: LET IT RESOLVE. A MANA ARTIFACT is the sharpest case of this line and the one you
    keep getting wrong: Chromatic Lantern {3}, Talisman of Impulse {2}, any artifact whose
    {target text:} says "Add" and nothing else. It deals no damage, draws no card and kills
    nothing; all it does is let them cast the spell you actually need the counter for. In a game
    you lost you spent Dream Fracture on a Chromatic Lantern on turn 7; the Sanguine Bond it was
    fixing the mana for resolved on turn 12 with no counter left in your hand.
COUNTER ON SIGHT regardless of the numbers - these are the names whose danger is not visible in a
P/T, and this list is short on purpose:
  - every planeswalker;
  - every permanent that hits you or grows them EVERY turn: Underworld Dreams, Fate Unraveler,
    Sanguine Bond, Exquisite Blood, Howling Mine, Staff of Nin, Nuisance Engine,
    Liliana's Caress;
  - Idyllic Tutor: it is not a permanent, but it fetches the enchantment half of their combo.
  - anything whose text makes a player DRAW extra cards, whatever its printed type: Howling Mine,
    Teferi's Puzzle Box, Dictate of Kruphix, Forced Fruition. This one outranks the "(artifact)
    with no P/T: let it resolve" line above. Extra cards cost you life whenever they have
    Underworld Dreams or Fate Unraveler out, and cost you library either way.

=== PATH TO EXILE - YOUR ONLY ANSWER TO A CREATURE THAT ALREADY LANDED ===
Path to Exile {W} instant, four copies: "Exile target creature." These are the ONLY cards in your
deck that remove one creature that is already on the battlefield. Essence Scatter and every other
counter you own can only stop a spell on the stack; once a creature has resolved they do nothing to
it.
The "legal targets right now:" clause prints each candidate's (P/T) and its tags right there on
the option line. Read them off that line.
BEFORE you use the power rule below, read each candidate's own rules text on the
opponent battlefield line. A creature whose text contains "{T}:", "Whenever ...
attacks", "At the beginning of", or "venture" DOES SOMETHING EVERY TURN, and printed
power 1 does not measure it. Path THAT creature the first window it is offered, at any
life total: it is the same danger the counter-on-sight list names, and Path is the only
answer you have once it has landed. Dwarven Blastminer (1/1, "{2}{R},{T}: Destroy
target nonbasic land"), Triumphant Adventurer (1/1, ventures on every attack) and
Luminarch Aspirant (1/1, a +1/+1 counter every combat) are this shape.
Only when NO candidate has such a line do you use the power rule:
Then: cast Path to Exile at the creature with the HIGHEST printed power. If EVERY candidate on that
clause reads printed power 0 or 1, or is tagged [defender], answer "Cast
nothing right now" and keep the Path - at any life total above 12, whatever else is on the board
and however many turns you have been holding it. A 0/4 wall is never a Path target.
Your life is 12 or less: that floor is off for creatures that can attack - exile the one with
the highest printed power. A creature tagged [defender] still never gets the Path, at any life
total: it cannot attack, so it is not what is killing you.
Path lets its controller fetch a basic land. That cost is the point of the card; always pay it.

=== SWEEPERS - THE HEADER COUNTS THE CREATURES FOR YOU ===
Your battlefield has no creatures, so a board wipe only ever costs the opponent. The opponent
battlefield header gives you the number: "Opponent battlefield (11 permanents listed, of which 3 are
creatures)". READ THAT NUMBER FIRST, before you look at anything else in the menu.
BEFORE YOU USE THAT NUMBER, SUBTRACT THE WALLS. Read the opponent battlefield line itself and take
off every creature tagged [defender] and every creature showing printed power 0. None of them can
attack you and you have no creature for any of them to block, so none of them is a reason to spend
a sweeper. The number you carry into the branches below is what is LEFT after that subtraction, and
it is often 0 when the header says 2 or 3. You have cast a Supreme Verdict into a board of Pride
Guardian (0/3) plus Overgrown Battlement (0/4) at 20 life, and another into Pride Guardian plus two
Overgrown Battlements at 24 life; the header read 2 and 3, the real count was 0 both times, and
both cost you a card and a turn of open mana.
IF THAT NUMBER IS 0, SUPREME VERDICT AND FINAL JUDGMENT ARE NOT CASTABLE PLAYS THIS TURN -
in your FIRST main phase and in your SECOND main phase alike. The header does not change
between them, and neither does this answer. Answer
"Cast nothing right now" and keep them. This line outranks every other reason to cast a sweeper -
being ahead, having spare mana, wanting to use the turn, the plan naming a wipe. A wipe with nothing
to kill is a card you threw away AND a turn you spent tapped out, and the turn you spend tapped out
is the turn their engine lands uncountered. Only when that number is 1 or more do you read on:
- 2 or more creatures: cast Supreme Verdict {1}{W}{W}{U} - its own line says "can't be countered", and
  it means it, so it resolves through their counterspells. With six mana, Final Judgment {4}{W}{W}
  EXILES instead of destroying; use Final Judgment against anything that returns from a graveyard.
- exactly 1 creature, and Path to Exile is in your list: take the Path and keep the sweeper. A
  {W} instant is the right price for one creature; a sweeper is not.
- exactly 1 creature, and that creature is tagged [defender] or shows printed power 0: cast
  NOTHING. It cannot attack you and you have no creature for it to block, so a sweeper aimed at
  it kills a card that was never going to deal you damage - and leaves you tapped out. This is
  the same rule the Path section runs ("a 0/4 wall is never a Path target"); it holds at any
  life total. You have cast a Supreme Verdict at a lone Fog Bank (0/2 defender) at 23 life and a
  Final Judgment at a lone Wall of Omens (0/4 defender) at 31 life; both cost you a card and a
  turn of open mana and killed nothing that mattered.
- exactly 1 creature, and Path to Exile is NOT in your list: cast the sweeper when that creature's
  printed power is 4 or more, otherwise answer "Cast nothing right now" and keep it - unless your
  life is 10 or less and nothing else in your list answers that creature: then cast the sweeper
  whatever its printed power.
- 3 or more creatures, and Lightmine Field is in your list: cast Lightmine Field. At 0, 1 or 2
  creatures on that header it kills nothing at any size, so keep it and spend the mana on a Path
  or a sweeper instead - unless nothing else in your list is worth casting this turn and your
  "Mana available:" line still reads 4 or more AFTER the mana you keep open for a counterspell:
  then cast it in EITHER main phase, so it is already there when the crowd arrives.
- 0 creatures: answer "Cast nothing right now" and keep the sweeper (the hard line at the top of
  this section).

Sphinx's Revelation {X}{W}{U}{U} instant: "You gain X life and draw X cards." At X = 0 it gains

THE TAP-OUT GATE, and it covers EVERY mana you spend on your own turn - a Sphinx's Revelation, a
Staff of Nin, an Elixir of Immortality cast OR activated, a Lightmine Field. Look at your hand for
ANY of Essence Scatter, Cancel, Dream Fracture or Fall of the Gavel - all four, not the three
cheap ones. If any counterspell at all is in your hand, the spell you are about to cast must leave
your "Mana available:" line reading 5 or more AFTERWARDS. Five, because Fall of the Gavel costs
{3}{U}{W} and it is the counter you are most often holding alone. If it does not, PASS: the spell
you wanted still costs the same next turn with a land behind it, and the turn you spend tapped out
is the turn their engine lands uncountered. A resolved Sanguine Bond, Exquisite Blood or
Underworld Dreams costs you the game; three extra cards do not win it.
Cast Sphinx's Revelation when your "Mana available:" line reads SIX or more - that is X = 3 or better.
Below six, answer "Cast nothing right now" and hold it - it is an instant and it will still be
there next turn with one more land behind it - unless your life is 10 or less and no other option
in your list is worth casting: then cast it, at the same X = 3 as any other Revelation.
BEFORE you take Sphinx's Revelation, or any other optional draw, read the line "Your library: N
cards". If N is 5 or less, answer "Cast nothing right now" and keep the card: drawing your last
card loses the game outright, and Sphinx's Revelation draws X on top of your normal draw step.
Cast it only when you have something to DO with the cards. If your "Your hand:" line already runs
past the width of a single reading - if you can see a counterspell, a Path and a sweeper in it
already - answer "Cast nothing right now" and keep the Revelation. It is an instant; it will still
be there on the turn your hand is empty and you need it. A Revelation cast onto a full hand is
three cards discarded at end of turn.
THE DRAW-IS-DAMAGE STOP, AND IT OUTRANKS EVERY OTHER LINE IN THIS SECTION. Read the OPPONENT
battlefield line before you take any draw. If any permanent there prints an {effect:} containing
"Whenever an opponent draws" - Underworld Dreams, Fate Unraveler, Ob Nixilis the Hate-Twisted are

any hand size, at any life total. Answer "Cast nothing right now" and hold it. The same goes for
any other optional draw. Your answer to those permanents is a counterspell on the stack, never a
bigger hand afterwards. You lost a game to this: their line showed Fate Unraveler, two Underworld
Dreams and an Ob Nixilis beside three Howling Mines, one draw step took you from 20 to 5, and you
then cast a Sphinx's Revelation at 6 life and drew yourself down to 4.
Once the opponent's printed life is 10 or less and you control a Staff of Nin, stop drawing
entirely: the Staff clock finishes the game on its own and every extra card is a turn of library
you are spending for nothing.
When your hand holds no counter and no Path to Exile you need the cards more than you need
anything else - but the draw-is-damage stop above and the library floor below still bind, because a
card drawn out of an empty library, or drawn under an Underworld Dreams, is not a card you get to
use.
YOUR X IS 3. NOT THE LARGEST NUMBER ON THE MENU.
The X menu prints its values largest first and its own sentence tells you the largest X you can
AFFORD. Affordable is not correct: the largest affordable X has been your answer in games you then
threw away, because everything you hold above seven cards at end of turn is DISCARDED - cards you
paid mana for and threw away - and if the opponent battlefield line shows Liliana's Caress,
Underworld Dreams or Fate Unraveler, each discarded card ALSO costs you life on the way to the

So: reply with the OPTION number whose line reads "X = 3". Every time, and with no arithmetic in

field to stand on, and this deck does not use one. Three is the number.
THE ONE COMPARISON THAT CAN LOWER IT, and nothing can raise it: read "Your library: N cards".
Subtract 2, and one more for EACH Staff of Nin on your battlefield. If that number is smaller than
3, announce THAT number instead. Drawing from an empty library loses the game outright.
If "X = 3" is not on the menu, take the largest value the menu does offer that is below 3.
Worked examples, both from real games of yours. Library 31, one Staff, menu up to 13: 31-2-1 = 28,
which is not below 3, so X = 3 - not the 13 the menu topped out at. Library 20, two Staffs, menu up

=== ELIXIR OF IMMORTALITY - THE RECYCLER ===
Elixir of Immortality {1} artifact, two copies: "{2},{T}: You gain 5 life. Shuffle Elixir of
Immortality and your graveyard into library." Every counterspell and every Path you have already
spent goes back into your deck, and the Elixir goes back with them, so you can draw it and cast it
again. That is why this deck never runs out of answers.
Cast it the turn it is listed when you have a spare {1} AND the tap-out gate above still passes.
ACTIVATING it is the same spend as casting a spell and the gate covers it too: the {2} has to leave
your "Mana available:" line reading 5 or more while any counterspell is in your hand. Otherwise
activate it only when your life is 15 or less or your library is 16 or less - those two are worth
a tapped-out turn and a spare {2} in a full hand is not. You lost a game to this: on your turn 11
you cast the Elixir and activated it down to two open mana with a Fall of the Gavel in hand, and
their Sanguine Bond - the first name on your counter-on-sight list - resolved on turn 12 with no
window offered, because you could not pay for one.
Read "Your library: N cards" at every decision. If N is 16 or less and an Elixir of
Immortality of yours is on the battlefield untapped, ACTIVATE IT IN THAT WINDOW, ahead
of a Staff ping and ahead of any spell. Sixteen, not twelve: with two Staffs of Nin you draw three
cards a turn, and the window that offers the Elixir is a priority window that only comes around
every other turn - by the time N reads 12 the next offer can be at 6.
Shuffling your graveyard back is the only thing that stops this deck from drawing its last
card, and drawing from an empty library loses the game outright at any life total. This rule has nothing to do with your life:
at 98 life with 17 cards left you are the player who is losing.

=== LIGHTMINE FIELD - IT ONLY PUNISHES A CROWD ===
Lightmine Field {2}{W}{W}: "Whenever one or more creatures attack, Lightmine Field deals damage to
each of those creatures equal to the number of attacking creatures." Three attackers take 3 each and
die; ONE attacker takes 1 damage, which kills nothing however large it is.
Its gate is the same header count you already read for your sweepers - see that list.

=== READING THE MANA LINE ===
Every rule in this guide that says "your Mana available: line" means the number on that line -
with one exception the render tells you about itself. When the line reads
"Mana available: 0 total (no untapped sources) | Already in pool: {u}{w}{w}... (N mana ALREADY
produced and floating right now)", the number you have to spend IN THIS DECISION is N, the
floating one. Use N wherever a rule below asks for "Mana available:".
But floating mana is spent-or-lost: it is produced mana, not an untapped land, and it does not
survive to the opponent's turn. Mana you are "keeping open" for a counterspell has to be an
UNTAPPED SOURCE, never a pool. So when the whole of your mana is floating, the counterspell
subtraction below cannot be satisfied at all - spend the pool this turn on whatever the rules
name, and do not tell yourself you are holding a counter up.

=== YOUR MANA AND YOUR WINDOWS ===
You get a window on the opponent's turn only when they cast a spell, and the prompt shows it to you
under "ON THE STACK". You are never offered an end-of-their-turn window, so mana you hold open
through a turn where they cast nothing is mana you threw away.
So keep {1}{U} open for Essence Scatter, or {1}{U}{U} for Cancel or Dream Fracture, and spend
everything ABOVE that on your own turn - a Staff of Nin, an Elixir activation, a Sphinx's Revelation,
a Lightmine Field. Ending your turn with all your mana unspent is right only when your hand is
nothing but counterspells.

=== MULLIGAN ===
STEP 1 - READ THE PRICE LINE FIRST, AND STOP THERE IF IT ANSWERS. The ask prints the number:
"having already taken 2 mulligans you will bottom 2 cards on a keep (keeping 5)". If it says
(keeping 5) or ANY lower number: KEEP, if the hand holds at least one land - you are DONE, do
not read another rule in this section. The bar is ONE LAND and nothing else: not a colour, not
a counterspell, not a curve.
The London mulligan already shaves a seven to six for one card, so a third look buys a fresh
seven at the price of TWO. Two seats in one corpus went to four cards and both lost.
STEP 2 - only on your untouched opening seven, or at "(keeping 6)", is the comparison below the
question at all.

The question is never "does this hand meet a number". It is: what are the odds THIS hand does
something in time, against the odds a six-card hand does something in time? Two facts about this
deck set that comparison, and the prompt gives you the rest:
  - 26 of your 60 cards are lands, so a two-land seven sees its third land inside two or three
    draws and its fourth by about turn five. Waiting for lands is a bet you usually win.
  - Your job is REACTIVE. Nothing here has to happen on turn two; the cost of a slow start is only
    the cost of the threats that land before you can answer them.
The prompt hands you the three numbers the comparison needs, and you read them rather than counting
the hand yourself:
  "Your hand (7 cards), counted by the engine: N lands, M spells."
  "Mana sources among those lands, counted by the engine: {W} n, {U} n."
  "Playing every land in this hand would cover the cost of: ..." - or "would not cover any spell
  in it."
KEEP any hand with 2 to 5 lands whose coverage line names at least one card. That hand can already
act on their first threat, and the odds of improving it by shipping a card are against you.
THINK AGAIN when the coverage line reads "would not cover any spell in it". That sentence means
this hand does nothing until you draw both a land and a colour, and it is the one shape where
seven cards can be worse than six - most sharply when the spells left in the hand are your
expensive ones (Final Judgment {4}{W}{W}, Staff of Nin {6}, Emrakul {15}) rather than a Path to
Exile {W} or an Elixir {1}. Weigh: how many draws until this hand casts ANYTHING, and can you
afford those turns against the deck across the table? Ship it when the answer is "four or more
turns and they have a creature on turn two"; keep it when one land makes it live.
A one-land seven ships. A hand of 2 to 5 lands with a Path or an Elixir in it never does.
Tiebreakers between keepable hands, never a reason to ship on their own: prefer the hand with a
land that makes both colours (Tundra, Glacial Fortress, Seachrome Coast), and prefer the hand with
at least one counterspell. Colour alone is not a reason to mulligan - 18 of your lands make {W} and
20 make {U}, so a one-colour opener is usually two draws from the other.

When a keep makes you bottom cards the ask prints your hand as a NUMBERED list. Walk that list from
the top and send the FIRST card that matches, in this order:
  1. Emrakul, the Aeons Torn.
  2. Any card whose line says "(copy 2 of 2 in your hand)" - a duplicate of anything, a duplicate
     LAND included. Two Islands in a seven is a duplicate and it is the answer; you bottomed a
     Sphinx's Revelation past two Islands instead.
  3. A land beyond the fifth.
  4. Only if none of those exist: your most expensive spell.
NEVER bottom a land when the hand you keep would hold fewer than THREE. NEVER bottom your last
counterspell and never bottom your last Path to Exile - this deck answers threats or it loses,
and a hand with no answer in it is a loss you chose at this ask, not one you were dealt.

- "Deal 1 Damage with Staff of Nin ... targeting a player" is in your list: take it, every window,
  with every Staff.
- "Cast Staff of Nin" is in your list and no answer is needed this turn: take it.
- Their spell on the stack prints (creature P/T) tagged [defender]: let it resolve, whatever the
  numbers say and whatever its text says. Walls never get a counter.
- Their spell on the stack prints (creature P/T) with printed power 0 or 1, or prints no P/T at
  all: let it resolve and keep the counter - unless its {target text:} contains "{T}:",
  "Whenever ... attacks", "At the beginning of" or "venture": then counter it now.
- Their spell on the stack prints (creature P/T) with printed power 2 or more, or is a

- "Cast Emrakul, the Aeons Torn" is printed in your numbered list: TAKE IT. The game only prints a

- The header says 2 or more creatures AFTER you subtract every [defender] and every printed-power-0
