INFECTED WOUNDS - BG INFECT (poison kill)

YOU DO NOT KILL WITH LIFE. Every creature here has INFECT: its combat damage puts POISON
COUNTERS on the opponent instead of reducing their life. 10 poison counters and they LOSE, at
any life total. The general prompt's "you win by reducing the opponent's life from 20 to 0"
DOES NOT APPLY TO THIS DECK - ignore it. Their life is not your target and their lifegain does
nothing to you. You need TEN points of unblocked attack damage, total, ever.

== THE CLOCK - THE BOARD PRINTS BOTH POISON TOTALS. READ THEM. ==
The prompt carries a line like
  "Poison counters (opponent): 4 of 10 - the opponent LOSES the game at 10 poison counters,
   whatever the life total is; 6 more end it."
and the same line for you whenever poison is in the game - yours may read "0 of 10", which is
a rendered fact, not an error. The game log also prints every change:
  "- Poison: the opponent takes 2 poison counters - now 6 of 10 (...)"
  "- Infect damage: 2 from Contagious Nim to the opponent - dealt as POISON COUNTERS, not life
   loss: no life was lost (see the Poison line)"
READ THE NUMBER OFF THAT LINE. Never add up the log by hand and never reconcile poison against
their life total: their life staying at 20 while their poison climbs is what infect looks like
working. To forecast a swing, take the printed poison number and add the POWER of each attacker
you are declaring - the power is printed on its own option line, e.g. "A1. Ichor Rats (2/1)".

== #1 RULE - THE BLOCK DECISION, IN ONE READING ==
At every "declare blockers" ask the prompt prints one line of the form
  "Your life: 17. Unblocked, these attackers deal up to 4 - you would be at 13 - NOT lethal: ..."
Call the number that comes after "you would be at" YOUR LIFE AFTER. In that example YOUR LIFE
AFTER is 13, not 4. It is never the damage number. Then, in order:

 1. If the line says LETHAL - block, with as many bodies as it takes to survive the turn.
 2. Otherwise look at the attackers list. If any attacker shows "[counters: Nx +1/+1]", or its
    printed power is bigger than it was the last time it attacked, subtract its power from YOUR
    LIFE AFTER one more time - that is the turn after this one, and it is already on the board.
 3. If YOUR LIFE AFTER (after step 2) is 13 or less - BLOCK NOW. Chump or trade with your
    SMALLEST bodies, as many as it takes.
 4. If YOUR LIFE AFTER is 14 or more - answer "BLOCKS: none". Every body stays alive and
    untapped for your attack.

The words printed after "NOT lethal:" on that line - "block only where the trade favors you;
taking damage while ahead is often correct" - are the general prompt's advice for ordinary
decks. For THIS deck, steps 1-4 REPLACE that advice: take the number off the line, run the
steps, and let the steps answer. Do not use that sentence as a reason to skip a block the
steps demand or to make a block they forbid.

PICKING WHO BLOCKS WHOM - BELIEVE THE PRINTED OUTCOME. Each of your blocker lines prints the
engine's computed result of the pairing in parentheses:
  "B1. Ichorclaw Myr (1/1) [infect] - may block A1 (your blocker dies, attacker lives (infect
   shrinks it to 2/2)), A3 (both die)"
"both die", "your blocker dies, attacker lives", "you kill it, your blocker lives" - that
parenthesis IS the outcome. If your own combat math disagrees with it, your math has a wrong
term in it (a trigger that will not fire, a pump that does not apply) - the parenthesis wins.
Use it to choose assignments: prefer pairings that say "both die" or "you kill it" over pure
chumps when either satisfies steps 1-3.

Blocking to remove a big threat is not a reason on its own: killing their creature advances your
clock by zero counters. Surviving is the reason, and steps 1-3 are how you know. Skithiryx and
Putrefax are attackers only - never name them as blockers at any life total.

Standing loop, every one of your turns, in order:
 1. Play a land if a "Play <land>" option is listed.
 2. Cast the biggest infect creature the Cast list offers. If a Cast option is listed the engine
    has already checked you can pay it - take it, do not recount your lands. Cast two if the
    mana allows, before combat. One exception: if the printed opponent-poison number plus the
    power of the attackers you can declare RIGHT NOW is 10 or more, the attack is the game -
    declare it; anything still castable can wait until after combat.
 3. Attack with every creature listed under "Your creatures that can attack" - that list is
    already the eligible set, summoning-sick bodies are dropped for you. "ATTACK: none" only
    when the list is empty. One exception, only at 5 life or less: add up the power of THEIR
    untapped creatures; if it is your life total or more, hold back the smallest set of your
    bodies whose blocks would keep you alive next turn, and attack with the rest.
 4. Mana left at end of turn is wasted. Spend it on step 2.

When one of your attackers is blocked by several creatures the engine asks you, one position at
a time: "Choose the blocker dealt damage in position 1." Assign to the blocker whose toughness
your attacker's power exactly covers, so the damage kills something instead of spilling.

== MULLIGAN ==
Your cheapest card costs TWO mana; this deck has no 1-drops at all, so "no one-drop" is never a
reason to mulligan - you will never find one.
The engine hands you the answer on two lines. Read them before anything else:
  "Mana sources among those lands, counted by the engine: {B} 2, {G} 1."
  "Playing every land in this hand would cover the cost of: ..." - or -
  "Playing every land in this hand would not cover any spell in it."
KEEP a hand with 2, 3 or 4 lands, at least one creature, AND a "would cover the cost of" line
that names at least one card. MULLIGAN a hand whose line says it would not cover any spell in
it, however many lands it holds - three Forests and a fistful of black spells is a hand that
casts nothing. Mulligan also on 0-1 lands, 5+ lands, or no creature at all.
AT MOST TWO MULLIGANS, then KEEP the best hand you are shown.
Bottoming on a keep: bottom LANDS first - there are 35 in the deck and you will draw more. Keep
every creature.

== SITUATIONS THAT DECIDE THESE GAMES ==
* "Your life: 20 ... you would be at 17 - NOT lethal", their attacker is the same size it was
  last turn -> YOUR LIFE AFTER is 17, which is 14 or more -> "BLOCKS: none".
* "Your life: 17 ... you would be at 11 - NOT lethal", and the attacker shows
  "[counters: 6x +1/+1]" -> subtract 6 again: 5. That is 13 or less -> BLOCK NOW, chump with
  your smallest body. Their army does not stop growing because you ignored it.
* "Your life: 10 ... you would be at 1 - NOT lethal" -> YOUR LIFE AFTER is 1 -> BLOCK, with
  everything you need to survive. "NOT lethal" on that line means you do not die THIS turn; it
  does not mean you can afford it.
* Their life is going UP, they are on 23 -> nothing changed. Keep attacking; life is not the
  number you are moving. Read the Poison counters line for the number that is.
* You are at 1 life, their untapped wolves can hit back for more than that -> hold back the one
  body whose block keeps you alive; attack with everything else. At 6 life or more this
  exception never applies - attack with every creature listed.
* You cast Skithiryx with one black mana left -> pay {B} for haste and attack for 4 now.
* Putrefax is castable -> cast it and attack with it this turn; there is no next turn for it.
* Corpse Cur enters with infect creatures in your graveyard -> take the return, biggest power.
* The Poison counters (opponent) line reads 8 of 10 and you can declare 2 or more power of
  attackers -> declare every one of them. That is the game.
