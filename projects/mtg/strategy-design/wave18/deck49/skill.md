# skill.md — deck49 (Dragons) wave-18 contributions to Step 0-ter (FIRST-GUIDED VALIDATION)

deck49's first guided corpus is a clean worked example of the Step 0-ter doctrine under a
concurrent ENGINE change. Proposed additions/confirmations (single-seat unless noted):

## CONFIRMED rungs (existing doctrine, re-proven)

- **(rule 1 re-proven) read the tax collapse, not the record.** deck49 went 3/6→2/6 while EVERY
  taught class died and all 9 fallbacks vanished. The record moved on the pool swap (deck17 out,
  deck14 in; flipped vs110/vs35/vs135) — exactly the coin-flip surface the rule warns about. The
  guide's JOB (classes dead? tax collapsed?) was met and is legible independent of the 2/6.

- **(rule 4 re-proven) the tax RELOCATED — name the successor, don't declare failure.** Fallbacks
  → 0, but 20/125 replies still ran ≥600 words (peak 2,800). The successor is NOT a taught deck49
  class: it is combat/sequencing math under pressure and stale-plan re-derivation, and every spike
  stayed PARSED. Reply-length is the gauge that survives the fallback collapse; route the successor
  to its layer (here: CORE/general-prompt, not the guide).

## NEW rung (proposed) — the "concurrent engine fix" apportionment discipline

**When a first-guided corpus ALSO ships an engine change that targets the same seam, apportion the
collapse before crediting the guide — and let the apportionment drive the TRIM.** deck49's 7
stale_echoes all lived at the target-sub-menu seam that the wave-17 TARGET CHOICE framing +
"(player, life N)" suffix-strip independently dissolve; the 2 unparsed loops were killed by E-49c
suppression. So the FALLBACK collapse was ~fully ENGINE-attributable, and the guide's paired Rule #2
(target-menu mechanics) became **redundant on the fallback dimension** — a representation-beats-
instruction trim candidate. Method: for each dead class, ask "would the engine change alone have
killed it?" If yes, the guide prose that duplicates the representation is trim-eligible; the guide's
real credit is the classes the engine did NOT touch (here: C1 mana-belief death — the engine never
changed mana representation, so Rule #1 owns that kill). This sharpens rule 5 ("trim validated-dead
sections"): trim not just because a class died, but because the ENGINE now carries the representation.

## NEW instrument (proposed, ADOPTABLE — biases no guide content)

**The burn-target-menu face-rate, scored against offered enemy creatures.** For any "aim your burn"
deck, tabulate: (target menus resolved to FACE) / (total burn/player target menus), AND how many of
the face choices had an enemy creature ON the menu and declined it. deck49 scored 17/17 face, 9 with
an enemy creature offered-and-declined — a countable proof the BURN-GOES-FACE rule is internalized
(vs wave-17's leaky "bolted the Ornithopter"). Cheaper and less arguable than reading prose;
sibling to the deck35 0-attacker-durdle instrument. (Adoptable single-seat: it only makes an
existing signal countable.)

## WATCH carried forward (do not generalize yet)

- **A suppressed class cannot be validated as DEAD — only as "had no chance to fire."** E-49c hid
  every enemy-only War Paint this corpus, so the fatal C4 loop is UNTESTED, not solved. The guide's
  never-aura rule PERSISTED-correct on the mixed-target cases (5/5) but the hard case is masked. Per
  the owner ruling (suppression → warning annotation next corpus), re-validate C4 next wave when
  enemy-only War Paint reappears. Doctrine: when an engine SUPPRESSION overlaps a taught class, record
  the class as "masked," keep the guide rule, and schedule the re-test for when the mask lifts.

- **A guide's permitted OFF-CASE branch can misfire on timing, not direction.** vs35 s17 cast
  Crucible with "a Dragon landing next turn" (the guide's own allowed branch) but the dragon needed
  2 more lands, not 1 — the model read the branch, miscounted the mana runway. Not a rule failure;
  a reminder that "landing next turn" branches inherit the model's curve-arithmetic tax.
