INTRUDERS OF THRABEN - WBU TOKEN COMBO

You win by putting Intruder Alarm onto the battlefield next to Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline
Keeper and tapping that creature for a token over and over: every token that enters untaps all
creatures, so the tap ability comes straight back. Removal buys the turns; the tokens end the
game. Every turn is the same three actions: play a land, deploy a token-maker or the Alarm, then
tap every maker you own until your board is an army.

=== RULE -1 - THE FIRST ASK OF THE GAME IS THE ONE YOU LOSE MOST OFTEN ===
The ask reads "Pre-game mulligan decision (London mulligan)" and, from the second look on, it
prints its own price: "having already taken N mulligans you will bottom N cards on a keep
(keeping M)". READ THE M, then answer by the MULLIGAN section below. In one line: at M of 5 or
less, ONE land in front of you is a keep, because shipping buys the same land odds with one card
fewer while the bottom ask sheds the uncastable cards; only ZERO lands, or a hand of nothing but
lands, buys a fresh look. Last corpus: one mulligan in six looks, correctly.
Read the header before you ship.
THE CARRIED PLAN IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT THIS HAND. From the second look on, "YOUR PLAN (as you
last stated it, N windows ago on turn T)" is your own older text, about the hand you threw away,
not a fact about this one. THAT HOLDS IN EVERY LATER WINDOW TOO: where a
carried plan and a "{right now: ...}" clause disagree, the clause is now and the plan is then -
this deck was handed back "Morbid applies, Rorix dies" beside a row reading "(no creature has
died this turn, so Morbid does NOT apply)", believed the plan, and threw the card away at 1
life. Each look is a NEW seven with its own "counted by the engine: N lands, M spells" header.
Decide from that header; if the plan says mulligan and the header says two or more lands, the
plan is out of date - say so and keep.
A three-land five-card hand beats every hand you have not seen.

=== RULE 0 - PLAY A LAND, EVERY TURN, BEFORE YOU DO ANYTHING ELSE ===
The land drop is its own question. It arrives as "Land drop: which land do you play now, if
any?" with one Play line per land in your hand and one "Play no land right now".
If ANY "Play" line is listed, take one. Every turn. Answer "Play no land right now" only when
that is the only option on the screen. No board and no plan makes holding a land better than
playing it, and the drop is not spent by casting a spell, by a Marsh Flats crack, or by
anything else that puts a land onto the battlefield from somewhere other than your hand. The
screen says so above the question, in these words: "Land drop: NOT yet used this turn - you can
still play a land". That line outranks your own memory of the turn. WHAT THIS COST: after
cracking Marsh Flats for a Tundra on turn 8 this deck answered "Play no land right now" TWICE
in that one turn and wrote "already played Tundra" in its plan while the screen read NOT yet
used; it stayed on three sources, its {2}{b}{b} maker waited two more turns, and it was dead on
turn 11 having cast one spell all game.
READ THE COSTS IN MANA, NOT IN SYMBOLS. {2}{u} is THREE mana, {2}{b}{b} is FOUR, {1}{w}{w} is
THREE. Compare that number to "Mana available: N total", which already counts every untapped
source. If your plan names a spell you cannot afford this window, the fix is the land drop, not
another pass.
THE LABEL IS "CHOICE:", BARE, ON A LINE OF ITS OWN. "ANSWER: CHOICE: 0 (pass)" is not read as an
answer at all: the whole reply is discarded and the built-in AI plays the window for you. Six
windows went that way last corpus - one tapped a maker in your own upkeep against a sweeper deck,
in the same window your reasoning had just said to pass. Reasoning, then the bare CHOICE line,
then PLAN.
A CHOICE NAMES A NUMBERED ROW ON THIS WINDOW. A card on your hand line with no "Cast" row is
there because a pip cannot be paid - read the mana line's per-colour count ("{W} 1" cannot pay
{1}{w}{w}). Naming it costs a re-ask and the engine answers "<refused: named_row_reask>": twice
two corpora back, once naming an equip on a menu of casts, once a Keeper the menu did not list.
WHICH LAND: when a spell in your hand is castable THIS turn off the land you play, play the one
that enters UNTAPPED - Underground Sea, Tundra, Scrubland, Plains, Swamp. Arcane Sanctum enters
tapped, and Isolated Chapel does unless you control a Plains or Swamp: play those on a turn you
were not going to spend all your mana. Check "Mana available: N total" BEFORE you build any line
around a card in hand.
A CARD ELITE SPELLBINDER EXILED FROM YOUR HAND IS STILL YOURS TO CAST, AND THE ROW SAYS SO:
"[from exile] {castable from exile - a legal cast, not a dead row; it costs {2} more than
printed, already counted in the cost shown}" - the same card at the same cast-list entry, and an
exiled card at entry 0 is still CL0. But it is the EXPENSIVE copy: when the same card is also on
the menu from your hand, the hand row is the one you take.

=== RULE 1 - THE TOKEN LINES, AND WHEN TO STOP TAKING THEM ===
The three lines, exactly as you will see them; a second copy adds a handle ("Thraben
Doomsayer #1") - same line, same rule:
  "Create human with Thraben Doomsayer [cost: Tap]"
  "Create vampire with Bloodline Keeper [cost: Tap]"
  "Create vampire with Lord of Lineage [cost: Tap]"
BEFORE you take one, read M: the repeat row prints it first - "[you control M creatures right
now; ...]" - and your own battlefield line prints the same number after "of which". The
"activated this turn N times" count belongs to ONE COPY and ONE TURN; it is not M.
THE STOP IS A HEADCOUNT, BECAUSE POWER CANNOT MEASURE THIS LOOP. With Intruder Alarm out the
line comes back after every activation and the new tokens are summoning sick, so the "power of
creatures that are not summoning sick" number does NOT MOVE when you take it: a stop written on
it can never be reached, and this deck once took the line 1,868 times in ONE turn.
Use the two numbers that DO move, both printed in every window:
  - M = the number after "of which" on YOUR battlefield line: "Your battlefield (K permanents
    listed, of which M are creatures)". Every activation adds one to M, summoning sick or not.
  - L = "Opponent life:", and C = the number after "of which" on the OPPONENT'S battlefield
    line, i.e. how many blockers they own.
Then:
  - M is BELOW L + C + 3: TAKE THE LINE. Take it again every time it comes back.
  - M reaches L + C + 3: STOP. Answer "CHOICE: 0 (pass)". At L + C + 3 bodies they cannot block
    enough of them to live through your next attack, and a body past that number wins nothing.
  - THE STOP IS AGAINST THE LIFE THEY WILL HAVE AND THE BODIES YOU WILL SEND, NOT M. One maker
    stays home (see below) and every token you made THIS turn is summoning sick, so the attack is
    M minus those - and L is read again at the attack, not at the tap. Against a deck that GAINS
    life (deck125, deck126) add their last gain to the stop a second time. Last corpus a stop of
    61 was set from L=58, the opponent was at 61 by the attack step, 60 attackers swung, and they
    lived at ONE. Recompute L+C+3 in the ATTACK window; an attack list short of it does not go.
  - THE REPEAT ROW IS HOW YOU TAKE THE LINE. From the second activation of a turn on, the
    screen adds a second row: "Create human with Thraben Doomsayer, repeated N times, then stop
    [you name N on the CHOICE line, e.g. "CHOICE: 2 (Create human with Thraben Doomsayer x<N>)";
    the engine performs it N times ... N is at most 200]" - the row's own example shows the
    format; the number is YOURS, from the PLAN line's subtraction. Take THAT row, ONCE, with
        N = (L + C + 3) - M
    written the way the row's own example writes it - CHOICE: 2 (Create human with Thraben
    Doomsayer x17). A take with no number is asked again, once, and that is a window spent. The
    next window after the repeat row is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)".
    DO THE SUBTRACTION ON THE SCREEN BEFORE YOU WRITE N, and carry ALL FOUR numbers in your PLAN
    line in this shape, blanks filled from the screen - L and C separately, so the stop can be
    checked against them:
        PLAN: L <opponent life>, C <their creature count>, stop <L+C+3>; M is <M> now;
        this window: <x<N> or pass>
    Fill the angle brackets with numbers and name ONE action in the last; last corpus this deck
    copied the bracket text into its plan verbatim.
    THIS PLAN SHAPE BELONGS TO THE TOKEN-ACTIVATION WINDOW AND TO NOTHING ELSE. A menu with no
    "Create ..." row takes the ritual ITS OWN rule names - Rule 3's "Damnation: N, K, M"
    whenever a Damnation row is listed - and carrying "L, C, stop" onto a casting menu is how
    this deck talked itself past three live sweepers: at 9 life with 0 creatures of its own and
    "destroys 4 of their creatures ... 0 of yours" on the menu it wrote "L=22, C=3, stop=26;
    M=0 now", cast Intruder Alarm instead, and was dead two turns later.
    "this window:" names the same action the CHOICE line above it named.
    THE FIRST CHOICE LINE IS THE ONE THAT RUNS, AND PROSE NEVER TAKES IT BACK. Reasoning your
    way out of an answer under it changes nothing: last corpus this deck answered "CHOICE: 2
    (Cast Devour Flesh)" and wrote in the same PLAN "Devour Flesh at 0 creatures does nothing
    ... I will hold priority. I will pass this turn" - the engine cast it and the card was gone.
    ONE THING REPLACES AN ANSWER: a line STARTING "Correction:", "Re-evaluating:", "Actually,"
    or "On reflection", and then a SECOND full CHOICE line naming the row you now want - both
    halves, or nothing happens. "Wait," and "Correct Plan:" are not among the four, and that
    pair with no second CHOICE line under it once cast a Damnation onto this deck's own 102
    creatures. With one, the four words saved three windows last corpus: "Correction: M=33,
    Stop=23. We should pass." then "CHOICE: 3 (Hold priority ...)", and the hold is what ran.
    Best is to need neither: change your mind BEFORE the CHOICE line.
    RECOMPUTE THE STOP FROM THIS WINDOW'S ROW EVERY TIME; a stop carried from the previous
    window is a number, not a rule. Take the repeat row ONCE per turn and then pass. If the two
    numbers in your own plan line disagree, the arithmetic is right and the sentence is wrong.
    Do the subtraction BEFORE you write the CHOICE line: when (L + C + 3) - M is 0 or less, that
    line is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)" - not "x1", not "x3", never "x0".
    AND WHEN THE ENGINE HANDS THE WINDOW BACK READING "[RE-ASK] ... but your own PLAN puts you at
    <M> with your stop at <S>, so every repeat in this window is past the stop you set", THE ANSWER
    IS "CHOICE: 0 (pass)". Not the same count again, not a smaller count, not a new stop invented
    to fit it. That message is the engine reading your own two numbers back to you. Last corpus
    this deck was told "you at 66 with your stop at 26" and answered "x34" unchanged, then took M
    to 100 in a game it lost with 102 bodies on the board.
PASSING IS ALWAYS AVAILABLE: the last row reads "0. Pass priority (take no action this window)",
and when M has reached L + C + 3 that row is the answer, whatever else is listed.
YOUR INSTANTS ARE NOW OFFERED ON THE OPPONENT'S TURN, AT EVERY PHASE OF IT. A "Casting decision
(<phase>, opponent's turn)" ask with Tragic Slip, Tribute to Hunger or Devour Flesh on it is a
real window, and it is the BEST window for those three: you see their board after they have
committed to it. That same ask reappears at every phase of their turn, and of yours, with the
same rows, and the LAST row of every one of them is the "Hold priority ... do not ask me again
unless the board changes" row. When the only cast row is dead - "{right now: they control 0
creatures - at 0 this does nothing}", or "{kills 0 of the N legal targets}" - take the hold row
at the FIRST dead window of the turn, not the fifth, and stop reading the menu. It is not a last
resort you reach after declining: a hold taken in the Cleanup step saves nothing, a hold taken in
their upkeep saves the whole turn. When a run comes back anyway - a drain moves a life number and
a row's price with it - answer the SAME way every time; "[you declined this exact list N times
already this turn]" is a counter, not new information.
Timing: the tokens you make CANNOT attack until your next turn, so making them early in your own
turn buys you nothing this turn and leaves them standing through the opponent's whole turn. Fire
the chain on the OPPONENT'S turn - the lines are offered there, in their upkeep, their main phase
and their attack step. A token made then blocks that same turn and attacks on your very next turn,
and a sweeper cast on their turn catches fewer of them.
THE ALARM IS ALSO WHY A BIG M IS NOT A BIG ATTACK. Nothing untaps in your untap step while
Intruder Alarm is out, so the creatures on "Your creatures that can attack:" are the ones you made
on the OPPONENT'S turn and have not spent - never the ones you made this turn. Last corpus this
deck stood on 37 creatures with the opponent at 20 and the attack screen printed FOUR rows, then
died before its next turn. The chain fired in its own main phase; every token it made was
summoning sick when the attack step arrived. M is the stop's number. The attack list is the
attack's number, and only the opponent's turn grows it.
Fire on your OWN turn only when the creatures you are about to attack with are already on the
board, or when the opponent has no untapped land left.
AND THE MAKER YOU SEND AS AN ATTACKER IS THE MAKER THAT CANNOT FIRE IT. Attacking taps the
creature; under your own Intruder Alarm a tapped creature untaps only when another creature
ENTERS, and the only thing that makes one enter is an UNTAPPED maker tapping for a token. So a
turn that attacks with every maker ends the chain for good - nothing enters and the whole team
stays tapped for the rest of the game. Two corpora paid for this: one sent two Doomsayers and a
Keeper on turn 12 and never fired the chain again; the next sent a LONE Doomsayer into "(both
die)" rows twice. Both ended with an empty board on turn 13. ONE MAKER STAYS HOME, every attack,
while the Alarm is on your battlefield - or fire
the chain to its stop in your own main phase FIRST, so the last token entering untaps the team
and the makers you send have already done their work this turn.
WITH THE ALARM IN HAND AND CASTABLE THIS TURN, THE MAKER WAITS UNTAPPED UNTIL THE ALARM HAS
RESOLVED: a maker tapped before it resolves sits tapped through their entire turn. Land, Alarm,
THEN the first tap - with the Alarm not castable this turn, tap freely; a maker with no Alarm
untaps on its own.
BUT THE ROW DOES NOT WAIT WITH IT: pass a "Create ..." row TWICE in one turn and it is retired
for the rest of that turn - the second offer says so, "{if you pass here, this option is not
offered again this turn}" - and the Alarm resolving does not bring it back. This deck passed both
makers in upkeep and again in its draw step, cast the Alarm in main 1, and its combo rows were
gone from the next window: three Greaves equips were the whole menu. On a turn you mean to cast
the Alarm, the FIRST Create window may be passed and the SECOND may not.
YOUR OWN UPKEEP IS THE WORST WINDOW AGAINST A SWEEPER. Tokens made in your own upkeep sit
through their whole turn before they attack. Against a deck whose log shows Starstorm, Supreme
Verdict or Damnation (deck130, deck125, the mirror), the answer in your upkeep is
"CHOICE: 0 (pass)" and the chain fires in THEIR turn.
A token's own line says it: "[summoning sick - cannot attack this turn, but CAN block]". Make
them anyway; they block this turn and swing next turn.

=== RULE 2 - MARSH FLATS IS NOT MANA UNTIL YOU CRACK IT; CRACK IT THE FIRST WINDOW IT IS LISTED ===
A Marsh Flats on your battlefield makes NO mana, and its own row says so:
"[cost: Tap, Life, Sacrifice] {this land makes no mana - crack it for a land: your untapped
sources go from N to N+1}". The "Mana available" line does not count it and never will until you
take that line. So the first window that lists "Put a card onto the battlefield with Marsh Flats
targeting ..." is the window - on either player's turn, in any phase; the row does the arithmetic
for you. CRACKING IT IS NOT YOUR LAND DROP: the fetched land comes off your library, so the
"Land drop:" question still arrives that same turn and Rule 0 still answers it with a "Play"
line.
WHICH LAND: read "Your hand" for the pips your spells need TWICE - Thraben Doomsayer needs
{w}{w}, Bloodline Keeper {b}{b}, Damnation {b}{b} - and take the dual that supplies both of
them with what is already on your battlefield:
  "targeting Underground Sea"  - makes {U} and {B}
  "targeting Tundra"           - makes {W} and {U}
  "targeting Scrubland"        - makes {W} and {B}
THE ROW NOW NAMES THE COLOURS AND MARKS THE MISSING ONE. Every fetch row ends
"...and it adds {W} or {B}", and a colour you cannot currently produce is tagged in the row
itself: "and it adds {W} or {B} (you cannot make {W} right now)". Take a row carrying that
parenthesis, and among several take the one that supplies the pip your hand needs twice. That
clause is the whole rule and it is free. Last corpus this deck answered "targeting Swamp" - a
basic, one colour, no parenthesis - off a menu that listed Scrubland, Tundra and Underground Sea
each marked "(you cannot make {W} right now)", against a deck with no land destruction.
WHEN NO ROW CARRIES THAT PARENTHESIS AT ALL, TAKE A DUAL ANYWAY - NEVER THE BASIC. The tie-break
is the row's own "and it adds" clause: "adds {W} or {B}" beats "adds {B}", for the same one life
and the same one card. Last corpus's only miss here was exactly that window - "adds {B}" taken
over "adds {W} or {B}" on turn 4 with a {b} and a {1}{b} spell in hand.
A colour at 0 on the mana line comes first (Intruder Alarm {2}{u} and Vision Skeins {1}{u} are
the reason: only 10 of 24 lands make blue). After that, the hand decides: with Tundra and
Underground Sea out and a Doomsayer plus a Keeper in hand, Scrubland is the fetch - it is the
second W AND the second B.
A basic only against a deck whose log shows Molten Rain, Lay Waste or Dwarven Blastminer
(deck130), and only when the colour you are short of is {W} or {B}: those three spells hit
NONBASIC lands only. THE CARVE-OUT STOPS AT BLUE - a Plains and a Swamp make no {U}, so when
{U} is the pip missing from "colours you can make", the fetch is "targeting Tundra" or
"targeting Underground Sea" whatever their deck destroys: a land they blow up next turn still
casts your spell this turn. Against every other deck, never a basic while a dual is listed.

=== RULE 3 - COUNT BOTH BOARDS BEFORE DAMNATION ===
DAMNATION DESTROYS CREATURES AND NOTHING ELSE. Its own row prints the whole card: "Destroy all
creatures. They can't be regenerated." An enchantment, an artifact, a planeswalker and a land
all survive it. "Cast Damnation to remove Sanguine Bond" is a sentence this deck wrote in its
own plan while holding fourteen Humans; the Damnation killed the fourteen Humans, the Bond was
still on the battlefield the next window, and it lost that game 42 to 0. Nothing in this deck
removes an enchantment. Read the row before you believe a card does something its text does not
say.
Damnation destroys ALL creatures, yours included. Both battlefield lines print a creature count
right after the words "of which" - read YOUR number and THEIR number, and compare them.
Cast Damnation when THEIR number is bigger than yours.
Equal numbers are not a reason to cast it: keep the Damnation and take another action.
When YOUR number is the bigger one, spend the window on a creature, a token activation or a
removal spell instead, and keep the Damnation - your creatures are how you win, and a wipe that
kills two of yours to kill one of theirs has handed them the game.
Your number is 0 and theirs is 2 or more: that is the best Damnation there is. Take it, and take
it BEFORE a token-maker and before Vision Skeins - it is CL0 of the cast list below. A maker
cast into attackers does not live to make a token; the Damnation that clears them buys the
turns the maker needs.
WRITE THE THREE NUMBERS BEFORE YOU WRITE THE CHOICE. Whenever "Cast Damnation" is on the menu,
your PLAN line carries this, blanks filled from the row:
    PLAN: Damnation: N <their number>, K <able to attack>, M <mine>; <cast / hold>
M 0 and K 2 or more is "cast" and it is the first line of your reply, above a maker. M above N is
"hold". Writing the three numbers is not the same as obeying them: the numbers are the answer,
the sentence after them is not.
WHEN A DAMNATION ROW IS ON THE MENU THIS IS THE ONLY PLAN LINE ON THE REPLY - not the token
line, not a restatement of the combo. Three numbers, then "cast" or "hold". And at M of 0, K of
2 or more, with YOUR life at 10 or less, "hold" is not an available answer: the combo you are
saving the window for needs turns this board is not going to give you, and the sweeper is what
buys them. WHAT THIS COST: 5 life, four of their creatures, TWO Damnations in hand and
"destroys 4 of their creatures (4 without a restriction against attacking), 0 of yours" printed
on the row - this deck cast Bloodline Keeper instead and lost at -12 in the opponent's next
combat.
THE ROW COUNTS FOR YOU. "Cast Damnation" prints "{right now: destroys N of their creatures (K
without a restriction against attacking), M of yours}" on your turn and "(K able to attack right
now)" on theirs; "(all of them carry a restriction against attacking)" is K = 0. N is their
number, M is yours, and
K is how many of theirs can actually hit you. Two 0/4 walls are N = 2 with K = 0: a sweeper
spent on them is a sweeper you do not have when six Vampires arrive. So at K of 0, or at N of
exactly 1, a Tragic Slip or an edict on the same menu that kills the one that matters is the
cast and the Damnation stays in hand.
THE ONE K = 0 BOARD YOU DO SWEEP: walls named PERIMETER CAPTAIN or PRIDE GUARDIAN. CHECK 1 in
COMBAT forbids you to attack while they stand, and Damnation is the removal that takes them off
that line.
"DESTROYS 0 OF THEIR CREATURES" IS A CARD THROWN AWAY - 0 against 0 included, where the row
prints "destroys 0 of their creatures ... 0 of yours" and the card does nothing whatever. At N of
0 the answer is "hold" whatever M is and whatever the plan says; your SECOND main phase is the
same board. This deck threw TWO Damnations away at N = 0 in one game, writing "Damnation does
nothing" in the same reply that cast it.
THE STOP, AND IT IS THE LAST NUMBER ON THE ROW: if M - "M of yours", the number after the final
comma in "{right now: destroys N of their creatures (K ...), M of yours}" - is 4 or more, do not
cast Damnation at all, whatever N says. Four bodies is a board you win with; there is no opposing
board this deck needs to trade four of its own creatures to answer. Take a token activation, a
removal spell or "Cast nothing right now" instead. READ THAT LAST NUMBER OUT LOUD BEFORE YOU
ANSWER, because the sentence you are about to write will contradict it. One of theirs is never
worth five of yours, and "saving" is not something a sweeper does. Held last corpus: 0 casts at
M of 4 or more, on a menu that printed "246 of yours" once.
AND WHEN THE SAME CARD IS ON THE MENU TWICE, TAKE THE CHEAPER ROW: "Cast Damnation {4}{b}{b}
[from exile]" beside "Cast Damnation {2}{b}{b}" printed identical "{right now:}" clauses on one
menu and this deck answered the exile row - two mana for the same board.

=== RULE 4 - TRAGIC SLIP KILLS TOUGHNESS 1 ===
Tragic Slip gives -1/-1. That kills a creature whose battlefield line shows toughness 1 - a
(2/1), a (1/1), a (0/1). Against anything with toughness 2 or more it shrinks the creature and
it lives.
THE ROW NOW PRINTS THE RESULT. Every Tragic Slip target row ends in the engine's own verdict -
"{right now: -1/-1 - DIES}" or "{right now: -1/-1 - SURVIVES (toughness 2, -1/-1 more kills it)}",
and with Morbid live "{right now: -13/-13 - DIES}". Read that clause and nothing else; do not
re-derive it from the "(P/T)". Take a "- DIES" row, and with several listed take the biggest
power. At 6 life this deck answered a window whose three rows all read "SURVIVES".
THE DECISION IS MADE AT THE CAST ROW, NOT THE TARGET ROW. The cast row prints
"- legal targets right now: <names>"; the target ask that follows it HAS NO PASS ROW - once you
answer "Cast Tragic Slip" the card is spent on one of the names it listed. So before you take
the cast row, read the KILL COUNT on it. "{kills 0 of the 3 legal targets at -1/-1}" means every
name on that row's list lives through the spell - the engine has done the toughness arithmetic
for you on every target at once, and that row is "Cast nothing right now". A row naming a victim
instead - "{kills: Elite Spellbinder}" - is the cast; at 1 life this deck cast the Slip off a
"{kills 0 of the 3 legal targets}" row and spent its last card on a 6/5 that survived.
The one upgrade: a line ending in "died" under the CURRENT turn header in the GAME LOG makes the
row read "{right now: -13/-13 (a creature died this turn, so Morbid applies)}", which kills
anything on the list - take the biggest printed power. That is the window this card is for.
No toughness-1 target on the list and no "died" line under this turn: answer "Cast nothing
right now" and keep the Slip. It costs {B} and it will kill something on a later turn.

=== RULE 5 - THE TWO EDICTS ARE DIFFERENT CARDS ===
IF YOUR PLAN NAMES WHICH CREATURE AN EDICT WILL REMOVE, THE PLAN IS ALREADY WRONG. "Tribute to
Hunger on Sigarda", "... on Briarbridge Tracker", "... to remove Sorin" are all sentences this
deck has written and all three are impossible. You NEVER pick. The row prints the whole card -
{card text: "Target opponent sacrifices a creature of their choice. You gain life equal to that
creature's toughness."} - and "of their choice" is the entire rule. The only thing you choose is
whether to cast it at all, and the answer to that is the count of their creatures.
"Cast Tribute to Hunger {2}{b}" - the opponent sacrifices a creature and YOU gain life equal to
its toughness. This is the one you want.
Cast it at the opponent whenever it is listed and their battlefield line shows exactly ONE
creature. With two or more of their creatures listed, they hand over their worst one and keep the
good one - take Tragic Slip, Damnation or a token activation instead.
"Cast Devour Flesh {1}{b}" - the player you name sacrifices a creature AND THAT SAME PLAYER
gains the life. Naming the opponent does NOT gain YOU anything: "remove their blocker and gain
4 life" is the sentence this deck wrote in its own plan on turn 3 while handing a Sanguine Bond
deck four life off a 0/4 wall. You gain life from Tribute to Hunger. You never gain life from
Devour Flesh. That said, giving them the life is still the right price when it takes their ONLY
body: use Devour Flesh on the opponent when Tribute to Hunger is not on the list and they have
exactly ONE creature - then the sacrifice takes their only body and the life they gain is worth
it.
AN EDICT CANNOT TOUCH A PLANESWALKER. Both rows say "sacrifices a creature"; a line printing
"[planeswalker] [counters: Nx loyalty]" is not a creature and is not a legal outcome of either
card. If a planeswalker is what is beating you, no card in this deck answers it: build the board
and attack it.
Before any edict, run this in order: (1) read N off the edict row itself - it prints "{right
now: they control N creatures - <name> is sacrificed ...}" at N = 1, "- they choose which one"
at N of 2 or more, and "- at 0 this does nothing" at N = 0. N is the only number that matters.
(2) If N is not exactly 1, do not cast an edict - not at 0, not at 2 or more. (3) A creature listed under
"ON THE STACK, waiting to resolve" is NOT on the battlefield and does not change N.
BUT WHEN THE HEADER READS "ON THE STACK: N damage to you - you would be at X; that would KILL
YOU", THAT IS THE ONLY FACT IN THE WINDOW. It is not the blockers header and it does not wait
for combat: those triggers resolve in the step you are standing in, before your main phase and
before the attack you are planning. It has already done the subtraction; do not redo it in your
own words. Take the row that moves the number: gain the life (Devour Flesh at Yourself), or
kill the source. A hold is never the answer to that line. WHAT THIS COST: at 2 life in its
own draw step, with "you would be at -1; that would KILL you" printed above the menu, 25 Humans
that were 3/3 under Fateful hour and able to attack, five untapped sources and "Cast Devour
Flesh" on row 1, this deck answered "Hold priority" and wrote "Stack resolves (damage to 2).
... Attack with all 25 Humans (3/3 each, total 75 damage) to win immediately" - it was dead
three lines later, holding lethal.
AT N = 1 THE EDICT IS THE CAST, NOT THE SLIP. A toughness-2 creature is the edict's job, not
the Slip's.
Never cast an edict when the opponent's life is at or below the life it gives them. Killing
their last creature and moving them from 1 life to 4 costs you a game you had already won.
Do not spend an edict on a creature that is not hurting you: if their only creature reads
"[defender]" or has power 0, keep the edict - you would pay a card and hand them life equal to
its toughness for something that was never going to kill you. The row prints the body's own tags
inside its verdict, so the test is on the screen: "they control 1 creature - Wall of Omens (0/4)
[defender] is sacrificed, they gain 4" is the row you decline. A 1-power creature is NOT in that
class when its "{card text:}" gives the rest of their board something - mana, counters, +1/+1 -
it is the engine and it grows.
Both edicts give life to the player who sacrifices. THE SCREEN NAMES THE DECK THAT PUNISHES YOU
FOR IT, IN ITS OWN BLOCK: "LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER on the battlefield: theirs - Sanguine Bond.
While it is in play, life ITS CONTROLLER gains also makes the other player lose that much life."
With that block on the screen an edict at the opponent is a two-way swing against you: the row's
own "they gain N" becomes N on their total AND N off yours.
So: the converter block on the screen, or a defender / power-0 creature as the N=1 body, is
"Cast nothing right now" - and both at once is not a decision, it is a mistake with two names.
"Yourself (player, life" is the right answer only when the creature you give up is a Human
or Vampire TOKEN you can remake next turn. Your Bloodline Keeper, your Thraben Doomsayer and
your flying Vampires stay on the battlefield.

=== RULE 6 - EQUIP LIGHTNING GREAVES ONCE PER GREAVES ===
An equip line for Lightning Greaves costs {0}, so the game re-offers it at every window. Take it
ONE time per Greaves, on Bloodline Keeper if it is on the battlefield, otherwise on Thraben
Doomsayer. The done-signal is on the battlefield line: a creature reading "{attached: Lightning
Greaves #1}" already has it and that Greaves is finished. At every later re-offer take a
"[cost: Tap]" line if one is listed, otherwise answer 0 (pass). Equip again only when the
equipped creature has DIED, or an unequipped token-maker has just arrived.
AND DO NOT CAST THE GREAVES ONTO AN EMPTY BOARD. The row carries its own verdict:
"Cast Lightning Greaves {2} {right now: you control 0 creatures - this equips nothing}". A row
that says it equips nothing is "Cast nothing right now", and being the ONLY cast row on the menu
does not change it.

cards; the number you write on your CHOICE line is the number printed beside that card ON THIS
WINDOW'S MENU, and a card that is not on this window's menu has no number and cannot be named.
Take the highest entry on this list that appears in your casting menu:
 CL0. "Cast Damnation {2}{b}{b}" - when YOUR battlefield line reads "of which 0 are creatures"

    Pride Guardian: sweep them, Rule 3.)
 CL1. "Cast Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b}" - when no token-maker is on your battlefield.
 CL2. "Cast Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w}" - when no token-maker is on your battlefield.
 CL3. "Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}" - when no Intruder Alarm is on your battlefield.
    Intruder Alarm on an empty battlefield does NOTHING. A token-maker with no Alarm still

    AND THE ALARM GOES BEFORE THE SECOND MAKER. CL1 and CL2 say "when no token-maker is on

    maker, before Lightning Greaves. The Alarm with one maker is the loop; a second maker

 CL4. "Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" - when no Intruder Alarm is on your battlefield or in your hand.
    Read BOTH lines before you take it: "Cast Idyllic Tutor to find Intruder Alarm (already on

 CL5. "Cast Lightning Greaves {2}" - ONLY when the row does NOT carry
    "{right now: you control 0 creatures - this equips nothing}". That clause is the answer.
 CL6. A removal spell, by Rules 3, 4 and 5.
 CL7. "Cast Vision Skeins {1}{u}".
 CL8. "Cast nothing right now" - the LAST entry, and correct only when no entry above appeared in
    your casting menu. Every card you can cast has a line above this one.
WHEN EVERY CAST ROW ON THE MENU IS PRICED DEAD, ENTRY 8 IS THE PLAY, AND IT IS NOT INACTION.
Read the {right now: ...} and [second copy: ...] clauses on every row before you pick: a menu
whose rows all read "destroys 0 of their creatures ... 0 of yours", "they control 0 creatures -
at 0 this does nothing" and "[second copy: ... a second copy changes nothing]" is a menu with
nothing on it, and "Cast nothing right now" is the only row that keeps a card. Last corpus 92 of
this deck's 345 casting menus were that menu. There is no rule in this file
that says you must cast something. A card in hand is a card you still have when the board changes;
a card spent off a dead row is gone, and the board that made it dead has not moved.

=== INTRUDER ALARM AND IDYLLIC TUTOR ===
"Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}": it is the engine, and it is CL3 on the cast list above -
behind both token-makers, because an Alarm with no maker out does nothing. One copy does the
whole job; with an Alarm already on your battlefield, spend the window on a creature, a removal
spell, or "Cast nothing right now".
It is symmetric: no creature untaps in its controller's untap step, theirs or yours, and ANY
creature entering - theirs or yours - untaps everything. Pure profit with a token-maker in play;
with none, a creature of yours that attacks stays tapped, so get Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline
Keeper down before you rely on attacking.
"Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" searches for an ENCHANTMENT; the only enchantment in this sixty is
Intruder Alarm and every other reveal row reads "[does NOT qualify]". IT CANNOT FIND A CREATURE.
Its bracket names which of three cases you are in, and only one is a cast:
  "[finds only an enchantment card - still in your library, not on your battlefield or in your
   hand: Intruder Alarm]"  -> a LIVE Tutor. Cast it and name Intruder Alarm.
  "[finds only an enchantment card - every enchantment left in your library is a copy of one you
   already control or hold: Intruder Alarm]"  -> DEAD. It fetches a second Alarm, which does
   nothing (see below). "Cast nothing right now" over it.
  "[finds only an enchantment card - none left in your library]"  -> DEAD. There is no card for
   it to find at all.
This deck read the middle bracket at turn 12 with an Alarm already on its line and cast the Tutor
anyway. An Alarm in "Your hand" is the same verdict - Vision Skeins is the card that digs for a
maker.
THE ONE TIME A DEAD TUTOR IS A PLAY: against a deck that counters (deck125 - Cancel, Essence
Scatter, Fall of the Gavel, Dream Fracture in its log), see the counter paragraph under KEY
CARDS - it is cast FIRST in the same main phase as a maker, to eat the counter.
THE ALARM YOU TUTOR MAY NOT BE CASTABLE THIS TURN. The engine taps your mana for you and has
paid a Tutor's {2}{w} with BOTH Underground Seas; the tutored Alarm then sat in hand for a whole
turn cycle with "{U} 0" on the mana line. Read "sources that can make each: ... {U} N" first: at
{U} 2 or less, plan the Alarm for NEXT turn and spend what the Tutor leaves on a maker.

A ROW TAGGED "[NAMED BY THEIR ...]" IS A SURCHARGE, NOT A BAN. Silverquill Silencer and its kind
name a card in your hand and the row prints the price in full - "[NAMED BY THEIR Silverquill
Silencer: casting this costs you 3 life and draws them a card]". The card is still castable and
the number is already counted for you: pay it when the card is the engine (a maker, the Alarm)
and your life is above 8; decline it for a Greaves, a Skeins or a dead removal spell.

=== COMBAT ===
ATTACK - six checks, in this order. Stop at the first one that answers and do what it says.
CHECK 3 is the one exception: it never ends your answer, it only removes creatures from the
list you are about to send, and then you go on.

CHECK 0 - THE CRACK-BACK, AND IT IS THE FIRST THING YOU READ. A creature you send is TAPPED and
cannot block on their turn. THE WINDOW PRINTS THE NUMBER FOR YOU whenever they have a body that
can swing: "CRACK-BACK NEXT TURN: N of their creatures will be able to attack (tapped ones untap
first), for up to P from combat as their board stands - you would be at X or lower - and that
number is a FLOOR, not a ceiling". READ X, do not re-derive it. X at 0 or below means the bodies
on your attack list are the blockers you need and the answer is "ATTACK: none" - unless the total
power you can send is at or above their PRINTED life this window, which ends the game before the
crack-back happens. NO CRACK-BACK line means no creature of theirs can attack next turn: the
check is free, go on. The "[held back, it CANNOT block: ...]" tag is not this check - it names
only what a body could NOT block, never what it CAN, and never your life.
WHAT THIS COST: at 3 life, with a 5/5 flier and a 3/4 sitting TAPPED on their line, this deck
sent both Doomsayers and its Human for 13 at an opponent on 20; 8 power came back and it died at
-7. Held home, nothing connects and Fateful hour is still on.

CHECK 1 - THE LIFEGAIN WALL BOARD. Read the "Opponent battlefield" line for these four names:
PERIMETER CAPTAIN, PRIDE GUARDIAN, SANGUINE BOND, EXQUISITE BLOOD.
FIRST: Sanguine Bond and Exquisite Blood are ENCHANTMENTS. Tragic Slip, Damnation and both
edicts kill CREATURES; nothing in this sixty removes an enchantment. So "wait until they are
gone" is not a plan against those two - held to the letter it kept fourteen Humans at home until
that game ended 42 to 0. THREE branches; the names on the line decide which one you are in.
BRANCH A - only PERIMETER CAPTAIN or PRIDE GUARDIAN (both are creatures, both are removable):
answer "ATTACK: none" this window and every attack window after it, until removal has taken the
LAST of them off that line. Then attack normally.
BRANCH B2 - BOTH SANGUINE BOND AND EXQUISITE BLOOD ARE ON THE LINE. Not an arithmetic board at
all: a closed loop, and the render says so - "their life LOOP is in play: any life they gain or
you lose in this combat chains without limit - fatal to you, not a trade". Believe it. ONE point
they gain - a Pride Guardian or Perimeter Captain blocking, one lifelink blocker connecting -
chains to YOUR zero, and it resolves in the declare-blockers step, BEFORE your damage is dealt.
The width rule below does not apply: this deck stood at 58 attackers against 6 blockers with them
on 28 life and still lost, because they never take the damage. So: "ATTACK: none" while any untapped
creature they own can BLOCK what you would send; keep your bodies home as blockers. NO BLOCK, NO GAIN
- the gate is BLOCK LEGALITY, never their creature count, and the screen computes it: "N of your
M possible attackers cannot be blocked by any of them", each such row tagged "[no creature they
control can block this attacker]". When that covers EVERY attacker you would send and the
ATTACK TOTAL says lethal - "that KILLS them whatever they block" - SEND THEM ALL: nothing of
theirs can block, so nothing of theirs gains, and the loop never gets a window. WHAT THE OTHER
READING COST: 50 flying tokens, 201 damage, that tag on every row, an opponent on 23 - this deck
answered "ATTACK: none" and lost 43 to 0.
BRANCH B - exactly ONE of SANGUINE BOND or EXQUISITE BLOOD is on the line: the gain is finite, so
you attack on ARITHMETIC instead of on the name. A is the length of your "Your creatures that can
attack:" list - the LIST, never your creature count; B is their UNTAPPED creatures. Attack when A
is at least 3 times B plus 3; below that answer "ATTACK: none" and make more tokens on their turn.
  - Lethal overrides both branches: if the total power of what you can send is at or above their
    printed life, send it all - a life gain that happens after they are dead is not a life gain.
A damage total overrides a "do not attack" rule in two places in this file: here on the printed
life number, and B2's unblockable-lethal line. Nowhere else.
YOUR ATTACKER TAGS CANNOT SEE THIS. "[their untapped blockers: ...]" reading "(neither dies)"
is TRUE - the tag counts deaths, not life, and the life is what kills you. Twelve attackers into
three of these walls took this deck 20 to 8 in one swing and 8 to dead in the next, while the
opponent went down by nine. "We deal 30 and they are at 11, so we win" is NOT an exception to
Check 1: that sum is what you had both times you died here.

CHECK 2 - LIGHTMINE FIELD. If their battlefield line names a permanent that damages each
attacker by the number of attackers - Lightmine Field on deck125's board says exactly that -
find the SMALLEST toughness among the creatures you would send and send that many minus one,
biggest power first. With 2/2 Vampires that is ONE: "ATTACK: Vampire #1" and nothing after the
name. This deck sent two into it twice in one game under a plan that had already priced the
trade as bad.

CHECK 3 - THE COLLAPSED TAG AND THE "GANG BLOCK:" CLAUSE. On a wide board the tag reads "they
have N untapped creatures that could block this one, biggest X (you kill it, your attacker
lives)", sometimes with "- GANG BLOCK: their 6 biggest together deal 7, enough to kill this
attacker; each result above is a LONE blocker only". The parenthesis prices ONE blocker; the
GANG BLOCK clause prices what actually happens - any number of them may block the same attacker
and your attacker's toughness is measured against the SUM.
  - The tag prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause, or N is 2 or more:
    a TOKEN goes anyway - a 1/1 Human or a 2/2 Vampire costs you nothing and you make more next
    turn - and a CARD stays home. The cards are Thraben Doomsayer, Bloodline Keeper, Lord of
    Lineage and anything wearing Lightning Greaves.
  - N is 1 and no GANG BLOCK clause: the parenthesis is the whole story; use it.
"But the biggest one dies to me" is the sentence the clause exists to refuse: they do not have
to block with the biggest one alone, and against your one real body they will not.
The lethal check overrides this: when the printed opponent life is at or below the total power
of everything you are sending, send it all.
CHECK 3 does not end your answer. Cross the cards it names off the list and go on to CHECK 4.

CHECK 4 - THE COUNT, AND WHAT ONE BLOCKER CAN DO. ONE BLOCKER BLOCKS EXACTLY ONE ATTACKER. A
single 5/5 flier does not "block everything" - it blocks ONE of your creatures and the rest
connect. Count the "Your creatures that can attack:" list against their UNTAPPED creatures; if
your number is bigger you have that many unblocked attackers and Check 5 applies. Outnumbering
them is NOT protection for any one creature: THEY choose which attacker each blocker takes, and
they choose the one their block wins. Answer "ATTACK: none" on the count alone only when their untapped creature
count is EQUAL TO OR HIGHER THAN the number you send and no attacker tag reads "you kill it,
your blocker lives".

CHECK 5 - SEND EVERYTHING. "Your creatures that can attack:" has already dropped every creature
that cannot legally attack, so do not re-check summoning sickness or tapped status yourself.
Send every creature on that list.
Know what you are spending: with Intruder Alarm out NOTHING untaps in your untap step - every
battlefield line says so, "[doesn't untap during its controller's untap step]". Attackers come
back untapped only when a creature ENTERS, which for this deck means an untapped token-maker
tapping for a token. So before you send everything, find a token-maker on your battlefield line
AND KEEP IT OFF THE ATTACK. A maker that attacks is tapped
too, and a tapped maker cannot tap for the token that untaps everyone: sending all of them is
the one attack that cannot be undone. With one maker held back the whole team is back the moment
you make your next token and Check 5 costs you nothing. If NO token-maker is on your battlefield,
the creatures you send stay tapped until one arrives, and they are not blockers next turn -
send them anyway when the attack advances your clock, but do not plan a block with them.
One hold-back only: do not send a LONE Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper - or your ONLY
creature of any kind - into a board that can block and kill it. Its own row says when: "[their
untapped blockers: ... (both die)]" or "(your attacker dies)" means "ATTACK: none", and a hundred
summoning-sick tokens behind it change nothing. Send the maker once tokens attack beside it.

THE INCOMING TOTAL IS PRINTED ON EVERY WINDOW OF THEIR COMBAT, NOT ONLY ON THE BLOCKERS ASK:
"INCOMING THIS COMBAT: 4 attackers, 16 unblocked damage - you would be at -8; this KILLS you (of
that, 10 from 2 attackers none of your creatures can block)". An instant, an edict or a token
line taken in their upkeep or main phase can be priced against it before the blockers ask. Read
the SECOND number: no assignment removes damage from attackers you cannot block.
WHEN THE HEADER SAYS "this KILLS you" IT ALSO TELLS YOU WHETHER BLOCKING SAVES YOU, AND YOU
NEVER DECIDE THAT BY YOUR OWN ARITHMETIC. It prints a second clause: "you would be at N AT BEST
(no assignment of your blockers does better)". THAT N IS THE VERDICT. N of 1 or more means
blocking SURVIVES - block by the LETHAL rule below and spend every blocker; one life with your
board standing is a whole extra turn. Only when that clause is absent, or its N is 0 or lower,
is "BLOCKS: none" right: at 8 life against 16, 10 of it from two fliers this deck could not
block, one 2/2 in front of a 3-power attacker still ends at -5, and the clause says so. WHAT THE
OTHER READING COST: at 5 life against "you would be at -3; this KILLS you ... you would be at 1
AT BEST", with twenty-six Human blockers on the screen and an army that wins next turn, this
deck read the first clause only, answered "BLOCKS: none", and died on a board it survives.

Blocking, in this order. Step -1 can end it outright; step 0 decides whether you HAVE a blocker;
steps 1-2 pick WHICH creature blocks; the rest decide whether to block at all.
 - STEP -1 - ON THE LOOP BOARD, A BLOCK THAT GAINS THEM LIFE IS LETHAL, NOT A TRADE. When the
   LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER block names BOTH Sanguine Bond and Exquisite Blood on THEIR side,
   every B row whose clause reads "they gain N from this block only" is fatal - that N chains
   to your zero in the damage step, and the header's "best case with every blocker assigned"
   does not price the loop, so it names that block as your best case and is wrong. Block only
   attackers whose rows carry no life-gain clause; if every one carries it, "BLOCKS: none" and
   take the damage. WHAT THIS COST: at 16 life against ONE 1/1 lifelink Vampire, this deck read
   "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" beside "you would be at 16", blocked, and that one point
   of lifelink gain took it 16 to 0 in a single damage step.
 - STEP 0 - A "B" ROW IS A BLOCKER. If any row on the screen begins "B1.", you have a blocker,
   and its "may block A1, A2" list is the legality list. "We have no blockers" and "I have no
   way to block" are false whenever that row is printed - this deck wrote both under a B1 row,
   once declining a free kill at 16 life and once at 4 life under "LETHAL if it all connects".
   When the header says LETHAL, the
   FIRST line of your answer is "BLOCKS: B1:A<the attacker with the largest deals N>" - write
   that line before you write anything else, then add blockers by the LETHAL rule below.
   A row on a token-maker reading "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" is a free kill: take it,
   unless STEP -1 above says it is not free.
 - STEP 0b - THE HEADER'S NAMED ASSIGNMENT IS A LIFE-MAXIMISER, NOT ADVICE. When the header
   prints "one legal assignment that reaches it, chosen for your blockers' material as well as
   for the life: <X blocks Y>" is the assignment leaving you at the highest LIFE; material only
   breaks ties among assignments that already reach that life, and it does not know this deck has
   one win condition. Take that name ONLY when the header also says "LETHAL" or "this KILLS you";
   when it says "NOT lethal", ignore the name and go on to STEP 1. This deck copied that line word
   for word three times out of three last corpus - at 14 life "Bloodline Keeper blocks Silverquill
   Silencer", at 12 life "Thraben Doomsayer blocks Nadaar, Selfless Paladin" - each spending the
   ONLY maker it had under its own Intruder Alarm, neither header lethal, both games lost.
 - STEP 1 - WHICH BODY. Every blocker row on the screen is one of your creatures. Spend a TOKEN
   ("Human", "Vampire" - no mana cost printed on the row) before you ever spend a CARD, and
   spend Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper LAST, after every token is used. Tokens are free
   and you make more every turn; the maker is the only reason you make any.
 - STEP 2 - READ THE PARENTHESIS ON THE ROW YOU CHOOSE. "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)"
   means that creature dies and the attacker does not. It is the engine's computed result and
   it is never wrong. Do not write "block X with Bloodline Keeper to kill it" against a row
   that says the Keeper dies and X lives - this deck did exactly that at 12 life to prevent
   three damage, made no token for the rest of the game, and lost with the opponent on 4 life.
   If the row on your maker says "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)", the maker does not
   block.
 - WHEN THE PROMPT SAYS LETHAL, OR WHEN THE UNBLOCKED DAMAGE IS YOUR LIFE MINUS 4 OR MORE,
   ASSIGN TO THE BIGGEST ATTACKERS FIRST AND SPEND EVERY BLOCKER. Every attacker row prints
   "deals N". Sort those numbers, put your first blocker on the LARGEST, your second on the
   next largest, and keep going until you run out of blockers or of attackers - Thraben
   Doomsayer and Bloodline Keeper included, down to your last one: a maker you keep alive at 1
   life makes tokens for a game you already lost, and one blocker still stops one attacker's
   whole damage. Blocking their smallest attacker is the one assignment that is always wrong
   here: with 10 life against 3+3+3+1, blocking the 1 leaves you at 1 and blocking a 3 leaves
   you at 3, and the difference is a whole turn of the game. At 10 life against 2+4+2+4, with
   the header itself naming "Thraben Doomsayer blocks Nadaar, Selfless Paladin" as the
   assignment that reaches 2, this deck blocked the 2-power Goblin instead and died at 0.
 - MENACE. An attacker row tagged "[menace ...]" is NOT blocked by one creature - a lone
   blocker on it does nothing at all and the attacker's full damage still lands. Put TWO
   blockers on it or none. Never spend your best blocker alone on a menace attacker; the
   prompt says so on the row and it is telling the truth.
 - A "GANG BLOCK: any 2 of yours together deal N, enough to kill this attacker" clause on an
   attacker with "deals 4" or more, while the header's "you would be at" is 6 or less: put the
   counted blockers on it, token first, then a Keeper. At 10 life this deck read that clause
   on Nadaar (4/4), answered "BLOCKS: none" with a Keeper and a Vampire on the screen, and went
   to 4.
 - Otherwise your token-makers do NOT block. Take the damage. A blocker line reading "(both
   die)" on Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper is a trade you decline while the combat
   header's "you would be at N" is above 5 - the maker is worth more than any one attacker.
 - A TOKEN IS NOT A MAKER. With a maker on your line you remake a token next window, so a Human
   or Vampire in front of the largest "deals N" costs you nothing, and under a "DRAW PUNISHERS"
   line every point it keeps is a draw you can afford. At 11 life with "B1. Human" and "B2.
   Vampire" up this deck answered "BLOCKS: none" to one 3-power attacker.

=== MULLIGAN ===
STEP 1 - WHAT DOES THE NEXT LOOK COST? Read the ask's "(keeping N)". Each look is a fresh seven
shaved by one more card at the SAME land odds: a mulligan buys a look, never a fix. At (keeping
5) or lower, ONE land in front of you is a keep. Colour is not a reason to ship at any N. Zero
lands is the one hand worth the card; all lands is the other. WHAT THIS COST once: three
mulligans in one game, a four-card keep, lost 42 to 0.
STEP 2 - on the untouched seven, or at "(keeping 6)", use the comparison below.

The two deck facts that decide every mulligan here: you run TWENTY-FOUR lands in sixty cards,
and the cards that actually win - Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w}, Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b},
Intruder Alarm {2}{u} - cost three or four mana. So the question is never "can I play on turn
one", it is "will this hand have three or four lands by the turn it needs them, and does it do
something when they arrive".
The comparison to make, on the header line "Your hand (7 cards), counted by the engine: N
lands, M spells" (read that number, never recount):
 - TWO OR MORE LANDS AND AT LEAST ONE SPELL: keep. Full stop, at every "(keeping N)". With
   twenty-four lands the third arrives in the first few draws and the fourth by about turn
   five. NEITHER COLOUR NOR THE COVERAGE LINE IS A REASON TO SHIP A TWO-LANDER: "Playing every
   land in this hand would not cover any spell in it" is a KEEP at two lands - it says your hand
   is a turn-three hand, not a dead one, and the third land fixes it. The coverage line decides
   ONE-land hands and nothing else.
 - ONE LAND: read the coverage line. "would cover the cost of:" naming a card is a keep - the
   second land is in your next two draws about two times in three (23 lands in 53), and the
   ship buys a fresh seven that is a one-lander or worse about one time in seven. "would not
   cover any spell in it" ships on the untouched seven AND at "(keeping 6)": nothing happens
   until a draw, and one card fewer does not change that. Colour and what the spells cost are
   not part of this call. Both mulligans this deck took last corpus were exactly this shape,
   both kept a three-land six, and one of them won.
 - ZERO LANDS, or ALL lands: ship.
 - SIX OR MORE LANDS with one spell: keep. You bottom the extra lands and you are a four-land
   hand with your removal spell.
 - AT FIVE CARDS OR FEWER: a hand with a land and a spell in it is a keep, whatever that spell
   costs - the ship buys the same land odds with one card fewer.

BOTTOMING - the same decision, second ask. After you keep, the prompt says "you took N
mulligans and kept, so you must put N cards from your hand on the BOTTOM" and lists your hand.
This ask is where the hand is actually BUILT: read "You will be left with an N-card hand" and
build the best N. Keep, in this PREFERENCE ORDER, and bottom what is left over - the order
works at any N because it is an order, not a set of floors:
 1. the lands that cover the cheapest spell you are keeping - at N of 3 that is two lands and
    one spell, never three lands; at N of 5 or 6 it is three or four lands and the spells they
    cover, up to FOUR mana;
 2. a token-maker - Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper - if the hand has one;
 3. Idyllic Tutor, which FINDS Intruder Alarm, or Vision Skeins, which digs;
 4. removal;
 5. Intruder Alarm LAST. An Alarm with no maker on the battlefield does nothing at all, and a
    small hand cannot afford a card that waits.
A hand of nothing but lands does nothing, and a hand of nothing but four-drops does nothing for
four turns; the order above avoids both at every N. Five lands and two Devour Flesh at N of 6:
the fifth land goes under - four lands cover everything this deck casts.
A LAND THAT MAKES MANA IS NEVER THE BOTTOM WHILE A CARD OUTSIDE THAT ORDER IS IN THE HAND.
Lightning Greaves is not on the list above - it is the leftover, and the leftover is what goes
under. At (keeping 6) this deck held Tundra, Tundra, Marsh Flats, Idyllic Tutor, Lightning
Greaves, Tragic Slip and Bloodline Keeper and bottomed a Tundra; Marsh Flats makes no mana, so
that keep was a ONE-source hand, and the Keeper it was built around was never cast.
Bottom a way to find or make a creature only after everything below it in the order is gone.
This deck kept a four-card hand of Marsh Flats, Tundra, Swamp and Intruder Alarm - bottoming
Idyllic Tutor, Vision Skeins and Damnation, both of its ways to find a maker and its only sweeper
- and lost that game without a creature ever hitting its battlefield.

=== DECIDING SITUATIONS ===
- A "Land drop:" question is on the screen and any "Play <land>" line is listed: take one.
  Every turn, before anything else. "Play no land right now" only when nothing else is offered.
- The Greaves row reads "{right now: you control 0 creatures - this equips nothing}": do not
  cast it, whatever else is or is not on the menu. Removal, or "Cast nothing right now".
- Your battlefield line reads "of which 0 are creatures", no Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline
  Keeper is in "Your hand", no "DRAW PUNISHERS" line is on the screen, and "Cast Vision Skeins
  {1}{u}" is listed: cast it, this window. A turn spent not looking for the maker is a turn
  this deck does not have.
- A "Cast Intruder Alarm" row tagged "[second copy: ...]" says LEGAL, not USEFUL: a second Alarm
  changes no number on any screen - a maker, or "Cast nothing right now".
- The Idyllic Tutor row's bracket reads "none left in your library", or "every enchantment left
  ... is a copy of one you already control or hold", or an Alarm is in "Your hand": not the Tutor
  - unless it is the bait cast before a maker against a counter deck (deck125). Only "still in
  your library, not on your battlefield or in your hand" is a live Tutor.
- A "Cast <card> ... [from exile] {castable from exile ...}" row (Elite Spellbinder took it):
  a real cast at that card's own cast-list entry, priced by the number printed on it - but if
  the same card is ALSO on the menu from your hand, take the cheaper row.
- The "Cleanup step" ask is on the screen: FIRST scan the listed hand for "copy 2 of 2" / "copy
  2 of 3" / "copy 3 of 3" - a duplicate is always the discard, and there was one in your hand
  every time this went wrong.
  Never your only maker, never your only Intruder Alarm, NEVER YOUR ONLY DAMNATION. Some rows
  carry the verdict for you - "{dead right now: 0 legal targets on the board for it}",
  "{spare: you control N lands}" - and a row that carries one is the send; a row that carries
  none is not thereby live. Read the number back against the card your PLAN names next.
- A sacrifice ask listing YOUR permanents one row each (annihilator): send LANDS, tapped ones
  first; Intruder Alarm and any creature go last, after every land.
- An edict row reads "they control 1 creature - <name> is sacrificed" and that creature's "{card
  text:}" gives their other creatures mana, counters or +X/+X: cast the edict, whatever its
  power. Only "[defender]" and power 0 are creatures an edict is wasted on.
- Their battlefield line names Lightmine Field and your attackers are 2/2 tokens: the ATTACK line
  names exactly ONE creature.
- The only maker you could send reads "(both die)" or "(your attacker dies)" against their
  blockers and no token is attacking beside it: "ATTACK: none".
- The row you were about to use on a token-maker reads "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)": that
  maker does not block.
- The blockers header says "NOT lethal" and names an assignment whose X is Thraben Doomsayer,
  Bloodline Keeper or Lord of Lineage: do not copy that line.
- You are on the London bottoming ask: read "You will be left with an N-card hand" and build the
  best N in this order - lands covering your cheapest spell (up to four mana), token-maker,
  Idyllic Tutor or Vision Skeins, removal, Intruder Alarm last. Never all lands, never all
  four-drops.
- A line ending in "[cost: Tap]" is in your list and M is below L + C + 3: take it, this window,
  whatever your mana says, on either player's turn - unless it is YOUR turn and the total power
  of your non-summoning-sick creatures already reaches their printed life, in which case go and
  attack instead.
- The "repeated N times, then stop" row is listed: take it ONCE, written "CHOICE: 2 (Create
  human with Thraben Doomsayer x<N>)" with N = (L + C + 3) - M, then pass at the next window.
  When (L + C + 3) - M is 0 or less: "CHOICE: 0 (pass)" - the stop is reached. The row's "you
  control M creatures right now" is M; the CHOICE line agrees with the PLAN line's "this
  window:" or it is wrong - "x0" and "x1" are takes, not passes.
- Intruder Alarm is in "Your hand" and castable this turn, and a "[cost: Tap]" token line is
  listed before it has resolved: 0 (pass) - the Alarm first, the tap after; a maker tapped
  under a fresh Alarm stays tapped through their whole turn.
- A token-maker is on your battlefield line and "Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}" is listed: cast
  the Alarm, before a second maker and before Lightning Greaves.
- "Put a card onto the battlefield with Marsh Flats targeting ..." is listed: take it THIS
  window - the Flats makes no mana until you do. The dual: a colour at 0 first, then the one
  that makes the pip your hand needs twice ({w}{w} Doomsayer, {b}{b} Keeper). A basic only
  against Molten Rain / Lay Waste / Blastminer (deck130).
- The last row reads "Hold priority for the rest of this turn: pass now, ...": take it at the
  FIRST dead ask of the turn (Rule 1), and answer a re-opened run the same way every time.
- Several casts are listed at once: answer with the highest CL entry on the cast list above that
  appears in your menu.
- "Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" is taken: choose Intruder Alarm on the reveal list.
- "Transform:backside with Bloodline Keeper [cost: {b}]" is listed: take it.
- "Cast Damnation {2}{b}{b}" is listed: write "Damnation: N <>, K <>, M <>" from the row first
  ("(all of them carry a restriction against attacking)" = K of 0). M at 4 or more - never.
  M at 0 and K at 2 or more - cast it, above a maker, the Tutor and the Alarm. N bigger than M
  and K at 1 or more - cast it. K at 0 on Perimeter Captain or Pride Guardian - cast it, they
  are CHECK 1's wall. Any other K at 0, or N exactly 1 with a Tragic Slip or an edict on the
  menu that kills it - the one-for-one, and keep the Damnation. M bigger than N, or N at 0 -
  never, whatever the plan says. Two Damnation rows on one menu: the cheaper number.
- "Cast Tragic Slip {b}" is listed: read the cast row's Morbid clause. "-13/-13 (a creature died
  this turn, so Morbid applies)" - cast it, any target on the list dies. "-1/-1 (no creature has
  died this turn, so Morbid does NOT apply)" - cast it ONLY if a name on its "legal targets right
  now:" list has printed toughness 1; the target ask has no pass row, so a cast you regret cannot
  be taken back. On the target ask itself, take the row ending "- DIES"; a row ending
  "- SURVIVES (toughness N, -1/-1 more kills it)" is the engine telling you the card is wasted.
- An edict row reads "{right now: they control 1 creature - <name> is sacrificed ...}": cast it
  this window - Tribute to Hunger at the opponent first, Devour Flesh at "The opponent" only if
  Tribute is not listed - before a Tragic Slip on a toughness-2 creature. "they choose which
  one" or "at 0 this does nothing" on the row: no edict. NAMING "YOURSELF" IS ALSO A LIFE GAIN,
  and on this deck a big one: the row reads "YOU control N creatures - targeting yourself
  sacrifices one of them, your choice, and you gain its toughness", and under Fateful hour your
  1/1 Humans are 3/3. Three life for one token out of twenty-five is the cheapest turn you will
  ever buy - take it whenever the life, not their board, is killing you.
- "Equip with Lightning Greaves targeting" is listed and no creature reads "{attached: Lightning
  Greaves}": equip your best token-maker. Every re-offer after that: 0 (pass).
- Their line names Perimeter Captain or Pride Guardian and it is your Attackers step: "ATTACK:
  none" until removal has taken the last of them off that line.
- Their battlefield line names ONE of Sanguine Bond / Exquisite Blood: you cannot remove it -
  attack when your ATTACK LIST is at least 3 times their untapped creature count plus 3, or when
  your total power is at or above their printed life. BOTH of them, with the render printing
  "their life LOOP is in play": "ATTACK: none" and make tokens on their turn - UNLESS every
  attacker you would send reads "[no creature they control can block this attacker]" and the
  ATTACK TOTAL says lethal, and then send them all.
- An edict is on the screen and a planeswalker is what is beating you: an edict cannot touch a
  planeswalker. Take another action.
- Their battlefield line names ONE untapped creature and your attack list holds five or more:
  send them all. One blocker stops one attacker.
- An attacker's tag reads "they have N untapped creatures that could block this one" with N of
  2 or more, or prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause: tokens go
  anyway, and Thraben Doomsayer, Bloodline Keeper, Lord of Lineage or anything wearing the
  Greaves stays home - unless their printed life is at or below the power you are sending.
- The blockers prompt says "- NOT lethal: block only where the trade favors you." and stops
  there: a TOKEN still goes in front of the biggest attacker (tokens first, makers last), and the
  "you would be at" number is the life your combo has to live on next turn - UNLESS the row gains
  them life under a Sanguine Bond + Exquisite Blood loop, which is STEP -1 and blocks nothing.
- The blockers prompt says LETHAL: your first blocker goes on the attacker with the largest
  "deals N", not on the smallest one you can kill.
- An attacker row says "[menace]": two blockers on it, or none.
