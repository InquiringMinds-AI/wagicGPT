BANT HUMAN MIDRANGE (green-white, light blue splash)

THE DECK. Put Humans and Wolves on the board, grow them with lords and counters, and ATTACK. Removal (Fateful
Absence, Brutal Cathar) clears blockers; lords (Adversary, Sigarda, Katilda) turn a modest board lethal. Your
board is your clock.

ORDER OF OPERATIONS. Play a land; run the LETHAL COUNT, and if it clears cast nothing and go to combat;
otherwise cast the best creature you can (removal only to clear a blocker you need gone now); attack; then in
MAIN 2 take the creature-tapping actions - Katilda's counter, Ranger Class levels, Adversary counters.

MANA
- "Mana available: N total (... colours you can make: {g}{w})": N is what you can spend, the colour list is
  which COLORED pips you can pay. Trust both; never re-derive it.
- GENERIC IS NOT A COLOUR: Ranger Class {1}{G} is one green pip plus one mana of any kind, so "2 total ...
  colours: {g}" pays it. If N covers the cost and every coloured pip is listed, you can pay it.
- A numbered "Cast ..." row IS payable - pick it, and never answer a number not on the list.
- ONE LAND PAYS ONE PIP: Deserted Beach makes {W} or {U}, not both; Overgrown Farmland {G} or {W}. So Sigarda
  {1}{G}{W}{W} needs TWO white sources; a card can be absent from the list even when its colours show - take
  the best row offered and move on.

LAND DROP
- A "Land drop:" question offering a land -> PLAY IT. It costs no mana and does not reduce what you cast this
  turn, so never "Play no land right now" - not when your sources already cover your hand, not on a surplus of
  lands, not because the row says the land enters TAPPED, and never to hold a colour for later; with several
  offered, take the colour your hand needs most. Lair of the Hydra is a land like any other: play it.
- PATHWAYS have two faces and BOTH get their own Play row on the same menu, each tagged "TWO FACES, ONE CARD"
  as the FRONT or the BACK. Either row is one land drop, costs nothing, and is the only route to that face.
  Take the face that turns an uncastable card in hand castable.

LETHAL COUNT, BEFORE SPENDING ANYTHING IN UPKEEP OR MAIN 1
Add the power of every creature on YOUR battlefield line carrying neither "[tapped" nor "[summoning sick". If
that sum is at or above their printed life AND their line reads "of which 0 are creatures", answer "Cast
nothing right now", pass to the attackers ask, and send everything; this outranks the deploy floor.
YOUR HUMANS ARE MANA SOURCES - Katilda gives every Human "{T}: add mana", so once your lands are tapped the
engine pays with your creatures and prints the bill on the row: "{paying this taps: Katilda, Luminarch
Aspirant - they cannot attack this turn}". SEARCH EVERY CAST ROW FOR THE
WORDS "paying this taps" BEFORE YOU TAKE IT - the source count beside it counts lands and creatures alike and
never names a body. THEN COUNT THE NAMES IN THE BILL AND OBEY THE COUNT - quoting the bill and
taking the row anyway is the same lost attack: two or more names in Upkeep or Main 1 is a PASS, whatever the
row is and whatever your plan says you will attack with afterwards; with exactly ONE name take the row only if
it puts a body of printed power 2 or more onto the battlefield, and pass if it is a level, an activation or a
removal spell leaving no body.

DEPLOY FLOOR - ON YOUR MAIN PHASE, IF A CREATURE IS OFFERED, CAST ONE
"Cast nothing right now" is right in exactly three cases: (a) the only offer is Fateful Absence and you hold
it as an instant for their turn; (b) the lethal count has cleared; (c) every creature row is tagged
"[legendary: you already control <name> ... casting this sends one copy to your graveyard]" and your copy is
healthy - that row is not a body, and is a real cast only when your copy is about to die anyway or is worse
than the fresh one. Otherwise pick a creature, and the fewer you have the more urgent it is. A quiet board is
no reason to wait, low life is a reason to deploy rather than hold, and creatures are never held to dodge
removal - not even in a game you think is lost.

ATTACKING
THE "ATTACK TOTAL:" LINE PICKS WHO GOES - READ ITS LAST CLAUSE BEFORE ANY TAG.
- "...that KILLS them whatever they block" -> send EVERY attacker listed; no block saves them.
- "...blocking can leave them as high as N": COMPARE N TO THE LIFE ON THEIR LINE RIGHT NOW. N AT OR ABOVE IT
  means the attack can leave them HEALTHIER than they are now - send ONLY the A-lines tagged "[no creature
  they control can block this attacker]", nobody else. Those cannot be blocked, so they fire no blocking
  trigger and hand back no life; every blockable body you add alongside them is pure life for them. N BELOW
  their current life is an attack that still gains ground: send the blockable bodies too, priced by the
  per-creature tags below. Re-run that compare EVERY combat, since N climbs as their board grows - a swing
  that was fine two turns ago is how an opponent at 15 reaches 50. If the unblockable A-lines alone reach
  their printed life that IS the win - bringing one more attacker turns a kill into their survival.
Otherwise send each listed creature with any of: no untapped blocker opposite it; flying they
cannot block (Elite Spellbinder and Sigarda FLY); toughness beating the biggest thing that can
block it; an even-or-better trade.
"ATTACK: none" only when every attacker would die for nothing or do nothing. A creature tagged "[held back,
THIS creature could not block ANY of their N creatures]" guards nothing by staying home - keeping it back buys
you no blocker at all - so send or hold it on its own per-creature tag alone. An A-line proves that creature is
untapped, unsick and legal now - tapped creatures print "[tapped]" and get no A-line - so never hold a name
back on memory or on your plan. Its "their untapped blockers" tag is a per-creature verdict:
- "(you kill it, your attacker lives)" -> send it.
- "(neither dies)" stops every point and kills nothing - the opposite of free damage. If EVERY outcome on a
  tag reads that, send only when your declared attackers OUTNUMBER their untapped blockers or it has evasion;
  one attacker never outnumbers one blocker.
- "your attacker dies, their blocker lives" ANYWHERE in the tag is a per-creature STOP: that creature stays
  home even while the team attacks, unless the lethal check clears. Price the WORST name listed, since they
  pick the block they win. Tokens are exempt: a
  stop-tagged Wolf goes with the team as free bait.
- GANG BLOCK: when the tag collapses and ends "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker", that creature
  stays home unless the lethal check has cleared, whatever the friendly parentheses beside it say. FLYING DOES
  NOT EXEMPT SIGARDA - a collapsed tag counts only creatures that CAN block her. No gang clause -> use the
  parenthesis printed.
- BLOCKING TRIGGERS: "(blocking trigger: they gain 3)" inside an outcome is life they get for BLOCKING. It
  resolves when blockers are DECLARED, so that life is theirs before any damage lands; first strike does not
  cancel it.

BLOCKING
Each blocker prints what it may stop: "B1. Intrepid Adversary (3/1) - may block A1 (both die)". An attacker
missing from that list cannot be blocked by that creature; the usual reason is FLYING, and an all-illegal
answer is thrown away and the engine blocks for you.
- ONE BLOCKER, AT MOST ONE ATTACKER: count the left-hand sides, each B-number appears at most once.
- Several B-numbers on ONE A-number IS legal, and "no 1-on-1 result exists - only a block by TWO OR MORE of
  your creatures counts" asks for exactly that: with fewer blockers than that needs, choose WHICH ONE to stop
  and put them all on it, or answer "BLOCKS: none"; one blocker on each is illegal and is the worst answer.
- The parenthesis is the computed result, first strike and deathtouch already applied; never override it.
FIRST STEP OF EVERY BLOCK DECISION: find the header's "you would be at N". "BLOCKS: none" is allowed in exactly
one case - every offered block reads "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)" AND N is 10 or more. The test is N,
not the size of the attack: at "you would be at 6" you block even if only 2 damage is coming. DECLARING A
BLOCKER DOES NOT TAP IT: the blocker untaps and attacks on your next turn exactly as if it had stayed home, so
a rung-0 or rung-1 block costs you nothing and keeping a body back "for the attack" is never a reason to
decline one. When the header
says the attack IS LETHAL, survival comes first - assign blockers so the damage still getting through is LESS
than your printed life, biggest "deals N" attackers first, whatever their parentheses say. Then the ladder:
 0. "(neither dies)" - TAKE IT; nothing dies and the damage is gone.
 1. "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" - TAKE IT, at every life total, always.
 2. "(both die)" - TAKE IT unless the attacker it kills is worth less than your blocker.
 3. "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)" - the chump line, the only rung N decides: take it at N of 9 or less
    or when the attack is lethal, above 9 leave that attacker unblocked. One blocker stops ALL of a
    non-trampler's damage however big. TRAMPLE: a parenthesis ending "...N tramples to your face" buys only
    (attacker power minus N) life - only when the attack IS lethal, and never two blockers on one trampler.
A B-ROW LISTING SEVERAL A-NUMBERS: the ladder also picks WHICH attacker it blocks - read its own list and
take its BEST rung, 0 before 1 before 2 before 3, never the biggest attacker and never the one the header's
"best case" assignment names.
KATILDA'S B-ROW: not on a "(both die)" or rung-3 line while N is 10 or more - she is every Human's mana
ability - but at N of 9 or less she blocks like everything else.

YOUR CARDS
- INTREPID ADVERSARY ({1}{W}, 3/1, lifelink): ETB pay {1}{W} any number of times, each a valor counter giving
  ALL your creatures +1/+1. PAY as many times as you can afford with a board out; "don't add any counter"
  only with no spare mana.
- SIGARDA ({1}{G}{W}{W}, 4/4 flying trample): Humans get +1/+1. Best card here; cast on curve, and she needs
  TWO separate white sources.
- KATILDA ({G}{W}, 1/1): protection from Werewolves, and your Humans each tap for one mana of their own colour
  - she is a Human, so she appears in your "Mana available" total. Her "{4}{G}{W}, {T}: +1/+1 counter on each
  creature you control" is MAIN 2 ONLY: after attackers are declared, three or more creatures out, spare mana.
- LUMINARCH ASPIRANT ({1}{W}, 1/1): free +1/+1 counter each combat. PUT IT ON A CREATURE THAT CAN CONNECT,
  NOT THE BIGGEST ONE - counters are worth zero on a body a wall reading "(neither dies)" stops every turn.
  THE TEST IS THE SIZE PRINTED ON THE ROW: if your pick reads 6/6 or larger, choose another,
  preferring a flier, and never the Aspirant while another creature on the list can attack THIS turn - a row
  tagged "[summoning sick" cannot.
- RANGER CLASS ({1}{G}): cast for the 2/2 Wolf at once. Level 2 ({1}{G}) counters an attacker each combat,
  Level 3 ({3}{G}) casts off the top; level with SPARE mana only.
- BRUTAL CATHAR ({2}{W}, 2/2, daybound): ETB exiles an opponent creature until the Cathar leaves. Every row it
  appears on then prints "{if this leaves the battlefield: their <name> comes back from exile}": while that
  clause is there its death hands them a creature back, so it is never an even trade. Send it ONLY on "(you
  kill it, your attacker lives)" or with no blocker listed, and block with it only on rung 0 or rung 1.
  Night side MOONRAGE BRUTE: 3/3, first strike, ward 3 life.
- TOVOLAR'S HUNTMASTER ({4}{G}{G}, 6/6, daybound): ETB two 2/2 Wolves. Night side PACKLEADER: 7/7 making TWO
  more Wolves every time it enters OR ATTACKS - with it out, attacking is pure profit, so swing every turn.
- FATEFUL ABSENCE ({1}{W}, instant): destroys a creature or planeswalker (they get a Clue). Your ONLY answer
  to a PLANESWALKER, so against a CREATURE it is your LAST answer - with a Brutal Cathar in hand, cast the
  CATHAR instead. STOP: when the option says "the only legal targets are YOUR OWN right now", DO NOT CAST IT.
  A row printing "{reserve: ... VERDICT: taking this row STRANDS <name> this turn}" is a PASS: the body you
  would strand outweighs a small creature killed early, and this instant still has its window at the end of
  THEIR turn.
- ELITE SPELLBINDER ({2}{W}, 3/1 flier): its hand look is a TAX, not an answer - they still cast the card for
  {2} more. Exile "Exquisite Blood" or "Sanguine Bond" above everything; with neither listed take IDYLLIC
  TUTOR, which fetches them.
- TEFERI ({2}{W}{U}), your ONLY blue card: he needs a SECOND white source and you never contort your mana for
  him. On the battlefield, take a loyalty ability EVERY turn - a Teferi you never activate is a blank. His +1
  walks three stages - land, then creature, then artifact - and EACH opens with its own "Decline - do nothing"
  row, so decline the stage you have nothing to pick for. Once you enter a stage the side pick and the
  target list have NO pass row: enter it only when you can already name a tapped permanent of YOURS or an
  untapped one of THEIRS. Tapping theirs lasts only the turn: on your turn it removes a BLOCKER, so tap an
  ATTACKER on THEIR turn. Otherwise the +1 is 2
  life; take the -2 whenever your line reads "0 are creatures".
- LAIR OF THE HYDRA taps for {G}, and {X}{G} makes it an X/X Hydra until end of turn. ANY row printing
  "[Upkeep offer: ...]", "[this land is TAPPED: ...]", "{right now: does nothing this turn}" or "[repeat:
  activated this turn ...]" is a PASS, with no exception for a turn you mean to win. Animate in MAIN 1 only,
  with mana left after your creature, at the largest rung your OTHER sources pay for, and only when its A-line
  would read "[no creature they control can block this attacker]"; once animated it is an attacker like any
  other and the ATTACKING rules bind it, so an A-line naming a blocker that kills it means you lose a LAND for
  nothing.

MULLIGAN - AN ODDS COMPARISON, NOT A CHECKLIST
The ask prints "having already taken N mulligans you will bottom N cards on a keep (keeping M)". READ THE M
FIRST, before the cards. You run 25 lands (14 green, 15 white; only the four Overgrown Farmlands make
both) and your cheapest spells cost TWO, so a mulligan rarely fixes lands.
- M of 5 or less: with even ONE land, KEEP - the bottom ask sheds the uncastable cards. Only ZERO lands buys a
  fresh look. Colour, two-drops and curve are bottom-ask questions, never ship reasons.
- M of 6: keep unless the hand is zero lands, all lands, or ONE land under "Playing every land in this hand
  would not cover any spell in it". "would cover the cost of:" naming a card is a KEEP.
- M of 7, the untouched seven: TWO OR MORE LANDS AND AT LEAST ONE SPELL is the floor - KEEP. ALL LANDS or ZERO
  LANDS: ship. ONE LAND: keep if the coverage line names a card, ship if it covers nothing. The floor is the
  whole test at seven: a hand that clears it is a KEEP, and no count of colours, of expensive spells, or of
  lands you would rather have reopens it. What a hand cannot cast on turn two it casts on turn four.
- THE CARRIED PLAN IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT THIS HAND: it was written about the hand you already threw away.
  Each look is a NEW seven - decide from its own "counted by the engine: N lands, M spells" header, never
  from the plan or your own recount.
- "would not cover any spell in it" IS A ONE-LAND TEST AND NOTHING ELSE; at TWO OR MORE lands it is not a
  ship whatever it brackets. A COLOUR reason ("no spell in it is castable off {W}{U} alone") is never a ship
  above the floor - it only says your lands and your spells want different colours.
- THE "Mana sources among those lands" LINE COUNTS ONLY THE FACE EACH PATHWAY SHOWS; you pick the face when
  you play it, so ADD each Pathway's other colour before pricing a keep.
- A one-colour opener is ordinary and the other colour arrives within a turn or two, so it is never dead.
  "No one-drop" is never a reason since you run none. Between hands that clear the floor, prefer more lands
  and the cheap threats.

BOTTOMING AND CLEANUP DISCARD
On a keep, "You will be left with a N-card hand": keep the lands that cover your cheapest spells, then those
cheap spells, then the rest by cost, bottoming the priciest spell you cannot reach. A CLEANUP DISCARD is that
order read from the end: "PUT: n, m" names the cards you SEND, priciest first.

THE HOLD-PRIORITY ROW ("pass now, and do not ask me again unless the board changes ...") is a pass with one
extra effect. NEVER take it while any row above it is an activated or loyalty ability you have not used this
turn: one hold can silence such a row for the rest of the game - answer that menu with the
ability itself or with the plain pass row. Otherwise: when you have ALREADY decided to take no action AND the
prompt carries "[you declined this exact list N times already this turn]", answer HOLD instead of "Cast
nothing right now". That N counts identical OPTION LISTS, not boards, so it is never itself a reason to pass.
The hold row's warning prices rows you still WANT; when every row above it is one you have already ruled out -
a Fateful Absence whose only legal targets are your own, a legend-tagged duplicate of a healthy creature - it
prices nothing, so hold at the FIRST such note rather than decline the same list again.

MATCHUPS
- COUNTERSPELLS / OPEN MANA: count the entries on their battlefield line with NO "[tapped]" and call it U. U
  of 2 or less, or their hand at 3 or below -> cast your BEST creature now. U of 3 or more with their hand at
  5 or more -> cheapest creature first.
- LIFEGAIN / FLIERS: kill or exile a lifelinker on sight, since you cannot outrace their life total; against
  fliers your only answer is Fateful Absence, and your ground creatures are not offered as blockers for a
  flier at all, so do not spend both copies on ground creatures you could have blocked. THEIR LIFE ENGINE IS
  WHATEVER THE ROWS SAY GAINS THEM LIFE, not just lifelink: a "[defender]" whose text reads "whenever ...
  blocks, you gain N" IS the engine, and a defender with no such line is the WORST exile on the list. When
  Brutal Cathar or Fateful Absence opens a target list, take the row with the biggest gain-on-block number -
  and take it FROM THE LIST IN FRONT OF YOU, since a plan that named a target was written before this list
  existed and does not choose here.
- A LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER: the attackers prompt prints "LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER on the battlefield:
  theirs" and you cannot answer it - nothing here destroys an enchantment. READ THEIR LINE FOR THE SECOND NAME.
  * BOTH names -> a LOOP without limit. No defensive line exists: from that turn on, every combat send every
    A-line tagged "[no creature they control can block this attacker]" and no other attacker.
  * BLOCKING UNDER THEIR CONVERTER: a lifelink attacker costs you the same life whether you block or not -
    unblocked it deals it, blocked it gains them that much and the converter takes it off you - so a block
    is free and is your only way to REMOVE that attacker. Take every "(you kill it, your blocker lives)"
    row, at every life total.
  * ONE of the two names -> race it with EVASION. An A-line reading "[no creature they control can block this
    attacker]" is free in both directions, since no block means no blocking trigger and no life gained: SEND
    every such creature, every combat, at every life total. A "blocking trigger" tag is a PRICE, not a
    prohibition - read the header's "They have N untapped creatures able to block", never re-derive N, and the
    surplus above N lands its full power. The tag prints both halves ("they gain 3, and their converter takes
    3 off you"), so an extra attacker past N is worth declaring when the power it adds beats TWICE that gain
    number. Hold ONLY the creature whose WHOLE blocker list both stops it AND pays them.
