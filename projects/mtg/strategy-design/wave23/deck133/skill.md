# deck133 wave-23 — skill proposal

## PROPOSAL (one sharpening to the CRUTCH-LIFECYCLE / rotation-in new-leak method): distinguish an engine surface that DEMOTES a guide line from one that INVITES a deck-specific ADD.

**Context.** The wave-22 crutch-lifecycle (method-headline-1) is stated entirely in the DEMOTE/RETIRE
direction: when the engine now SHOWS a fact the guide used to narrate (a printed count, an option-line
cost/target annotation, a marker string), the guide crutch is demoted or retired. deck133 this corpus
hit a NEW shape that the lifecycle as written does not cover, and it points the OPPOSITE way.

**The observation.** The wave-23 engine batch's forced-self-loss framing (item a) is NOT a fact
surface — it is a **generic JUDGMENT heuristic** rendered on the prompt: "pick the card you can best
AFFORD TO LOSE (usually your LEAST valuable ...)." deck133 obeyed it with no guide rule (it sacrificed
Bloodghast to Cruel Edict, kept its finisher/removal on forced discard). Correctly, deck133 added NO
forced-loss RULE — that would be redundant with the framing (the method-1a anti-pattern). BUT the
generic heuristic is BLIND to a deck-specific fact it cannot know: for deck133, Bloodghast is UNIQUELY
affordable to lose because it recurs from the graveyard on the next land — it is not "least valuable"
in the abstract, it is "always the safest to give up" for a reason the engine's generic prior has no
access to. That deck-specific EXCEPTION/refinement to the generic heuristic is exactly what the
per-deck guide exists to supply, and it COEXISTS with the engine framing rather than being demoted by
it.

**The rung.** When a new engine surface lands, classify it before applying the crutch-lifecycle:
- **FACT surface** (shows a value/target/cost/count that was previously computed in the guide) ->
  DEMOTE/RETIRE the guide crutch per method-headline-1 (the fact is now carried; a crutch that narrates
  it is redundant, or worse, a lie if it narrates the fact's ABSENCE).
- **JUDGMENT surface** (renders a generic heuristic/nudge: "pick the least valuable," "block the
  biggest threat," "answer first") -> do NOT add a guide rule duplicating the generic heuristic (still
  method-1a), BUT audit whether the deck has a SPECIFIC card/line where the generic heuristic is wrong
  or under-informed, and ADD that deck-specific exception. The judgment surface RAISES the floor
  generically; the guide's remaining job shifts from "provide the judgment" to "provide the deck
  exception the generic judgment gets wrong or cannot see."

This complements method-headline-2 (rotation-in = new-leak hunt): the new-leak an engine JUDGMENT
surface exposes is not a missing rule (the engine now supplies the generic rule) — it is the
deck-specific EXCEPTION to that rule. Hunt for the exception, not the rule.

**Evidence strength.** Single-seat, single-corpus (deck133, the Bloodghast-fodder ADD). Offered as a
sharpening for synthesis to weigh, not a load-bearing change — the underlying discipline (don't
duplicate an engine surface; find the deck-specific edge) is already implicit in method-1a; this just
names the FACT-vs-JUDGMENT split so the ADD case isn't mistaken for a demotion-debt case.
