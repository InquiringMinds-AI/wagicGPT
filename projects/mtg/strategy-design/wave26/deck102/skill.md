## THE LESSON -- separate an ENVIRONMENT collapse from a DECISION regression before you touch the guide.

A seat can go from 4/6 to 0/6 with the model's DECISION QUALITY essentially unchanged. deck102 this
wave is the clean case study, and the decomposition procedure that proved it is the transferable
skill:

1. **Re-key the win column against the POOL, not the seat.** Every opponent 102 beat last wave
   (131/133/137/27) improved; deck133 alone went 1/6 -> 6/6. Two of the six games were fresh-deck
   debuts (18, 22). Before blaming the seat, ask: how many of these losses are the SAME opponent
   playing BETTER, or a NEW opponent? Here: 4 of 6 are pool-rise/fresh-deck. That is not the seat
   regressing -- it is the bar rising underneath a converged guide.

2. **Trace each loss to a DECIDING FACTOR and label it pool-rise / draw-variance / decision.** Do this
   game by game, citing the actual line. deck102: 133 = pool-rise (mirror opponent got 6/6); 22 =
   can't-close grind (correct Damnation, attacked with everything, just could not push 20 life);
   131/27 = threat-light rock-heavy draws raced out; 18 = fresh aggro + a mild double-rock durdle.
   The ONLY decision-shaped soft spots (t2 Smallpox marginal, t8 double-rock at 13 life) did not
   decide their games and are within the guide's existing latitude.

3. **A 0/6 does NOT license a guide edit.** The temptation is to "do something" about a collapse. But
   the guide's #1-leak paragraph (disruption-is-not-a-clock, deploy-a-threat-and-attack) ALREADY
   covers the only recurring soft factor, and the model FOLLOWED it where it drew threats (Tourach,
   Witch of the Moors, Syr Konrad, Liliana DG, Animate Dead reanimation). More guide text cannot fix
   draw variance or an opponent getting better. When the deciding factors are environment + variance
   and decisions are sound, the correct action is NO edit -- freeze and rotate. Editing a converged
   guide in response to an environment collapse is a crutch, not calibration.

**General skill candidate for synthesis:** win-column swings are a NOISY signal for guide work at a
converged seat. Gate any collapse-driven edit on a per-game deciding-factor trace: only losses whose
deciding factor is a REPEATED, guide-addressable DECISION (not pool-rise, not draw variance, not an
already-covered leak the model actually followed) justify touching the guide. deck102's 0/6 produced
ZERO such losses -- it is a converged-seat environment collapse, and the right response is rotation.
