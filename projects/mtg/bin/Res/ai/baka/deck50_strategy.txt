DECK IDENTITY: EROSION - a W/U/B mill deck with NO creatures in it at all. You win by
one route only: the opponent's library reaches zero and they must draw from it. Damage
never wins this game for you and their life total is not a target you are working on.
Every other card exists to keep you alive long enough for their library number to run
out. Damnation and Wrath of God destroy all creatures; Moat turns off every attacker
without flying for the rest of the game; Evacuation buys a single turn at instant speed;
Dream Fracture is the only counter you have; Howling Mine and Forced Fruition make the
opponent draw faster, and a card they draw is a card you no longer have to mill.

Two printed numbers decide almost everything. "Opponent library: N" is your clock.
"Your library: N" is the clock you must not lose to first: Howling Mine draws YOU an
extra card every one of your draw steps as well, so the two numbers are in a race.

ORDER OF OPERATIONS. 1. Get lands down, in the colours your answers need. 2. Stop the
board that is killing you, the turn a row can stop it. 3. Put permanent mill and extra
draws on the battlefield. 4. Point the one-shot mill spells at the opponent. Step 2
outranks steps 3 and 4 in the same window. Steps 3 and 4 are the whole plan when the
opponent battlefield line reads "0 are creatures".

THE OPENING-HAND ASK

This deck is 24 lands in 60 cards, in three colours, and its cheapest spells are Tome
Scour {u} and Glimpse the Unthinkable {u}{b}.

The ask prints a line beginning "Playing every land in this hand would". Answer on that
line and read no further:
 1. If it reads "would cover the cost of:" and names any spell, keep this hand.
 2. If it reads "would not cover any spell in it", mulligan.
That is the whole rule at every look, including the last one. A hand of five lands and
two spells with a covered spell is a keep - lands are what this deck is short of when
its answers cost {2}{B}{B} and {2}{W}{W}. A hand with three answers and no land that
casts them is the hand that loses.


AFTER A MULLIGAN: WHICH CARD LEAVES THE HAND

The ask prints the hand numbered with each card's cost, plus the same "Playing every
land in this hand would cover the cost of:" line. Send to the bottom, stopping at the
first that matches:
 1. The spell with the highest printed mana value that the coverage line does NOT name.
    Forced Fruition {4}{u}{u}, Evacuation {3}{u}{u} and Traumatize {3}{u}{u} are the
    cards this catches; a six-mana enchantment in a hand of three lands is not a card
    you will cast from this hand.
 2. If the coverage line names every spell in the hand, a land - and then only while
    the hand still holds more lands than the mana value of its cheapest spell.
 3. If neither applies, the spell with the highest mana value.
Never send your cheapest mill spell or your only land away. Two spells and five lands
is a hand that functions; five lands and one spell is a hand that does nothing, and one
land and six spells is the hand you already mulliganed.


THE LAND ROW

A "Play <land>" row is taken in every window it appears, with one exception below. It
costs no mana and closes nothing: the casting list is put to you again in the same turn
with the new land counted, so taking the land first can only add rows. "Play no land
right now" is an answer you take only when there is no Play row you would rather have.

WHICH land, when several Play rows are on the menu: read your hand line for the spell
you most want to cast and the "sources that can make each" figures on the Mana
available line, then take the Play row for the colour that is short. The order to
satisfy, highest first:
 1. A second source of the colour of a sweeper in hand that reads "[cannot pay now:
    needs {2}{b}{b}" or "needs {2}{w}{w}" - a Swamp for Damnation, a Plains for Wrath
    of God or Moat. A hand holding Damnation and one Swamp is a hand with no answer;
    the next Swamp is worth more than a fifth Island.
 2. {U}, up to two sources, for Memory Erosion {1}{u}{u} and Dream Fracture {1}{u}{u}.
 3. {B}, one source, for Glimpse the Unthinkable {u}{b}.
 4. Anything, for raw count.
A card in hand that reads "[castable now]" needs no help; the land you want is the one
that changes a "[cannot pay now: needs {2}{b}{b}, your N untapped sources cannot pay
it]" tag into a castable one.


THE CASTING MENU

Take the first entry below that matches a row on this menu; stop at the first that
answers. Every entry is a row the menu prints, and if none of them is on the menu take
the decline row ("Cast nothing right now"), which is a real answer.

 0. If the prompt prints "Opponent library: 0", or a mill row's own text would take
    their library to 0, take that row now. Nothing outranks finishing the game.

 1. A "Cast Damnation" or "Cast Wrath of God" row whose brackets read "destroys N of
    their creatures" with N of 2 or higher - or with N of 1, when that one creature is
    the whole of a printed "CRACK-BACK NEXT TURN" line that says it would KILL you. Take it this
    window. If both rows are on the menu, take the one whose colour the "sources that
    can make each" line has more of, so the other stays castable next turn.

 2. A "Cast Moat" row. Take it the first window it appears. Moat is not an answer to a
    board that already exists - it is the wall you build before the board exists, and
    it keeps working for the rest of the game with no further mana. An empty opponent
    battlefield is the ideal moment for it, not a reason to wait; a board of creatures
    that are already attacking you is the late moment. The one board on which Moat is
    not the answer: one where every creature on the opponent's battlefield line carries
    the [flying] tag, because those still attack through it. Do not hold Moat for a
    better turn; there is no better turn.

 3. A "Cast Evacuation" row, when the prompt prints a "CRACK-BACK NEXT TURN" line that
    says it would KILL you, or would take you to 5 or lower, and no row above matched.
    Evacuation returns the creatures to their hands; they recast them. It buys exactly
    one turn, so take it only when you can name what the extra turn is for - a sweeper
    you can pay for next turn, or the mill that finishes them.

 4. A "Cast Memory Erosion" row. It mills two every time the OPPONENT casts a spell,
    and copies stack: each copy mills its own two. Three copies on the battlefield turn
    every spell they cast into six cards. Take it whenever rows 1-3 do not match.

 5. A "Cast Forced Fruition" row. It makes the opponent draw seven for every nonland
    spell they cast, and drawing is how they lose. This is a mill card with a big cost,
    not a gift to them - the seven cards leave their library. Take it when the mana is
    there and rows 1-4 do not match.

 6. A "Cast Howling Mine" row, while "Your library:" is larger than "Opponent
    library:". Both players draw the extra card, so each copy speeds your own deck up
    as much as theirs. When "Your library:" is equal to or smaller than "Opponent
    library:", do not take a Howling Mine row at all - take a mill row or the decline
    row instead. This applies to the first copy and to every "[second copy: you already
    control Howling Mine]" row.

 7. A "Cast Glimpse the Unthinkable", "Cast Traumatize" or "Cast Tome Scour" row,
    biggest first: Traumatize takes half of what is left, Glimpse takes ten, Tome Scour
    takes five. Take Traumatize while the "Opponent library:" number is still large -
    half of 40 is 20 and half of 8 is 4. When two of these rows are on one menu and the
    mana pays for both, take the larger one first in the turn.

 8. A "Cast Dream Fracture" row - see the target section; it only has a row when there
    is something on the stack to counter.

Rows this list never reaches: a "Cast Damnation" or "Cast Wrath of God" row whose
brackets read "destroys 0 of their creatures (0 without a restriction against
attacking), 0 of yours". That row destroys nothing and the card is gone. You hold four
Damnation and four Wrath of God for the board that is going to arrive; a menu showing
only those two rows and a decline row is a menu you answer with the decline row.

When the prompt prints "NO LIVE CAST ROW ON THIS MENU", take the decline row.


HOLD AND DECLINE ROWS

"Cast nothing right now" closes this window only and the list comes back this turn.
"Stop asking me this turn - pass now ..." closes the whole run of casting
windows until this turn ends or until a row changes.

Take a hold row only when every row on the menu is one you have refused for a stated
reason - a sweeper reading "destroys 0", a Howling Mine while your library is the
shorter one, a mill row you cannot pay for. If a "Cast Moat" row, a sweeper row with a
nonzero destroy count, or a mill row you can pay for is on the menu, the hold row is
wrong: take that row. A turn on which you decide there is "nothing to do" while a
castable answer is printed on the menu is a turn you gave away.

A plan you wrote a window ago is not a reason. When the prompt shows you what you last
wrote and the row you named is on this menu, take that row now.


TARGET ASKS

Every mill spell - Tome Scour, Glimpse the Unthinkable, Traumatize - offers "The
opponent (player, life N)" and yourself. Take the opponent, at every one of these asks,
in every game. Milling yourself loses the race you are running.

Dream Fracture's target ask lists what is on the stack. Counter, in this order: a spell
that would put their graveyard back into their library, a sweeper or removal aimed at
your own permanents, then the largest spell offered. Dream Fracture also makes its
target's controller draw a card and draws you one, so countering their spell still
costs them a card off the top - a counter is a small mill. It is your only counter; a
window with nothing worth countering prints no row for it, so there is nothing to hold
it for.


THE CLEANUP HAND-SIZE ASK

The ask prints your hand numbered. Name, stopping at the first that applies:
 1. A land, while the Mana available line already shows more sources than the largest
    mana value in your hand.
 2. A duplicate sweeper, while the hand still holds two or more of Damnation and Wrath
    of God together.
 3. The highest mana value card you have no plan for.
Never discard your last copy of Moat, your last Dream Fracture, or a mill spell.


COMBAT ASKS

This deck contains no creatures. You are never asked to declare attackers, and a
blockers ask reaches you with nothing to assign, so answer its decline row. Windows
whose phase line reads "Combat begins", "Attackers" or "Combat ends" are still casting
windows: read them with the casting list above, and note that Evacuation is the one
card in the deck with a row at those seams.


CARD FACTS THE PROMPT DOES NOT PRINT

- Memory Erosion triggers on the OPPONENT casting a spell. Your own spells mill nobody.
  Casting a spell of your own to "trigger" your Memory Erosion is not a thing that
  happens.
- Damnation {2}{B}{B} and Wrath of God {2}{W}{W} do the same thing - destroy all
  creatures, no regeneration. They differ only in colour, and you control no creature,
  so they are one-sided in your favour on every board.
- Moat {2}{W}{W} stops attacks by creatures without flying. It does not stop blocking,
  it does not stop activated abilities, and it does not stop creatures with [flying].
- Evacuation {3}{U}{U} is an instant and returns ALL creatures to their owners' hands,
  including any of the opponent's that entered this turn.
- Traumatize takes half of the library rounded down, counted when it resolves, so its
  value falls every turn you wait.
- Howling Mine's extra draw goes to whichever player's draw step it is, both of them.
- Forced Fruition is a single copy; there is no second one coming.
- A card in the opponent's graveyard is not milled for good if they can return it. When
  their battlefield or their log shows a permanent that shuffles a graveyard back into
  a library, the "Opponent library:" number can jump upward - re-read it every window
  rather than carrying the number you saw last turn.


SITUATIONS

Turn 6, your life 19, four lands in hand and two on the battlefield, the menu offering
Play Plains, Play Island and Play no land right now, with Damnation in hand reading
"[cannot pay now: needs {2}{b}{b}, your 2 untapped sources cannot pay it]" and your one
black source already down. Take a Play row, and take the Swamp if one is offered: the
second black source is the difference between having Damnation in two turns and having
a dead card. "Play no land right now" with four lands in hand is never the answer.

Their battlefield line reads 4 creatures, your life is 17, and the menu offers Cast
Moat and Cast Wrath of God with brackets reading "destroys 4 of their creatures".
Take the Wrath of God row. Four bodies die now; Moat would leave them alive to block
nothing and to be pumped or given flying later.

Their battlefield line reads 0 creatures, your life is 10, and the menu offers Cast
Moat, Cast Evacuation, Cast Howling Mine, three Play rows and a hold row. Take Cast
Moat. An empty board is when Moat is cheapest to resolve and it is the only card in
your hand that will still be working in ten turns.

Your life is 15, the menu offers Cast Damnation and Cast Wrath of God, both reading
"destroys 0 of their creatures (0 without a restriction against attacking), 0 of
yours", plus a Play row and a decline row. Take the Play row, then the decline row.
Two sweepers spent on an empty board are two sweepers missing from the turn their
board arrives.

"Your library: 14", "Opponent library: 18", and the menu offers Cast Howling Mine
[second copy], Cast Tome Scour and a decline row. Take Cast Tome Scour. You are behind
in the race that decides this game, and a second Howling Mine moves both numbers down
together - it does not close a gap, it shortens the game you are losing.

"Opponent library: 31", your life 13, their battlefield line reads 0 creatures, and the
menu offers Cast Traumatize, Cast Glimpse the Unthinkable and Cast Howling Mine.
Take Cast Traumatize first: half of 31 is 15 cards, and Glimpse's ten is worth the same
whether you cast it now or after.

The prompt prints "CRACK-BACK NEXT TURN: 5 of their creatures will be able to attack
... you would be at -4 or lower; that would KILL you", and the menu offers Cast Memory
Erosion and a Play row. Nothing on this menu stops the attack, so take the Play row
that most improves your colours, then the decline row - and the window before this one,
with a sweeper row on the menu, was the window that decided it.
