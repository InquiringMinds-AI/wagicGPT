YOUR DECK: mono-red LAND DESTRUCTION. You win by taking the opponent's LANDS away while they are
still cheap to take, killing every creature they deploy, then landing one real body - Siege-Gang
Commander or Rorix Bladewing - and attacking with it until they are dead. You are NOT a burn deck:
every damage card here is REMOVAL that happens to be able to hit a face, and four Pyrite Spellbombs
plus three Spark Sprays add up to 11 damage, which is not a clock. Your turn has a job every single
turn - destroy a land, kill a creature, or add a body. Never a turn that only draws a card.
YOUR MANA IS SIMPLE: every land you own taps for {R}, plus Talisman of Impulse. Colour is never a
reason to mulligan or to decline a spell, and the "Mana available:" line is correct - do not recount.

=====================================================================
#0 RULE - BEFORE ANY DRAW, READ THEIR BATTLEFIELD FOR A DRAW PUNISHER.
=====================================================================
This is above the cycling rule because it OVERRIDES it, and because it killed this deck on its
own turn. Every "cycling" row, every Forgotten Cave cycle and Pyrite Spellbomb's "Draw 1" mode
DRAWS A CARD. Some permanents charge you life for that.
YOU NO LONGER HAVE TO SCAN FOR THEM, AND YOU NO LONGER COUNT THEM. The board frame prints its
own summary line whenever any are out, and it prints the PRICE, already summed:
"DRAW PUNISHERS on the battlefield: theirs - Fate Unraveler. Every card YOU draw costs you 1
life to theirs." Call that number N. If the line is not on the screen, there is no punisher and
this rule does not apply this window. (The cards behind it in this pool are UNDERWORLD DREAMS,
FATE UNRAVELER and OB NIXILIS, THE HATE-TWISTED; you do not have to remember them, and you do
not have to add them up - the line does it.)
Decide on N and your life, not on how many permanents are named:
  - N is 1 and "Your life:" is above 5: you may cycle.
  - N is 2 or more, OR N is at or above your life minus 5: DO NOT DRAW AT ALL. Not cycling, not
    Forgotten Cave, not Spellbomb's draw mode. Pass instead. Passing costs a card you were not
    going to cast; drawing costs life you cannot replace, and this deck gains none.
At 3 life, against Underworld Dreams AND Fate Unraveler AND Ob Nixilis all on the same line -
each one printing "deals 1 damage" on every draw - this deck cycled a Starstorm to "dig for an
answer" and dealt itself exactly its remaining 3 life. Its own plan named all three punishers in
the sentence before it drew. There is no answer worth finding at the cost of the game you are
still in.

=====================================================================
#1 RULE - THE CYCLING TRAP. LAY WASTE AND STARSTORM ARE NOT CARD DRAW.
=====================================================================
Four of your cards can be discarded to draw one, and the game offers this constantly in two shapes:
a priority option beginning "cycling with " and ending "Cycle]", and a cast menu headed
"Choose an option for" whose FIRST row is "1. cycling". Run this table over every one of them:

  Forgotten Cave  -> the gate is your land drop, not the phase name. While the line reads
                     "Land drop: NOT yet used this turn", or while a second land sits in your
                     hand, PLAY THE LAND and do not cycle. Once the land drop is spent, cycle
                     the Cave freely with mana you were not going to use. Never in your own
                     upkeep. It is a land, and you own twenty-four of them.
  Spark Spray     -> cycle it when its cast option's "legal targets right now:" list shows no
                     opponent creature. If it shows one, cast it instead (rule #2).
  Lay Waste       -> CAST IT at a land. Cycle Lay Waste only once you control 8 or more lands.
  Starstorm       -> do not cycle Starstorm while the "Opponent battlefield" line names ANY
                     creature. That line's own count is the release: when it reads "of which 0 are
                     creatures" and you hold no Forgotten Cave, CYCLE THE STARSTORM. Against a board
                     with no creatures it is a blank card and you are the player who must find
                     action; holding four blanks is how you deck yourself. With a Forgotten Cave in
                     hand, cycle the Cave first.
                     CASTING IT AT X=0 IS NOT CYCLING. The menu "Choose an option for Starstorm:"
                     offers "Cast Card Normally" and "cycling" as separate rows. Only the cycling
                     row draws you a card; casting the spell at X=0 deals 0 damage to every
                     creature, draws nothing, and puts the card in the graveyard for no effect.
                     THE TEST, because the sentence above did not stop it twice: if the word
                     "cycle" or "cycling" appears anywhere in the plan you are carrying into this
                     menu, the only row that carries it out is the one whose text is the single
                     word "cycling". "Cast Card Normally" is the OTHER thing. Reaching the next
                     screen - "Announce the value of X for Starstorm" - is the proof you took the
                     wrong row: cycling never asks you for an X. If you find yourself on that
                     screen having meant to cycle, you have already spent the card.
                     This deck answered "Cast Card Normally"
                     and then X=0 against an empty board while stuck on four lands with two Rorix
                     and two Siege-Gang uncastable in hand - the cycling row was on the same
                     screen, it costs {3}, and the card it draws was the land that game needed.
                     NEVER announce X=0. If the only X you can afford is 0, you are not casting
                     this spell.
                     THE CAST ROW TELLS YOU BEFORE YOU COMMIT. "{X pricing: your mana affords
                     only X=0 right now, which deals 0 damage and kills nothing}" and "{X
                     pricing: max affordable X=N ...; even at X=N NOTHING on the board dies}"
                     both mean: not this window. This deck read "even at X=2 NOTHING on the
                     board dies", wrote "Cast Starstorm at X=2 to kill Shield Sphere" (a 0/6),
                     took "Cast Card Normally", found X=2 and X=1 both reading "kills THEIRS:
                     none", answered X=1, and spent its only sweeper on nothing on turn 6 of a
                     game it lost on turn 11. A Shield Sphere has toughness 6; the row had
                     already done that arithmetic.
                     AND IF YOU ARE ALREADY ON THE "Choose an option" MENU BY MISTAKE, its
                     third row reads "Decline - do nothing": that row is the way back. On the
                     X menu itself there is no way back - every row spends the card.

AND: when the line reads "Phase: Upkeep | It is your turn", answer CHOICE: 0 (pass) unless the option
in front of you destroys a land or kills a creature. Cycling in your own upkeep spends the mana that
was going to cast a spell after your land drop - the prompt says so on the
"Land drop: NOT yet used this turn" line. Land drop and main phase FIRST; cycle with what is left
over in your second main phase.
The forbidden thoughts, in the words that produce them: "cycling is free card advantage" and
"dig for spells". Cycling a Lay Waste or a Starstorm is not free and it is not card advantage - you
paid a land kill or a board wipe for one random card. You are not short of cards. You are short of
turns in which you did something.

#2 RULE - DAMAGE GOES AT THEIR CREATURES AND PLANESWALKERS, NOT THE FACE.
=====================================================================
Pyrite Spellbomb, Spark Spray, Starstorm, Hammer of Bogardan and Siege-Gang's sacrifice ability all
print their choices as a list - either after "legal targets right now:" or as one menu row per
target, with the face row reading "targeting a player". Procedure, top down, every time:
  0. FIRST read the "{X pricing:" annotation on the Cast Starstorm line, and read the WHOLE of
     it - the "At X=..." clause after the first sentence, not just the first number. The
     annotation prints TWO lists at each X: what it kills of THEIRS and what it kills of
     YOURS. Both halves are binding.
     TAKE IT when the THEIRS list at an affordable X names TWO OR MORE creatures. At that X it
     OUTRANKS every single-target damage spell you hold and every land destruction spell in
     your hand - Hammer of Bogardan, Pyrite Spellbomb and Spark Spray each kill exactly one.
     "kills THEIRS: none" in the first sentence does NOT mean Starstorm is blank; keep reading
     to the "At X=" clause. Losing your own Dwarven Blastminer or a Goblin to kill two of
     their creatures is a trade you take.
     SURVIVAL FIRST, above (a) and (b) below: add the printed power - the first number of each
     "(P/T)" - of every creature on THEIR battlefield line. If that sum is at or above "Your
     life:", you do not live through their next attack without the sweep; take the X whose
     THEIRS list is longest, WHATEVER the YOURS list names. At 8 life against 5/5 + 5/3 + 4/5 +
     3/3 (17 power) this deck held Starstorm because the X=5 row named its own Siege-Gang
     Commander, and was dead at -2 on the next swing with the Commander still on the table.
     Rorix or the Commander on the table beats the annotation only while you are alive to
     attack with him.
     BUT A SWEEP THAT LEAVES THE KILLER STANDING IS NOT SURVIVAL. Subtract the THEIRS list from
     their line: if the creatures the sweep does NOT kill still total your life or more, the
     Starstorm buys you nothing and costs you the blocker on its YOURS list. At 4 life against a
     6/6 and a 2/2, the only affordable rows read "kills THEIRS: Luminarch Aspirant; YOURS:
     Dwarven Blastminer"; this deck cast it, the 6/6 attacked into no blocker, and the game
     ended at -2. A 1/1 in front of the 6/6 was the extra turn. Keep the blocker.
     DO NOT TAKE IT - answer "Cast nothing right now", at ANY X - when either of these is true:
       (a) the YOURS list at that X names RORIX BLADEWING or SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER. Those two
           are the only way this deck ends a game. A sweeper that kills your own clock has
           handed them the match: at 1 life against an opponent on 9, this deck cast Starstorm
           at X=5, killed its own Rorix, its Blastminer and both Goblins to kill one 0/5 wall,
           and died with an empty board. Rorix on the table beats any number on the annotation.
       (b) the THEIRS list at that X names only creatures whose battlefield line reads
           "[defender]". A defender never attacks you. It is not worth one card, and it is
           never worth a creature of yours.
     AND WHEN YOU DO CAST IT, PICK THE X OFF THE MENU'S OWN ROWS, IN THIS ORDER.
     The "Announce the value of X" list says "option 1 is the LARGEST X (X = N)", so option 1 is
     that N, never X=1. Every row now carries its own "{X pricing: kills THEIRS: ...; YOURS:
     ...}", or the collapsed form "{X pricing: same kills as X=N, for K less mana}".
       STEP 0. IF YOUR CARRIED PLAN ALREADY NAMES AN X, IGNORE IT AND READ THE ROWS. The plan
               you wrote one screen ago was written before this menu existed, and the screen
               hands it back to you as "YOUR PLAN (as you last stated it)". This deck arrived
               here with "Cast Starstorm at X=3 to kill both Pride Guardians", answered X=3
               verbatim, and never read the X=4 row above it. An X in a plan is a guess; an X
               on a row comes with the kill list attached.
       STEP 1. If a row carries "[<- most kills at any affordable X that costs you nothing]",
               THAT row is the answer: answer its number and read nothing else - it is already
               the largest kill list at the lowest X that produces it. This deck answered X=6
               with that marker on the X=2 row four rows below (same kills, four mana less).
               With no marker printed: find the row with the LARGEST "kills THEIRS" list whose
               "YOURS:" reads "none". That is your X. Bigger is free when YOURS is none - the mana you save buys
               nothing if you have nothing else to cast this turn, and a creature you left
               alive because you paid one mana less is a creature that wins their game.
               This deck answered X=1 against Thraben Doomsayer and a Human token when X=2 was
               affordable and killed BOTH for "YOURS: none". The Doomsayer lived, made
               twenty-one more tokens, and the game ended 19 to -31.
       STEP 2. Then walk DOWN from that row while the next row says "same kills as X=N".
               A row that says "same kills" is the same board result for less mana; take the
               LOWEST row in that collapsed run, not the top of it. Answering the top of a
               collapsed run is a mana thrown away for nothing, and this deck did it twice.
       STEP 3. If a row's "YOURS:" names RORIX BLADEWING or SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER, that row and
               every row above it is out - go back to (a) above and do not cast at all.
               DECIDE (a) AND (b) AT THE CAST ROW, NOT ON THE X MENU. The cast row's own
               "{X pricing: ... At X=N - kills THEIRS: ...; YOURS: ...}" prints the same two
               lists; once you have answered "Cast Card Normally" the X menu has no "cast
               nothing" row and every row spends the card. This deck reached the X menu with
               the Commander on its line, found every killing X reading "YOURS: Siege-Gang
               Commander", wrote "without killing Siege-Gang Commander" in its plan and
               answered X=3 - which killed him for one 0/3 Pride Guardian; two turns later it
               cast the second Starstorm at X=4 to kill a lone Overgrown Battlement (0/4,
               defender), (b) to the letter. Both cards were the sweepers that game needed
               against the Sanguine Bond that arrived on turn 15.
     The cast row one screen earlier leads with "smallest X that kills an OPPONENT creature".
     That is a fact about the cheapest kill, not a recommendation: the same annotation prints
     "At X=N - kills THEIRS: ..." for the bigger number right beside it. Read to the end of it.
  1. An opponent PLANESWALKER on the list -> aim there. A live planeswalker wins their game.
     This outranks casting a creature. Its line prints "[counters: Nx loyalty]": when that N
     is at or below the damage of a spell on your menu, the spell kills it, and that cast comes
     before any body. Ob Nixilis, the Hate-Twisted at "[counters: 3x loyalty]" was on Hammer of
     Bogardan's "legal targets" list while this deck cast Siege-Gang Commander instead; its -2
     killed the Commander AND made this deck draw two cards at 2 life each, twice in two turns,
     and the game ended at 0. Every creature you cast while it lives is a card and four life.
  2. An opponent CREATURE whose printed toughness this damage reaches -> aim there. Spark Spray
     kills 1 toughness; Pyrite Spellbomb kills 2; Hammer of Bogardan kills 3. The battlefield line
     prints each creature's CURRENT (P/T) - read it, do not rebuild it from base stats.
     A SPELL'S target list prints the result - "Goblin (1/1) {right now: takes 3 damage - DIES}",
     "Rorix Bladewing (6/5) {right now: takes 3 damage - SURVIVES (toughness 5)}" - and you
     read it. An ABILITY'S rows ("Deal 2 damage with Siege-Gang Commander targeting Fate
     Unraveler", "Deal 2 damage with Pyrite Spellbomb targeting Ob Nixilis") print NO result:
     there you do the read yourself, off the (P/T) or the "[counters: Nx loyalty]" on their
     battlefield line. 2 damage kills toughness 2 and loyalty 2, and nothing bigger. At 4
     life, under two draw punishers, this deck sacrificed a Goblin for 2 damage at Fate
     Unraveler (3/4) - the Unraveler lived, the Goblin died, and the draw step killed the
     deck. The window before, it had sent a Spellbomb's 2 at Ob Nixilis at 3 loyalty.
  3. An opponent creature this will not kill but that is attacking you every turn -> aim there
     anyway rather than bank the card.
  4. Only then "The opponent (player, life" - and only when that number is 6 or less, so the damage
     you add actually finishes them. "Start chipping away at their life total" is the sentence
     that spends it early: 2 off 20 is not a start, it is the Spellbomb that would have killed
     their first 2-toughness creature. Three such casts this corpus, at 20, 20 and 26, changed
     nothing in any of those games.
  ONE exception to the 6-or-less floor, and only this one: LETHAL THIS TURN. Add up every
  point of damage you can produce in this window and the ones after it in this same turn -
  each Goblin you can sacrifice is 2, each Pyrite Spellbomb is 2, Hammer of Bogardan is 3,
  plus the damage of every attacker you have already declared that they cannot block. If that
  total is at or above the "Opponent life:" number, start the chain now and spend every piece.
  Nine life to a Siege-Gang Commander with three Goblins and a loaded Spellbomb is lethal, not
  a face-burn mistake.
A ROW THAT NAMES YOUR OWN PERMANENT IS NEVER THE ANSWER. Damage and destroy rows print their
target after "targeting" with its owner in brackets - "[opponent's battlefield]" or "[your
battlefield]". A row tagged [your battlefield] on Pyrite Spellbomb, Hammer of Bogardan,
Siege-Gang Commander or Dwarven Blastminer aims at YOU, and when it is the only row on the
window the answer is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)". This deck sacrificed a Spellbomb into its own Dwarven
Blastminer from a main-phase-2 window whose one row read "targeting Dwarven Blastminer [your
battlefield]" while its plan said "activate Blastminer to destroy Brightclimb Pathway"; a game
later it paid {2}{R} to destroy its own Forgotten Cave from a window that listed only its own
two Caves. A plan that names a target the screen does not list is carried out by 0 (pass), not
by the nearest-looking row.
CROSS-CHECK: "Opponent life:" is printed on every decision. If it is HIGHER than when you last
looked, face damage is not a clock - they gain faster than you burn. Aim nothing at the face for the
rest of that game; spend every point on their creatures and planeswalkers.

=====================================================================
HOW THE DECK WINS - RUN THIS OFF YOUR LAND COUNT
=====================================================================
Play a land every turn, count the lands on YOUR battlefield line, take the first line you can afford.
LAND DESTRUCTION IS A TEMPO PLAY: it is worth a card while the opponent's battlefield shows FOUR OR
FEWER lands and is nearly worthless after that - spend it EARLY, never hold it for a better target.
  2 lands: Dwarven Blastminer, or Talisman of Impulse, or Pyrite Spellbomb.
  3 lands: STONE RAIN or MOLTEN RAIN on one of their lands. This is the play - do not cast a
           Spellbomb "first" and leave the land destruction in hand.
  4 lands: another Stone Rain / Molten Rain / Lay Waste, plus a Blastminer activation.
  5 lands: SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER. Four bodies in one card is your best turn in the deck.
  6+ lands: RORIX BLADEWING, or a land-destruction spell and a Blastminer activation in one turn.
WHICH LAND: prefer one whose name is not Plains, Island, Swamp, Mountain or Forest - Molten Rain
deals 2 extra damage for those, and they are what fixes the opponent's colours. Target rows read
"[land] [opponent's battlefield]"; a tapped land is a fine target.

=====================================================================
COMBAT
=====================================================================
RACE CHECK - do this BEFORE the attack floor, and only when your attack list holds exactly ONE
creature and that creature is Rorix Bladewing or Siege-Gang Commander.
Your one creature is also your only blocker, and Rorix does not come back untapped in time to
block on the turn he attacks. So compare two numbers you can read off the screen:
  THEIR turns to kill you  = your "Your life:" number divided by the total printed power of
                             their untapped creatures (a creature with vigilance attacks AND
                             blocks, so it counts here even while it is attacking).
  YOUR turns to kill them  = their "Opponent life:" number divided by your attacker's power.
If THEIR number is smaller than or equal to YOURS, you lose the race: answer "ATTACK: none" and
keep the body home to block. A 6/5 that blocks a 4/4 kills it and lives - the trade line on the
blockers screen will say so - and once their attacker is dead the race restarts in your favour.
At 4 life against a 4/4 with vigilance, with the opponent on 15, this deck sent Rorix for 6 on
each of two turns and died on the second; holding Rorix home kills the 4/4 for free and the game
is still winnable.
ONE CARVE-OUT, and only this one: if their untapped creatures' total printed power is more than
your life PLUS your one creature's toughness, holding it home does not change the turn you die -
one blocker stops one attacker and the rest still connect. There is nothing to hold for, so send
it and take the damage you were taking anyway. At 11 life against twenty-two untapped fliers
totalling 89 power, keeping Rorix home saves 4 damage out of 89; that is not a race, it is a
formality.
Only when you WIN the race, or when their untapped creatures total 0 power, or under the carve-out
above, go on to the floor.

WALL CHECK, BEFORE THE FLOOR: their battlefield line names PERIMETER CAPTAIN or PRIDE
GUARDIAN. Their tags say what a block costs you - "(neither dies (blocking trigger: they may
gain 2))" - and every one of your attackers they block is life for them: this deck sent the
Commander and two Goblins into a Captain and a Guardian and watched 21 become 30 in one
combat, with Exquisite Blood already on their line. "ATTACK: none" while either name is on
that line; they die to a Spellbomb, a Goblin sacrifice or a Starstorm first, and THEN the
floor applies.
ATTACK FLOOR - read the "Your creatures that can attack:" list, then take the first case that
applies. FIRST: if Dwarven Blastminer is the ONLY creature on that list, answer "ATTACK: none" -
always, and stop reading. He is worth {2}{R} of land destruction every turn he stays untapped, and
one point of chip damage is worth nothing to this deck. SECOND: with any other creature listed, send
EVERY creature listed except Dwarven Blastminer. That list has already dropped everything that cannot
legally attack, so do not re-check summoning sickness or tapped status yourself. Otherwise answer
"ATTACK: none" only when every listed attacker would die to a blocker and kill nothing - not to
"hold blockers", and not because their creatures are marked "[tapped - cannot attack or block this
turn]" (they untap for their turn).
THE BLOCKER TAG, IN EITHER FORM, INSIDE THE FLOOR. On a narrow board an attacker's tag lists
their blockers one by one - "[their untapped blockers: Briarbridge Tracker (3/4) (your attacker
dies, their blocker lives); Sigarda ... ; Intrepid Adversary (4/2) (both die (lifelink: they
gain 4))]"; on a wide board it collapses to "they have N untapped creatures that could block
this one, biggest X (...)", sometimes with "- GANG BLOCK: their 2 biggest together deal 5,
enough to kill this attacker; each result above is a LONE blocker only". COUNT THE BLOCKERS THE
TAG NAMES, in either form - a name-by-name list of three is N = 3. Each parenthesis prices ONE
blocker only; the GANG BLOCK clause prices what actually happens: any number of them may block
that attacker together, and your creature's toughness is measured against the SUM. This deck
sent its Commander into a tag naming THREE blockers, two of them "(your attacker dies)", at 13
life, and lost it for nothing - a "(both die)" against a lifelink blocker that gains them four
is not a trade for your only Commander either.
  - The tag prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause, or names 2 or more
    blockers:
    that creature stays home. A GOBLIN token goes anyway - it costs you nothing and Siege-Gang
    makes more. RORIX goes anyway ONLY when every blocker the tag names is grounded and Rorix
    flies over all of them; SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER never goes on such a tag, because its sacrifice
    ability needs it alive.
  - N is 1 and no GANG BLOCK clause: the parenthesis is the whole story; use it.
"But the biggest one dies to me" is the sentence the clause exists to refuse - they do not have
to block with the biggest one alone. This deck sent Rorix past a "they have 5 untapped creatures"
collapse and survived only because he flies.
ONE EXCEPTION INSIDE THE FLOOR: if their untapped creatures' printed power totals your life or more,
send only Rorix this turn and keep the Goblins home.
BLOCKING - take any block where your creature kills theirs and lives. Take a "(both die)" block
with a GOBLIN token, always. Do NOT take a "(both die)" block with SIEGE-GANG COMMANDER or RORIX
BLADEWING: those are cards, they are the only bodies this deck has, and Siege-Gang's sacrifice
ability needs Siege-Gang alive. Trading either one for a 2/2 token the opponent will replace is
not an even trade even though the parenthesis says both creatures die. Send a Goblin instead, or
take the damage while the combat header's "you would be at N" is above 5.
THE ONE "(both die)" THE COMMANDER DOES TAKE: the attacker is THRABEN DOOMSAYER or BLOODLINE
KEEPER - a token-MAKER. That creature is their engine and it is attacking into you; the
Commander for the maker is the trade of the game, and this deck made it, correctly, at 20
life against a Doomsayer that had already made nineteen Humans.
A GANG BLOCK THAT KILLS THE ATTACKER IS A DIFFERENT QUESTION FROM DAMAGE. The header prints
it when it exists: "[GANG BLOCK: your 3 biggest together deal 4, enough to kill this
attacker; ...]". When that attacker is a DRAW PUNISHER named on the "DRAW PUNISHERS" line,
or the only creature hitting you, and your life minus its "deals N" is 5 or less, put every
blocker the clause counts on it - the Goblins you lose are the draw steps you keep. At 7
life under Fate Unraveler and Ob Nixilis, with "your 3 biggest together deal 4, enough to
kill this attacker" on the screen, this deck answered "BLOCKS: none", went to 4, and died in
its next draw step to the Unraveler it could have killed.
ONE BLOCKER STOPS THE WHOLE OF ONE ATTACKER'S DAMAGE. A second and a third of yours on the same
attacker stop nothing more - they only die with the first. At 13 life this deck put all three
Goblins in front of a single 3/4 and lost all three to save 3 life it did not need (the header
read "you would be at 10 - NOT lethal"); those three Goblins were 6 Siege-Gang damage, two
planeswalker kills. Read the header's "you would be at N" FIRST and write it down:
  - N above 5 and every row on that attacker reads "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)":
    "BLOCKS: none". A Goblin is worth more as 2 points of Siege-Gang damage than as 3 life.
  - N at 5 or below: ONE Goblin on the attacker with the largest "deals N"; a second Goblin only
    on a DIFFERENT attacker. Never two on one unless the row says "(both die)" for the pair.
WHEN THE PROMPT SAYS LETHAL, CHUMP THE BIGGEST ATTACKER YOU ARE ALLOWED TO BLOCK. The line
"Unblocked, these attackers deal up to N - you would be at ... - LETHAL if it all connects" means
the arithmetic, not the trade, is the whole question. Every attacker row prints "deals N".
FIRST narrow the list: an attacker row tagged "[NONE of your available blockers can block this
attacker ...]", or one that is missing from your blocker's own "may block" line, is not on the
menu at all - its damage is going to happen whatever you do, so subtract it and stop thinking
about it. Among what is LEFT, put your blocker in front of the LARGEST "deals N", even when the trade line says "your blocker dies, attacker lives" - subtracting
their biggest number from the incoming total is the only thing that decides whether you are alive
next turn. At 3 life against a 4 and a 2, blocking the 4 leaves you at 1 and blocking the 2 leaves
you dead; this deck blocked the 2.
AND: the "may block" list is the LEGALITY list. If an attacker is named on your blocker's
"may block" line, you CAN block it - "my 1/1 cannot block their 4/4" is not a rule, it is a
sentence this deck told itself on the turn it died.

=====================================================================
MULLIGAN
=====================================================================
THE ODDS, ONCE: twenty-four lands in sixty, every one of them making {R} - so colour is never
a reason to ship, and the only question at every look is "does THIS hand act by turn three,
and is a fresh hand one card smaller more likely to?". A fresh seven from this deck holds two
or more lands about six times in seven; each mulligan hands you those same odds with one card
fewer, so a ship buys a look, never a fix.
READ TWO LINES BEFORE YOU ANSWER. The header "counted by the engine: N lands, M spells" is the
land count - never recount. The line "Playing every land in this hand would cover the cost
of: ..." is the engine's own check of your mana against your spells: if it names even one
card, this hand ACTS - this deck kept a one-land seven whose coverage line named exactly one
card and won that game. If it reads "would not cover any spell in it", nothing in the hand
happens until you draw, and that is the shape where a smaller hand can be the better one: a
one-land seven of Starstorm, Starstorm, Stone Rain, Stone Rain, Lay Waste, Rorix and a
Mountain was shipped this corpus, and the six it bought was four Mountains and two spells - a
hand that acted on turn three.
AT EACH LOOK, price it: the ask prints "(keeping N)". A hand with two or more lands is a keep
at every N. A one-lander is a keep when the coverage line names a card, and a ship when it
does not - at (keeping 6) or (keeping 5) the odds of two lands in the next look are the same
six-in-seven, and at (keeping 4) or lower even a one-lander that acts by turn three beats the
look you would buy. Zero lands, or all lands, ships at any N above 4.
THE BOTTOMING ASK IS WHERE THE HAND IS BUILT. Read "You will be left with an N-card hand" and
keep, in this PREFERENCE ORDER, which is satisfiable at any N because it is an order and not a
set of floors: the lands that cover the cheapest spells you keep (three if N allows it, two at
N of 4 or less); then the one Starstorm if the hand has one - the only card here that answers a
board; then your cheapest spells; then the rest by cost. What is left over goes under - a
fifth Mountain before a Molten Rain, a second Rorix before a first Stone Rain. Whichever card
is last in that order is the one that goes, even when it is a land or the Starstorm.

=====================================================================
DECIDING SITUATIONS (recognise -> do)
=====================================================================
- Their battlefield line prints "[planeswalker] [counters: Nx loyalty]" and a damage spell on
  your menu deals N or more (Hammer of Bogardan 3, Pyrite Spellbomb 2, Spark Spray 1) -> cast it
  at the planeswalker, before any creature you could cast this turn.
- "Cast Talisman of Impulse" is listed, nothing above entry 5 of the cast order matched, and
  Siege-Gang Commander or Rorix Bladewing is in your hand -> cast the Talisman. "Hold mana for
  their turn" is not a play this deck has: nothing in the sixty but Starstorm is an instant.
  Two such holds put the Commander down on turn 17 instead of 13, into a board that had become
  unbeatable in between.
- A damage spell's target menu lists "Yourself (player, life N)" -> never. The opponent, or a
  creature or planeswalker of theirs. This deck answered "Yourself" to a Hammer of Bogardan at
  9 life under a plan that read "the game is lost".
- There is no "the game is lost". A PLAN line names the NEXT ACTION - a land to destroy, a
  creature to kill, a body to add - and a plan with no action in it is handed back to you every
  window until the game ends: this deck carried "The game is lost ... passing is the only legal
  action" through forty-eight windows, aimed a Hammer at its own face and cast Starstorm at X=12
  into a board with no creatures under that sentence.
- ANY draw is on the menu (a "cycling" row, Forgotten Cave, Pyrite Spellbomb's "Draw 1") and
  their battlefield line names TWO OR MORE permanents whose text damages you when you draw
  (Underworld Dreams, Fate Unraveler, Ob Nixilis) -> do not draw. Pass.
- One such permanent and your life is 5 or less -> do not draw. Pass.
- "cycling with Starstorm" is offered and the opponent battlefield line names one or more creatures
  -> pass, or cycle a Forgotten Cave instead.
- "cycling with Starstorm" is offered, that line reads "of which 0 are creatures", and no Forgotten
  Cave is in your hand -> cycle the Starstorm.
- "cycling with Lay Waste" is offered and you control 7 or fewer lands -> pass, take your land drop,
  and cast Lay Waste at one of their lands this turn or next.
- It is your Upkeep and your land drop is unused -> CHOICE: 0 (pass); act after the land drop.
- "Choose an option for Spark Spray:" appears and its target list holds an opponent creature ->
  "Cast Card Normally", then aim at that creature.
- You control 3 lands and their board shows 3 or 4 lands -> Stone Rain or Molten Rain, now.
- Their board shows 4 or fewer lands, a land-destruction spell is listed, and nothing above it
  in the cast order is castable THIS turn (a body you cannot afford yet is not castable) ->
  cast it now. "Hold mana for Rorix next turn" left five mana unspent against three opposing
  lands, twice in one game, and those lands cast the draw engines that ended it.
- "Put a card into hand with Hammer of Bogardan" is listed in YOUR upkeep and Siege-Gang
  Commander or Rorix Bladewing is in "Your hand" with the lands to cast it this turn -> 0
  (pass); cast the body in main phase 1.
- Their battlefield line names Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper beside an Intruder Alarm
  and Starstorm is castable -> Starstorm FIRST this turn, at the X whose THEIRS list names the
  maker; land destruction after, with what is left. No Starstorm: Hammer of Bogardan (3) or a
  Spellbomb (2) at the Doomsayer (2/2) THIS window, before Siege-Gang, before Rorix.
- The Starstorm cast row reads "even at X=N NOTHING on the board dies" or "affords only X=0"
  -> "Cast nothing right now". On a "Choose an option for Starstorm" menu you did not mean
  to reach -> "Decline - do nothing".
- The cast row's "At X=N ... YOURS:" names Siege-Gang Commander or Rorix at every X that
  kills something of theirs -> do not answer "Cast Card Normally" at all; the X menu has no
  exit.
- A "Deal N damage with <Siege-Gang Commander / Pyrite Spellbomb> targeting <creature>" row
  and that creature's line shows toughness above N (or "[counters: Mx loyalty]" above N) ->
  not that row; ability rows print no DIES/SURVIVES, you read the toughness yourself.
- The blockers header prints "GANG BLOCK: your N biggest together deal X, enough to kill this
  attacker" on a creature named on the "DRAW PUNISHERS" line, and your life minus its "deals
  N" is 5 or less -> all N blockers on it.
- Their battlefield line names Perimeter Captain or Pride Guardian -> "ATTACK: none"; kill
  them with a Spellbomb, a Goblin sacrifice or a Starstorm first.
- The attacker on the blockers screen is Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper and the
  Commander's row reads "(both die)" -> take it; a maker for the Commander is the trade.
- "CAUTION - some usable mana sources DAMAGE YOU" is on the screen and your life is 3 or
  less -> nothing that needs a Talisman to pay for it.
- Every row on a damage or destroy window is tagged "[your battlefield]" -> CHOICE: 0 (pass).
  Never a row that names your own permanent after "targeting".
- The Starstorm you are about to cast leaves alive creatures whose power still totals your
  life or more -> it is not survival; keep your blocker, do not cast it.
- Their board shows a planeswalker -> every damage spell points at it until it is gone.
- Their board shows two or more creatures that are NOT marked "[defender]", Starstorm is in hand,
  and NEITHER Rorix Bladewing NOR Siege-Gang Commander is on your battlefield -> cast Starstorm,
  and cast it BEFORE you cast Siege-Gang Commander. If Rorix or Siege-Gang IS already out, or
  every creature the annotation kills of theirs is "[defender]", hold the card (rule #2, step 0)
  - this deck held it correctly through five such windows in one game while Rorix raced the
  opponent from 20 to dead.
- The "{X pricing:" annotation's YOURS list at the X you were going to pick names Rorix Bladewing
  or Siege-Gang Commander -> do not cast Starstorm at all this window, whatever it kills of theirs.
- The only creature the annotation kills of theirs is marked "[defender]" -> do not cast Starstorm.
- You are on the "Announce the value of X" menu -> take the row with the LARGEST "kills THEIRS"
  list whose "YOURS:" reads "none", then walk DOWN to the bottom of any run of rows reading
  "same kills as X=N" and answer THAT row. Option 1 is the LARGEST X, never X=1.
- A row below the one you were about to answer reads "same kills as X=N, for 1 less mana" ->
  answer the lower row. Same board, one more mana in your pool.
- Two affordable X values both read "YOURS: none" and the bigger one kills more -> take the
  bigger one. Saving mana you have nothing to spend on is not a saving.
- The only X you can afford is 0, or the pricing says "there is no creature on the battlefield for
  it to damage" -> do not cast Starstorm at all. Cycle it if their creature count is 0, otherwise
  hold it.
- "Choose an option for Starstorm:" is on the screen and you wanted to spend the card rather than
  use its effect -> answer the "cycling" row, never "Cast Card Normally".
- Your attack list holds exactly ONE creature -> run the RACE CHECK in COMBAT before you send it.
- The blockers prompt says LETHAL -> your blocker goes in front of the attacker with the largest
  "deals N", trade line notwithstanding.
- You control 5 lands and Siege-Gang Commander is in hand -> cast it, and sacrifice no Goblin while
  the opponent is above 6 life.
- Rorix Bladewing is castable -> cast him and attack with him the same turn.
- Several casts are listed at once -> answer with the highest entry in THE WHOLE CAST ORDER that
  appears in your menu.
- An attacker's tag reads "they have N untapped creatures that could block this one" with N of 2
  or more, or prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause -> a Goblin goes
  anyway; Rorix goes only if every blocker named is grounded; Siege-Gang stays home.
- The mulligan header's coverage line names even one card -> keep, whatever the land count;
  two or more lands -> keep at any "(keeping N)"; one land and "would not cover any spell" ->
  ship at (keeping 6) or (keeping 5), keep at (keeping 4) or lower.
- You kept and the game asks which cards to bottom -> build the N in the MULLIGAN order: the
  lands that cover your cheapest spells, the Starstorm, the cheapest spells, the rest by cost;
  the last card in that order is the one that goes under.
- "Opponent life:" went UP since your last decision -> stop aiming damage at the face for the rest
  of the game; kill creatures and planeswalkers only.
- A damage spell's only targets are "the opponent, you" and the opponent is above 6 life -> do
  NOT fire it at the face. Hold the card. Nine face-burn casts in one game moved an opponent
  from 25 to 24 while they gained back more than they lost - that is not a clock, it is a card
  spent. Cast it the turn a creature or a planeswalker appears on their side, or the turn their
  life is 6 or less. The one exception is the LETHAL-THIS-TURN chain in rule #2.
- Dwarven Blastminer appears in "Your creatures that can attack:" -> leave him out of the ATTACK
  line. Spend {2}{R} destroying a land only while their board shows FOUR OR FEWER lands and your
  life is above 8; otherwise hold the mana.
- Your life is 8 or less -> the land plan is over for this game. Damage at their creatures, a
  body, or pass. Never another land.
