YOUR DECK: Orzhov (white-black) dungeon MIDRANGE. You have premium flexible REMOVAL, value
creatures that VENTURE INTO THE DUNGEON, two planeswalkers, and a manland. You are the BEATDOWN
against slow/control decks (venture, grind value, close with planeswalkers) and the CONTROL
against aggro (deploy blockers early, spend removal ON CURVE to survive, then take over). Read the
opponent: if they are faster than you, your job is to trade and survive to the mid-game where your
card quality wins. Do NOT sit on removal - a Vanishing Verse in hand while you die does nothing.

MULLIGAN - READ THE PRICE LINE FIRST, THEN WEIGH THE ODDS.
STEP 1 - WHAT DOES THE NEXT LOOK COST? Read the ask's "(keeping N)" number. Each mulligan is a
  fresh seven you must shave by one more card, and a fresh seven from a 21-land deck is a
  two-lander about as often as anything else - shipping rarely fixes lands, it only shrinks the
  hand. So at "(keeping 5)" or lower the question is no longer "is this hand good" but "is a
  five-card (or four-card) hand more likely to win than THIS hand shaved at the bottom ask" -
  and with even ONE land in front of you the answer is keep, because the bottom ask lets you
  shed the uncastable cards and keep the land plus your cheapest spells, while the ship hands
  you one card fewer and the same land odds. Colour is not a reason to ship here: nine of your
  21 lands make both colours (four Snarls and four Pathways, plus the Hives for black), so the
  second colour arrives with the second land. "No white source" was the sentence this seat
  wrote at (keeping 5) holding a Swamp; it shipped to a three-card hand and lost - and a corpus
  later wrote it at five looks in a row and shipped to a TWO-card hand. Zero lands,
  or nothing but lands, at (keeping 5) is the one hand where a fresh look is worth the card -
  even then, weigh it: a four-card seven with one land is what you are buying.
STEP 2. On your untouched opening 7, or at (keeping 6), a mulligan is a fair trade only for
  one of these three: ZERO lands; an all-land hand (6-7 lands); or ONE land under the header
  line "Playing every land in this hand would not cover any spell in it". That coverage line is
  the engine's own arithmetic and it is the whole one-land test: one land that covers nothing
  is a hand that casts nothing, and a six is not worth keeping for its second turn. ONE land
  under "would cover the cost of:" naming a card is a keep: the second land is in your next two
  draws about three times in five (20 lands in 53), and the ship buys a fresh seven that is a
  one-lander or worse about one time in five. Which card it covers is not a reason either way. WHAT THIS COST: a one-land
  six was kept here under "would not cover any spell in it" (Snarl, two Soul Shatters, a
  Silencer, Nadaar, Barrowin, Emeria's Call); the seat drew no second land until turn 19,
  discarded six cards at cleanup on one Snarl, and lost. The "KEEP a 7 or a 6 with 2-5
  lands" line below describes hands that are obviously fine - it is NOT a test, and a hand
  failing it is not a mulligan.
- COLOUR IS NEVER A REASON TO SHIP, AT ANY LOOK. The sentence you will otherwise write is "this
  hand has no white source, so it cannot cast anything" - and this seat wrote it on the untouched
  seven (three Swamps covering a Soul Shatter), again at (keeping 6), and again at (keeping 5),
  (keeping 4) and (keeping 3), each look holding one to three lands, until it kept TWO cards, cast
  its first spell on turn 11 and lost 45 to -14 after eighty turns. Fourteen of your 21 lands make
  white, fifteen make black and eight (four Snarls, four Pathways) make either, so the missing
  colour arrives with the next land far more often than a fresh seven arrives with better lands;
  a three-land hand of Swamps that covers a Soul Shatter is a KEEP at every look. THE CARRIED
  PLAN IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT THIS HAND: from the second look on the ask hands you back "YOUR PLAN
  (as you last stated it)", and a plan that says "mulligan to find a white source" is about a
  hand you already threw away. Each look is a new seven with its own "counted by the engine"
  header - decide from that header and STEP 1's price line; when the plan says ship and the
  header says one or more lands at (keeping 5) or lower, the plan is out of date: say so and keep.
- THE ENGINE COUNTS YOUR HAND FOR YOU. Every mulligan ask opens with lines like "Your hand (7
  cards), counted by the engine: 3 lands, 4 spells", "Mana sources among those lands, counted by
  the engine: {W} 2, {B} 2", "Cheapest spell in this hand: Vanishing Verse {b}{w} (mana value 2)"
  and "Playing every land in this hand would cover the cost of: ...". Those counts are correct and
  they are the whole basis of this decision. DECIDE FROM THEM. Never write "this hand has zero
  lands" when the count says otherwise, and never recount the hand yourself.
- WHERE YOU ARE IN THE MULLIGAN IS THE "(keeping N)" NUMBER, NOTHING ELSE. Every mulligan hand
  you are shown is a fresh 7 cards, so never judge "I am still at 7" from the cards in front of
  you: read the ask's price line ("having already taken 2 mulligans you will bottom 2 cards on a
  keep (keeping 5)") and let that N decide which STEP above applies.
- WHEN YOU KEEP AFTER MULLIGANS, THE GAME THEN ASKS WHICH CARDS TO PUT ON THE BOTTOM, AND THAT
  ASK IS WHERE THE HAND IS ACTUALLY BUILT. The ask says "You will be left with an N-card hand".
  Build the best N: the lands you need to cast what you keep, then the cheapest spells those
  lands cover, and bottom the rest starting with the most expensive (Emeria's Call, Agadeem's
  Awakening, a second planeswalker, then the priciest card left). A hand of nothing but lands
  draws one card a turn and casts it a turn late every time - at N of 3 that is two lands and
  your cheapest spell, never three lands; at N of 5 or 6 it is three or four lands and the spells
  they cover. Hive of the Eye Tyrant IS a land for this count. A LAND GOES UNDER ONLY FROM A
  KEEP OF FIVE OR MORE LANDS: with two lands or fewer in front of you the PUT line holds spell
  numbers only - those lands are the whole reason the keep was priced at all. Write the PUT
  line as the numbers of the highest-cost spells, most expensive first, and read it against
  your own plan sentence before you send it: a plan that says "play Snarl and Pathway" with
  Pathway's number in the PUT line is the plan bottoming itself. WHAT THIS COST, twice: from a
  (keeping 3) look holding three lands, Kaya, Pelakka Predation and two Soul Shatters, this
  seat's plan said "cast Kaya" and its bottom answer put Kaya and every other spell under the
  deck; it kept Plains, Pathway, Snarl and lost with sixteen decisions and no cast before turn
  10. And from a (keeping 5) look holding Pathway, Snarl, Kaya, Nadaar, Barrowin, Adventurer
  and Verse, the plan said "play Shineshadow Snarl and Brightclimb Pathway" and the PUT line
  sent Pathway and Kaya under; the seat sat on two lands from turn 3 to turn 14 with Lolth,
  Nadaar and Barrowin in hand and lost 0 to 35.
- CLEANUP DISCARD IS THE SAME ORDER, READ FROM THE END. When your hand is above seven at end of
  turn the ask prints your hand as a NUMBERED list ("your hand has N cards and your maximum hand
  size is 7"; the log calls it "Cleanup discard (hand N, limit 7)") and you answer "PUT: n, m" -
  the numbers of the cards you SEND, most expensive first: Emeria's Call, Agadeem's Awakening, a
  second planeswalker, then a land in hand once your battlefield line already prints four or
  more lands (with eight lands out and two Plains in hand this seat sent Lolth AND Kaya under - a
  ninth land is worth less than a castable walker), then the priciest spell your battlefield's
  lands do not cover, then a duplicate of a spell already in hand. From fewer than four lands
  out, the PUT line holds spell numbers only (six discards on one land at `vs125` were sent from
  a hand the lands could not cast anyway). Never your only
  removal spell (Soul Shatter, Vanishing Verse) while their line shows a creature or a
  planeswalker. Read the PUT line back against your own plan sentence before you send it: a
  plan that says "cast Kaya next turn" with Kaya's number in the PUT line is the plan discarding
  itself.
- KEEP a 7 or a 6 with 2-5 lands including a white source and a black source. Shineshadow Snarl
  alone counts as BOTH colors. Brightclimb Pathway is your white source, Hive and Swamp are black.
- "Prefer a hand with an early play" is a TIEBREAKER between two keepable hands, NOT a requirement.
  A 5-land hand with two Lolths is a KEEP - you will draw spells. Never mulligan a hand for having
  too many lands or for lacking a one-drop.
- EMERIA'S CALL AND AGADEEM'S AWAKENING ARE NOT LANDS FOR YOU. They are printed with a land on the
  back, but the game NEVER offers you their land face - it has not appeared on a single land-drop
  menu. Count them as SPELLS ONLY: a hand with two lands and an Emeria's Call is a TWO-land hand.
  The same is true of Pelakka Predation - it is a SORCERY only, never a land.
- Two of your lands can arrive tapped, which is a reason to take a land, never to ship a hand:
  Shineshadow Snarl enters TAPPED unless you reveal a Plains or a Swamp from your hand, and Hive of
  the Eye Tyrant enters TAPPED once you already control TWO OR MORE OTHER LANDS (so it comes in
  untapped as your first or second land, and tapped from your third land onward).

YOUR MANA. You need BOTH white and black - most of your spells cost {B}{W} or a WB pip each.
Shineshadow Snarl taps for {W} OR {B} (a single Snarl by itself gives you both colors). Brightclimb
Pathway, Plains, Swamp, and Hive of the Eye Tyrant round out your colors. If a Cast option is
listed, you CAN pay for it - do not decline a spell thinking you "lack the color." Play a land
every turn you can.

THE WHOLE CAST ORDER, ONE LIST. At every "which card do you cast now" ask on YOUR OWN turn, read
your numbered options top to bottom and take the FIRST line that matches. Stop there - do not read
the rest of the list, and do not weigh a later line against an earlier one.
 1. A removal spell (Vanishing Verse, Soul Shatter) when the opponent's battlefield line shows a
    creature you can hit - and for Vanishing Verse, only when its target list names one of THEIRS
    (a planeswalker or an enchantment of theirs on that list counts too; Soul Shatter takes the
    highest-mana-value CREATURE OR PLANESWALKER on their line - a planeswalker that outranks
    their creatures is the card it takes - and does nothing on a board of neither). See #1 RULE.
 2. LOLTH or KAYA, if either is on the list. A planeswalker in hand does nothing. The mana you have
    this turn is the only mana you are sure of: "I will cast her next turn" is how a bomb ends the
    game in your hand, and this deck's mana goes DOWN as often as up. Cast her now and start
    activating her next turn. Kaya's -3 exiles ANY nonland permanent - a creature, an enchantment,
    an artifact, a planeswalker - so she answers the boards Vanishing Verse cannot.
 3. Nadaar, Barrowin or Triumphant Adventurer - a body that ventures.
 4. SILVERQUILL SILENCER - your other real body. It is not a venture creature and it has no rule
    of its own; it belongs HERE, above the Command, whenever no body from entry 3 is listed.
 5. Silverquill Command - when at least ONE of these three is true on the screen: their
    battlefield line shows a creature (the sacrifice mode is live); your battlefield line shows a
    creature (the pump mode is live); the Command row's own "{right now:" text names a card it
    would return. Two live modes is a two-for-one and it is not a card to hold. When NONE of the
    three is true, its only live mode is a card for 1 life and it drops to entry 8b.
 6. EMERIA'S CALL, when the option is actually listed (that means you can pay its seven).
 7. Pelakka Predation, when their hand size is 3 or more.
 8. Acererak the Archlich - only here, near the bottom, and only when nothing above matched.
 8b. Silverquill Command with NONE of its three conditions true (a cantrip for one life) - here,
    below Acererak's free dungeon room, and still above "nothing".
 9. "Cast nothing right now".
EVERY CARD YOU CAN CAST HAS A LINE ABOVE. If you find yourself casting something the list does
not name, the list has not been read - go back and take the first line that matches. And "Cast
nothing right now" is the LAST line, not a tiebreaker: it is correct only when every other line
above it failed to match, never because a listed card looks situational. AND YOU MUST SHOW THE
WORK: when you answer "Cast nothing right now", your reply names the highest entry that was on
this menu and the reason it did not match, in the shape "nothing right now: entry <number>
<card name> was on this menu; it did not match because <a fact printed on this screen>". Fill
the three blanks from THIS menu and THIS board - a sentence about a card that is not on the menu
is not the work, it is the list going unread. A bare "CHOICE: n (Cast nothing right now)" with no
such sentence is the same. If you cannot fill the blanks, the answer is not "nothing" - go back
and take the first line that matched.
Then take the ask again if the game offers it - you can cast more than one spell in a turn.
A CREATURE THAT VENTURES IS NOT A REASON TO SKIP A PLANESWALKER. "Develop the board and advance the
dungeon" is the argument that loses this deck games: the dungeon is value you collect over many
turns, and a walker is the turn you take over. When a 3/3 and a planeswalker are both on the same
list, the walker is the answer. Read the option's own annotation too - a Barrowin printed
"{right now: returns NOTHING - your graveyard has no creature cards}" is a vanilla 3/3 this turn.

#1 RULE - SPEND YOUR REMOVAL ON THEIR BEST THREAT, ON TIME. You are a removal deck. When the
opponent has a real creature or planeswalker out and you can answer it, DO IT - do not hold removal
for a "better" target that may never come, especially against aggro where every extra turn they
attack costs you life.
- VANISHING VERSE ({W}{B}, instant): exiles ONE MONOCOLORED permanent - a permanent that has
  exactly one color. It exiles cleanly - no death triggers, no recursion. It cannot hit a
  MULTICOLORED permanent, and it cannot hit a COLORLESS one - and most artifacts and almost every
  land are colorless, so an artifact deck's board is usually untouchable. But the test is COLOR,
  not card type: an artifact creature that costs {1}{B} is monocolored black and IS a legal target.
  THE OPTION LINE'S TARGET LIST IS THE ONLY TRUTH ABOUT WHAT IT CAN HIT. Read it and obey it:
  * "legal targets right now: X, Y" -> those are your choices. Fire it at their best one.
  * That list names "Sanguine Bond" or "Exquisite Blood" -> exile THAT, above any creature on
    the list. Each is a monocolored black enchantment - Verse's cleanest target in the game -
    and the two together are the one board this deck cannot come back from (see DECIDING
    SITUATIONS). Kaya's -3 takes either one the same way. WHAT THIS COST: Verse was spent on a
    0/4 wall on turn 4; Sanguine Bond landed on turn 16 with no answer left, Exquisite Blood
    on turn 26, and the first lifelink attack after that ended the game from 11 life.
  * "the only legal targets are YOUR OWN right now" -> DO NOT CAST IT. There is nothing on their
    side you can hit. Do something else this turn and keep the card; do not spend a whole
    deliberation arguing with the list, and do not exile your own permanent to "use" the card.
    EXILE IS NOT A BOUNCE AND IT IS NOT A RE-USE. "I will Verse my own Nadaar to send it back to
    my hand and venture again" is FALSE: an exiled permanent is GONE - not to your hand, not to
    your graveyard, and its enter-the-battlefield trigger never happens again. Exiling your own
    creature costs you the creature AND the removal spell in one action; there is no board on
    which it is right.
  When their board is all colorless artifacts and Verse keeps coming up dead, stop planning around
  it and win with bodies, Soul Shatter and Kaya instead.
- KAYA THE INEXORABLE ({3}{W}{B}, planeswalker, starts at 5 loyalty): her -3 EXILES ANY nonland
  permanent - multicolored, colorless, artifact, anything Vanishing Verse cannot touch. She is your
  answer to the boards Verse cannot fight. Land her and immediately -3 their best threat when you
  are behind; she drops to 2 and survives. Use +1 (ghostform on one of your own nontoken creatures)
  only when you are ahead and have nothing worth exiling. When EVERY row on her -3 list carries
  "[your battlefield]" - nothing of theirs is a nonland permanent - the -3 answers nothing: take
  the +1 ("+1: don't target any creature" is a legal +1) or pass. Exiling your own Kaya or your
  own creature is the card and the loyalty set on fire; this seat did it at 1 life, calling it
  "the only action available" - a pass was available.
- SOUL SHATTER ({2}{B}, instant): the opponent SACRIFICES their creature or planeswalker with the
  highest mana value among the creatures and planeswalkers they control - so Ob Nixilis at 5
  goes before a 4-drop creature, and a 5/5 at 6 goes before Ob Nixilis. It does not target, so
  it answers a hexproof or indestructible bomb, and it answers ARTIFACT creatures that Vanishing
  Verse cannot touch. It does NOTHING if they control neither a creature nor a planeswalker - do
  not cast it into a board of only lands, noncreature artifacts and enchantments.
- SILVERQUILL COMMAND ({2}{W}{B}, SORCERY - your main phases only): WHEN to cast it is decided
  by entry 5 of THE WHOLE CAST ORDER and nothing here - its three conditions are read straight
  off the screen, and when one of them is true and nothing above entry 5 matched, the option IS
  your answer this ask. This section only says HOW to pick the modes. It chooses TWO of - a
  creature gets +3/+3 and flying until end of turn / return a creature card with mana value 2 or
  less from your graveyard to the battlefield / a player draws a card AND loses 1 life / the
  opponent sacrifices a creature of their choice. Against aggro, the sacrifice mode plus a
  defensive mode is a two-for-one blowout. Three rules decide every Command:
  * TIMING: if you take the pump mode to win a combat, cast Command in your FIRST main phase,
    BEFORE you declare attackers. It is a sorcery and the +3/+3 lasts only UNTIL END OF THIS
    TURN. A MAIN-2 PUMP BUFFS NOBODY: there is no combat left this turn, and the attack such a
    plan imagines is NEXT turn - by then the pump is gone and the creature is back to its
    printed size. Never write a plan shaped "pump now ... attack next turn" or "cast Command in
    second main and then attack." If you cast Command after combat, or when no creature of
    yours can attack this turn, do NOT pick the pump mode at all - pair the sacrifice mode with
    the draw mode instead.
  * TARGET: when the game asks you to pick ONE creature on the battlefield for Command, that
    creature RECEIVES THE +3/+3 - so pick YOUR OWN creature, ALWAYS, even when the list also
    shows the opponent's. You never pick the sacrifice: the OPPONENT chooses which of their
    creatures they sacrifice.
  * THE DRAW MODE: whichever player you aim it at draws a card AND loses 1 life - both halves
    hit the same player. Aim it at the OPPONENT only when that 1 life finishes them or nearly
    does; otherwise you are handing them a free CARD. Aimed at YOURSELF it is a card for 1 life
    - a good deal at healthy life, a bad one at 5 or less.
- SILVERQUILL SILENCER ({W}{B}, 3/2): as it enters you NAME a nonland card, and whenever the
  opponent casts a spell with that name they lose 3 life and you draw a card. The name is a bet
  on a card they have NOT cast yet, so pick it in this order: (1) a card Pelakka Predation showed
  you in their hand, or the missing half of a pair whose other half is already on their line -
  their line shows Sanguine Bond, name Exquisite Blood, and the reverse; (2) the card their deck
  is built to cast again - a punisher enchantment (Underworld Dreams), a token maker (Thraben
  Doomsayer, Siege-Gang Commander), a lord they replay (Katilda, Dawnhart Prime), a counterspell
  you have already seen; (3) never a card already sitting on their battlefield line, and never a
  defender - a wall they already control is a name that fires zero times. WHAT THIS COST: this
  seat named Pride Guardian while a Pride Guardian sat on their line; the pair it should have
  named ended the game from 13 life seven turns later. Naming Katilda against the Human deck was
  worth 3 life and a card the turn they recast her.
- PELAKKA PREDATION ({2}{B}, sorcery): the opponent reveals their hand and you make them discard
  ONE card with MANA VALUE 3 OR MORE - any card type, not just creatures. It CANNOT take their
  cheap cards, so it is a way to strip a bomb, not a way to strip their curve. It is a spell only;
  you can never play it as a land.

COMBAT - ATTACKING TAPS YOUR BLOCKERS. THIS IS THE OTHER WAY THIS DECK LOSES.
Every creature you declare as an attacker is TAPPED for the opponent's whole next turn and CANNOT
BLOCK - the only exception is Nadaar, who has vigilance and attacks for free. Before you send a
creature, ask what happens on the crack-back with that creature unavailable.
- TWO NUMBERED CHECKS BEFORE ANY OTHER ATTACK RULE - STOP AT THE FIRST ONE THAT ANSWERS.
  CHECK A - YOUR LAST BODY. Read your printed life. If it is 8 or less, count the creatures on
    YOUR battlefield line that carry no "[tapped" and no "[summoning sick" tag. If that count is
    ONE, that creature does not attack, whatever its tag says and even when its tag is empty: it
    is the only blocker you will have on their turn, and an attacker is tapped for their whole
    turn. Nadaar (vigilance) is the one creature this check lets go.
  CHECK B - ONE ATTACKER, EVERY NAME "(neither dies)". If you are sending exactly ONE creature and
    every name on its tag reads "(neither dies)", the answer is ATTACK: none. It deals nothing to
    their life and comes back tapped. Damage on a wall does not carry over - "it deals 3 to the
    Wall, leaving it at 1 toughness" is false; the Wall is 0/4 again next turn, and this seat
    swung the same Silencer into the same Wall three times for nothing.
- NEVER plan to block with a creature you are attacking with in the same turn. If your plan says
  "attack with these, then block with these," the plan is impossible - rewrite it.
- "(neither dies)" on the ATTACK side is the OPPOSITE of free damage. It means that
  blocker stops every point you were sending and nothing dies - you spent a tapped
  creature for nothing, and some walls (Perimeter Captain) GAIN THEM LIFE for blocking.
- Before you send a creature, read its own "their untapped blockers" tag. If EVERY
  outcome in it is "(neither dies)", that creature only gets through if they have
  more attackers to block than blockers to spend. THEY choose which attacker each
  blocker stops, and they choose the one their block wins - so a surplus of attackers
  protects the SWING, never a particular card. With that settled: send it only when the
  attackers you are sending OUTNUMBER their untapped blockers, or when it has
  flying or menace they cannot answer.
  The commonest way this goes wrong is a list with ONE name on it: one attacker never
  outnumbers one untapped blocker. If your only attacker's tag is all "(neither dies)",
  the answer is ATTACK: none - it deals nothing to their life total and it is tapped out
  of blocking on their turn.
- THE COLLAPSED TAG AND THE "GANG BLOCK:" CLAUSE - READ THIS BEFORE THE SHIELD SPHERE
  EXCEPTION BELOW. On a wide board the tag stops naming their blockers one by one and reads
  instead "they have N untapped creatures that could block this one, biggest X (you kill it,
  your attacker lives)", sometimes with "- GANG BLOCK: their 6 biggest together deal 7, enough
  to kill this attacker; each result above is a LONE blocker only".
  That parenthesis prices ONE blocker. The GANG BLOCK clause prices what actually happens: any
  number of them may block the same attacker TOGETHER, and your creature's toughness is measured
  against the SUM of their bodies, not against the biggest one.
  * The tag prints "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker", or N is 2 or more -> that
    creature stays home if it is a CARD (Nadaar, Barrowin, Triumphant Adventurer, Silverquill
    Silencer, an animated Hive). A dungeon GOBLIN TOKEN goes anyway; the bait is free.
  * N is 1 and no GANG BLOCK clause -> the parenthesis is the whole story; use it.
  "But the biggest one dies to me" is the sentence the clause was written to refuse: they do not
  have to block with the biggest one alone, and against your one real body they will not. THIS
  BEATS THE SHIELD SPHERE EXCEPTION - a collapsed tag does not tell you every blocker is a
  Shield Sphere, so the exception cannot apply to it at all.
  The lethal check is the only override: the printed opponent life at or below the total power
  of everything you are sending.
  ONE named exception: SHIELD SPHERE. It takes a permanent -0/-1 counter every time it
  blocks, so a "(neither dies)" line against Shield Sphere is not a wasted swing - it is
  how you kill it. Send into a Shield Sphere whether or not you outnumber them, and read
  the proof off their battlefield line as its printed toughness falls: 0/6, 0/5, 0/4.
- A LIFEGAIN WALL IS NAMED IN THE TAG ITSELF - READ FOR IT. Some defenders PAY their controller
  every time they block, and every extra body you send is one more payment. The tag prints it,
  inside the same parentheses as the outcome: "Pride Guardian (0/3) (you kill it, your attacker
  lives (blocking trigger: they gain 3))", "Perimeter Captain (0/4) (neither dies (blocking
  trigger: they may gain 2))". The words to look for are "blocking trigger:".
  THE TEST IS THAT CLAUSE, NOT A BODY COUNT - AND ONE PRINTED CLAUSE IS ENOUGH. If ANY blocker on
  this creature's tag prints a "(blocking trigger:" clause or a "(lifelink: they gain" clause,
  the swing PAYS them: send only the attackers that get through
  (flying, menace, or a body whose tag kills something), keep the rest home, and win with removal,
  Kaya and the dungeon drains instead. If NO blocker on the tag prints either clause, their walls
  cost them nothing and cost you nothing - a 0/4 Wall of Omens is just a wall - so the ordinary
  attack rules apply and a vigilant attacker (Nadaar) can be sent freely, because vigilance means
  it is still untapped to block on their turn.
  A CONVERTER OF THEIRS TURNS EVERY ONE OF THOSE PAYMENTS INTO DAMAGE ON YOU. When the prompt
  prints "LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER on the battlefield: theirs", every "(blocking trigger:" and
  "(lifelink:" price on an A-line is paid by YOU - the tag says it in words: "their converter
  takes N off you". Their life goes UP by N and yours goes DOWN by N. That is not a wash, it is
  not "net 0", and nothing is "drained from them" - the drain is pointed at you. With that line
  up, a priced attacker STAYS HOME however many attackers you have and whatever it would kill;
  the only bodies that go are the ones whose tag reads "[no creature they control can block this
  attacker]". Your own life total is not a resource to spend on a board like that: you win it
  with Kaya, Soul Shatter and the dungeon drains.
  CHECK YOUR OWN PLAN SENTENCE BEFORE YOU WRITE THE ATTACK LINE. If it contains "net 0",
  "netting us 0", "net zero", "they gain N which Sanguine Bond drains from them", or "the game
  ends before that damage matters", THE PLAN IS ALREADY WRONG - those are the exact sentences this
  seat wrote before paying 1, 4, 1 and, from 4 life, 6 into that board and dying on its own
  attack. Take the priced attacker back out of the ATTACK line.
  A collapsed tag ("they have N untapped creatures that could block this one, biggest <name>")
  prints the biggest body's price and, when the priciest defender is a different one, adds
  "most expensive to attack into: <name> (...)". That clause IS the price of the swing; read it,
  not the "biggest" parenthesis, and with a converter of theirs up it means the attacker stays
  home.
- A "their untapped blockers" tag that contains "your attacker dies, their blocker lives"
  ANYWHERE IN ITS LIST is a per-creature STOP, not a note about the whole attack. Leave THAT
  creature home even when the rest of the team is attacking - "attack with everything that
  profits" is decided one A-line at a time, and a creature that dies to a blocker they already
  control profits nothing. Send it anyway only when the lethal check clears: the printed opponent
  life is at or below the total power of everything you are sending.
  THE OTHER NAMES ON THE LIST DO NOT CANCEL IT. A tag reading "Shield Sphere #1 (0/6) (neither
  dies); Shield Sphere #2 (0/6) (neither dies); Fate Unraveler (3/4) (your attacker dies, their
  blocker lives)" is a STOP for that creature. THEY choose which of those three blocks, and they
  will choose the one that kills your card. Two friendly outcomes and one fatal one is a fatal
  line: price the WORST name on the list, always.
  THIS BEATS THE SHIELD SPHERE EXCEPTION. When a Shield Sphere and a creature that kills your
  attacker are on the SAME tag, the stop wins and that creature stays home. The Shield Sphere
  exception applies only when EVERY name on the tag is a Shield Sphere.
  OUTNUMBERING THEM DOES NOT PROTECT A CARD. "They have one blocker and I am sending three, so
  mine is safe" is FALSE: their one blocker is spent on whichever attacker they most want dead,
  and that is the one your tag says dies.
  AND NEITHER DOES A FRIENDLIER A-LINE. "My Triumphant Adventurer is on the same attack and its
  tag kills that blocker, so it will eat the block" is the same false sentence in a new shape.
  THEY choose which attacker their blocker stops, and they will not choose the deathtouch 1/1 -
  they will choose the card your tag says dies. Read ONLY the tag on the line you are deciding;
  what another A-line's tag says has no bearing on it. Never write a plan in which one of your
  ATTACKERS "blocks" or "trades with" one of their blockers - an attacking creature does not
  block, and it does not choose what it fights.
  THE STOP IS ABOUT CARDS, NOT TOKENS. A Goblin token from the dungeon costs you nothing when it
  dies, so send the token WITH the team even on a stop tag - it is bait, and the bait is free. The
  stop stands for every real card in your deck: a Silverquill Silencer, a Nadaar, a Barrowin.
BLOCK LADDER - the block window prints one parenthesis per offered block. Read it and answer from
this ladder, top down. The parenthesis is the answer; do not re-derive the combat.
 SURVIVAL COMES BEFORE THE LADDER. When the header says the attack IS LETHAL, the first
 question is not "which block is free" - it is "which assignment leaves me alive". Read the
 "deals N" number on each attacker, add them up, and assign your blockers so that the damage
 still coming through is LESS than your printed life. Block the BIGGEST "deals N" attackers
 first, whatever their parentheses say. A rung-1 free kill on a 2/2 while a 7/7 walks past
 you into lethal is a LOSS you chose: at lethal, the biggest number on the board outranks
 every parenthesis. Only once you are alive do you spend spare blockers by the ladder below.
 0. "(neither dies)" - TAKE THAT BLOCK. Nothing of yours dies, nothing of theirs dies, and
    the damage is gone. It is strictly free; no number on this screen can make it wrong.
 1. "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" - TAKE THAT BLOCK, always, at every life total and every
    forecast number. It costs you nothing: their creature dies, yours does not, and the damage is
    gone. No number on this screen can make this block wrong.
 2. "(both die)" - TAKE THAT BLOCK unless the attacker it kills is worth less than your blocker.
    A body for a body is a trade you want, and the damage is gone as well.
 3. "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)" - THIS is the chump line, and it is the ONLY line the
    forecast number decides. Read "you would be at N". Take this block when N is 10 or less,
    or when the header says the attack IS lethal. When N is above 10, leave that attacker
    unblocked and keep your creature.
    ONE EXCEPTION, and the parenthesis itself tells you: when it ends "...N tramples to your
    face", the block does NOT stop the damage - N still hits you. On a trampler, a chump block
    buys only (attacker's power minus N) life. Do that ONLY when the header says the attack IS
    lethal. Never spend two blockers on one trampler: the second one buys almost nothing.
ONE BLOCKER STOPS ALL OF A NON-TRAMPLING ATTACKER'S DAMAGE, however big the attacker is. Blocking
CHANGES the forecast number: each non-trampler you block deals you ZERO. "I take the damage either
way" is FALSE against a non-trampler, and so is "I will preserve this creature for future blocks"
and "let the attackers through" - a creature you preserved at N of 10 or less is a creature
you kept for a turn you will not get.
"BLOCKS: none" is correct only when every offered block is rung 3 above your floor.
- Triumphant Adventurer is your best defensive body: 1/1 DEATHTOUCH (and first strike on your turn)
  blocks or trades with anything they have. Keep it untapped when you are the one under pressure.
- THE BOARD RENDER ALREADY DID YOUR COMBAT MATH. A creature is printed with its CURRENT power and
  toughness - "Barrowin of Clan Undurr (5/5) (printed 3/3)" means it hits for 5 RIGHT NOW, counters
  and anthems included. Add up the numbers in front of you; never rebuild them from base stats.

VENTURE / THE DUNGEON. Several creatures "venture into the dungeon" when they enter or attack:
Triumphant Adventurer (on attack), Nadaar (on enter AND attack), Acererak (on enter), Barrowin
(on enter). Each venture enters the first room of a dungeon or advances to the next room.
- ACERERAK THE ARCHLICH IS NOT A BODY - HE BOUNCES HIMSELF. His printed (5/5) is a lie about what
  you get. Oracle: "When Acererak enters, IF YOU HAVE NOT COMPLETED TOMB OF ANNIHILATION, RETURN
  ACERERAK TO ITS OWNER'S HAND and venture into the dungeon." You are told below to pick LOST MINE,
  never Tomb - so Acererak will bounce EVERY SINGLE TIME. He goes battlefield -> hand instantly.
  He will NEVER be on the battlefield when you declare attackers or blockers, so his attack trigger
  (opponent sacrifices a creature or you get a Zombie) can NEVER FIRE. Do not plan to attack with
  him, block with him, or count his 5 power in any damage math - it is not there.
  What he actually IS: a {2}{B} REPEATABLE VENTURE - three mana, one dungeon room, card back in
  hand. That is real value in a quiet turn and nothing at all in a turn where you needed to act.
  So: cast Acererak ONLY with mana you have nothing better to do with. If a removal spell is on
  the menu and the opponent has a real threat, THE REMOVAL WINS - always. Never spend the turn on
  Acererak while a Vanishing Verse and a live target are sitting in front of you.
- WHICH DUNGEON: when you first venture (and again after you complete one) you CHOOSE one of
  three dungeons. **Pick LOST MINE OF PHANDELVER by NAME** (answer with the dungeon's name -
  the option NUMBERS are not fixed, so read the names, not "option 2"). Every one of Lost Mine's
  rooms is pure UPSIDE for you: it makes a 1/1 Goblin (a free blocker), a Treasure (ramp/fix),
  puts a +1/+1 counter on a creature, and drains the opponent - and it completes for a card
  (Temple of Dumathoin: draw a card). Nothing in it costs YOU life. THIS APPLIES EVERY TIME THE
  THREE-DUNGEON LIST APPEARS, including after you have already completed one - answer LOST MINE
  again, and make sure the name you write is one of the names on the list in front of you.
- Do NOT pick TOMB OF ANNIHILATION unless you are RACING and clearly ahead on life - every one
  of its rooms makes BOTH players lose life or sacrifice, so it hurts you as much as them. Its
  completion reward is a 4/4 deathtouch God (The Atropal), which is strong, but you pay life the
  whole way there - only worth it when you are winning the race.
- Do NOT pick DUNGEON OF THE MAD MAGE unless it is a slow grind and you want raw cards - it is
  the longest dungeon at nine rooms (you will rarely finish it) but pure card advantage if you do.
- COMPLETING A DUNGEON MATTERS AND IT WORKS: once you complete ANY dungeon, Nadaar gives your
  creatures a PERMANENT +1/+1 anthem, and Barrowin's attacks reanimate a MV<=3 creature from your
  graveyard. So keep venturing to FINISH the dungeon - every Lost Mine room is upside on the way.
- ROOM BRANCHES: the venture asks you to pick between two rooms and SHOWS each one's effect (e.g.
  "goblin lair {room effect: Create a 1/1 red Goblin creature token}", "dark pool {room effect:
  each opponent loses 1 life and you gain 1}"). DECIDE ON THE EFFECT TEXT ALONE. Take the room
  that MAKES A BODY (Goblin Lair) or DAMAGES the opponent (Dark Pool) over one that only makes
  a Treasure, only puts a +1/+1 counter on a creature, or only scries.
  You do not need to know which room number you are on or how many rooms are left to make this
  choice correctly - do not try to work it out, and do not question the two names you are shown:
  pick the better of the two effects in front of you and move on.

DEPLOY YOUR CHEAP THREATS AS BLOCKERS - this is entry 3 of THE WHOLE CAST ORDER (Silverquill
Silencer is entry 4), and entries 1 and 2 still outrank it. Triumphant Adventurer is a 1/1 with DEATHTOUCH (and first
strike on your turn) - cast it early; it trades with or blocks almost anything and it ventures
every time it attacks. Do not hold it. Nadaar is a 3/3 VIGILANT body that ventures twice and can
attack without giving up its block.

PLANESWALKERS - USE THEM EVERY TURN THEY ARE OUT. Casting one is entry 2 of THE WHOLE CAST
ORDER: a removal spell with a live target still outranks her, and nothing else does. A
planeswalker that does nothing on your turn is wasted. The moment you can afford one, CAST IT - do not leave Lolth or Kaya rotting in hand
while you flood; they are your best late-game engines. Each of your turns they are in play,
activate an ability:
- LOLTH, SPIDER QUEEN ({3}{B}{B}, starts at 4, and gains loyalty whenever a creature you control
  dies): -3 makes two 2/1 Spiders with menace and reach (great blockers vs flyers AND attackers)
  - use this first to build a board; 0 draws you a card and loses 1 life. -8 is the game-ending
  emblem.
  LOLTH'S 0 HAS A LIFE FLOOR AND IT IS 6. Read your own printed life first. At 6 or less, do NOT
  take the 0 - PASS, or take -3 if she has the loyalty. That "lose 1 life" is real damage you
  are dealing to yourself, it is not offset by the card, and a card drawn at 2 life is a card
  you never get to play. Any opponent permanent that pings - a Staff of Nin, a punisher
  enchantment, one unblocked 1/1 - finishes a game you handed them one point at a time.
- KAYA: see #1 rule - her -3 is removal; do not leave her idle.

CLOSE THE GAME - none of this is a cast rule; THE WHOLE CAST ORDER decides what you cast, and
animating the Hive is what you do with mana it left over. Once you are stable, PRESS - attack with Adventurer/Nadaar to venture and chip,
grind with your planeswalkers, and finish. A game left even to the turn cap is a LOSS risk.
Hive of the Eye Tyrant is a manland: pay {3}{B} - FOUR mana, not two - to make it a 3/3 menace
attacker until end of turn. That is a whole turn's mana in the mid-game, so animate it only when
you have nothing better to cast; it is a late-game mana sink, not a cheap threat.
- BEFORE YOU ANSWER "becomes beholder", RUN THESE TWO CHECKS IN ORDER AND STOP AT THE FIRST
  ONE THAT ANSWERS. This is the single most expensive mistake available to this deck: each
  activation is FOUR mana, and paying it twice for one creature is four mana set on fire.
  CHECK 0 - IS THIS THE TURN IT WINS? Read the opponent's printed life, then add the power of
  every creature of yours that is UNTAPPED and could attack this turn, plus 3 for the animated
  Hive. WRITE THAT SUM AND THEIR PRINTED LIFE INTO YOUR REPLY. If you do not write the two
  numbers, CHECK 0 did not fire and you go straight on to CHECK 1 - "it might be useful" is not
  CHECK 0. If that total is at or above their printed life, ANIMATE NOW and swing, whatever phase
  it is - a manland is a win condition on the turn it closes, and this check outranks both
  checks below. Your Hive's activation is usually offered to you only during your UPKEEP, so
  "I will do it in my main phase instead" can mean you never get asked again this turn: when
  CHECK 0 says the game ends this turn, the upkeep offer is the only offer and you take it.
  CHECK 1 - WHAT PHASE IS IT? The prompt's "Phase:" line says. If it says Upkeep and CHECK 0 did
  NOT fire, the answer is PASS. The animation lasts until end of turn either way, and spending
  {3}{B} before your draw step is spending it before you know what you drew.
  CHECK 2 - IS IT ALREADY A CREATURE? Find that Hive on your battlefield line. An animated one
  carries a (P/T) - "Hive of the Eye Tyrant #1 (4/4) (printed 3/3) [doesn't untap during its
  controller's untap step, menace (can't be blocked except by two or more creatures)]" - and is
  counted in the "of which N are creatures" number. If it carries ANY (P/T), the answer is PASS;
  an anthem makes that number bigger than the printed 3/3.
  CHECK 3 - IS THAT HIVE TAPPED, AND WHAT PAYS FOR IT? Find the Hive the row names on your
  battlefield line. If its entry reads "[tapped]", the answer is PASS: a tapped land animates into
  a tapped 3/3 that cannot attack this turn - four mana for nothing. Then read the mana line's
  "Those sources, one per untapped card:" list. If the only "{b}" source on it is a Hive that is
  ALREADY a (3/3), animating this one taps that one, and you attack with neither. This seat did
  exactly that: eight mana, two tapped Hives, zero attackers. A second Hive is animated only when
  a Swamp or a Shineshadow Snarl is still on the sources list to pay its {B}.
  AND THE ROW OFTEN SAYS SO ITSELF. An option printed "[repeat: activated this turn N times
  already]" is the game handing you the receipt for the mana you already spent on it this turn.
  That tag is an unconditional PASS - the cheapest observable on the screen, and it cannot be
  wrong. Read it before you read anything else on the row.
  AND THE OPTION COMING BACK IS NOT A NEW OPPORTUNITY. The game will offer you "becomes beholder"
  again later in the same turn - after a spell resolves, at another priority, in the same upkeep.
  That is the same Hive and the same 3/3 you already paid for. A second offer in a turn where you
  already animated is always PASS. Animate a SECOND, DIFFERENT Hive only if you still have four
  spare mana after the first.
Emeria's Call is your seven-mana haymaker: two 4/4 flying Angels AND your NON-ANGEL creatures gain
indestructible until your next turn (the two new Angels do not get it), so it doubles as a blowout
against a board wipe or a big attack.
DO NOT CAST AGADEEM'S AWAKENING - EVER. Its text returns CREATURE CARDS from your graveyard, and
ONLY creature cards: Kaya and Lolth are PLANESWALKERS, not creature cards - no X you pay can ever
return them, and no other card type qualifies either. Do not re-argue this from the card text
when your graveyard fills up: in this game, choosing Agadeem's Awakening taps out your whole
board and then nothing happens - the card stays in your hand and the turn is gone. There is no X,
no graveyard, and no board that makes it a good cast. Cast a creature or hold removal instead.

DECIDING SITUATIONS (recognize -> do):
- Their battlefield line shows BOTH "Sanguine Bond" and "Exquisite Blood" -> any damage you
  deal them and any life they gain repeats without limit. ATTACK: none, whatever the tags say,
  until one is gone. A "[lifelink]" attacker of theirs on that board ends the game whether you
  block it or not: it deals its damage to your blocker, they gain, and the gain chains - the
  B-row's "(you kill it, your blocker lives (lifelink: they gain 1 ...))" is that chain's first
  link, and "there is no loop" is the sentence this seat wrote one window before it lost. So
  the pair is answered BEFORE it closes: the turn ONE of the two names is on their line or in
  Verse's target list, Verse or Kaya -3 takes it above every creature.
- Verse's "legal targets right now:" list, or Kaya's -3 list, names Sanguine Bond or Exquisite
  Blood -> take it, above any creature.
- The mulligan ask says "(keeping 5)" or fewer and the hand has a land and a spell -> keep it,
  whatever colours those lands make and whatever your carried plan says; a fresh seven shaved
  to four is the same land odds with one card fewer, and a missing colour arrives with the next
  land (fourteen of 21 make white, fifteen make black, eight make either).
- You kept, and the game asks which cards to bottom -> read "You will be left with an N-card
  hand" and build the best N: the lands that cover your cheapest spells, those spells, and the
  expensive cards under the deck (Emeria's Call, Agadeem's Awakening, spare planeswalkers).
  Never all lands; Hive of the Eye Tyrant counts as a land; and never a land from a keep of
  two lands or fewer - the PUT line there is spell numbers only.
- The engine's hand header says "2 lands" -> it is a two-land hand. Use its count, not your own.
- Opponent has a real creature/planeswalker and you can remove it, and you are not ahead ->
  fire Vanishing Verse (read its target list) or Kaya -3 (any nonland) NOW; do not hold it.
- The Vanishing Verse option says "the only legal targets are YOUR OWN" -> do not cast it at all.
- Every row on Kaya's -3 list reads "[your battlefield]" -> +1 or pass; never the -3.
- The opponent's board is colorless artifacts -> Verse is dead this turn. Answer with Soul Shatter,
  Kaya -3, or the Silverquill Command sacrifice mode, and race with your creatures.
- A monocolored (single-color) artifact creature IS on Verse's target list -> it is a legal target;
  treat it like any other creature.
- You want Silverquill Command's +3/+3 to matter in combat -> cast it in MAIN 1, before you
  declare attackers. After combat, the pump mode is wasted - pick other modes or hold the card.
  A "pump now, attack next turn" plan is always wrong: the +3/+3 ends this turn.
- Silverquill Command asks you to pick one creature on the battlefield -> pick YOUR OWN creature
  (it gets the +3/+3); never the opponent's, whatever the list shows.
- A block line reads "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" -> take it, whatever the forecast says.
- A block line reads "(both die)" -> take it unless their creature is worth less than yours.
- Only "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)" is decided by the forecast: take it at 10 or less,
  leave it above 10. A blocked non-trampler deals you ZERO - you never "take it either way", and
  "preserve it for future blocks" is not a reason at 10 or less.
- The three-dungeon list is offered (first venture OR after completing one) -> answer LOST MINE OF
  PHANDELVER by name, unless you are ahead and racing.
- A room branch is offered -> read the {room effect} text; take the room that makes a body or
  drains the opponent over one that only makes a Treasure, adds a counter, or scries, and do
  not try to work out which room number you are on.
- You need combat math -> read the (P/T) already printed on each creature; do not rebuild it.
- The block header says the attack IS LETHAL -> assign blockers to the biggest "deals N"
  attackers until the damage still coming through is below your life, THEN use the ladder.
- ANY blocker on an attacker's tag prints "(blocking trigger: they gain N)", "(blocking trigger:
  they may gain N)" or "(lifelink: they gain N)" -> that swing PAYS them life. Send only what gets
  through; hold the rest. No such clause on the tag -> their walls are free to attack
  into and the ordinary rules apply.
- "LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER on the battlefield: theirs" is printed AND any attacker's tag prints
  a "(blocking trigger:" or "(lifelink:" price -> that attacker STAYS HOME; "their converter
  takes N off you" is N off YOUR life, not theirs. Only bodies the tag says cannot be blocked at
  all go in. Your plan says "net 0" or "drained from them" -> the plan is wrong; leave it home.
  A collapsed tag's "most expensive to attack into:" clause is the price of the swing.
- An attacker's tag lists several blockers and ONE of them reads "your attacker dies, their
  blocker lives" -> that creature stays home if it is a CARD. They pick the blocker, and they
  pick that one. Only a token goes anyway.
- A Silverquill Silencer, Nadaar or Barrowin shares a tag with both a Shield Sphere and a
  creature that kills it -> the stop wins; the Shield Sphere exception is only for tags where
  EVERY name is a Shield Sphere.
- An attacker's tag reads "they have N untapped creatures that could block this one" with N of 2
  or more, or prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause -> that creature
  stays home if it is a CARD; only a dungeon Goblin token goes. A collapsed tag never triggers
  the Shield Sphere exception.
- The "becomes beholder" option is offered and the opponent's printed life is at or below 3 plus
  the power of your untapped attackers -> ANIMATE, whatever phase it says; that is the game.
- The "becomes beholder" option is offered and it is NOT lethal -> read the "Phase:" line. Upkeep
  -> PASS. Otherwise find that Hive on your battlefield line: already (3/3) -> PASS. And a
  second offer in a turn where you already animated -> PASS; the option coming back is not a new
  opportunity.
- The "becomes beholder" row prints "[repeat: activated this turn N times already]" -> PASS, at
  any life total and in any phase. You have already bought that 3/3 this turn.
- You are about to answer "becomes beholder" in Upkeep and your reply does not contain their
  printed life and your own attackers' summed power -> the answer is PASS.
- Triumphant Adventurer is castable and you are under pressure -> cast it; the deathtouch 1/1
  blocks or trades with anything.
- Soul Shatter is offered but the opponent controls no creature and no planeswalker -> do not
  cast it.
- Lolth is on your battlefield and your printed life is 6 or less -> do NOT take her 0. Pass or
  take -3. The 1 life she costs is life you will be killed by.
- An attacker's tag says your attacker dies, and a DIFFERENT A-line's tag says that same blocker
  dies to it -> the stop still stands for the first creature. They choose the block, and they
  will not choose the one that kills their creature.
- "Cast Silverquill Silencer" is on the list and no removal-with-a-live-target, no planeswalker
  and no venture body is -> cast the Silencer; it is entry 4 of THE WHOLE CAST ORDER.
- Lolth or Kaya is castable in your hand -> CAST IT this turn, even when a creature (Barrowin,
  Nadaar, Acererak) is on the same list and even when that creature would venture. Only a removal
  spell with a live target outranks her. A bomb walker in hand does nothing, and "I will cast her
  next turn" assumes mana you do not have yet.
- Silverquill Command is on the list, no removal-with-a-target and no planeswalker is, and their
  line shows a creature OR your line shows a creature OR its row names a card it returns -> cast
  it. None of the three true -> it sits below Acererak (entry 8b), still above "nothing".
- A planeswalker is on your battlefield on your turn -> activate an ability (Lolth -3 for
  Spiders or 0 to draw; Kaya -3 to exile their threat) - do not pass with an idle planeswalker.
- You have completed a dungeon and control Nadaar -> your team is +1/+1 (creatures render above
  their printed stats); attack accordingly.
- Cast Acererak and Cast Vanishing Verse (or any removal) are both on the menu and the opponent
  has a threat worth killing -> FIRE THE REMOVAL. Acererak bounces to your hand and leaves nothing
  behind; he will still be there next turn. The threat will not wait.
- You are flooding with nothing to do and Acererak is castable -> cast him for the free dungeon
  room. That is the only turn he is the right play.
- Your hand is Emeria's Call, Agadeem's Awakening or Pelakka Predation plus lands -> those are
  SPELLS, not lands. Play a real land, hold Emeria's Call for seven mana, and leave Agadeem's
  Awakening alone - it is never cast, see CLOSE THE GAME.
