SANGUINE BLOOD - WGB WALL PRISON WITH A TWO-CARD KILL

You win by getting Exquisite Blood AND Sanguine Bond onto the battlefield at the same time. The
moment both are there, the very next life you gain - or the very next life the opponent loses -
loops between them and drains the opponent from whatever they are at down to 0. That is the whole
game. Sixteen walls buy the turns. Every turn you do the same three things: play a land if one is
offered, put a wall or a combo piece onto the battlefield, and pass with your Battlement UNTAPPED.

=== RULE #1 - AT YOUR UPKEEP, PASS. THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT LINE IN THIS GUIDE ===
At your Upkeep the engine will offer you a single action: tapping Overgrown Battlement for green
mana. Answer CHOICE: 0 (pass). Every time. Upkeep, Draw, the opponent's turn - pass all of them.
You never have to tap the Battlement by hand. When you cast a spell in your MAIN PHASE the engine
taps your sources for you, and the "Mana available:" line already counts the Battlement in with
your lands. Taking that upkeep option instead does two bad things at once: the green mana is gone
before your main phase ever starts, and a tapped Battlement is a 0/4 wall that cannot block on the
opponent's next turn - a wall you needed.
The thought "I should ramp first so I can afford Sanguine Bond" is ALWAYS FALSE in the upkeep
window: mana made there does not survive to the main phase where the enchantment is cast.
The same is true in EVERY window, your main phase included: tapping the Battlement by hand is
right only when the very next thing you do in that same decision is cast a spell with that mana.
If your answer to the following ask would be "Cast nothing right now", the tap was wasted and the
wall is now a 0/4 that cannot block on their turn.
The ONE time you take it: you are casting Tribute to Hunger in that same window and need the mana
to pay for it right now.

=== RULE #2 - THE WIN BUTTON ===
Check your battlefield line at every decision. If it lists BOTH "Exquisite Blood" and "Sanguine
Bond", you are one life-gain away from winning outright. Take the first of these the list offers,
immediately, and the game ends:
 - Declare a block with ANY wall, if Perimeter Captain is on your battlefield (2 life).
 - Declare a block with Pride Guardian (3 life, Captain or no Captain).
 - Deal 1 Damage with Staff of Nin targeting a player - pick the OPPONENT.
 - Cast Tribute to Hunger while the opponent has a creature (you gain its toughness).
 - Attack with a lifelink Vampire and connect.
Any one of these wins from any life total, yours or theirs. You do not need a big number - 1 life is
enough, because the loop runs itself until the opponent is at 0. Do not wait for a better turn and
do not build a bigger board first.

=== RULE #3 - ASSEMBLING THE PAIR (your only win condition) ===
Exquisite Blood {4}{B}: whenever an opponent loses life, you gain that much life.
Sanguine Bond {3}{B}{B}: whenever you gain life, the opponent loses that much life.
Neither does anything useful alone. Both together end the game.
 1. The turn either one appears in your casting list, CAST IT. Not later, not after a wall, not
    once you can afford both.
 2. If one is already on your battlefield and the other is not in your hand, "Cast Idyllic Tutor
    {2}{w}" is your best play in that list - ahead of every wall and every other card.
 3. Idyllic Tutor puts the enchantment into your HAND, not onto the battlefield. You still have to
    cast it for 5, and Tutor plus enchantment in one turn costs 8 mana. So cast the Tutor the FIRST
    turn it is listed - the turn you spend tutoring is a turn you are not casting the enchantment.
 4. The Tutor then shows you your whole library as a long numbered list. Pick the one of the two you
    do NOT already have; if you have neither, pick either.
 5. You run four Idyllic Tutors and two copies of each enchantment. If a piece is countered or
    exiled, tutor for the next copy and cast it again. There is no other way to win this game.

=== RULE #4 - TRIBUTE TO HUNGER IS AN EDICT, NOT A KILL SPELL ===
"Cast Tribute to Hunger {2}{b}" - the OPPONENT picks which of their creatures dies, and they will
pick their worst one. You gain life equal to THAT creature's toughness.
The thought "cast Tribute to Hunger on their best creature" is ALWAYS FALSE - you never choose the
victim, and naming one in your plan makes you expect a kill and a life total you will not get.
Cast it when any of these is true:
 - Both enchantments are on your battlefield and the opponent has a creature. This WINS the game.
 - The opponent controls exactly ONE creature - then their choice is your choice.
 - You need the life right now to survive the turn.
Otherwise hold it. It is an instant, it stays castable on their turn, and it will be offered again.
The engine also lists it when the opponent has no creature at all; with an empty opponent board,
take a different option.

=== RULE #5 - BLOCKING IS HOW THIS DECK DEALS DAMAGE ===
The combat prompt prints a general hint about trades and about taking damage while ahead on
life. It also says your strategy guide overrides it. THIS SECTION IS THAT OVERRIDE: run the
rules below and nothing the hint says. Your walls have 0 power, so no block of yours ever wins
a trade - blocks are how you gain life, and life is how you win.
Assign EVERY blocker the list offers you, in every combat:
 - Give each attacker one blocker first, then put the leftovers on the biggest attacker. Several of
   your blockers may gang up on one attacker; each triggers its own life gain.
 - Perimeter Captain gains you 2 life for EACH defender you control that blocks. Three walls
   blocking with the Captain out is 6 life on top of what those walls gain themselves.
 - Pride Guardian gains you 3 life whenever it blocks.
 - The life arrives when blockers are DECLARED, before damage. A wall that dies in combat has
   already gained you the life.
Which attacker each blocker takes: your blocker's line lists several attackers with a
parenthesis each. Pick, for that blocker, an attacker whose parenthesis says your blocker
LIVES ("neither dies" or "you kill it") ahead of one that says it dies. The life gain is
identical either way - a wall that survives gains it again next combat, and a wall that
dies gains it once. When two or more attackers all let that blocker live, take the one with the
BIGGEST "deals N" number on its A-line that no other blocker of yours is already assigned to -
one blocker per attacker first, as above; this tiebreak only chooses among attackers
still unblocked. The life gain is the same either way, so the only thing
left to decide is how much damage you stop - stop the bigger number.
Only when every attacker offered to that blocker kills it do you put it on the attacker with the
biggest "deals N" number on its A-line - read the number off the line, do not judge by name, by
size in words, or by which one you blocked last turn. An attacker tagged
[deathtouch] kills your wall whatever its toughness; its line will already say so.
The one blocker you leave out: neither enchantment is on your battlefield AND that blocker's line
reads "your blocker dies, attacker lives" on EVERY attacker it is offered. A line reading "both
die" is not that line - take it; a body of theirs for a body of yours is a trade you want, and a
lifelink blocker gains you life on the way. Once either enchantment is on your battlefield, every
blocker blocks, on any line at all.
That carve-out is OFF whenever the combat header's "you would be at N" number is 8 or
less. At that life total the damage a body stops is worth more than the body, so block
with everything the list offers, whatever the parenthesis says.
"BLOCKS: none" is right only when every blocker the list offers you falls under the carve-out
above.

=== RULE #6 - ATTACKING ===
All sixteen of your creatures are Defenders and can never attack; the attacker list already leaves
them out, so never re-derive who is eligible. An empty attacker list means ATTACK: none, and that
costs you nothing.
When the list DOES offer a Vampire token, it has lifelink: attack with every Vampire offered, every
turn. Each point that connects is life gained, and with Sanguine Bond out that is the win.
ONE stop, and read it off your own narration: if the last time you attacked it says your attacker
took damage and died before combat damage - "<something> dealt N damage to Vampire" then "Your
Vampire died" - the opponent controls a permanent that kills anything you send, and the next
Vampire dies to it exactly the same way. Answer ATTACK: none and keep the Vampire on your
battlefield instead: it is a lifelink blocker, and with both enchantments out one block wins the
game outright. Attack again only once that permanent is gone from their battlefield line.

=== RULE #7 - WHAT TO CAST WHEN SEVERAL THINGS ARE LISTED ===
Take the highest entry on this list that appears in your casting menu:
 1. Exquisite Blood or Sanguine Bond - whichever is missing from your battlefield.
 2. Idyllic Tutor, when either enchantment is missing from both your hand and your battlefield.
 3. Perimeter Captain - it turns every future block by every wall into 2 more life.
 4. Any other wall: Wall of Omens (it replaces itself), Overgrown Battlement, Pride Guardian.
 5. Staff of Nin.
 6. Sorin, Lord of Innistrad.
 7. Chromatic Lantern - only when BOTH are true: the "colours you can make:" line does NOT include
    {b}, AND no Chromatic Lantern is already on your battlefield line. Your Bayous, Scrublands,
    Savannahs and Urborg already make black; when {b} is on that line the Lantern does nothing for
    3 mana. A SECOND Lantern does nothing at any time - once one is on your battlefield this entry
    is finished for the rest of the game, so take a wall or a combo piece instead.
7a. If BOTH enchantments are already on your battlefield, STAFF OF NIN and SORIN move to the
    TOP of this list, above walls and above everything else. With both out the game ends the
    moment any life changes, and Staff's ping and Sorin's lifelink Vampires are the only two
    triggers you can make without help from the opponent - blocks need them to attack, Tribute
    to Hunger needs them to have a creature. Against an opponent whose battlefield line shows
    no creature, those two are your ONLY win.
Keep casting while the list keeps offering. "Cast nothing right now" is correct only when it is the
ONLY option left.

=== STAFF OF NIN AND SORIN ===
Staff of Nin {6}: draws you an extra card at each of your upkeeps, and offers "Deal 1 Damage with
Staff of Nin targeting a player" every turn. Always aim it at the OPPONENT, every upkeep - this is
the one upkeep option you always TAKE. With Exquisite Blood down that damage is life for you; with
both enchantments down it wins the game on the spot.
Sorin, Lord of Innistrad {2}{W}{B}: cast him when offered, then take "+1: create a 1/1 vampire with
Sorin, Lord of Innistrad" every turn. The Vampires have lifelink, so they block for life, attack for
life, and win the game once both enchantments are out.
If neither is in your hand, nothing above changes.

=== MULLIGAN ===
Keep almost everything. The pregame prompt prints "Your hand (7 cards), counted by the engine: N
lands, M spells." KEEP whenever N is 2 or more. Also KEEP whenever the line "Playing every land in
this hand would cover the cost of:" names even one card - that line already checked your mana
against your hand and it is right.
Never mulligan more than once. If the prompt contains the words "having already taken", this is your
last look: answer 1 (Keep this hand).
The thought "I will keep whatever I draw next" is how you end up with a four-card hand - keep THIS
one instead.
Your cheapest spell costs ONE mana, so "no early play" is never a reason to mulligan, and a hand
with no combo piece is not one either: your lands and walls buy the turns you need to draw one.

=== SITUATIONS THAT DECIDE YOUR GAMES ===
 - Both Exquisite Blood and Sanguine Bond on your battlefield: take the first block, Staff ping,
   Tribute or lifelink attack the list offers. You win this turn.
 - Your Upkeep, and the only offered action taps Overgrown Battlement for mana: answer CHOICE: 0 (pass)
   and keep the wall untapped for their turn.
 - One enchantment down, the other not in hand, "Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" listed: cast the Tutor
   and take the missing one.
 - An enchantment in hand and castable: cast it this turn, at any life total, ahead of anything else.
 - The opponent attacks: assign every blocker on the list.
 - The opponent has exactly one creature and you need life: cast Tribute to Hunger.
