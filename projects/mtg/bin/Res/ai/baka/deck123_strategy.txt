INTRUDERS OF THRABEN - WBU TOKEN COMBO

You win by putting Intruder Alarm onto the battlefield next to Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline
Keeper, then tapping that creature for a token over and over: every token that enters untaps
all creatures, so the tap ability comes straight back. Your removal buys the turns; the tokens
end the game. Every turn is the same three actions: play a land, deploy a token-maker or
Intruder Alarm, then tap every token-maker you own until your board is an army.

=== RULE -1 - THE FIRST ASK OF THE GAME IS THE ONE YOU LOSE MOST OFTEN ===
This rule is first because the ask it governs is first. Two of six games were decided here,
before turn one, and neither of them was close.
The ask reads "Pre-game mulligan decision (London mulligan)" and, from the second look on, it
prints its own price: "having already taken N mulligans you will bottom N cards on a keep
(keeping M)". READ THE M.
  - M is 5 or less: weigh the odds, not the hand. A fresh seven from a 24-land deck is a
    two-lander about as often as anything else, so shipping does not fix lands - it hands you
    the same land odds with one card fewer, while a KEEP lets the bottom ask shed the
    uncastable cards. With even ONE land in front of you the keep wins that comparison; only
    ZERO lands buys a fresh look, and even then you are buying a four-card hand at the same
    odds. Not a colour, not a curve, not a token-maker: those are bottom-ask questions.
  - M is 6 or 7 (your opening seven, or the first look after it): the comparison in the
    MULLIGAN section applies - a one-land seven with "would not cover any spell in it" ships at
    (keeping 6) exactly as it does at 7; two lands is a keep at either.
THE CARRIED PLAN IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT THIS HAND. From the second look on, the ask shows you
"YOUR PLAN (as you last stated it)", and the plan it shows is the one you wrote about the hand
you already threw away. A plan that says "mulligan to find 4+ lands" will be handed back to you
at every single look, and answering it again is how a chain runs. Each look is a NEW seven with
its own "counted by the engine: N lands, M spells" header. Decide from that header. If your plan
tells you to mulligan and the header says two or more lands, the plan is out of date - say so
and keep.
A three-land five-card hand beats every hand you have not seen.

=== RULE 0 - PLAY A LAND, EVERY TURN, BEFORE YOU DO ANYTHING ELSE ===
The land drop is its own question. It arrives as "Land drop: which land do you play now, if
any?" with one Play line per land in your hand and one "Play no land right now".
If ANY "Play" line is listed, take one. Every turn. Answer "Play no land right now" only when
that is the only option on the screen.
There is no board and no plan that makes holding a land in hand better than playing it. This
deck has twenty-four lands and its engine costs three and four mana; every land you did not
play is a turn you spend at your old mana total.
"Land drop: NOT yet used this turn" on the CURRENT SITUATION block means the drop is still
there. It is not spent by casting a spell, and taking it does not cost you a cast.
READ THE COSTS IN MANA, NOT IN SYMBOLS. {2}{u} is THREE mana - a generic 2 plus one blue.
{2}{b}{b} is FOUR. {1}{w}{w} is THREE. Compare that number to the "Mana available: N total"
line, which already counts every untapped source for you. Two untapped lands do NOT cast
Intruder Alarm. This deck once held a two-land board for seven of its own turns with three
lands in hand, re-stating "cast Intruder Alarm using Tundra and Underground Sea" in its plan
every window, and lost that game 42 to 0 without ever making a token.
If your plan names a spell you cannot afford this window, the fix is the land drop, not
another pass.
WHICH LAND: when a spell in your hand is castable THIS turn off the land you play, play the
land that enters UNTAPPED - Underground Sea, Tundra, Scrubland, Plains, Swamp. Arcane Sanctum
"enters tapped" (its own text says so) and Isolated Chapel enters tapped unless you control a
Plains or Swamp: play those on a turn you were not going to spend all your mana. With two
lands out, an Underground Sea and an Arcane Sanctum in hand and Intruder Alarm {2}{u}
castable off three, this deck played the Sanctum, then answered "Cast nothing right now"
twice with two mana and the Alarm in hand.
This applies hardest in COMBAT. On a blockers screen at 9 life this deck worked out, correctly,
that Tribute to Hunger after blockers would let it survive - with "Mana available: 1 total" on
the same screen and Tribute costing {2}{b}, three mana. A spell you cannot pay for is not a
survival plan; check "Mana available: N total" BEFORE you build a line around a card in hand.

=== RULE 1 - THE TOKEN LINES, AND WHEN TO STOP TAKING THEM ===
These are the three lines, exactly as you will see them. A second copy of the same card adds a
handle - "Thraben Doomsayer #1", "Thraben Doomsayer #2" - same line, same rule:
  "Create human with Thraben Doomsayer [cost: Tap]"
  "Create vampire with Bloodline Keeper [cost: Tap]"
  "Create vampire with Lord of Lineage [cost: Tap]"
BEFORE you take one, read M: the repeat row prints it first - "[you control M creatures right
now; ...]" - and your own battlefield line prints the same number after "of which". The
"activated this turn N times" count belongs to ONE COPY and ONE TURN; it is not M.
THE STOP IS A HEADCOUNT AFTER ALL, BECAUSE POWER CANNOT MEASURE THIS LOOP.
With Intruder Alarm out, the line comes back after every single activation, and the tokens you
just made are summoning sick, so the "power of creatures that are not summoning sick" number
does NOT MOVE when you take the line. A stop written on that number can never be reached: this
deck took the line 1,868 times in ONE turn, wrote "on my next main phase, attack with all
creatures to win the game" in its plan every time, and never once got to that main phase - the
game was killed with the loop still running. Two other games took it 1,540 and 214 times.
Use the two numbers that DO move, both printed in every window:
  - M = the number after "of which" on YOUR battlefield line: "Your battlefield (K permanents
    listed, of which M are creatures)". Every activation adds one to M, summoning sick or not.
  - L = "Opponent life:", and C = the number after "of which" on the OPPONENT'S battlefield
    line, i.e. how many blockers they own.
Then:
  - M is BELOW L + C + 3: TAKE THE LINE. Take it again every time it comes back.
  - M reaches L + C + 3: STOP. Answer "CHOICE: 0 (pass)".
    Every token is at least a 1/1, so at L + C + 3 bodies they cannot block enough of them to
    live through your next attack, and a body past that number wins nothing.
  - THE REPEAT ROW IS HOW YOU TAKE THE LINE. From the second activation of a turn on, the
    screen adds a second row: "Create human with Thraben Doomsayer, repeated N times, then stop
    [you name N on the CHOICE line, e.g. "CHOICE: 2 (Create human with Thraben Doomsayer x<N>)";
    the engine performs it N times ... N is at most 200]" - the row's own example shows the
    format; the number is YOURS, from the PLAN line's subtraction. Take THAT row, ONCE, with
        N = (L + C + 3) - M
    and write N the way the row's own example writes it: the short name, a space, then
    "x" and the number, inside the parentheses -
        CHOICE: 2 (Create human with Thraben Doomsayer x17)
    Name the number the first time: a take with no number is asked again, once, and that is a
    window spent. The next window after the repeat row is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)"; the log will
    show "(you named 17 repeats of it)".
    DO THE SUBTRACTION ON THE SCREEN BEFORE YOU WRITE N, and carry both numbers in your PLAN
    line in this shape, blanks filled from the screen:
        PLAN: stop at M = <L + C + 3>; M is <M> now; this window: <x<N> / pass>
    When (L + C + 3) - M is 0 or less the repeat row is not yours: CHOICE: 0 (pass).
    THE NUMBER ON YOUR CHOICE LINE IS THE PLAN LINE'S ANSWER, NOT A GUESS WRITTEN BEFORE IT.
    Your reply's first line is the CHOICE and the subtraction comes after it, so do the
    subtraction in your head BEFORE the first line: if the row's "you control M creatures right
    now" is already at or past your stop, the first line is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)" - not "x1",
    not "x3", and never "x0", which the engine carried out ONE time. Four windows this corpus
    wrote "this window: pass" in the PLAN under a CHOICE line that named x0, x1, x3 and x25;
    each one was carried out, and the x25 put 58 creatures on a board whose stop was 30. With
    the count on the row the stop now holds within a few tokens - the x100 days are over -
    and the last leak is the first line disagreeing with the plan beneath it.
PASSING IS ALWAYS AVAILABLE. The last row on these windows reads "0. Pass priority (take no
action this window)"; when M has reached L + C + 3 that row is the answer, whatever else is
listed.
Timing: the tokens you make CANNOT attack until your next turn, so making them early in your own
turn buys you nothing this turn and leaves them standing through the opponent's whole turn. Fire
the chain on the OPPONENT'S turn - the lines are offered there, in their upkeep, their main phase
and their attack step. A token made then blocks that same turn and attacks on your very next turn,
and a sweeper cast on their turn catches fewer of them.
Fire on your OWN turn only when the creatures you are about to attack with are already on the
board, or when the opponent has no untapped land left.
WITH THE ALARM IN HAND AND CASTABLE THIS TURN, THE MAKER WAITS UNTAPPED UNTIL THE ALARM HAS
RESOLVED. Under Intruder Alarm a tapped creature untaps only when a creature ENTERS; nothing
of theirs may enter for a whole turn. This deck tapped its Doomsayer in its own upkeep, cast
the Alarm in the main phase after it, and then went through the opponent's entire next turn
with no "Create human" line on any screen - the Doomsayer sat tapped, Brutal Cathar exiled
it, and the game ended two turns later at -10. Land, Alarm, THEN the first tap - or, with
the Alarm not castable this turn, tap freely: a maker with no Alarm untaps on its own.
YOUR OWN UPKEEP IS THE WORST WINDOW OF ALL. The 323 Vampires this deck made in its own turn-9
upkeep attacked with ONE creature that turn - every token's line read "[summoning sick]" - and
the opponent's turn 10 cleared all of them before a single token had attacked; the game ended
0 to 62 forty turns later without another maker. The line is offered in your upkeep; the answer
there is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)" and the chain fires in THEIR turn.
A token you make this turn CANNOT ATTACK this turn. Its battlefield line says so:
"[summoning sick - cannot attack this turn, but CAN block]". It can block right away, and it
attacks on your NEXT turn.
So when you plan, count only the creatures whose lines do NOT say "summoning sick". A plan that
says "attack with all 12 for lethal" is wrong on the turn you made those 12 - the attack list
will offer you one creature. Make the tokens anyway; they block this turn and they swing next
turn.

=== RULE 2 - MARSH FLATS TAKES A DUAL, NOT A BASIC ===
When lines beginning "Put a card onto the battlefield with Marsh Flats targeting" appear, read
the colour counts on the mana line - "sources that can make each: {W} n, {U} n, {B} n" - and
take the DUAL that raises the colour you have the least of, a colour at 0 first:
  "targeting Underground Sea"  - makes {U} and {B}
  "targeting Tundra"           - makes {W} and {U}
  "targeting Scrubland"        - makes {W} and {B}
Ties go to Underground Sea, then Tundra, then Scrubland. With an Underground Sea already on
your battlefield and Idyllic Tutor {2}{w} in hand, Tundra is the right fetch - it was, once, in
this corpus. Never a basic while a dual is listed: Underground Sea does everything a Swamp does
AND makes blue; Tundra does everything a Plains does AND makes blue. Both come in untapped.
Why this decides games: Intruder Alarm costs {2}{u} and Vision Skeins costs {1}{u}, and only
10 of your 24 lands make blue. Fetch a basic and your combo piece sits in your hand for the
rest of the game.

=== RULE 3 - COUNT BOTH BOARDS BEFORE DAMNATION ===
DAMNATION DESTROYS CREATURES AND NOTHING ELSE. Its own row prints the whole card: "Destroy all
creatures. They can't be regenerated." An enchantment, an artifact, a planeswalker and a land
all survive it. "Cast Damnation to remove Sanguine Bond" is a sentence this deck wrote in its
own plan while holding fourteen Humans; the Damnation killed its fourteen Humans, the Sanguine
Bond was still on the battlefield in the very next window, and it lost that game 42 to 0 having
never rebuilt. Nothing in this deck removes an enchantment. Read the row before you believe a
card does something its text does not say.
Damnation destroys ALL creatures, yours included. Both battlefield lines print a creature count
right after the words "of which" - read YOUR number and THEIR number, and compare them.
Cast Damnation when THEIR number is bigger than yours. That is the whole test.
Equal numbers are not a reason to cast it: keep the Damnation and take another action.
When YOUR number is the bigger one, spend the window on a creature, a token activation or a
removal spell instead, and keep the Damnation - your creatures are how you win, and a wipe that
kills two of yours to kill one of theirs has handed them the game.
Your number is 0 and theirs is 2 or more: that is the best Damnation there is. Take it, and take
it BEFORE a token-maker and before Vision Skeins - it is entry 0 of the cast list below. At 0
against 4 this deck cast Vision Skeins "to dig for a maker" with Damnation castable and was dead
two turns later to those same four bodies; at 4 life against 4 it cast a Thraben Doomsayer into
them and died on the next attack. A maker cast into four attackers does not live to make a
token; the Damnation that clears them buys the turns the maker needs.
THE ROW COUNTS FOR YOU. "Cast Damnation" prints "{right now: destroys N of their creatures (K
without a restriction against attacking), M of yours}" on your turn and "(K able to attack
right now)" on theirs; when every one of theirs is a wall it prints "(all of them carry a
restriction against attacking)" instead, and that is K = 0. N is their number, M is yours, and
K is how many of theirs can actually hit you. Two 0/4 walls are N = 2 with K = 0: a sweeper
spent on them is a sweeper you do not have when six Vampires arrive. So at K of 0, or at N of
exactly 1, a Tragic Slip or an edict on the same menu that kills the one that matters is the
cast and the Damnation stays in hand. This deck cast Damnation on a lone 1/1 Vampire token
with "Cast Tragic Slip" on the same menu, and ten turns later watched ten creatures on their
line with no sweeper in hand.
THE ONE K = 0 BOARD YOU DO SWEEP: walls named PERIMETER CAPTAIN or PRIDE GUARDIAN. CHECK 1 in
COMBAT forbids you to attack while they stand, and Damnation is the removal that takes them
off that line - this deck swept two Captains and a Battlement at K = 0 and won at -208.
"DESTROYS 0 OF THEIR CREATURES" IS A CARD THROWN AWAY. This deck cast Damnation three times
in one game on rows reading "destroys 0 of their creatures ... 0 of yours", with plans that
said "clear the board (currently empty)"; an empty board needs no clearing and the sweeper
was not there when it mattered. At N = 0 the Damnation stays in hand, whatever the plan says.
ENTRY 0 HAS NO EXCEPTIONS FOR YOUR OWN CARDS. At 13 life this deck read "destroys 2 of their
creatures (2 without a restriction against attacking), 0 of yours" and cast Idyllic Tutor;
at 9 life it read "destroys 4 ... 0 of yours" and cast Intruder Alarm; at 5 life it finally
cast the Damnation. Eight life for two turns of a Tutor and an Alarm that could wait.
THE STOP, and why (this rule was broken at 14 against 6): if YOUR "of which N are creatures"
number is 4 or more, do not cast Damnation at all, whatever theirs says. Four bodies is a board
you win with; there is no opposing board this deck needs to trade four of its own creatures to
answer. Take a token activation, a removal spell or "Cast nothing right now" instead.

=== RULE 4 - TRAGIC SLIP KILLS TOUGHNESS 1 ===
Tragic Slip gives -1/-1. That kills a creature whose battlefield line shows toughness 1 - a
(2/1), a (1/1), a (0/1). Against anything with toughness 2 or more it shrinks the creature and
it lives.
Pick the target with toughness 1. If several are listed, take the one with the biggest power.
The one upgrade: under the CURRENT turn header in the GAME LOG, a line ending in "died" means
Tragic Slip gives -13/-13 instead and kills anything on the list -
then take the target with the biggest printed power on the battlefield line.
No toughness-1 target on the list and no "died" line under this turn: answer "Cast nothing
right now" and keep the Slip. It costs {B} and it will kill something on a later turn.

=== RULE 5 - THE TWO EDICTS ARE DIFFERENT CARDS ===
IF YOUR PLAN NAMES WHICH CREATURE AN EDICT WILL REMOVE, THE PLAN IS ALREADY WRONG. Check your
own sentence before you check the board: "Tribute to Hunger on Sigarda", "Tribute to Hunger on
Briarbridge Tracker", "Tribute to Hunger to remove Sorin" are all sentences this deck has
written, and all three are impossible. You NEVER pick. The row itself prints the whole card -
{card text: "Target opponent sacrifices a creature of their choice. You gain life equal to that
creature's toughness."} - and "of their choice" is the entire rule. The only thing you choose is
whether to cast it at all, and the answer to that is the count of their creatures.
"Cast Tribute to Hunger {2}{b}" - the opponent sacrifices a creature and YOU gain life equal to
its toughness. This is the one you want.
Cast it at the opponent whenever it is listed and their battlefield line shows exactly ONE
creature. With two or more of their creatures listed, they hand over their worst one and keep the
good one - take Tragic Slip, Damnation or a token activation instead. This deck cast it at FIVE
of their creatures and at THREE of their creatures in the same corpus, and got a body it did not
want both times.
"Cast Devour Flesh {1}{b}" - the player you name sacrifices a creature AND THAT SAME PLAYER
gains the life. Naming the opponent does NOT gain YOU anything: "remove their blocker and gain
4 life" is the sentence this deck wrote in its own plan on turn 3 while handing a Sanguine Bond
deck four life off a 0/4 wall. You gain life from Tribute to Hunger. You never gain life from
Devour Flesh. That said, giving them the life is still the right price when it takes their ONLY
body: use Devour Flesh on the opponent when Tribute to Hunger is not on the list and they have
exactly ONE creature - then the sacrifice takes their only body and the life they gain is worth
it.
AN EDICT CANNOT TOUCH A PLANESWALKER. Both rows say "sacrifices a creature". A planeswalker on
their battlefield line - it prints "[planeswalker] [counters: Nx loyalty]" - is not a creature
and is not a legal outcome of either card. "Use Tribute to Hunger to remove Sorin" is a sentence
this deck wrote on a board with SEVEN of their creatures listed; the opponent handed over a 1/1
token and Sorin kept ticking up. If a planeswalker is what is beating you, an edict is not the
answer and neither is any other card in this deck: build the board and attack it.
Before any edict, run this in order: (1) read N off the edict row itself - it prints "{right
now: they control N creatures - <name> is sacrificed ...}" at N = 1, "- they choose which one"
at N of 2 or more, and "- at 0 this does nothing" at N = 0. N is the only number that matters.
(2) If N is not exactly 1, do not cast an edict - not at 0, not at 2 or more. (3) A creature
listed under "ON THE STACK, waiting to resolve" is NOT on the battlefield and does not change
N. Naming a creature spell on the stack in your PLAN is the mistake itself: at N=0 the opponent
sacrifices nothing and you have thrown away the card.
AT N = 1 THE EDICT IS THE CAST, NOT THE SLIP. The row "they control 1 creature - Silverquill
Silencer is sacrificed" was on this deck's menu twice at two mana; it answered "Cast nothing",
then spent a Tragic Slip on that 3/2 - which shrank it for a turn and let it live - and took
nine more combat damage from it in a game lost at -2. A toughness-2 creature is the edict's
job, not the Slip's.
Never cast an edict when the opponent's life is at or below the life it gives them. Killing
their last creature and moving them from 1 life to 4 costs you a game you had already won.
Do not spend an edict on a creature that is not hurting you: if their only creature reads
"[defender]" or has power 0, keep the edict and take another action - you paid a card and
handed them life equal to its toughness to remove something that was never going to kill
you. A 1-power creature is NOT in that class when its "{card text:}" gives the rest of their
board something - mana, counters, +1/+1 - it is the engine and it grows: this deck answered
"Cast nothing" to "they control 1 creature - Katilda, Dawnhart Prime is sacrificed, they gain
1" on turn 3 and cast Vision Skeins instead; the 1/1 was a 3/3 by turn 7, the board behind it
was four wide by turn 9, and the game ended at -10.
Both edicts give life to the player who sacrifices. If the OPPONENT'S deck list contains a card
that rewards them for gaining life, "Cast nothing right now" beats either edict at the
opponent.
"Yourself (player, life" is the right answer only when the creature you give up is a Human
or Vampire TOKEN you can remake next turn. Your Bloodline Keeper, your Thraben Doomsayer and
your flying Vampires stay on the battlefield.

=== RULE 6 - EQUIP LIGHTNING GREAVES ONCE PER GREAVES ===
An equip line for Lightning Greaves costs {0}, so the game re-offers it at every window.
Take it ONE time per Greaves, on Bloodline Keeper if
it is on the battlefield, otherwise on Thraben Doomsayer.
The done-signal is on the battlefield line: a creature reading "{attached: Lightning Greaves
#1}" already has it, and that Greaves is finished. Count your Greaves: one equip each, no more.
At every later re-offer, take a "[cost: Tap]" line if one is listed, otherwise
answer 0 (pass) and let combat happen - your remaining windows belong to tokens and removal.
Equip again only when the equipped creature has DIED, or a token-maker that has never been
equipped has just arrived.
Greaves gives haste, so a token-maker can tap for a token the turn it lands, and shroud, which
means that creature can no longer be targeted by anything - including your own spells.

=== WHAT TO CAST WHEN SEVERAL THINGS ARE LISTED ===
Take the highest entry on this list that appears in your casting menu:
 0. "Cast Damnation {2}{b}{b}" - when YOUR battlefield line reads "of which 0 are creatures"
    and the Damnation row's K - "(K without a restriction against attacking)" - reads 2 or
    more. Nothing else first: not a maker, not a dig, not the Tutor, not the Alarm. (K at 0,
    or their count exactly 1: Rule 3 - the one-for-one instead; K at 0 on Perimeter Captain or
    Pride Guardian: sweep them, Rule 3.)
 1. "Cast Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b}" - when no token-maker is on your battlefield.
 2. "Cast Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w}" - when no token-maker is on your battlefield.
 3. "Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}" - when no Intruder Alarm is on your battlefield.
    Intruder Alarm on an empty battlefield does NOTHING. A token-maker with no Alarm still
    makes one token a turn. The maker goes first, every time.
    AND THE ALARM GOES BEFORE THE SECOND MAKER. Entries 1 and 2 say "when no token-maker is on
    your battlefield": once one IS there, they are done and this entry is next - before a second
    maker, before Lightning Greaves. With Bloodline Keeper on its line, five mana open and the
    Alarm in hand, this deck cast Thraben Doomsayer and then the Greaves, made one token a turn
    for two turns, and cast the Alarm at 2 life. The Alarm with one maker is the loop; a second
    maker without it is one more token a turn.
 4. "Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" - when no Intruder Alarm is on your battlefield or in your hand.
    Read BOTH lines before you take it. "Cast Idyllic Tutor to find Intruder Alarm (already on
    battlefield, but must cast it to dig for Bloodline Keeper)" is a sentence this deck wrote
    this corpus with the Alarm on its line, and the reveal list marked every creature "[does
    NOT qualify]" again. A second "Cast Intruder Alarm" with one on your line is entry
    nothing: it does nothing the first one is not already doing.
 5. "Cast Lightning Greaves {2}" - ONLY when your own battlefield line reads "of which 1 is
    a creature" or more AND that creature is a token-maker. Read that clause before you take
    this line: "of which 0 are creatures" means the Greaves equips nothing, does nothing, and
    is a wasted turn - answer with a removal spell or "Cast nothing right now" and keep it for
    the turn a maker lands. A Greaves in HAND is not a play you are behind on; a Greaves on an
    empty board is a card you have already spent. This deck cast it at "of which 0 are
    creatures" three times in six games.
 6. A removal spell, by Rules 3, 4 and 5.
 7. "Cast Vision Skeins {1}{u}".
 8. "Cast nothing right now" - the LAST entry, and correct only when no entry above appeared in
    your casting menu. Every card you can cast has a line above this one.

=== INTRUDER ALARM AND IDYLLIC TUTOR ===
"Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}": it is the engine, and it is entry 3 on the cast list above -
behind both token-makers, because an Alarm with no maker out does nothing.
One copy does the whole job. With an Intruder Alarm already on your battlefield, spend the
window on a creature or a removal spell instead.
It is symmetric: no creature untaps in its controller's untap step, theirs or yours, and ANY
creature entering - theirs or yours - untaps everything. With a token-maker in play that is
pure profit. With no token-maker in play, a creature of yours that attacks stays tapped, so get
Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper down before you rely on attacking.
"Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" searches for an ENCHANTMENT, and the only enchantment in this sixty
is Intruder Alarm. The answer on the reveal list is Intruder Alarm, every time; every other row
there reads "[does NOT qualify]". IT CANNOT FIND A CREATURE. "Cast Idyllic Tutor to find a
creature" is a sentence this deck wrote at 3 life with an Alarm on the battlefield and a second
in hand; the Tutor found a third Alarm and the game ended without a creature cast. With an
Intruder Alarm on your battlefield OR in your hand, the Tutor is a dead card: never cast it -
Vision Skeins is the card that digs for a maker.
"Cast Idyllic Tutor to find Bloodline Keeper" and "the Tutor is the best available play to dig
for a creature" are sentences this deck wrote with TWO Intruder Alarms on its battlefield line,
and it cast the Tutor after each one; the reveal list marked Bloodline Keeper "[does NOT
qualify]" both times. The reveal list cannot be argued with. When "Cast Idyllic Tutor" is
listed and "Intruder Alarm" is on your battlefield line or in "Your hand", write this in your
reply, blanks filled from the screen, and take another row:
    Tutor: dead - Alarm <on my battlefield / in my hand>; it finds only an Alarm.
THE ALARM YOU TUTOR MAY NOT BE CASTABLE THIS TURN. The engine taps your mana for you, and it
paid a Tutor's {2}{w} with BOTH Underground Seas when a Swamp and two Scrublands would have done
it; the tutored Alarm then sat in hand for a whole turn cycle with "{U} 0" on the mana line.
Read "sources that can make each: ... {U} N" before you cast the Tutor: at {U} 2 or less, plan
the Alarm for NEXT turn, and spend what the Tutor leaves on a maker - "Cast Thraben Doomsayer
{1}{w}{w}" was on the menu with three W/B sources open, twice, and was not taken.

=== KEY CARDS ===
Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w} (2/2) - "{T}: Put a 1/1 white Human creature token onto the
  battlefield". Cast it by the cast list above. Fateful hour: while YOUR life is 5 or less,
  every OTHER creature you control gets +2/+2, so your 1/1 Humans arrive as 3/3s and your
  battlefield line shows "(3/3) (printed 1/1)". Low life makes your tokens bigger, not smaller.
Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b} (3/3) flying - "{T}: Put a 2/2 black Vampire creature token with
  flying". Your best creature. Cast it by the cast list above.
  When "Transform:backside with Bloodline Keeper [cost: {b}]" appears in your list, take it:
  it becomes Lord of Lineage, a 5/5 that gives every other Vampire +2/+2 - your 2/2 tokens
  become 4/4s - and it still makes a token every turn with the same tap. If that line is not
  listed, nothing changes; keep tapping the Keeper.
  Transforming needs {B} and five or more Vampires. Casting the Keeper off exactly four lands
  taps you out, and Intruder Alarm untaps CREATURES, not lands - so the "Transform:backside with
  Bloodline Keeper [cost: {b}]" line will not appear again that turn. When you can cast the
  Keeper and still leave one black source untapped, do it: Lord of Lineage gives every OTHER
  Vampire +2/+2, which turns eleven 2/2 tokens into eleven 4/4s.
Vision Skeins {1}{u} - EACH player draws two cards, you and them.
  Cast it only when you have no token-maker and no Intruder Alarm you could cast this window -
  it is your dig, not your default. They draw two as well, so it is the last card you cast, never
  the first.
  COUNT "Your hand (N cards)" FIRST. Your hand goes back to seven at the end of your turn:
  at N of 6 or more a Skeins you cannot follow with a cast ends in a "Cleanup step" ask that

  When the OPPONENT'S battlefield holds any card that draws them extra cards or punishes card

  When the opponent's battlefield holds a permanent that damages or drains a player for drawing

Lightning Greaves {2} - see Rule 6.

=== COMBAT ===
ATTACK - five checks, in this order. Stop at the first one that answers and do what it says.
CHECK 3 is the one exception: it never ends your answer, it only removes creatures from the
list you are about to send, and then you go on.

CHECK 1 - THE LIFEGAIN WALL BOARD. Read the "Opponent battlefield" line for these four names:
PERIMETER CAPTAIN, PRIDE GUARDIAN, SANGUINE BOND, EXQUISITE BLOOD.
FIRST, THE PART THAT WAS WRONG AND COST A GAME: Sanguine Bond and Exquisite Blood are
ENCHANTMENTS. Tragic Slip, Damnation and both edicts kill CREATURES. Nothing in this deck
removes an enchantment - there is no such card in the sixty. So "wait until they are gone" is
not a plan against those two; it is a decision never to attack again. Held to the letter it kept
fourteen Humans at home from turn 15 until the game ended 42 to 0.
So the check has TWO branches, and which one you are in is decided by which names are on the
line.
BRANCH A - only PERIMETER CAPTAIN or PRIDE GUARDIAN (both are creatures, both are removable):
answer "ATTACK: none" this window and every attack window after it, until removal has taken the
LAST of them off that line. Then attack normally.
BRANCH B - SANGUINE BOND or EXQUISITE BLOOD is on the line: you are never removing it, so you
attack on ARITHMETIC instead of on the name. Count two numbers off the screen: A, the creatures
on your "Your creatures that can attack:" list, and B, their UNTAPPED creatures on their
battlefield line. Every one of their bodies that blocks gains them at most 3 (Pride Guardian) or
2 (Perimeter Captain, once per blocking defender) or 1 (a lifelink blocker), and Sanguine Bond
turns each of those gains into that much off YOUR life.
  - Attack when A is at least 3 times B plus 3. At that width the unblocked damage beats every
    life they can gain from the blocks, and it keeps beating it every turn after.
  - Below that, answer "ATTACK: none", and spend the window making more tokens. A is the number
    you grow; B is the number they have to keep paying for.
  - Lethal overrides both branches: if the total power of what you can send is at or above their
    printed life, send it all - a life gain that happens after they are dead is not a life gain.
This is the ONE place in this file where a damage total does override a "do not attack" rule,
and it overrides it only in BRANCH B, only on the printed life number, and only for lethal.
Why, in the numbers the render gives you: Perimeter Captain gains them 2 life for EVERY
creature with defender that blocks, and each Captain triggers for each blocking defender;
Pride Guardian gains them 3 more when IT blocks; and every Sanguine Bond turns every point
they gain into a point you LOSE. Three defenders blocking with two Captains and two Bonds is
twenty-four life off YOU in one combat step, from an attack in which nothing of yours died.
YOUR ATTACKER TAGS CANNOT SEE THIS. The "[their untapped blockers: ...]" tag reads
"(neither dies)" and that is TRUE - the tag counts deaths, it does not count life, and the
life is what kills you. Twelve attackers into three of these walls took this deck from 20 life
to 8 in one swing and from 8 to dead in the next, while the opponent went down by nine.
An arithmetic answer of the form "we deal 30 and they are at 11, so we win" is NOT an
exception to Check 1. That sum is what you had both times you died here. You do not survive
to collect it. Damage they will never take does not outrank life you will certainly lose.

CHECK 2 - LIGHTMINE FIELD. If their battlefield line names a permanent that damages each
attacker by the number of attackers - Lightmine Field on deck125's board says exactly that -
find the SMALLEST toughness among the creatures you would send and send that many minus one,
biggest power first. Leave the rest home. With 2/2 Vampires that is ONE: the line you write
is "ATTACK: Vampire #1" and nothing after the name. This deck wrote "ATTACK: Vampire,
Vampire" under a plan that said "both Vampires die - this is a bad trade", twice in one game,
and lost four tokens to a field its own plan had priced.

CHECK 3 - THE COLLAPSED TAG AND THE "GANG BLOCK:" CLAUSE. On a wide board their side stops
being listed blocker by blocker and the tag reads instead "they have N untapped creatures that
could block this one, biggest X (you kill it, your attacker lives)", sometimes with a clause
"- GANG BLOCK: their 6 biggest together deal 7, enough to kill this attacker; each result above
is a LONE blocker only".
That parenthesis prices ONE blocker. The GANG BLOCK clause prices what actually happens: any
number of them may block the same attacker together, and your attacker's toughness is measured
against the SUM of their bodies.
  - The tag prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause, or N is 2 or more:
    a TOKEN goes anyway - a 1/1 Human or a 2/2 Vampire costs you nothing and you make more next
    turn - and a CARD stays home. The cards are Thraben Doomsayer, Bloodline Keeper, Lord of
    Lineage and anything wearing Lightning Greaves.
  - N is 1 and no GANG BLOCK clause: the parenthesis is the whole story; use it.
"But the biggest one dies to me" is the sentence the clause exists to refuse - they do not have
to block with the biggest one alone, and against your one real body they will not. This is what
killed another deck's 7/7 in one swing while the render had priced it in advance.
The lethal check overrides this: when the printed opponent life is at or below the total power
of everything you are sending, send it all.
CHECK 3 does not end your answer. Cross the cards it names off the list and go on to CHECK 4.

CHECK 4 - THE COUNT, AND WHAT ONE BLOCKER CAN DO. ONE BLOCKER BLOCKS EXACTLY ONE ATTACKER.
A single 5/5 flier on their side does not "block everything" - it blocks ONE of your creatures
and the rest connect. Count the creatures on the "Your creatures that can attack:" list, then
count their UNTAPPED creatures on their battlefield line. If your number is bigger, you have
that many unblocked attackers and Check 5 applies. Outnumbering them is NOT protection for any
one creature: THEY choose which attacker each blocker takes, and they choose the one their
block wins. It tells you damage gets through; it never tells you a card of yours is safe. Answer "ATTACK: none" on the count alone
only when their untapped creature count is EQUAL TO OR HIGHER THAN the number you are sending
and no attacker tag reads "you kill it, your blocker lives".

CHECK 5 - SEND EVERYTHING. "Your creatures that can attack:" has already dropped every creature
that cannot legally attack, so do not re-check summoning sickness or tapped status yourself.
Send every creature on that list.
Know what you are spending: with Intruder Alarm on the battlefield NOTHING untaps in your untap
step - every battlefield line says so, "[doesn't untap during its controller's untap step]".
Attackers come back untapped only when a creature ENTERS the battlefield, which for this deck
means an untapped token-maker tapping for a token. So before you send everything, find a
token-maker on your battlefield line. If one is there, the whole team is back the moment you
make your next token and Check 5 costs you nothing. If NO token-maker is on your battlefield,
the creatures you send stay tapped until one arrives, and they are not blockers next turn -
send them anyway when the attack advances your clock, but do not plan a block with them.
One hold-back only: do not send a LONE Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper into a board that
can block and kill it - the maker is the engine, the tokens are the attack. Its own row says
when: "[their untapped blockers: ... (both die)]" or "(your attacker dies)" on the maker's
line means "ATTACK: none". This deck sent a lone Doomsayer into "(both die)" with twenty
Humans summoning sick behind it and traded the engine for a Siege-Gang Commander. Send it
once tokens are attacking beside it.

Blocking, in this order. Step 0 decides whether you HAVE a blocker; steps 1 and 2 pick WHICH
creature blocks; the rest decide whether to block at all.
 - STEP 0 - A "B" ROW IS A BLOCKER. If any row on the screen begins "B1.", you have a blocker,
   and its "may block A1, A2" list is the legality list. "We have no blockers" and "I have no
   way to block" are false whenever that row is printed - this deck wrote both, under a B1 row,
   once declining a free kill ("(you kill it, your blocker lives)") at 16 life and once at 4
   life under a header reading "LETHAL if it all connects", where the block on the 4-power
   attacker was the whole difference between 2 life and dead. When the header says LETHAL, the
   FIRST line of your answer is "BLOCKS: B1:A<the attacker with the largest deals N>" - write
   that line before you write anything else, then add blockers by the LETHAL rule below.
   A row on a token-maker reading "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" is a free kill: take it.
 - STEP 1 - WHICH BODY. Every blocker row on the screen is one of your creatures. Spend a TOKEN
   ("Human", "Vampire" - no mana cost printed on the row) before you ever spend a CARD, and
   spend Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper LAST, after every token is used. Tokens are free
   and you make more every turn; the maker is the only reason you make any.
 - STEP 2 - READ THE PARENTHESIS ON THE ROW YOU CHOOSE. "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)"
   means that creature dies and the attacker does not. It is the engine's computed result and
   it is never wrong. Do not write "block X with Bloodline Keeper to kill it" against a row
   that says the Keeper dies and X lives - this deck did exactly that at 12 life against a
   single 3-power attacker, threw away its Bloodline Keeper and its Lightning Greaves to
   prevent three damage, made no token for the rest of the game, and lost with the opponent
   on 4 life. If the row on your maker says "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)", the maker
   does not block.
 - Block with your Human and Vampire TOKENS first - they are free and you make more every turn.
 - WHEN THE PROMPT SAYS LETHAL, ASSIGN TO THE BIGGEST ATTACKERS FIRST. The line
   "Unblocked, these attackers deal up to N - you would be at ... - LETHAL if it all connects"
   is the trigger. Every attacker row prints "deals N". Sort those numbers, put your first
   blocker on the LARGEST one, your second on the next largest, and keep going until you run
   out of blockers or out of attackers. Blocking their smallest attacker is the one assignment
   that is always wrong here: with 10 life against 3+3+3+1, blocking the 1 leaves you at 1 and
   blocking a 3 leaves you at 3, and the difference is a whole turn of the game.
 - MENACE. An attacker row tagged "[menace ...]" is NOT blocked by one creature - a lone
   blocker on it does nothing at all and the attacker's full damage still lands. Put TWO
   blockers on it or none. Never spend your best blocker alone on a menace attacker; the
   prompt says so on the row and it is telling the truth.
 - Add up the damage of every attacker with no blocker on it. If that total is your life total
   minus 4 or more, put a blocker on EVERY attacker - biggest attacker first, Thraben Doomsayer
   and Bloodline Keeper included - and keep assigning until no attacker is left unblocked. A
   token-maker you keep alive at 1 life makes tokens for a game you already lost.
   This rule spends EVERY blocker you have, including your last one. One blocker on one attacker
   still stops that attacker's whole damage - "I only have one, so it does not matter" is how you
   go from 10 life to 2 in one combat step. Assign blockers until you run out of blockers or run
   out of unblocked attackers, whichever comes first.
 - Otherwise your token-makers do NOT block. Take the damage. A blocker line reading "(both
   die)" on Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper is a trade you decline while the combat
   header's "you would be at N" is above 5 - the maker is worth more than any one attacker.
 - A TOKEN IS NOT A MAKER. With a maker on your battlefield line you remake a token next
   window, so a Human or Vampire in front of the largest "deals N" costs you nothing, and when a
   "DRAW PUNISHERS" line is on the screen every point it keeps is a draw you can afford. At 11
   life with "B1. Human" and "B2. Vampire" on the screen this deck answered "BLOCKS: none" to a
   single 3-power attacker; the draw steps that followed cost 2 life a card from 8.

=== MULLIGAN ===
STEP 1 - WHAT DOES THE NEXT LOOK COST? Read the ask's own price line for the "(keeping N)"
number - "having already taken 2 mulligans you will bottom 2 cards on a keep (keeping 5)". Each
look is a fresh seven you must shave by one more card, at the SAME land odds: twenty-four lands
in sixty, so a fresh seven is a two-lander about as often as anything else, and a mulligan
rarely fixes lands - it only shrinks the hand. At (keeping 5) or lower the question is no
longer "is this hand good" but "is a five-card (or four-card) hand more likely to win than
THIS hand shaved at the bottom ask" - and with even ONE land in front of you the answer is
keep, because the bottom ask lets you shed the uncastable cards and keep the land plus your
cheapest spells. Colour is not a reason to ship: the Marsh Flats and Tundras make more than one
colour and the second colour arrives with the second land. Zero lands is the one hand where
a fresh look is worth the card - even then, weigh it: a four-card seven is what you are buying.
WHAT THIS COST: this deck took three mulligans in one game, kept four cards, bottomed both of
its ways to find a token-maker, never put a creature on the battlefield, and lost 42 to 0.
STEP 2 - on your untouched opening seven, or at "(keeping 6)", a mulligan is a fair trade only
by the comparison below.

The two deck facts that decide every mulligan here: you run TWENTY-FOUR lands in sixty cards,
and the cards that actually win - Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w}, Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b},
Intruder Alarm {2}{u} - cost three or four mana. So the question is never "can I play on turn
one", it is "will this hand have three or four lands by the turn it needs them, and does it do
something when they arrive".
The comparison to make, on the header line "Your hand (7 cards), counted by the engine: N
lands, M spells" (read that number, never recount):
 - TWO OR MORE LANDS: keep. With twenty-four lands the third arrives in the first few draws and
   the fourth by about turn five, and a six-card hand after shipping a two-lander is most often
   a two-lander again with one fewer card. Colour is not a reason to ship: read the "Mana
   sources among those lands" and "Playing every land in this hand would cover the cost of:"
   lines, and if either token-maker or Intruder Alarm is on that cover list the hand is fine.
 - ONE LAND: compare the two sides out loud. A one-land seven is a keep only if it is nearly all
   two-mana cards AND that one land makes the colour they need. A one-land seven whose spells
   cost three and four - two Intruder Alarms, Idyllic Tutor, Bloodline Keeper - does nothing
   for four turns and ships.
 - ZERO LANDS, or ALL lands: ship.
 - SIX OR MORE LANDS with one spell: this is a keepable hand, not a mulligan. You bottom the
   extra lands on the keep and you are a four-land hand with your removal spell, which is a
   functioning hand. Shipping it costs you a card and gives you a fresh seven that may be worse.
 - AT FIVE CARDS OR FEWER: STEP 1's odds apply - a hand with a land in it is a keep, spell or
   no spell, because the ship buys the same land odds with one card fewer.

BOTTOMING - the same decision, second ask. After you keep, the prompt says "you took N
mulligans and kept, so you must put N cards from your hand on the BOTTOM" and lists your hand.
This ask is where the hand is actually BUILT: read "You will be left with an N-card hand" and
build the best N. Keep, in this PREFERENCE ORDER, and bottom what is left over - the order
works at any N because it is an order, not a set of floors:
 1. the lands that cover the cheapest spell you are keeping - at N of 3 that is two lands and
    one spell, never three lands; at N of 5 or 6 it is three or four lands and the spells they
    cover, up to FOUR mana;
 2. a token-maker - Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper - if the hand has one;
 3. Idyllic Tutor, which FINDS Intruder Alarm, or Vision Skeins, which digs;
 4. removal;
 5. Intruder Alarm LAST. An Alarm with no maker on the battlefield does nothing at all, and a
    small hand cannot afford a card that waits.
A hand of nothing but lands does nothing at all, and a hand of nothing but four-drops does
nothing for four turns; the order above avoids both at every N. Five lands and two Devour
Flesh at N of 6: the fifth land goes under, not a Devour - four lands cover everything this
deck casts. This deck bottomed the Devour. Those are losses you chose at
this ask, not losses you were dealt.
Bottom a way to find or make a creature only after everything below it in the order is gone.
This deck kept a four-card hand of Marsh
Flats, Tundra, Swamp and Intruder Alarm - it bottomed Idyllic Tutor, Vision Skeins and
Damnation, i.e. both of its ways to find a maker and its only sweeper - and lost that game
without a creature ever hitting its battlefield.
