INTRUDERS OF THRABEN - WBU TOKEN COMBO

You win by putting Intruder Alarm onto the battlefield next to Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline
Keeper and tapping that creature for a token over and over: every token that enters untaps all
creatures, so the tap ability comes straight back. Removal buys the turns; the tokens end the
game. Every turn is the same three actions: play a land, deploy a token-maker or the Alarm, then
tap every maker you own until your board is an army.

=== RULE -1 - THE FIRST ASK OF THE GAME IS THE ONE YOU LOSE MOST OFTEN ===
This rule is first because the ask it governs is first. Two of six games were decided here,
before turn one, and neither of them was close.
The ask reads "Pre-game mulligan decision (London mulligan)" and, from the second look on, it
prints its own price: "having already taken N mulligans you will bottom N cards on a keep
(keeping M)". READ THE M, then answer by the MULLIGAN section below - it is the whole rule and
it held at every look last corpus. In one line: at M of 5 or less, ONE land in front of you is
a keep, because shipping buys the same land odds with one card fewer while the bottom ask sheds
the uncastable cards; only ZERO lands, or a hand of nothing but lands, buys a fresh look.
THE CARRIED PLAN IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT THIS HAND. From the second look on, the ask shows you
"YOUR PLAN (as you last stated it, N windows ago on turn T)" - the stamp is the point: it is your
own older text, about the hand you threw away, not a fact about this one. THAT HOLDS IN EVERY
LATER WINDOW TOO. Where a carried plan and a "{right now: ...}" clause disagree, the clause is
now and the plan is then: this deck was handed back "Morbid applies, Rorix dies" beside a row
reading "(no creature has died this turn, so Morbid does NOT apply)" and "{kills 0 of the 3
legal targets}", believed the plan, and threw the card away at 1 life. A plan that says "mulligan to find 4+ lands" will be handed back to you
at every single look, and answering it again is how a chain runs. Each look is a NEW seven with
its own "counted by the engine: N lands, M spells" header. Decide from that header. If your plan
tells you to mulligan and the header says two or more lands, the plan is out of date - say so
and keep.
A three-land five-card hand beats every hand you have not seen.

=== RULE 0 - PLAY A LAND, EVERY TURN, BEFORE YOU DO ANYTHING ELSE ===
The land drop is its own question. It arrives as "Land drop: which land do you play now, if
any?" with one Play line per land in your hand and one "Play no land right now".
If ANY "Play" line is listed, take one. Every turn. Answer "Play no land right now" only when
that is the only option on the screen.
There is no board and no plan that makes holding a land in hand better than playing it. This
deck has twenty-four lands and its engine costs three and four mana; every land you did not
play is a turn you spend at your old mana total.
"Land drop: NOT yet used this turn" on the CURRENT SITUATION block means the drop is still
there. It is not spent by casting a spell, and taking it does not cost you a cast.
READ THE COSTS IN MANA, NOT IN SYMBOLS. {2}{u} is THREE mana - a generic 2 plus one blue.
{2}{b}{b} is FOUR. {1}{w}{w} is THREE. Compare that number to the "Mana available: N total"
line, which already counts every untapped source for you. Two untapped lands do NOT cast
Intruder Alarm; a two-land board held for seven turns with three lands in hand, under a plan
that re-stated "cast Intruder Alarm using Tundra and Underground Sea" every window, lost that
game 42 to 0 with no token ever made. If your plan names a spell you cannot afford this window,
the fix is the land drop, not another pass.
A CHOICE NAMES A NUMBERED ROW ON THIS WINDOW. A card on your hand line with no "Cast" row is
there because a pip cannot be paid - read the mana line's per-colour count ("{W} 1" cannot pay
{1}{w}{w}). Naming it costs a re-ask: EIGHT last corpus, every one of them naming Bloodline
Keeper or Vision Skeins off a menu that did not list it (see WHAT TO CAST, below).
WHICH LAND: when a spell in your hand is castable THIS turn off the land you play, play the land
that enters UNTAPPED - Underground Sea, Tundra, Scrubland, Plains, Swamp. Arcane Sanctum enters
tapped and Isolated Chapel enters tapped unless you control a Plains or Swamp: play those on a
turn you were not going to spend all your mana. This deck once played a Sanctum over an
Underground Sea with Intruder Alarm {2}{u} castable off three, and passed twice with two mana.
This applies hardest in COMBAT: at 9 life this deck built a survival line on Tribute to Hunger
with "Mana available: 1 total" on the same screen and Tribute costing three. Check "Mana
available: N total" BEFORE you build a line around a card in hand.
A CARD ELITE SPELLBINDER EXILED FROM YOUR HAND IS STILL YOURS TO CAST, AND THE ROW SAYS SO IN
WORDS. It comes back as "Cast Damnation {4}{b}{b} [from exile] {castable from exile - a legal
cast, not a dead row; it costs {2} more than printed, already counted in the cost shown}" - the
same card at the same cast-list entry, with the surcharge already inside the number you compare
to "Mana available". Nothing about that row is a reason to skip it: this deck read one for four
windows writing "Intruder Alarm is exiled, I cannot cast it", and at 4 life cast a Thraben
Doomsayer over an exiled Damnation reading "destroys 3 ... 0 of yours". An exiled card at entry
0 is still CL0.

=== RULE 1 - THE TOKEN LINES, AND WHEN TO STOP TAKING THEM ===
These are the three lines, exactly as you will see them. A second copy of the same card adds a
handle - "Thraben Doomsayer #1", "Thraben Doomsayer #2" - same line, same rule:
  "Create human with Thraben Doomsayer [cost: Tap]"
  "Create vampire with Bloodline Keeper [cost: Tap]"
  "Create vampire with Lord of Lineage [cost: Tap]"
BEFORE you take one, read M: the repeat row prints it first - "[you control M creatures right
now; ...]" - and your own battlefield line prints the same number after "of which". The
"activated this turn N times" count belongs to ONE COPY and ONE TURN; it is not M.
THE STOP IS A HEADCOUNT, BECAUSE POWER CANNOT MEASURE THIS LOOP. With Intruder Alarm out the
line comes back after every activation and the new tokens are summoning sick, so the "power of
creatures that are not summoning sick" number does NOT MOVE when you take it. A stop written on
that number can never be reached: this deck took the line 1,868 times in ONE turn (1,540 and 214
in two others) writing "on my next main phase, attack with all creatures to win the game" every
time, and never reached that main phase.
Use the two numbers that DO move, both printed in every window:
  - M = the number after "of which" on YOUR battlefield line: "Your battlefield (K permanents
    listed, of which M are creatures)". Every activation adds one to M, summoning sick or not.
  - L = "Opponent life:", and C = the number after "of which" on the OPPONENT'S battlefield
    line, i.e. how many blockers they own.
Then:
  - M is BELOW L + C + 3: TAKE THE LINE. Take it again every time it comes back.
  - M reaches L + C + 3: STOP. Answer "CHOICE: 0 (pass)".
    Every token is at least a 1/1, so at L + C + 3 bodies they cannot block enough of them to
    live through your next attack, and a body past that number wins nothing.
  - THE REPEAT ROW IS HOW YOU TAKE THE LINE. From the second activation of a turn on, the
    screen adds a second row: "Create human with Thraben Doomsayer, repeated N times, then stop
    [you name N on the CHOICE line, e.g. "CHOICE: 2 (Create human with Thraben Doomsayer x<N>)";
    the engine performs it N times ... N is at most 200]" - the row's own example shows the
    format; the number is YOURS, from the PLAN line's subtraction. Take THAT row, ONCE, with
        N = (L + C + 3) - M
    and write N the way the row's own example writes it: the short name, a space, then
    "x" and the number, inside the parentheses -
        CHOICE: 2 (Create human with Thraben Doomsayer x17)
    Name the number the first time: a take with no number is asked again, once, and that is a
    window spent. The next window after the repeat row is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)"; the log will
    show "(you named 17 repeats of it)".
    DO THE SUBTRACTION ON THE SCREEN BEFORE YOU WRITE N, and carry ALL FOUR numbers in your PLAN
    line in this shape, blanks filled from the screen - L and C separately, so the stop can be
    checked against them:
        PLAN: L <opponent life>, C <their creature count>, stop <L+C+3>; M is <M> now;
        this window: <x<N> / pass>
    THE PLAN LINE MAY NOT CONTAIN THE WORD "PASS", OR "STOP REACHED", ON A WINDOW WHERE YOUR
    CHOICE LINE TAKES A ROW. "this window:" is the ONLY place the action is named, and what it
    names must be the same action the CHOICE line above it named. A plan reading "creating 2
    humans to reach M=30 (stop reached)" beside "CHOICE: 2 (... x2)", or "Stop reached. Pass
    token creation." beside "CHOICE: 1 (Create human ...)", is read by the engine as a reply
    that reverses itself: it is stamped, thrown away and asked again. That cost this deck FIVE
    re-asks in one game last corpus and two more in another, and in four of the seven the
    re-ask came back with the identical answer, so the only thing spent was the window. If the
    stop IS reached, the CHOICE line is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)" and the plan says pass. If it is not,
    neither line says pass.
    RECOMPUTE THE STOP FROM THIS WINDOW'S ROW EVERY TIME; a stop carried from the previous
    window is a number, not a rule. Take the repeat row ONCE per turn and then pass. If the two
    numbers in your own plan line disagree, the arithmetic is right and the sentence is wrong.
    When (L + C + 3) - M is 0 or less the repeat row is not yours: CHOICE: 0 (pass).
    THE NUMBER ON YOUR CHOICE LINE IS THE PLAN LINE'S ANSWER, NOT A GUESS WRITTEN BEFORE IT.
    Your reply's first line is the CHOICE and the subtraction comes after it, so do the
    subtraction BEFORE you write that line: if the row's "you control M creatures right now" is
    already at or past your stop, the first line is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)" - not "x1", not "x3", and
    never "x0", which the engine carried out ONE time. Windows that wrote "this window: pass" in
    the PLAN under a CHOICE naming x0, x1, x3 and x25 were each carried out, and the x25 put 58
    creatures on a board whose stop was 30. The last leak is the first line disagreeing with the
    plan beneath it - and, last corpus, taking x17 and x13 at M of 45 against a stop of 34.
PASSING IS ALWAYS AVAILABLE: the last row reads "0. Pass priority (take no action this window)",
and when M has reached L + C + 3 that row is the answer, whatever else is listed.
YOUR INSTANTS ARE NOW OFFERED ON THE OPPONENT'S TURN, AT EVERY PHASE OF IT. A "Casting decision
(<phase>, opponent's turn)" ask with Tragic Slip, Tribute to Hunger or Devour Flesh on it is a
real window, and it is the BEST window for those three: you see their board after they have
committed to it. Two of the three casts this deck made on an opponent's turn last corpus were
its best plays of the game (a Tragic Slip on an Elite Spellbinder mid-main-phase, a Tribute to
Hunger in their upkeep for +2 life and their only creature). The same ask reappears at Upkeep,
Draw, Main 1, Combat begins, Attackers, Blockers, Combat damage, Combat ends, Main 2, End and
Cleanup with the same rows, and the LAST row of every one of them now says how to stop that, in these words:
"Pass priority, and do not ask me again this turn unless the board changes (any change re-opens
this window; you give up no cast)". When the only cast row is dead - "{right now: they control 0
creatures - at 0 this does nothing}", or "{kills 0 of the N legal targets}" - take that row at the
FIRST dead window of the turn, not the fifth. It is not a last resort you reach after declining:
this deck took it 32 times last corpus and took it LATE almost every time - turn 41 declined ten
identical menus and then held on the Cleanup ask, turn 55 declined eleven and held at End, turns
43 and 57 declined twelve and thirteen and never held at all. A hold taken in the Cleanup step
saves nothing; a hold taken in their upkeep saves the whole turn. The board changing brings the
window straight back, so holding early costs you no window you would have used.
Timing: the tokens you make CANNOT attack until your next turn, so making them early in your own
turn buys you nothing this turn and leaves them standing through the opponent's whole turn. Fire
the chain on the OPPONENT'S turn - the lines are offered there, in their upkeep, their main phase
and their attack step. A token made then blocks that same turn and attacks on your very next turn,
and a sweeper cast on their turn catches fewer of them.
Fire on your OWN turn only when the creatures you are about to attack with are already on the
board, or when the opponent has no untapped land left.
WITH THE ALARM IN HAND AND CASTABLE THIS TURN, THE MAKER WAITS UNTAPPED UNTIL THE ALARM HAS
RESOLVED. Under Intruder Alarm a tapped creature untaps only when a creature ENTERS; nothing
of theirs may enter for a whole turn. This deck tapped its Doomsayer in its own upkeep, cast
the Alarm in the main phase after it, and then went through the opponent's entire next turn
with no "Create human" line on any screen - the Doomsayer sat tapped, Brutal Cathar exiled
it, and the game ended two turns later at -10. Land, Alarm, THEN the first tap - or, with
the Alarm not castable this turn, tap freely: a maker with no Alarm untaps on its own.
YOUR OWN UPKEEP IS THE WORST WINDOW AGAINST A SWEEPER. Tokens made in your own upkeep sit
through their whole turn before they attack: the 323 Vampires this deck made in its own turn-9
upkeep were swept on turn 10 and the game ended 0 to 62. Against a deck whose log shows
Starstorm, Supreme Verdict or Damnation (deck130, deck125, the mirror), the answer in your upkeep
is "CHOICE: 0 (pass)" and the chain fires in THEIR turn. Against a deck with no sweeper the
own-turn chain has won four games running at the L + C + 3 stop.
A token you make this turn CANNOT ATTACK this turn. Its battlefield line says so:
"[summoning sick - cannot attack this turn, but CAN block]". It can block right away, and it
attacks on your NEXT turn.
So when you plan, count only the creatures whose lines do NOT say "summoning sick". A plan that
says "attack with all 12 for lethal" is wrong on the turn you made those 12 - the attack list
will offer you one creature. Make the tokens anyway; they block this turn and they swing next
turn.

=== RULE 2 - MARSH FLATS IS NOT MANA UNTIL YOU CRACK IT; CRACK IT THE FIRST WINDOW IT IS LISTED ===
A Marsh Flats on your battlefield makes NO mana, and its own row says so:
"[cost: Tap, Life, Sacrifice] {this land makes no mana - crack it for a land: your untapped
sources go from N to N+1}". The "Mana available" line does not count it and never will until you
take that line. So the first window that lists "Put a card onto the battlefield with Marsh Flats
targeting ..." is the window - on either player's turn, in any phase; the row does the arithmetic
for you. Passing it in six windows across four turns lost a game at -2 with no token made.
WHICH LAND: read "Your hand" for the pips your spells need TWICE - Thraben Doomsayer needs
{w}{w}, Bloodline Keeper {b}{b}, Damnation {b}{b} - and take the dual that supplies both of
them with what is already on your battlefield:
  "targeting Underground Sea"  - makes {U} and {B}
  "targeting Tundra"           - makes {W} and {U}
  "targeting Scrubland"        - makes {W} and {B}
A colour at 0 on the mana line comes first (Intruder Alarm {2}{u} and Vision Skeins {1}{u} are
the reason: only 10 of 24 lands make blue). After that, the hand decides: with Tundra and
Underground Sea out and a Doomsayer plus a Keeper in hand, Scrubland is the fetch - it is the
second W AND the second B. This deck fetched a second Sea there; the Doomsayer sat in hand
from turn 1 to turn 14 with "{W} 1" on the mana line, and the game was lost at 0.
A basic only against a deck whose log shows Molten Rain, Lay Waste or Dwarven Blastminer
(deck130), and only when the colour you are short of is {W} or {B}: those three spells hit
NONBASIC lands only. THE CARVE-OUT STOPS AT BLUE - a Plains and a Swamp make no {U}, so when
{U} is the pip missing from "colours you can make", the fetch is "targeting Tundra" or
"targeting Underground Sea" whatever their deck destroys: a land they blow up next turn still
casts your spell this turn. Holding TWO Intruder Alarms with no {U} on the mana line, this deck
once answered "targeting Swamp" over those two rows and died at 1 life with both Alarms in hand.
Against every other deck, never a basic while a dual is listed.

=== RULE 3 - COUNT BOTH BOARDS BEFORE DAMNATION ===
DAMNATION DESTROYS CREATURES AND NOTHING ELSE. Its own row prints the whole card: "Destroy all
creatures. They can't be regenerated." An enchantment, an artifact, a planeswalker and a land
all survive it. "Cast Damnation to remove Sanguine Bond" is a sentence this deck wrote in its
own plan while holding fourteen Humans; the Damnation killed its fourteen Humans, the Sanguine
Bond was still on the battlefield in the very next window, and it lost that game 42 to 0 having
never rebuilt. Nothing in this deck removes an enchantment. Read the row before you believe a
card does something its text does not say.
Damnation destroys ALL creatures, yours included. Both battlefield lines print a creature count
right after the words "of which" - read YOUR number and THEIR number, and compare them.
Cast Damnation when THEIR number is bigger than yours. That is the whole test.
Equal numbers are not a reason to cast it: keep the Damnation and take another action.
When YOUR number is the bigger one, spend the window on a creature, a token activation or a
removal spell instead, and keep the Damnation - your creatures are how you win, and a wipe that
kills two of yours to kill one of theirs has handed them the game.
Your number is 0 and theirs is 2 or more: that is the best Damnation there is. Take it, and take
it BEFORE a token-maker and before Vision Skeins - it is CL0 of the cast list below. A maker
cast into attackers does not live to make a token; the Damnation that clears them buys the
turns the maker needs.
WRITE THE THREE NUMBERS BEFORE YOU WRITE THE CHOICE. Whenever "Cast Damnation" is on the menu,
your PLAN line carries this, blanks filled from the row:
    PLAN: Damnation: N <their number>, K <able to attack>, M <mine>; <cast / hold>
M 0 and K 2 or more is "cast" and it is the first line of your reply, above a maker. FOUR CORPORA
RUNNING have broken this and every one lost the game it happened in - "destroys 2 ... 0 of yours"
at 14 life, "destroys 5 ... 0 of yours" at 1 life, and a Thraben Doomsayer cast over "destroys 3
... 0 of yours" at 4 life. Write "Damnation: N <>, K <>, M <>" and then read your own M: if it is
0 and K is 2 or more, the Damnation is the first line of your reply. M above N is "hold": this
deck cast a Damnation reading "destroys 1 of their creature ... 2 of yours" under a plan that said
"board is clear" - it cleared its own Doomsayer and Human for one Elite Spellbinder.
AND WRITING THE THREE NUMBERS IS NOT THE SAME AS OBEYING THEM. Last corpus this deck finally wrote
the line - "PLAN: Damnation: N 0, K 0, M 0; cast." - and cast it anyway, on turn 40, into an empty
board, against a deck that had countered everything else it owned. N of 0 is "hold" no matter what
the second half of your own sentence says.
THE ROW COUNTS FOR YOU. "Cast Damnation" prints "{right now: destroys N of their creatures (K
without a restriction against attacking), M of yours}" on your turn and "(K able to attack
right now)" on theirs; when every one of theirs is a wall it prints "(all of them carry a
restriction against attacking)" instead, and that is K = 0. N is their number, M is yours, and
K is how many of theirs can actually hit you. Two 0/4 walls are N = 2 with K = 0: a sweeper
spent on them is a sweeper you do not have when six Vampires arrive. So at K of 0, or at N of
exactly 1, a Tragic Slip or an edict on the same menu that kills the one that matters is the
cast and the Damnation stays in hand.
THE ONE K = 0 BOARD YOU DO SWEEP: walls named PERIMETER CAPTAIN or PRIDE GUARDIAN. CHECK 1 in
COMBAT forbids you to attack while they stand, and Damnation is the removal that takes them
off that line - this deck swept two Captains and a Battlement at K = 0 and won at -208.
"DESTROYS 0 OF THEIR CREATURES" IS A CARD THROWN AWAY. An empty board needs no clearing, and
the sweeper is then not there when it matters. At N = 0 the Damnation stays in hand, whatever
the plan says - and your SECOND main phase is the same board: this deck has twice declined that
row in main phase 1 and cast the same Damnation off the same row later in the same turn.
ENTRY 0 HAS NO EXCEPTIONS FOR YOUR OWN CARDS: at 13 life this deck cast Idyllic Tutor over
"destroys 2 ... 0 of yours", at 9 life cast Intruder Alarm over "destroys 4 ... 0 of yours", and
cast the Damnation at 5. Eight life for two cards that could wait.
THE STOP, and why (this rule was broken at 14 against 6): if YOUR "of which N are creatures"
number is 4 or more, do not cast Damnation at all, whatever theirs says. Four bodies is a board
you win with; there is no opposing board this deck needs to trade four of its own creatures to
answer. Take a token activation, a removal spell or "Cast nothing right now" instead.

=== RULE 4 - TRAGIC SLIP KILLS TOUGHNESS 1 ===
Tragic Slip gives -1/-1. That kills a creature whose battlefield line shows toughness 1 - a
(2/1), a (1/1), a (0/1). Against anything with toughness 2 or more it shrinks the creature and
it lives.
THE ROW NOW PRINTS THE RESULT. Every Tragic Slip target row ends in the engine's own verdict -
"{right now: -1/-1 - DIES}" or "{right now: -1/-1 - SURVIVES (toughness 2, -1/-1 more kills it)}",
and with Morbid live "{right now: -13/-13 - DIES}". Read that clause and nothing else; do not
re-derive it from the "(P/T)". Take a "- DIES" row, and with several listed take the biggest
power. At 6 life this deck answered a window whose three rows all read "SURVIVES".
THE DECISION IS MADE AT THE CAST ROW, NOT THE TARGET ROW. The cast row prints
"- legal targets right now: <names>"; the target ask that follows it HAS NO PASS ROW - once you
answer "Cast Tragic Slip" the card is spent on one of the names it listed. So before you take
the cast row, read the KILL COUNT on it. "{kills 0 of the 3 legal targets at -1/-1}" means every
name on that row's list lives through the spell - the engine has done the toughness arithmetic
for you on every target at once, and that row is "Cast nothing right now". A row naming a victim
instead - "{kills: Elite Spellbinder}" - is the cast; at 1 life this deck cast the Slip off a
"{kills 0 of the 3 legal targets}" row and spent its last card on a 6/5 that survived.
The one upgrade: a line ending in "died" under the CURRENT turn header in the GAME LOG makes the
row read "{right now: -13/-13 (a creature died this turn, so Morbid applies)}", which kills
anything on the list - take the biggest printed power. That is the window this card is for.
No toughness-1 target on the list and no "died" line under this turn: answer "Cast nothing
right now" and keep the Slip. It costs {B} and it will kill something on a later turn.

=== RULE 5 - THE TWO EDICTS ARE DIFFERENT CARDS ===
IF YOUR PLAN NAMES WHICH CREATURE AN EDICT WILL REMOVE, THE PLAN IS ALREADY WRONG. Check your
own sentence before you check the board: "Tribute to Hunger on Sigarda", "Tribute to Hunger on
Briarbridge Tracker", "Tribute to Hunger to remove Sorin" are all sentences this deck has
written, and all three are impossible. You NEVER pick. The row itself prints the whole card -
{card text: "Target opponent sacrifices a creature of their choice. You gain life equal to that
creature's toughness."} - and "of their choice" is the entire rule. The only thing you choose is
whether to cast it at all, and the answer to that is the count of their creatures.
"Cast Tribute to Hunger {2}{b}" - the opponent sacrifices a creature and YOU gain life equal to
its toughness. This is the one you want.
Cast it at the opponent whenever it is listed and their battlefield line shows exactly ONE
creature. With two or more of their creatures listed, they hand over their worst one and keep the
good one - take Tragic Slip, Damnation or a token activation instead. This deck cast it at FIVE
of their creatures and at THREE of their creatures in the same corpus, and got a body it did not
want both times.
"Cast Devour Flesh {1}{b}" - the player you name sacrifices a creature AND THAT SAME PLAYER
gains the life. Naming the opponent does NOT gain YOU anything: "remove their blocker and gain
4 life" is the sentence this deck wrote in its own plan on turn 3 while handing a Sanguine Bond
deck four life off a 0/4 wall. You gain life from Tribute to Hunger. You never gain life from
Devour Flesh. That said, giving them the life is still the right price when it takes their ONLY
body: use Devour Flesh on the opponent when Tribute to Hunger is not on the list and they have
exactly ONE creature - then the sacrifice takes their only body and the life they gain is worth
it.
AN EDICT CANNOT TOUCH A PLANESWALKER. Both rows say "sacrifices a creature". A planeswalker on
their battlefield line - it prints "[planeswalker] [counters: Nx loyalty]" - is not a creature
and is not a legal outcome of either card. "Use Tribute to Hunger to remove Sorin" is a sentence
this deck wrote on a board with SEVEN of their creatures listed; the opponent handed over a 1/1
token and Sorin kept ticking up. If a planeswalker is what is beating you, an edict is not the
answer and neither is any other card in this deck: build the board and attack it.
Before any edict, run this in order: (1) read N off the edict row itself - it prints "{right
now: they control N creatures - <name> is sacrificed ...}" at N = 1, "- they choose which one"
at N of 2 or more, and "- at 0 this does nothing" at N = 0. N is the only number that matters.
(2) If N is not exactly 1, do not cast an edict - not at 0, not at 2 or more. The N = 0 row
prints its own verdict - "{right now: they control 0 creatures - at 0 this does nothing}" - and
this deck answered that row's number on turn 44 of a game it was losing, off a menu whose other
two rows were a dead Damnation and "Cast nothing right now". A row that says it does nothing does
nothing. "- they choose
which one" is the row telling you N is 2 or more and that the body you want is the one they will
keep: this deck cast Tribute to Hunger twice and Devour Flesh once off that exact clause in one
game, against a board whose Rorix Bladewing was still there afterwards every time, and lost it. (3) A creature
listed under "ON THE STACK, waiting to resolve" is NOT on the battlefield and does not change
N. Naming a creature spell on the stack in your PLAN is the mistake itself: at N=0 the opponent
sacrifices nothing and you have thrown away the card.
AT N = 1 THE EDICT IS THE CAST, NOT THE SLIP. Offered "they control 1 creature - Silverquill
Silencer is sacrificed" twice at two mana, this deck answered "Cast nothing", then shrank the
3/2 with a Tragic Slip and took nine more damage from it in a game lost at -2. A toughness-2
creature is the edict's job, not the Slip's.
Never cast an edict when the opponent's life is at or below the life it gives them. Killing
their last creature and moving them from 1 life to 4 costs you a game you had already won.
Do not spend an edict on a creature that is not hurting you: if their only creature reads
"[defender]" or has power 0, keep the edict - you would pay a card and hand them life equal to
its toughness for something that was never going to kill you. A 1-power creature is NOT in that class when its "{card text:}" gives the rest of their
board something - mana, counters, +1/+1 - it is the engine and it grows: passing "they control 1
creature - Katilda, Dawnhart Prime is sacrificed" on turn 3 for a Vision Skeins left a 3/3 by
turn 7 behind a four-wide board by turn 9, in a game that ended at -10.
Both edicts give life to the player who sacrifices. THE SCREEN NAMES THE DECK THAT PUNISHES YOU
FOR IT, IN ITS OWN BLOCK: "LIFE-TO-DAMAGE CONVERTER on the battlefield: theirs - Sanguine Bond.
While it is in play, life ITS CONTROLLER gains also makes the other player lose that much life."
With that block on the screen an edict at the opponent is a two-way swing against you: the row's
own "they gain N" becomes N on their total AND N off yours: at 14 against 26, with that block
printed and their only creature an "Overgrown Battlement (0/4) [defender]", a Devour Flesh made
it 10 against 30 in one window, for a wall that had never attacked.
So: the converter block on the screen, or a defender / power-0 creature as the N=1 body, is
"Cast nothing right now" - and both at once is not a decision, it is a mistake with two names.
"Yourself (player, life" is the right answer only when the creature you give up is a Human
or Vampire TOKEN you can remake next turn. Your Bloodline Keeper, your Thraben Doomsayer and
your flying Vampires stay on the battlefield.

=== RULE 6 - EQUIP LIGHTNING GREAVES ONCE PER GREAVES ===
An equip line for Lightning Greaves costs {0}, so the game re-offers it at every window. Take it
ONE time per Greaves, on Bloodline Keeper if it is on the battlefield, otherwise on Thraben
Doomsayer. The done-signal is on the battlefield line: a creature reading "{attached: Lightning
Greaves #1}" already has it and that Greaves is finished. At every later re-offer take a
"[cost: Tap]" line if one is listed, otherwise answer 0 (pass). Equip again only when the
equipped creature has DIED, or an unequipped token-maker has just arrived.
AND DO NOT CAST THE GREAVES ONTO AN EMPTY BOARD. On a battlefield line reading "of which 0 are
creatures" the Greaves equips nothing and the {2} is a token-maker you did not deploy; this deck
cast it there on turn 15 of a game it lost with two lands left.
Greaves gives haste (a maker taps for a token the turn it lands) and shroud (that creature can
no longer be targeted by anything, your own spells included).

=== WHAT TO CAST WHEN SEVERAL THINGS ARE LISTED ===
THESE LABELS ARE "CL0" TO "CL8" AND THEY ARE NOT CHOICE NUMBERS. They are a PRIORITY ORDER over
cards; the number you write on your CHOICE line is the number printed beside that card ON THIS
WINDOW'S MENU, and a card that is not on this window's menu has no number and cannot be named.
Last corpus this deck wrote "CHOICE: 1 (Cast Bloodline Keeper)" three times and "CHOICE: 7 (Cast
Vision Skeins)" twice with neither card on the menu - those are this list's old entry numbers,
answered as if they were rows. Eight windows were spent re-asking, and one re-ask came back
"CHOICE: 7 (Cast Bloodline Keeper)", which is neither. Find the card ON THE MENU first; the CL
label only tells you WHICH of the cards that ARE on the menu you take.
Take the highest entry on this list that appears in your casting menu:
 CL0. "Cast Damnation {2}{b}{b}" - when YOUR battlefield line reads "of which 0 are creatures"
    and the Damnation row's K - "(K without a restriction against attacking)" - reads 2 or
    more. Nothing else first: not a maker, not a dig, not the Tutor, not the Alarm. (K at 0,
    or their count exactly 1: Rule 3 - the one-for-one instead; K at 0 on Perimeter Captain or
    Pride Guardian: sweep them, Rule 3.)
 CL1. "Cast Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b}" - when no token-maker is on your battlefield.
 CL2. "Cast Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w}" - when no token-maker is on your battlefield.
 CL3. "Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}" - when no Intruder Alarm is on your battlefield.
    Intruder Alarm on an empty battlefield does NOTHING. A token-maker with no Alarm still
    makes one token a turn. The maker goes first, every time.
    AND THE ALARM GOES BEFORE THE SECOND MAKER. CL1 and CL2 say "when no token-maker is on
    your battlefield": once one IS there, they are done and this entry is next - before a second
    maker, before Lightning Greaves. The Alarm with one maker is the loop; a second maker
    without it is one more token a turn.
 CL4. "Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" - when no Intruder Alarm is on your battlefield or in your hand.
    Read BOTH lines before you take it: "Cast Idyllic Tutor to find Intruder Alarm (already on
    battlefield, but must cast it to dig for Bloodline Keeper)" is a sentence this deck wrote
    with the Alarm on its line, and the reveal list marked every creature "[does NOT qualify]".
 CL5. "Cast Lightning Greaves {2}" - ONLY when your own battlefield line reads "of which 1 is
    a creature" or more AND that creature is a token-maker. Read that clause before you take
    this line: "of which 0 are creatures" means the Greaves equips nothing and is a wasted turn -
    keep it for the turn a maker lands. This deck has now cast it at "of which 0 are creatures"
    four times, the latest on turn 15 against deck130 with three lands left standing.
 CL6. A removal spell, by Rules 3, 4 and 5.
 CL7. "Cast Vision Skeins {1}{u}".
 CL8. "Cast nothing right now" - the LAST entry, and correct only when no entry above appeared in
    your casting menu. Every card you can cast has a line above this one.
WHEN EVERY CAST ROW ON THE MENU IS PRICED DEAD, ENTRY 8 IS THE PLAY, AND IT IS NOT INACTION.
Read the {right now: ...} and [second copy: ...] clauses on every row before you pick: a menu
whose rows all read "destroys 0 of their creatures ... 0 of yours", "they control 0 creatures -
at 0 this does nothing" and "[second copy: ... a second copy changes nothing]" is a menu with
nothing on it, and "Cast nothing right now" is the only row that keeps a card. Last corpus this
deck, holding a full grip against a counter deck, spent FIVE cards off such menus in one game -
two dead Intruder Alarms at turns 42 and 58, a third at turn 36, a Damnation at "destroys 0" on
turn 40 and a Tribute to Hunger at "they control 0" on turn 44 - and wrote the reason in its own
plan: "cast second Intruder Alarm (dead, but only way to proceed)". There is no rule in this file
that says you must cast something. A card in hand is a card you still have when the board changes;
a card spent off a dead row is gone, and the board that made it dead has not moved.

=== INTRUDER ALARM AND IDYLLIC TUTOR ===
"Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}": it is the engine, and it is CL3 on the cast list above -
behind both token-makers, because an Alarm with no maker out does nothing. One copy does the
whole job; with an Alarm already on your battlefield, spend the window on a creature, a removal
spell, or "Cast nothing right now".
THE SECOND-COPY BRACKET IS NOT PERMISSION. A second Alarm's row reads "Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}
[second copy: you already control Intruder Alarm; both stay on the battlefield - no legend rule]".
The bracket now ends in the verdict itself: "... no legend rule, but its effect is already on the
battlefield and a second copy changes nothing". That clause answers "is this LEGAL" - it is - and
then tells you it does nothing. It does not: the Alarm's two lines ("creatures don't untap",
"whenever a creature enters, untap all creatures") are already true from the first copy, so the
second changes no number on any screen. This deck has now cast a second Alarm under that bracket
SEVEN times across four games - three of them last corpus in one game, at turns 36, 42 and 58,
the last of those at 7 life - and each time its own plan said the row was dead first: "second
copy is dead", "dead, but only way to proceed", "legal but redundant". Writing DEAD next to the
row and then answering its number is the whole failure. Read the bracket as the word DEAD and
then answer a DIFFERENT number.
It is symmetric: no creature untaps in its controller's untap step, theirs or yours, and ANY
creature entering - theirs or yours - untaps everything. With a token-maker in play that is
pure profit. With no token-maker in play, a creature of yours that attacks stays tapped, so get
Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper down before you rely on attacking.
"Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" searches for an ENCHANTMENT, and the only enchantment in this sixty
is Intruder Alarm; every other row on the reveal list reads "[does NOT qualify]". IT CANNOT
FIND A CREATURE. The row's own bracket tells you which of three cases you are in, and only one
of them is a cast:
  "[finds only an enchantment card - still in your library, not on your battlefield or in your
   hand: Intruder Alarm]"  -> a LIVE Tutor. Cast it and name Intruder Alarm.
  "[finds only an enchantment card - every enchantment left in your library is a copy of one you
   already control or hold: Intruder Alarm]"  -> DEAD. It fetches a second Alarm, which does
   nothing (see below). "Cast nothing right now" over it.
  "[finds only an enchantment card - none left in your library]"  -> DEAD. There is no card for
   it to find at all.
This deck read the middle bracket at turn 12 with an Alarm already on its line and cast the Tutor
anyway. An Alarm in "Your hand" is the same verdict - Vision Skeins is the card that digs for a
maker.
THE ONE TIME A DEAD TUTOR IS A PLAY: against a deck that counters (deck125 - Cancel, Essence
Scatter, Fall of the Gavel, Dream Fracture in its log), see the counter paragraph under KEY
CARDS - it is cast FIRST in the same main phase as a maker, to eat the counter.
THE ALARM YOU TUTOR MAY NOT BE CASTABLE THIS TURN. The engine taps your mana for you, and it
paid a Tutor's {2}{w} with BOTH Underground Seas when a Swamp and two Scrublands would have done
it; the tutored Alarm then sat in hand for a whole turn cycle with "{U} 0" on the mana line.
Read "sources that can make each: ... {U} N" before you cast the Tutor: at {U} 2 or less, plan
the Alarm for NEXT turn, and spend what the Tutor leaves on a maker - "Cast Thraben Doomsayer
{1}{w}{w}" was on the menu with three W/B sources open, twice, and was not taken.

=== COMBAT ===
ATTACK - five checks, in this order. Stop at the first one that answers and do what it says.
CHECK 3 is the one exception: it never ends your answer, it only removes creatures from the
list you are about to send, and then you go on.

CHECK 1 - THE LIFEGAIN WALL BOARD. Read the "Opponent battlefield" line for these four names:
PERIMETER CAPTAIN, PRIDE GUARDIAN, SANGUINE BOND, EXQUISITE BLOOD.
FIRST: Sanguine Bond and Exquisite Blood are ENCHANTMENTS. Tragic Slip, Damnation and both
edicts kill CREATURES; nothing in this sixty removes an enchantment. So "wait until they are
gone" is not a plan against those two - held to the letter it kept fourteen Humans at home from
turn 15 until the game ended 42 to 0. The check has THREE branches, and which one you are in is
decided by which names are on the line.
BRANCH A - only PERIMETER CAPTAIN or PRIDE GUARDIAN (both are creatures, both are removable):
answer "ATTACK: none" this window and every attack window after it, until removal has taken the
LAST of them off that line. Then attack normally.
BRANCH B2 - BOTH SANGUINE BOND AND EXQUISITE BLOOD ARE ON THE LINE. This is not an arithmetic
board at all; it is a closed loop, and the render says so in words: "their life LOOP is in play:
any life they gain or you lose in this combat chains without limit - fatal to you, not a trade",
and "Both halves of their life LOOP are in play". Believe it. One point of life gained by them -
a Pride Guardian blocking, a Perimeter Captain blocking, one lifelink blocker connecting - starts
a chain that ends at YOUR zero, and it resolves in the declare-blockers step, BEFORE your damage
is dealt. The width rule below does not apply: this deck stood at 58 attackers against 6 blockers
with them on 28 life, and the attack still loses, because they never take the damage. Nothing in
this sixty removes an enchantment. So: answer "ATTACK: none" while both are on the line and any
untapped creature they own can gain them life, keep your bodies home as blockers, and the ONE
attack that is real is an attack no life-gaining body can block - if every untapped creature they
control is tapped or gone, and your total power is at or above their printed life, send it.
BRANCH B - exactly ONE of SANGUINE BOND or EXQUISITE BLOOD is on the line: the gain is finite, so
you are never removing it and you attack on ARITHMETIC instead of on the name. Count two numbers off the screen: A, the creatures
on your "Your creatures that can attack:" list, and B, their UNTAPPED creatures on their
battlefield line. Every one of their bodies that blocks gains them at most 3 (Pride Guardian) or
2 (Perimeter Captain, once per blocking defender) or 1 (a lifelink blocker), and Sanguine Bond
turns each of those gains into that much off YOUR life.
  - Attack when A is at least 3 times B plus 3. At that width the unblocked damage beats every
    life they can gain from the blocks, and it keeps beating it every turn after.
  - Below that, answer "ATTACK: none", and spend the window making more tokens. A is the number
    you grow; B is the number they have to keep paying for.
  - Lethal overrides both branches: if the total power of what you can send is at or above their
    printed life, send it all - a life gain that happens after they are dead is not a life gain.
This is the ONE place in this file where a damage total does override a "do not attack" rule,
and it overrides it only in BRANCH B, only on the printed life number, and only for lethal.
YOUR ATTACKER TAGS CANNOT SEE THIS. The "[their untapped blockers: ...]" tag reads
"(neither dies)" and that is TRUE - the tag counts deaths, it does not count life, and the
life is what kills you. Twelve attackers into three of these walls took this deck from 20 life
to 8 in one swing and from 8 to dead in the next, while the opponent went down by nine.
An arithmetic answer of the form "we deal 30 and they are at 11, so we win" is NOT an
exception to Check 1. That sum is what you had both times you died here. You do not survive
to collect it. Damage they will never take does not outrank life you will certainly lose.

CHECK 2 - LIGHTMINE FIELD. If their battlefield line names a permanent that damages each
attacker by the number of attackers - Lightmine Field on deck125's board says exactly that -
find the SMALLEST toughness among the creatures you would send and send that many minus one,
biggest power first. With 2/2 Vampires that is ONE: "ATTACK: Vampire #1" and nothing after the
name. This deck sent two into it twice in one game under a plan that had already priced the
trade as bad.

CHECK 3 - THE COLLAPSED TAG AND THE "GANG BLOCK:" CLAUSE. On a wide board their side stops
being listed blocker by blocker and the tag reads instead "they have N untapped creatures that
could block this one, biggest X (you kill it, your attacker lives)", sometimes with a clause
"- GANG BLOCK: their 6 biggest together deal 7, enough to kill this attacker; each result above
is a LONE blocker only".
That parenthesis prices ONE blocker. The GANG BLOCK clause prices what actually happens: any
number of them may block the same attacker together, and your attacker's toughness is measured
against the SUM of their bodies.
  - The tag prints a "GANG BLOCK: ... enough to kill this attacker" clause, or N is 2 or more:
    a TOKEN goes anyway - a 1/1 Human or a 2/2 Vampire costs you nothing and you make more next
    turn - and a CARD stays home. The cards are Thraben Doomsayer, Bloodline Keeper, Lord of
    Lineage and anything wearing Lightning Greaves.
  - N is 1 and no GANG BLOCK clause: the parenthesis is the whole story; use it.
"But the biggest one dies to me" is the sentence the clause exists to refuse: they do not have
to block with the biggest one alone, and against your one real body they will not.
The lethal check overrides this: when the printed opponent life is at or below the total power
of everything you are sending, send it all.
CHECK 3 does not end your answer. Cross the cards it names off the list and go on to CHECK 4.

CHECK 4 - THE COUNT, AND WHAT ONE BLOCKER CAN DO. ONE BLOCKER BLOCKS EXACTLY ONE ATTACKER.
A single 5/5 flier on their side does not "block everything" - it blocks ONE of your creatures
and the rest connect. Count the creatures on the "Your creatures that can attack:" list, then
count their UNTAPPED creatures on their battlefield line. If your number is bigger, you have
that many unblocked attackers and Check 5 applies. Outnumbering them is NOT protection for any
one creature: THEY choose which attacker each blocker takes, and they choose the one their
block wins. It tells you damage gets through; it never tells you a card of yours is safe. Answer "ATTACK: none" on the count alone
only when their untapped creature count is EQUAL TO OR HIGHER THAN the number you are sending
and no attacker tag reads "you kill it, your blocker lives".

CHECK 5 - SEND EVERYTHING. "Your creatures that can attack:" has already dropped every creature
that cannot legally attack, so do not re-check summoning sickness or tapped status yourself.
Send every creature on that list.
Know what you are spending: with Intruder Alarm on the battlefield NOTHING untaps in your untap
step - every battlefield line says so, "[doesn't untap during its controller's untap step]".
Attackers come back untapped only when a creature ENTERS the battlefield, which for this deck
means an untapped token-maker tapping for a token. So before you send everything, find a
token-maker on your battlefield line. If one is there, the whole team is back the moment you
make your next token and Check 5 costs you nothing. If NO token-maker is on your battlefield,
the creatures you send stay tapped until one arrives, and they are not blockers next turn -
send them anyway when the attack advances your clock, but do not plan a block with them.
One hold-back only: do not send a LONE Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper - or your ONLY
creature of any kind - into a board that can block and kill it. Its own row says when:
"[their untapped blockers: ... (both die)]" or "(your attacker dies)" on that line means
"ATTACK: none". This deck traded a lone Doomsayer for a Siege-Gang Commander with twenty Humans
summoning sick behind it, and at 4 life sent its only Human into "(your attacker dies, their
blocker lives)" under a plan that said its death would "untap all creatures" - nothing untaps
for a creature LEAVING. Send the maker once tokens are attacking beside it.

Blocking, in this order. Step 0 decides whether you HAVE a blocker; steps 1 and 2 pick WHICH
creature blocks; the rest decide whether to block at all.
 - STEP 0 - A "B" ROW IS A BLOCKER. If any row on the screen begins "B1.", you have a blocker,
   and its "may block A1, A2" list is the legality list. "We have no blockers" and "I have no
   way to block" are false whenever that row is printed - this deck wrote both under a B1 row,
   once declining a free kill at 16 life and once at 4 life under "LETHAL if it all connects".
   When the header says LETHAL, the
   FIRST line of your answer is "BLOCKS: B1:A<the attacker with the largest deals N>" - write
   that line before you write anything else, then add blockers by the LETHAL rule below.
   A row on a token-maker reading "(you kill it, your blocker lives)" is a free kill: take it.
 - STEP 1 - WHICH BODY. Every blocker row on the screen is one of your creatures. Spend a TOKEN
   ("Human", "Vampire" - no mana cost printed on the row) before you ever spend a CARD, and
   spend Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper LAST, after every token is used. Tokens are free
   and you make more every turn; the maker is the only reason you make any.
 - STEP 2 - READ THE PARENTHESIS ON THE ROW YOU CHOOSE. "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)"
   means that creature dies and the attacker does not. It is the engine's computed result and
   it is never wrong. Do not write "block X with Bloodline Keeper to kill it" against a row
   that says the Keeper dies and X lives - this deck did exactly that at 12 life to prevent
   three damage, made no token for the rest of the game, and lost with the opponent on 4 life.
   If the row on your maker says "(your blocker dies, attacker lives)", the maker does not
   block.
 - WHEN THE PROMPT SAYS LETHAL, ASSIGN TO THE BIGGEST ATTACKERS FIRST. The line
   "Unblocked, these attackers deal up to N - you would be at ... - LETHAL if it all connects"
   is the trigger. Every attacker row prints "deals N". Sort those numbers, put your first
   blocker on the LARGEST one, your second on the next largest, and keep going until you run
   out of blockers or out of attackers. Blocking their smallest attacker is the one assignment
   that is always wrong here: with 10 life against 3+3+3+1, blocking the 1 leaves you at 1 and
   blocking a 3 leaves you at 3, and the difference is a whole turn of the game.
 - MENACE. An attacker row tagged "[menace ...]" is NOT blocked by one creature - a lone
   blocker on it does nothing at all and the attacker's full damage still lands. Put TWO
   blockers on it or none. Never spend your best blocker alone on a menace attacker; the
   prompt says so on the row and it is telling the truth.
 - Add up the damage of every attacker with no blocker on it. If that total is your life total
   minus 4 or more, put a blocker on EVERY attacker - biggest attacker first, Thraben Doomsayer
   and Bloodline Keeper included - and keep assigning until no attacker is left unblocked. A
   token-maker you keep alive at 1 life makes tokens for a game you already lost.
   This rule spends EVERY blocker you have, including your last one: one blocker still stops one
   attacker's whole damage. Assign until you run out of blockers or of unblocked attackers.
 - A "GANG BLOCK: any 2 of yours together deal N, enough to kill this attacker" clause on an
   attacker with "deals 4" or more, while the header's "you would be at" is 6 or less: put the
   counted blockers on it, token first, then a Keeper. At 10 life this deck read that clause
   on Nadaar (4/4), answered "BLOCKS: none" with a Keeper and a Vampire on the screen, and went
   to 4.
 - Otherwise your token-makers do NOT block. Take the damage. A blocker line reading "(both
   die)" on Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper is a trade you decline while the combat
   header's "you would be at N" is above 5 - the maker is worth more than any one attacker.
 - A TOKEN IS NOT A MAKER. With a maker on your battlefield line you remake a token next
   window, so a Human or Vampire in front of the largest "deals N" costs you nothing, and when a
   "DRAW PUNISHERS" line is on the screen every point it keeps is a draw you can afford. At 11
   life with "B1. Human" and "B2. Vampire" up this deck answered "BLOCKS: none" to one 3-power
   attacker and the draw steps that followed cost 2 life a card from 8.

=== MULLIGAN ===
STEP 1 - WHAT DOES THE NEXT LOOK COST? Read the ask's own price line for the "(keeping N)"
number. Each look is a fresh seven you must shave by one more card at the SAME land odds, so a
mulligan buys a look, never a fix. At (keeping 5) or lower, ONE land in front of you is a keep:
the bottom ask sheds the uncastable cards and leaves you the land plus your cheapest spells.
Colour is not a reason to ship at any N. Zero lands is the one hand a fresh look is worth the
card for; a hand of nothing but lands is the other. WHAT THIS COST: three mulligans in one
game, a four-card keep, both ways to find a token-maker bottomed, no creature ever on the
battlefield, lost 42 to 0.
STEP 2 - on your untouched opening seven, or at "(keeping 6)", a mulligan is a fair trade only
by the comparison below.

The two deck facts that decide every mulligan here: you run TWENTY-FOUR lands in sixty cards,
and the cards that actually win - Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w}, Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b},
Intruder Alarm {2}{u} - cost three or four mana. So the question is never "can I play on turn
one", it is "will this hand have three or four lands by the turn it needs them, and does it do
something when they arrive".
The comparison to make, on the header line "Your hand (7 cards), counted by the engine: N
lands, M spells" (read that number, never recount):
 - TWO OR MORE LANDS AND AT LEAST ONE SPELL: keep. Full stop, at every "(keeping N)". With
   twenty-four lands the third arrives in the first few draws and the fourth by about turn
   five, and a six-card hand after shipping a two-lander is most often a two-lander again
   with one fewer card. NEITHER COLOUR NOR THE COVERAGE LINE IS A REASON TO SHIP A TWO-LANDER. "Playing every land in this hand
   would not cover any spell in it" is a KEEP at two lands - it says your hand is a turn-three
   hand, not a dead one, and the third land fixes it; a seven shipped on that line was played
   out of a five-card hand. The coverage line decides ONE-land hands and nothing else, and it
   holds at THREE lands too: last corpus this deck kept a three-land six reading "would not
   cover any spell in it (you have 3 lands)" and won that game on turn 10.
 - ONE LAND: read the coverage line. "would cover the cost of:" naming a card is a keep - the
   second land is in your next two draws about two times in three (23 lands in 53), and the
   ship buys a fresh seven that is a one-lander or worse about one time in seven. "would not
   cover any spell in it" ships on the untouched seven AND at "(keeping 6)": nothing happens
   until a draw, and one card fewer does not change that. Colour and what the spells cost are
   not part of this call. Both mulligans this deck took last corpus were exactly this shape,
   both kept a three-land six, and one of them won.
 - ZERO LANDS, or ALL lands: ship.
 - SIX OR MORE LANDS with one spell: this is a keepable hand, not a mulligan. You bottom the
   extra lands on the keep and you are a four-land hand with your removal spell, which is a
   functioning hand. Shipping it costs you a card and gives you a fresh seven that may be worse.
 - AT FIVE CARDS OR FEWER: STEP 1's odds apply - a hand with a land and a spell in it is a keep,
   whatever that spell costs, because the ship buys the same land odds with one card fewer.

BOTTOMING - the same decision, second ask. After you keep, the prompt says "you took N
mulligans and kept, so you must put N cards from your hand on the BOTTOM" and lists your hand.
This ask is where the hand is actually BUILT: read "You will be left with an N-card hand" and
build the best N. Keep, in this PREFERENCE ORDER, and bottom what is left over - the order
works at any N because it is an order, not a set of floors:
 1. the lands that cover the cheapest spell you are keeping - at N of 3 that is two lands and
    one spell, never three lands; at N of 5 or 6 it is three or four lands and the spells they
    cover, up to FOUR mana;
 2. a token-maker - Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper - if the hand has one;
 3. Idyllic Tutor, which FINDS Intruder Alarm, or Vision Skeins, which digs;
 4. removal;
 5. Intruder Alarm LAST. An Alarm with no maker on the battlefield does nothing at all, and a
    small hand cannot afford a card that waits.
A hand of nothing but lands does nothing, and a hand of nothing but four-drops does nothing for
four turns; the order above avoids both at every N. Five lands and two Devour Flesh at N of 6:
the fifth land goes under, not a Devour - four lands cover everything this deck casts.
A LAND THAT MAKES MANA IS NEVER THE BOTTOM WHILE A CARD OUTSIDE THAT ORDER IS IN THE HAND.
Lightning Greaves is not on the list above - it is the leftover, and the leftover is what goes
under. At (keeping 6) this deck held Tundra, Tundra, Marsh Flats, Idyllic Tutor, Lightning
Greaves, Tragic Slip and Bloodline Keeper and bottomed a Tundra; Marsh Flats makes no mana, so
that keep was a ONE-source hand, and the Keeper it was built around was never cast.
Bottom a way to find or make a creature only after everything below it in the order is gone.
This deck kept a four-card hand of Marsh
Flats, Tundra, Swamp and Intruder Alarm - it bottomed Idyllic Tutor, Vision Skeins and
Damnation, i.e. both of its ways to find a maker and its only sweeper - and lost that game
without a creature ever hitting its battlefield.

  it - a fresh seven shaved to four is the same land odds with one card fewer. The plan shown
  above that ask was written about a hand you already threw away; it is not evidence about
  this one.
- Your plan names a spell and "Mana available: N total" is smaller than that spell's mana count

- "Cast Lightning Greaves {2}" is listed and your battlefield line reads "of which 0 are
