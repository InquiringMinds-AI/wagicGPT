INTRUDERS OF THRABEN - WBU TOKEN COMBO

You win by putting Intruder Alarm onto the battlefield next to Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline
Keeper, then tapping that creature for a token over and over: every token that enters untaps
all creatures, so the tap ability comes straight back. Your removal buys the turns; the tokens
end the game. Every turn is the same three actions: play a land, deploy a token-maker or
Intruder Alarm, then tap every token-maker you own until your board is an army.

=== RULE 1 - THE TOKEN LINES, AND WHEN TO STOP TAKING THEM ===
These are the three lines, exactly as you will see them. A second copy of the same card adds a
handle - "Thraben Doomsayer #1", "Thraben Doomsayer #2" - same line, same rule:
  "Create human with Thraben Doomsayer [cost: Tap]"
  "Create vampire with Bloodline Keeper [cost: Tap]"
  "Create vampire with Lord of Lineage [cost: Tap]"
BEFORE you take one, read your creature count. Use the FIRST source that is on the screen:
  1. the option line's own note, "[repeat: ... you control M creatures]" - M is your count.
  2. no such note on the line? Then your battlefield line: "Your battlefield (K permanents
     listed, of which M are creatures)" - that M is your count, printed in every window.
     When the count is one it reads "of which 1 is a creature".
The note belongs to ONE COPY and ONE TURN: each copy's first two activations of a turn carry no
note, so with two makers out four activations a turn have none. The battlefield line always does.
Then, and only then:
  - M is 11 or less: take the line. Take it again every time it comes back.
  - M is 12 or more: answer 0 (pass). Take nothing from this list, this window.
The 12 counts creatures ON YOUR BATTLEFIELD. It does not reset when a new turn starts, it does
not reset when the "[repeat:]" note disappears, and "I have tapped it 0 times this turn" is not
a reason to start again. Twelve creatures already standing is the whole of the reason to stop.
ONE exception: if the opponent has attackers on the board whose damage would take you to 0 this
turn, keep making tokens past 12 - each new one is another blocker, and a board you never get
to use is worth nothing.
Timing: the tokens you make CANNOT attack until your next turn, so making them early in your own
turn buys you nothing this turn and leaves them standing through the opponent's whole turn. Fire
the chain on the OPPONENT'S turn - the lines are offered there, in their upkeep, their main phase
and their attack step. A token made then blocks that same turn and attacks on your very next turn,
and a sweeper cast on their turn catches fewer of them.
Fire on your OWN turn only when the creatures you are about to attack with are already on the
board, or when the opponent has no untapped land left.
A token you make this turn CANNOT ATTACK this turn. Its battlefield line says so:
"[summoning sick - cannot attack this turn, but CAN block]". It can block right away, and it
attacks on your NEXT turn.
So when you plan, count only the creatures whose lines do NOT say "summoning sick". A plan that
says "attack with all 12 for lethal" is wrong on the turn you made those 12 - the attack list
will offer you one creature. Make the tokens anyway; they block this turn and they swing next
turn.

=== RULE 2 - MARSH FLATS TAKES A DUAL, NOT A BASIC ===
When lines beginning "Put in Play with Marsh Flats targeting" appear, answer in this order and
stop at the first one that is listed:
  1. "targeting Underground Sea"  - makes {U} and {B}
  2. "targeting Tundra"           - makes {W} and {U}
  3. "targeting Scrubland"        - makes {W} and {B}
  4. any basic
Underground Sea does everything a Swamp does AND makes blue; Tundra does everything a Plains
does AND makes blue. Both come in untapped. There is nothing a basic gives you that they do not.
Why this decides games: Intruder Alarm costs {2}{u} and Vision Skeins costs {1}{u}, and only
10 of your 24 lands make blue. Fetch a basic and your combo piece sits in your hand for the
rest of the game.

=== RULE 3 - COUNT BOTH BOARDS BEFORE DAMNATION ===
Damnation destroys ALL creatures, yours included. Both battlefield lines print a creature count
right after the words "of which" - read YOUR number and THEIR number, and compare them.
Cast Damnation when THEIR number is bigger than yours. That is the whole test.
Equal numbers are not a reason to cast it: keep the Damnation and take another action.
When YOUR number is the bigger one, spend the window on a creature, a token activation or a
removal spell instead, and keep the Damnation - your creatures are how you win, and a wipe that
kills two of yours to kill one of theirs has handed them the game.
Your number is 0 and theirs is 2 or more: that is the best Damnation there is. Take it.

=== RULE 4 - TRAGIC SLIP KILLS TOUGHNESS 1 ===
Tragic Slip gives -1/-1. That kills a creature whose battlefield line shows toughness 1 - a
(2/1), a (1/1), a (0/1). Against anything with toughness 2 or more it shrinks the creature and
it lives.
Pick the target with toughness 1. If several are listed, take the one with the biggest power.
The one upgrade: under the CURRENT turn header in the GAME LOG, a line ending in "died" means
Tragic Slip gives -13/-13 instead and kills anything on the list -
then take the target with the biggest printed power on the battlefield line.
No toughness-1 target on the list and no "died" line under this turn: answer "Cast nothing
right now" and keep the Slip. It costs {B} and it will kill something on a later turn.

=== RULE 5 - THE TWO EDICTS ARE DIFFERENT CARDS ===
"Cast Tribute to Hunger {2}{b}" - the opponent sacrifices a creature and YOU gain life equal to
its toughness. This is the one you want.
Cast it at the opponent whenever it is listed and their battlefield line shows exactly ONE
creature. With two or more of their creatures listed, they hand over their worst one and keep the
good one - take Tragic Slip, Damnation or a token activation instead.
"Cast Devour Flesh {1}{b}" - the player you name sacrifices a creature AND THAT SAME PLAYER
gains the life. Naming "The opponent (player, life" gives them the life, so use Devour Flesh
on the opponent when Tribute to Hunger is not on the list and they have exactly ONE creature -
then the sacrifice takes their only body and the life they gain is worth it.
Before any edict, run this in order: (1) find "of which N are creatures" on the OPPONENT'S
battlefield line. N is the only number that matters. (2) If N is not exactly 1, do not cast
an edict - not at 0, not at 2 or more. (3) A creature listed under "ON THE STACK, waiting to
resolve" is NOT on the battlefield and does not change N. Naming a creature spell on the
stack in your PLAN is the mistake itself: at N=0 the opponent sacrifices nothing and you have
thrown away the card.
Never cast an edict when the opponent's life is at or below the life it gives them. Killing
their last creature and moving them from 1 life to 4 costs you a game you had already won.
Do not spend an edict on a creature that is not hurting you. If their only creature is a
defender, or has power 0 or 1, or its line says it cannot attack, keep the edict and take
another action - you paid a card and handed them life equal to its toughness to remove
something that was never going to kill you.
Both edicts give life to the player who sacrifices. If the OPPONENT'S deck list contains a card
that rewards them for gaining life, "Cast nothing right now" beats either edict at the
opponent.
Both cards let the SACRIFICING player pick, so with TWO OR MORE of their creatures listed no
edict is ever the play: they hand you their worst. A board of two Fate Unravelers (3/4) and a
Fog Bank (0/2 defender) gives up the Fog Bank and gains 2 life, and both Unravelers stay. Count
the number printed after "of which" on the OPPONENT'S battlefield line. If it is not exactly 1,
take Tragic Slip, Damnation or a token activation instead.
"Yourself (player, life" is the right answer only when the creature you give up is a Human
or Vampire TOKEN you can remake next turn. Your Bloodline Keeper, your Thraben Doomsayer and
your flying Vampires stay on the battlefield.

=== RULE 6 - EQUIP LIGHTNING GREAVES ONCE PER GREAVES ===
An equip line for Lightning Greaves costs {0}, so the game re-offers it at every window.
Take it ONE time per Greaves, on Bloodline Keeper if
it is on the battlefield, otherwise on Thraben Doomsayer.
The done-signal is on the battlefield line: a creature reading "{attached: Lightning Greaves
#1}" already has it, and that Greaves is finished. Count your Greaves: one equip each, no more.
At every later re-offer, take a "[cost: Tap]" line if one is listed, otherwise
answer 0 (pass) and let combat happen - your remaining windows belong to tokens and removal.
Equip again only when the equipped creature has DIED, or a token-maker that has never been
equipped has just arrived.
Greaves gives haste, so a token-maker can tap for a token the turn it lands, and shroud, which
means that creature can no longer be targeted by anything - including your own spells.

=== WHAT TO CAST WHEN SEVERAL THINGS ARE LISTED ===
Take the highest entry on this list that appears in your casting menu:
 1. "Cast Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b}" - when no token-maker is on your battlefield.
 2. "Cast Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w}" - when no token-maker is on your battlefield.
 3. "Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}" - when no Intruder Alarm is on your battlefield.
    Intruder Alarm on an empty battlefield does NOTHING. A token-maker with no Alarm still
    makes one token a turn. The maker goes first, every time.
 4. "Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" - when no Intruder Alarm is on your battlefield or in your hand.
 5. "Cast Lightning Greaves {2}" - ONLY when a token-maker is already on your battlefield.
    Read your own battlefield line: "of which 0 are creatures" means the Greaves equips
    nothing and does nothing. Cast a removal spell or answer "Cast nothing right now" and
    keep the Greaves for the turn a maker lands.
 6. A removal spell, by Rules 3, 4 and 5.
 7. "Cast Vision Skeins {1}{u}".

=== INTRUDER ALARM AND IDYLLIC TUTOR ===
"Cast Intruder Alarm {2}{u}": it is the engine, and it is entry 3 on the cast list above -
behind both token-makers, because an Alarm with no maker out does nothing.
One copy does the whole job. With an Intruder Alarm already on your battlefield, spend the
window on a creature or a removal spell instead.
It is symmetric: no creature untaps in its controller's untap step, theirs or yours, and ANY
creature entering - theirs or yours - untaps everything. With a token-maker in play that is
pure profit. With no token-maker in play, a creature of yours that attacks stays tapped, so get
Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper down before you rely on attacking.
"Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" searches for an enchantment. The answer on the reveal list is
Intruder Alarm, every time. Cast the Alarm the next window it appears in your list.

=== KEY CARDS ===
Thraben Doomsayer {1}{w}{w} (2/2) - "{T}: Put a 1/1 white Human creature token onto the
  battlefield". Cast it by the cast list above. Fateful hour: while YOUR life is 5 or less,
  every OTHER creature you control gets +2/+2, so your 1/1 Humans arrive as 3/3s and your
  battlefield line shows "(3/3) (printed 1/1)". Low life makes your tokens bigger, not smaller.
Bloodline Keeper {2}{b}{b} (3/3) flying - "{T}: Put a 2/2 black Vampire creature token with
  flying". Your best creature. Cast it by the cast list above.
  When "Transform:backside with Bloodline Keeper [cost: {b}]" appears in your list, take it:
  it becomes Lord of Lineage, a 5/5 that gives every other Vampire +2/+2 - your 2/2 tokens
  become 4/4s - and it still makes a token every turn with the same tap. If that line is not
  listed, nothing changes; keep tapping the Keeper.
  Transforming needs {B} and five or more Vampires. Casting the Keeper off exactly four lands
  taps you out, and Intruder Alarm untaps CREATURES, not lands - so the "Transform:backside with
  Bloodline Keeper [cost: {b}]" line will not appear again that turn. When you can cast the
  Keeper and still leave one black source untapped, do it: Lord of Lineage gives every OTHER
  Vampire +2/+2, which turns eleven 2/2 tokens into eleven 4/4s.
Vision Skeins {1}{u} - EACH player draws two cards, you and them.
  Cast it only when you have no token-maker and no Intruder Alarm you could cast this window -
  it is your dig, not your default. They draw two as well, so it is the last card you cast, never
  the first.
  When the OPPONENT'S battlefield holds any card that draws them extra cards or punishes card
  draw - Howling Mine and Liliana's Caress on deck162's board both count - do not cast Vision
  Skeins at all.
  When the opponent's battlefield holds a permanent that damages or drains a player for drawing
  cards, take a different action - that permanent turns your own draw spell against you.
Lightning Greaves {2} - see Rule 6.

=== COMBAT ===
ATTACK - four checks, in this order. Stop at the first one that answers and do what it says.

CHECK 1 - THE LIFEGAIN WALL BOARD. Read the "Opponent battlefield" line for these four names:
PERIMETER CAPTAIN, PRIDE GUARDIAN, SANGUINE BOND, EXQUISITE BLOOD. If ANY of them is on that
line, answer "ATTACK: none" - this window and every attack window after it - until a Tragic
Slip, a Damnation or an edict has taken the LAST of them off that line.
Why, in the numbers the render gives you: Perimeter Captain gains them 2 life for EVERY
creature with defender that blocks, and each Captain triggers for each blocking defender;
Pride Guardian gains them 3 more when IT blocks; and every Sanguine Bond turns every point
they gain into a point you LOSE. Three defenders blocking with two Captains and two Bonds is
twenty-four life off YOU in one combat step, from an attack in which nothing of yours died.
YOUR ATTACKER TAGS CANNOT SEE THIS. The "[their untapped blockers: ...]" tag reads
"(neither dies)" and that is TRUE - the tag counts deaths, it does not count life, and the
life is what kills you. Twelve attackers into three of these walls took this deck from 20 life
to 8 in one swing and from 8 to dead in the next, while the opponent went down by nine.
An arithmetic answer of the form "we deal 30 and they are at 11, so we win" is NOT an
exception to Check 1. That sum is what you had both times you died here. You do not survive
to collect it. Damage they will never take does not outrank life you will certainly lose.

CHECK 2 - LIGHTMINE FIELD. If their battlefield line names a permanent that damages each
attacker by the number of attackers - Lightmine Field on deck125's board says exactly that -
find the SMALLEST toughness among the creatures you would send and send that many minus one,
biggest power first. Leave the rest home.

CHECK 3 - THE COUNT, AND WHAT ONE BLOCKER CAN DO. ONE BLOCKER BLOCKS EXACTLY ONE ATTACKER.
A single 5/5 flier on their side does not "block everything" - it blocks ONE of your creatures
and the rest connect. Count the creatures on the "Your creatures that can attack:" list, then
count their UNTAPPED creatures on their battlefield line. If your number is bigger, you have
that many unblocked attackers and Check 4 applies. Answer "ATTACK: none" on the count alone
only when their untapped creature count is EQUAL TO OR HIGHER THAN the number you are sending
and no attacker tag reads "you kill it, your blocker lives".

CHECK 4 - SEND EVERYTHING. "Your creatures that can attack:" has already dropped every creature
that cannot legally attack, so do not re-check summoning sickness or tapped status yourself.
Send every creature on that list. One hold-back only: do not send a LONE Thraben Doomsayer or
Bloodline Keeper into a board that can block and kill it - the maker is the engine, the tokens
are the attack. Send it once tokens are attacking beside it.

Blocking, in this order:
 - Block with your Human and Vampire TOKENS first - they are free and you make more every turn.
 - WHEN THE PROMPT SAYS LETHAL, ASSIGN TO THE BIGGEST ATTACKERS FIRST. The line
   "Unblocked, these attackers deal up to N - you would be at ... - LETHAL if it all connects"
   is the trigger. Every attacker row prints "deals N". Sort those numbers, put your first
   blocker on the LARGEST one, your second on the next largest, and keep going until you run
   out of blockers or out of attackers. Blocking their smallest attacker is the one assignment
   that is always wrong here: with 10 life against 3+3+3+1, blocking the 1 leaves you at 1 and
   blocking a 3 leaves you at 3, and the difference is a whole turn of the game.
 - MENACE. An attacker row tagged "[menace ...]" is NOT blocked by one creature - a lone
   blocker on it does nothing at all and the attacker's full damage still lands. Put TWO
   blockers on it or none. Never spend your best blocker alone on a menace attacker; the
   prompt says so on the row and it is telling the truth.
 - Add up the damage of every attacker with no blocker on it. If that total is your life total
   minus 4 or more, put a blocker on EVERY attacker - biggest attacker first, Thraben Doomsayer
   and Bloodline Keeper included - and keep assigning until no attacker is left unblocked. A
   token-maker you keep alive at 1 life makes tokens for a game you already lost.
   This rule spends EVERY blocker you have, including your last one. One blocker on one attacker
   still stops that attacker's whole damage - "I only have one, so it does not matter" is how you
   go from 10 life to 2 in one combat step. Assign blockers until you run out of blockers or run
   out of unblocked attackers, whichever comes first.
 - Otherwise your token-makers do NOT block. Take the damage. A blocker line reading "(both
   die)" on Thraben Doomsayer or Bloodline Keeper is a trade you decline while your life is
   comfortable - the maker is worth more than any one attacker.

=== MULLIGAN ===
KEEP any 7-card hand with two or more lands. The prompt counts them for you on the line that
begins "Your hand (7 cards), counted by the engine:". Read that land number, do not recount.
Never keep fewer than 5 cards.
Your cheapest card costs ONE mana, so "no early play" is never a reason to ship a two-land hand.

=== DECIDING SITUATIONS ===
- A line ending in "[cost: Tap]" is in your list AND your creature count is 11 or less: take it,
  this window, whatever your mana says, on either player's turn. Count 12 or more: answer 0.
- You just took that line and it appears again: read the count again first. 11 or less, take it
  again. 12 or more, stop - and it stays stopped on later turns and after the "[repeat:]" note
  has gone.
- "Put in Play with Marsh Flats targeting Underground Sea" is listed: take it. Otherwise
  "targeting Tundra". Otherwise "targeting Scrubland".
- Several casts are listed at once: answer with the highest entry on the cast list above that
  appears in your menu.
- "Cast Idyllic Tutor {2}{w}" is taken: choose Intruder Alarm on the reveal list.
- "Transform:backside with Bloodline Keeper [cost: {b}]" is listed: take it.
- "Cast Damnation {2}{b}{b}" is listed: compare the two creature counts printed after "of which".
  Theirs bigger - cast it. Yours bigger - take another action and keep it.
- "Cast Tragic Slip {b}" is listed: take a target whose line shows toughness 1, or any target if
  a "died" line sits under this turn's header. Otherwise "Cast nothing right now".
- An edict is listed and they have exactly one creature: Tribute to Hunger at the opponent
  first, Devour Flesh at "The opponent" only if Tribute is not listed. Naming "Yourself" is for
  giving up a token, nothing else.
- "Equip with Lightning Greaves targeting" is listed and no creature reads "{attached: Lightning
  Greaves}": equip your best token-maker. Every re-offer after that: 0 (pass).
- Their battlefield line names Perimeter Captain, Pride Guardian, Sanguine Bond or Exquisite
  Blood and it is your Attackers step: "ATTACK: none". No damage total overrides this.
- Their battlefield line names ONE untapped creature and your attack list holds five or more:
  send them all. One blocker stops one attacker.
- The blockers prompt says LETHAL: your first blocker goes on the attacker with the largest
  "deals N", not on the smallest one you can kill.
- An attacker row says "[menace]": two blockers on it, or none.
